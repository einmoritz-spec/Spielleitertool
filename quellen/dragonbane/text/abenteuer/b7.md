## INTRODUCTION
elcome to *Path of Glory*, a classic campaign for *Dragonbane* that was first published in 1985. *Path of Glory* consists of three adven­ tures – *The Dead Forest*, *Gates of Power*, and *Heart of* *Darkness*, all of which are included in this book. The three adventures must be played in order.
*Path of Glory* is set in the region around the great mountain range of Heaven’s Rim in the harsh north of the world. To the west of the mountains lies the legendary Dead Forest, and on the other side the plains of the East­ ern Lands open to the forest Fang-­Forren, the lake Is-­Anor, and the foaming Sea of Dreams.
These areas are shown on the large map that is included in this book. One side of the map shows the Dead Forest and may be shared with the players at the start of the cam­ paign. The other side depicts the Eastern Lands and should be kept hidden from the players until their characters find it during the third adventure, *Heart of Darkness*. The maps are also printed on the inside of the covers.

## HISTORY
A thousand years ago, the demon prince Sathmog came to the world of *Dragonbane*. With his herald Azrahel Koth (see *The Secret of the Dragon Emperor* campaign in the *Dragonbane* Core Set), Sathmog forged a demonic realm in the world of humans that lasted two centuries. It was a time of darkness and evil, but also one of incredibly rapid human expansion at the expense of the firstborn peoples – dwarves, elves, orcs, and halflings.
The opposition to Sathmog grew, and one day a young hero named Eledain managed to summon the ancient dragons charged with guarding the world from demonic influence. Mounted on dragonback, with the elder folk as his allies, Eledain finally defeated Sathmog. On a hill in the depths of the forest, the demon’s physical form in our world was destroyed, but as his final act he cast a curse that would spread a sickness throughout the world and one day be its undoing.
One of Sathmog’s secret servants, the dwarf prince Kalin who had been driven into the demon’s service by envy and resentment of his sister, Queen Gwinna, man­ aged to save the demon’s dark heart from destruction. As long as the Heart of Darkness remained intact, the curse of the Dead Forest could not be broken.
### THE COUNCIL OF THE FIRSTBORN
Sathmog was defeated, but human expansion continued.
The firstborn peoples watched in horror as humanity left a changed world in its wake. In their eagerness to out-­shout each other, the humans became blind to what they were trampling, razing, breaking, and burning.
Dwarves, elves, orcs, and halflings gathered one last time in a council where they decided to go their separate ways and return to their old realms. However, some indi­ viduals embraced the new era and stayed behind to make a home for themselves in humanity’s new civilization.
Queen Gwinna of the dwarves decided to bring her people back to her underground kingdom and close its gates to the outside world. These Gates of Power were locked with three magical keys – a seashell horn and two large pearls, one black and one white – which were given to the returning elves, orcs, and halflings.
The elves forged a pendant with a poem explaining how the keys worked. If the need ever arose, the four firstborn peoples would gather again. With all three keys, the Gates of Power could be reopened.
The gates were sealed and the years passed. But one by one, the three key-­bearers fell victim to wicked humans who feared the elder folk. The keys that would reunite the firstborn fell into human hands and the knowledge of their purpose was lost. But in time, perhaps by a twist of fate, the keys that had been scattered to the winds found their way back to the area around the Gates of Power.

### THE DWARVEN PRINCE
The kingdom behind the Gates of Power did not last long, for the dwarves had taken the evil of Sathmog with them into the depths of the mountain. Prince Kalin, Queen Gwinna’s brother, had brought the demon’s dark heart in a case and its evil soon spread through the deep halls.
More and more dwarven minds were darkened by the demonic energies and Kalin’s band of rebels grew. The conflict soon escalated into a ruthless civil war between Gwinna’s forces and Kalin’s followers. The battle ended in disaster as Kalin, in an act of desperation, destroyed much of the underground city and its means of sustenance.
The dwarven kingdom behind the Gates of Power fell and its few survivors fled, including Kalin himself. With the Heart of Darkness in his possession, Kalin reached the lands east of Heaven’s Rim, where he spread the teachings of Sathmog among the villages. Upon his death many years later, he was laid to rest with the Heart of Darkness in a temple tower on Wolf Peaks in the dark forest of Fang-­ Forren. The troll witch Morgasa vowed to watch over it until the end of time.
### THE THREE MAGES
Eight hundred years passed and the stories of the Dead Forest, the Gates of Power, and the Heart of Darkness became myth and legend, all but forgotten – until the day three apprentice mages in the southern city of Arkand happened upon an ancient record of the story.
The three young mages – the elementalist Stormhand and the two siblings Doriath and Dakoth, mentalist and necromancer respectively – realized the riches that might still be found behind the magical gates and the potential power of an artifact such as the Heart of Darkness.
However, they made the mistake of believing that the Heart of Darkness had remained in the dwarven realm and became obsessed with finding the way there, which they came to refer to as “the path to wealth and glory,” or in short: the Path of Glory.
Instead of working together, an intense rivalry arose between the three mages, a struggle to show who could find the Path of Glory first. This tore their friendship apart, and after completing their studies they continued the search separately.

### STORMHAND’S TRAVELS
Initially, Stormhand was the most successful. He settled near the Dead Forest, where legend claimed Sathmog had been banished, and used his magic to create an under­ ground dwelling from which he made trips into the forest.
In Deepmire he met the hermit Ulat-­A’ar who told him where to find the Gates of Power and the three keys required to open them. The hermit also had in his possession the elven pendant that explained how to use the keys, but kept it secret as he doubted Stormhand’s intentions.
Many years later, having almost given up the search, Stormhand chanced upon one of the keys – the seashell horn. He resumed his quest for the two pearls, but made the mistake of bragging about his find. Eventually the news reached both Doriath and Dakoth, who had also put aside the search for the Path of Glory. ## OVERVIEW
Below is an overview of what might happen in the three adventures *The Dead Forest*, *Gates of Power*, and *Heart of* *Darkness*. Naturally, the course of events depends largely on the player characters’ actions.
### THE DEAD FOREST
The player characters are adventurers and fortune hunters who arrive in the Dead Forest, lured by rumors of the riches supposedly waiting behind the Gates of Power – the entrance to an ancient and long-­lost dwarven kingdom in the area.
Legend has it that the key to lifting the curse placed on the Dead Forest by Sathmog can also be found behind the Gates of Power, but opening them requires three other keys, lost in the mists of time.
In their search for the three keys, the player characters can explore everything from ancient temples and tombs to deep swamps and filthy hobgoblin caves. They will encounter both friends and foes along the way, such as the minotaur brothers Grim & Grum, the hobgoblin chief Bloodhand and his band of robbers, the witch Doriath, the mage Stormhand, the monk Tefalas, and the deadly Knight in Black. Tefalas can tell them about the dreadful necro­ mancer Dakoth who is spreading terror in the Eastern Lands beyond the mountains.
If the player characters manage to overcome the dangers in their path and find the three keys, the strange hermit Ulat-­A’ar in Deepmire can show them the way to the Gates of Power and explain how to use the keys – if he finds them worthy, that is.

[P] The group should also have one or more magical weapons in their possession.

### GATES OF POWER
Having arrived at the Gates of Power, the player charac­ ters again cross paths with Tefalas of the Keepers of the Immaculate Flame. He tells them that Dakoth has sent his greatest servant, the death wight Mogdath, to the old dwarven realm to find the dark heart of Sathmog and bring it to his master.
With an artifact as powerful as the Heart of Darkness, Dakoth would become invincible. Tefalas asks the player characters to help him destroy Mogdath, bind his dark soul to a magical dagger, and bring it to Tefalas’ chapter master Godvindel in the Eastern Lands beyond the mountains.
Behind the Gates of Power are ancient catacombs, dug by dwarves deep into the mountain. The player characters soon realize that they are not alone in this underground realm – they encounter orcs and living skeletons, and sense even greater horrors lurking in the depths.
While exploring the ruins of the old kingdom, they can also get their hands on valuable treasures and learn the sad fate of the dwarven realm – and realize that the dark heart of Sathmog is no longer there.
Sooner or later, the characters come face to face with the dreaded Mogdath and his minions, the nightwarg Sharch and the shadow wraiths Daz and Gozar. If they manage to defeat these formidable adversaries, they can leave the Gates of Power via an underground stream lead­ ing out of the mountain to the Eastern Lands.
### HEART OF DARKNESS
The player characters soon realize that times are hard in the beautiful Eastern Lands. In the village of Stonemouth, they face Dakoth’s brutal army of undead which threatens to crush the villagers under their iron-­shod heels. They can also rescue the mysterious assassin Ankh-­Tah-­Leh from captivity, after which she will join them if they let her.
The next leg of the journey likely leads to the moun­ tain temple of Otag, which turns out to be controlled by Dakoth’s forces. Godvindel is held captive and the player characters must sneak or break into the fortress in order to see him.
If they succeed, a dying Godvindel reveals the truth behind the quest he gave Tefalas: the curse of the Dead Forest can only be lifted if the Heart of Darkness is destroyed by a magical dagger infused with the spirit of a powerful undead in the same place where the curse was cast – on the hill in the center of the Dead Forest.
But, the Archpriest warns, whoever destroys the Heart of Darkness runs a high risk of being killed by the energies released.
Godvindel has concluded that the Heart of Darkness must be in one of three places in the Eastern Lands: a dwarven barrow near the town of Ankvarn, the elven burial isle of Is-­Hel in the middle of lake Is-­Anor, or a temple tower by the Wolf Peaks in the forest of Fang-­Forren.

## THE THREE POWERS
This campaign involves a struggle between three powers – the demon prince Sathmog and his followers, the dragon-­ worshiping Keepers of the Immaculate Flame, and the nec­ romancer Dakoth. The first two already appeared in *The* *Secret of the Dragon Emperor* in the *Dragonbane* Core Set, while Dakoth is introduced here. It is important to make the three powers different and distinct for the players.
### SATHMOG
The demon prince symbolizes darkness, night, filth, lies, deceit, egotism, corruption, and decay, but also change and personal freedom.
Sathmog originated in another world and wants to return to the human world to corrupt its people – or liberate them, as the demon sees it.
Sathmog has lesser demons, monsters, cultists, and corrupted creatures at his disposal, mainly humans and nightkin. The demon’s servants wear his black mark branded somewhere on their body, usually on their fore­ arm hidden under their clothes. The mark is a stylized demon head with two sharp horns.
Sathmog hates Eledain’s order, which has thwarted his plans on many occasions in history.
### THE KEEPERS OF
### THE IMMACULATE FLAME
This religious order of monks and knights worships the ancient dragon emperor Eledain. The order represents fire, light, day, purity, truth, duty, fellow­ ship, and tradition, but also immutability and conformity.
The Keepers of the Immaculate Flame see themselves as heirs to the dragon knights of old and seek to re-­establish Eledain’s empire, based on the principle of the cleansing fire. They abhor demons – especially Sathmog, whom their scriptures describe as the root of all evil. Eledain’s symbol is a stylized dragon.
### DAKOTH
A necromancer of much more recent origin than Sathmog and Eledain. Dakoth symbolizes coldness, death, meaningless­ ness, hopelessness, and the transience of everything. He is the enemy of all living things.
Dakoth has his stronghold in the East­ ern Lands and has recently subjugated large areas with his army of undead and enslaved orcs. Dakoth’s conquests have led to armed conflict with the Keepers of the Immaculate Flame and deeply angered Sathmog, who is still worshiped in the Eastern Lands and views the necromancer as his rival.

## THE THREE KEYS
There is much for the player characters to do in the Dead Forest, but their main goal is to find the Gates of Power and the three keys required to open them. Few know that the Gates of Power even exist and even fewer know about the three keys. But fewest of all are those who know the poem that describes the use of the keys:
*On the heart side, moonshine bright* *sleek ebony, the eye’s delight* *from the shell each season calls* *opening the dwarven halls* The poem is engraved on a pendant forged by the elves for the key bearers to protect them from danger on their jour­ ney and remind them how to use the keys. It has changed hands many times over the years, finally ending up with the hermit in Deepmire (page 51).
The three keys are made up of two fist-­sized pearls, one white as “the moon” and the other black as “ebony”, and a horn made from a thirty-­centimeter-­long seashell. The poem above reveals how to use the keys to open the Gates of Power, and thus the way to the second adventure of the campaign. Read more about this on page 56.

he Hungry Hobgoblin, located on the southern edge of the Dead Forest, is meant to serve as a hub for adventures in the area and a place where the player characters can return between journeys to recover and stock up on supplies. See the map on page 16.

Describe these NPCs to the players and show their portraits. Let the player characters be the ones who initiate contact, at least initially. If the characters are not interested in them, you can have the NPCs come up to them and ask for help.

## NATURE
The area known as the Dead Forest consists of two sections – the outer forest outside Hill Creek, which is a relatively normal old-­growth forest, and the inner forest inside Hill Creek, which is dead and lifeless.
The outer forest is a deep and wondrous old-­growth forest mostly made up of deciduous trees but with elements of conifers, meadows, and marshes. The vegetation is for the most part easily traversable, and the outer forest is home to a lot of game in the form of hare, deer, stag, fox, wild boar, birds, squirrels, and wolves.
The inner forest is barren and terrifying. Strong oaks and spruces with healthy green leaves and needles have been reduced to skeletal, dead trees. There are no animals here except for insects and angry ravens watching from the treetops. It is not possible to hunt or forage in the inner forest (page 103 in the Rulebook).

## DEEPMIRE
rom the foot of the Heaven’s Rim mountains to the edge of the Dead Forest stretches a sludgy, watery wetland, called Deepmire because of its unknown depth. ## THE HERMIT’S TENT
Ulat-­A’ar is the name of the ancient hermit who lives on a small raft by the side of the path. On the raft he has pitched a tent, now overgrown with winding plants and moss, with a flap for a door. A bit of smoke rises from the opening at the top.
To reach the raft, the player characters must leave the path and make their way across four moss-­covered rocks that lead to the opening of the tent. This requires an ACROBATICS roll – those who fail fall into the marsh and become both Disheartened and cold.
### THE MEETING WITH ULAT-A’AR
> Inside the tent is a small fireplace with a simmering caul­
> dron above it. Dried plants and the skins of rats and frog
> are hanging from strings. On the ground are bowls and
> bottles filled with strange-­smelling concoctions. Opposite
> the entrance, a bent-­backed figure is crouched over a pipe
> he is filling. A gray hood shrouds his dark face and only
> his gnarled hands reveal his advanced age. “I’ve seen you
> in my dreams,” the old man croaks. “You, hah, your nose
> looked muuuch bigger, but... well, one wonders, are you
> worthy to tread the path of glory?”
Ulat-­A’ar is the keeper of the golden pendant that was forged many generations ago, and whose inscription explains how to use the three keys.
He was told in a dream that one or more individuals would one day come to his home for guidance in their search for the Gates of Power. They must not be greedy for wealth or power. To these people, the hermit will hand over the golden pendant in the hope that good souls will be the first to enter the ancient dwarf realm.
Stormhand came here years ago and was told the location of the Gates of Power and learned about the three keys required to open them. But Ulat-­A’ar kept quiet about the elven pendant as he doubted Stormhand’s intentions.
### THE HERMIT’S DECISION
The meeting with Ulat-­A’ar should be role-­played. The hermit speaks in a hoarse, almost hissing voice. If the player characters present their case well and succeed with a PERSUASION roll (with a boon if they have been partic­ ularly convincing), Ulat-­A’ar hands them the pendant.
If the roll fails, they must prove their worthiness by doing the hermit a favor before they can try again. Ulat-­ A’ar could ask them to go hunting on his behalf – this is handled according to the rules on page 103 of the Rulebook but with a bane, and only voles and crows can be found in the marshes. For each failed roll, the character must also roll on the mishap table on page 102 of the Rulebook.

## CONCLUSION
If the characters eventually gain Ulat-­A’ar’s trust, he hands over the pendant. See the adjacent sidebar. He can also tell them where to find the Gates of Power if they have not already figured it out.
### OTHER HELP
Ulat-­A’ar can use his magic to help the player characters if they are injured. In his tent he also has three bottles of healing potion (page 79 of the Rulebook) which he gives to the characters as a parting gift. If the characters want to stay a while, Ulat-­A’ar can take one of them on as his apprentice (page 61 of the Rulebook).
### A WORD OF CAUTION
Before the player characters leave, Ulat-­A’ar croaks out a warning: *“Beware. It is said that the dark heart of the vile* *demon Sathmog has been hidden behind the Gates of Power* *since the day his body was banished from this world. The heart* *of darkness has the power to corrupt even the purest of souls –* *but legend says that the heart can also lift the curse of the Dead* *Forest. The fate of the forest rests with you, adventurers!”* hen the player characters reach the Gates of Power, with the keys and the instructional poem in their possession, this adventure comes to an end. ### A FINAL SHOWDOWN?
The player characters’ long search is over, but there is still time for one last showdown before this adventure ends. If Stormhand, Doriath, or the Knight in Black are still alive, one or all of them will show up here before the characters can open the Gates of Power. Stormhand and Doriath want the keys, and propose a (temporary) alliance if the odds are against them. The Knight in Black does not speak – all he wants is to kill the player characters here and now.
### AT THE GATES OF POWER
> You step onto the podium, ready to open the Gates of
> Power, when a shadow suddenly emerges from the bushes
> behind you. You are not alone...
At this point, *The Dead Forest* is concluded, and each player character is rewarded with a heroic ability of their choice (chapter 3 in the Rulebook) for having reached this mile­ stone. Their adventures will continue in *Gates of Power*.
