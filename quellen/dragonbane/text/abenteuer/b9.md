*eart of Darkness* is the final installment of the *Path of Glory* campaign. With the resolution around the corner, this adventure is more linear than the previous ones. The player characters’ task is clear:
to find the heart of the demon Sathmog and bring it back to the Temple of Winds, where it must be destroyed in order to lift the curse on the Dead Forest – and the world.
The player characters start out lost, with no map or directions – all they know is that the archpriest ­Godvindel is supposed to be at the mountain temple of Otag, and that he has information that can hopefully bring them closer to their goal. Their first challenge, then, is to find directions to the temple somewhere in this war-­torn land…
## ADVENTURE OPENING
*Heart of Darkness* is designed to be played as the final adventure of the *Path of Glory* trilogy. It is therefore assumed that the player characters emerge from the Gates of Power via the underground river from the dwarven dock. If *Gates of Power* ended differently, you may need to adjust the beginning of *Heart of Darkness* accordingly.
The journey along the underground river takes two shifts and passes without incident, as long as the characters have a light source. If they do not, the person steering the boat must make a SEAMANSHIP roll to move forward during the shift. All characters except the one steering the boat can rest. It should be morning when they approach the opening to the Eastern Lands. Read or play the following, without showing the players the map of the Eastern Lands:

## THE EASTERN LANDS
From their fortress of Cith Dakoth in the north, the necromancer’s forces have conquered much of the Eastern Lands. Small bands of rebels, bravely fighting knight-­ monks, and the recently arrived dragon rider Karlak Sol-­ Tar (page 150) sometimes manage to take out advance parties or smaller units of orcs and undead, but not enough to halt the necromancer’s advance, and often at the cost of heavy casualties. When the player characters arrive, most of the villages west of Wolf Peaks have been looted and burned, and their inhabitants killed or routed.
### DAKOTH
When Dakoth’s old companion Stormhand boastfully announced that he had found one of the keys to the Gates of Power (page 6), the necromancer dispatched troops to search for the “entrance to the dwarven realm from east of Heaven’s Rim”
that the legends suggested would be there. And it was not long before the search bore fruit. The mines inside not only increased his resources but also gave him a chance to find the Heart of Darkness – the artifact that would make him powerful enough to bring the entire world under his control.
Before the beginning of this adventure, however, Dakoth started to lose patience with his henchman Mog­ dath’s inability to find the heart. When Mogdath was ban­ ished or destroyed, he finally lost hope. The necromancer concluded that if the Heart of Darkness was indeed hidden under the mountain, he would already have found it.
Instead, Dakoth intensified his search for the artifact east of the mountains. The first place he targeted was the mountain temple of Otag, the last stronghold of the Keep­ ers of the Immaculate Flame in the region. When he failed to locate it there, he took personal command of his army’s main force and sent it to other locations where the Heart of Darkness might be found.

With the capture of Otag by Dakoth’s forces, there is little left of the Keepers of the Immaculate Flame in the Eastern Lands. Archpriest Godvindel lives but is imprisoned and gravely wounded.
Some twenty knights are also still alive.
Divided into groups of three, they do their best to disrupt Dakoth’s conquests, organizing the local population or attacking smaller enemy units, but they are unable to inflict much damage on the enemy and are forced to spend most of their time running or hiding from Dakoth’s forces.
Half a moon ago Godvindel assigned two units to search for the Heart of Darkness in a couple of locations he had identified as likely hiding places (page 123) – the Hills of Kalin and the Demon Tower in the Fang-­Forren forest. The three knights sent to the hills are already dead (see random event #4 on page 99) while those sent into the forest have been captured by its inhabitants (page 139) and can be found during the player characters’ visit there.
### KARLAK SOL-TAR
The learned dragon rider Karlak Sol-­Tar decided to inves­ tigate the veracity of the legends and historical fragments that spoke of a powerful artifact hidden in the old country, west of the Sea of Dreams.
Riding Ash-­Ark-­Afar, the strongest and mightiest of all the rainbow-­colored dragons bred for war in the southern neighboring land of Drak-­Sol-­Faar, he left the city Urdrak-­ Kalt-­Immer in the realm of Immer-­Drak, hoping to find the Heart of Darkness. With the artifact in his possession, Karlak envisioned himself becoming a legendary demon slayer, or perhaps a supreme ruler with absolute power over the will and soul of the demon lord, and thus over all the demons in his domain.
He has not been in the Eastern Lands for more than a few days when the adventure begins, but that has proved to be enough to split his focus. Demon worshipers are everywhere! And on top of that, a tyrannical necro­ mancer with hordes of orcs and undead is sweeping like a vicious typhoon over the land. Initially, Karlak flies around striking at various places that catch his and the dragon’s attention, but eventually he realizes that Dakoth is close to finding the Heart of Darkness and starts watching him from above.
### SATHMOG
Sathmog himself has only a small part to play in this adventure (page 144), but his followers are every­ where in the Eastern Lands. Cultists live and work in almost every town and hamlet, and in some places, they operate openly. Although considered an abomination by the Keepers of the Immaculate Flame, it is fair to say that Sathmog worship is accepted by most, but many are reluctant to admit it openly for fear of the dragon knights.
The village of Stonemouth is an example of how this works in practice.
Sathmog is also important in that he is as much an enemy of Dakoth as of the dragon knights. Although one of them is currently much stronger (Dakoth), it is impossible to understand the history of the Eastern Lands and the design of this adventure without recognizing that we are dealing with three powers that are all at each other’s throats.

## NATURE
The ice-­and-­snow-­laden Heaven’s Rim mountain range forms the western boundary of the Eastern Lands, rising above the clouds. From the eastern slopes of the moun­ tains, rolling green steppes stretch eastward, turning into desert steppes where the streams run dry, before becoming grassy again at the southern border of the two great forests, Fang-­Forren and Lum-­Forren.
The lush deciduous Lum-­Forren forest in the northeast is rich with heather and a variety of berries and teeming with wildlife. There are even rumors of elven realms hidden in its depths. Fish-­filled streams flow from within the forest, some leading west through watery bogs to the inland lake Is-­Anor. Others lead east to the barren High Coast.
The bare and slippery cliffs of the High Coast plunge several horse lengths straight into the ever-­crashing saltwater waves of the Sea of Dreams. From here, death-­ defying crews can launch voyages to distant continents, across vast and treacherous waters – with thick blankets of fog hiding jagged underwater rocks rising out of the sea like spearheads, ready to cut holes in any hull that comes their way.
Just a day’s journey across the plains south of Lum-­ Forren lie the Wolf Peaks, their western foothills com­ pletely covered by the dark trees of Fang-­Forren, whose dense foliage keeps the sunlight from reaching its swampy, root-­tangled undergrowth. Fang-­Forren is feared by all, with good reason…
## RANDOM EVENTS
This section presents a table for random events, intended to be used during the player characters’ travels through the Eastern Lands. There should normally be a random event each shift during journeys, but it can happen more often or less frequently if you wish. Some of the random events are described in greater detail on the following pages. Roll D12 on the table to the right to determine which event occurs, but remember that you as GM are always free to choose an event instead of leaving it to chance.
**Initial Event:**  During the initial journey to Stonemouth, we recommend that you forgo random events entirely, or roll D4 on the table or choose from the first four.
**Repeated Events:**  As a rule, each event should only occur once – re-­roll or select another if you get an event that has already happened. Events with the Dragon Rider (11–12) are exceptions and may occur multiple times.

### THE CULTISTS
Behind a facade of smiles and contentment, the people of Deepdale are desperate. The local demon who has so far helped them repel Dakoth’s forces has threatened to turn on them if they do not soon provide him with a blood sac­ rifice – preferably a yummy elf, but he is not a picky eater.
The village elder, Mother Kunda, is determined not to sacrifice any of the villagers, and she does not want to be left without the protection of the village’s “guardian angel.”
She will do everything in her power to get the player characters to stay the night in Deepdale, in separate rooms at the Mogens’ Tears Inn (Mogens is a local name for Sathmog). If she succeeds, she will try to drug one of them by offering a glass of warm goat’s milk before bedtime. She uses a sleeping poison with potency 12. Everyone is offered milk, but she only poisons the one who seems the weakest, preferably an elf.
If the characters insist on moving on, or if they dis­ cover the attempt to poison them, the desperate villagers will take up arms. Stats can be found on page 105 of the Rulebook, according to the adjacent table. Once the char­ acters have killed six of them, the guardian angel decides it is time to intervene – for stats, see page 85 of the Rulebook.
When this happens, all the villagers retreat. If the charac­ ters defeat the demon, all villagers flee into the wilderness and do not return until the “angel killers” have left.
### THE FREEDOM FIGHTERS
Altfoss Dregel was mayor of the free village of Fossfirth before Dakoth’s hordes burned it to the ground and massacred most of its population. He himself fled with his four guards but was forced to leave his wife and sixteen children behind. Seething with shame, grief, and hatred, he decided to gather a guerrilla force with the aim of defeating, or at least fighting, the evil conqueror.
Since then, there has been a high turnover of fighters in the unit – many have been recruited, but almost as many have died in battle. Now the group consists of Altfoss plus as many fighters as there are player characters.
The only way for the player characters to avoid combat is to quickly put their hands in the air. If they succeed with AWARENESS and win the initiative, they have time to do so before any projectiles are fired – otherwise they are shot at with arrows before they get a chance to surrender and PERSUADE the freedom fighters that they are on the same side, i.e. against Dakoth.
If successfully persuaded, a remorseful Altfoss helps any wounded and offers the characters a meal. He does not know much about the situation in the country at large, but he can tell them what happened to his beloved Fossfirth.

### BROWBEATEN ORCS
The shadow wraith Urial (stats as Gozar on page 60) thoroughly enjoys commanding her squad of orcs, but she is as hard on her troops as she is on any enemy they encounter. Unless the player characters intervene, the wraith soon orders a massacre of the prisoners, and the orcs obey.
If the characters attack Urial, half of the orcs will take the opportunity to escape. If the characters also shout out that the shadow wraith is their only target and succeed at a PERSUASION roll, all the orcs run away.
If the two human families (four adults, two elderly people, and seven children) are saved, their gratitude knows no bounds. They hand the characters a master­ crafted broadsword, a beautifully decorated bagpipe, and a gemstone worth 7 gold.

### HISTORY
Stonemouth was founded during the brief heyday of the dwarves in the region, as a trading hub between them and the surface dwellers, but also as a gathering place for dwarves who for various reasons chose to leave the mines beneath Heaven’s Rim. Over time, humans also found their way there –merchants, adventurers, and fortune hunters lured by tales of uncharted lands, mystical elven forests, and riches left behind by ancient dwarven and draconic civilizations.
**The Fall & The Savior:**  Then came the war under the mountain, the fall of the dwarven kingdom, and everything changed, especially for Stonemouth whose main livelihood came from trading with Queen Gwinna and her subjects. The village was quickly plunged into misery. Some villagers traveled east, but many chose to stay, despite the abject poverty and the resulting conflicts.
Things looked bleak indeed – until the savior came.
The dwarf’s name was Prince Kalin, and he possessed wealth as well as the charm and bearing of a leader.
And he had powers, bestowed by his god Sathmog.
Whether because of his regal charisma or the idealized picture he presented of Sathmog, focusing on personal freedom and empowerment, he soon united the people of Stonemouth against a common enemy: the entire outside world.
**High Priest Gultrub:**  Once a temple had been built and Kalin felt certain that Sathmog worship was firmly estab­ lished in the village, he handed over the congregation to his successor, High Priest Gultrub, and set out to spread the teachings of Sathmog. He was joined by most of the village’s dwarven founding families.
Under Gultrub’s leadership, Stonemouth grew into an isolated but free and relatively prosperous village with a strong sense of community. Before Dakoth’s conquests, about one hundred villagers, including some twenty dwarves, led a mostly peaceful existence there, with the Sathmog Temple and the Copper Tent inn as their natural gathering places. After hundreds of years, having had his already long dwarven life prolonged by demonic blessings, Gultrub reluctantly relinquished his position to the human priest Fidelia.
### CURRENT SITUATION
When Dakoth recently restarted production in the old mines and forges under the mountain, he sent enslaved orcs to occupy Stonemouth, which became a hub for the transportation of iron and weapons for the necromancer’s war machine. Half of the inhabitants fled, but those who remained were mostly left alone. However, discontent grew among the orcs as they were not allowed to loot the village.
**The Orc Revolt:**  A few days before the arrival of the player characters, the orcs, led by the chieftain Uhuluk, revolted and decided to conquer Stonemouth for themselves. The inhabitants took refuge in a fortified citadel in the center of the village, which has since been under siege.
**The Betrayal:**  Bitter about being sidelined and forgotten by both Sathmog and the villagers, the former High Priest Gultrub saw his chance to get back at Stonemouth and especially the new High Priest Fidelia. He secretly contacted Dakoth by magical means and informed him of what had happened. He offered to help the necromancer’s forces take the village in exchange for becoming its new steward in Dakoth’s service. Dakoth accepted but has no intention of honoring the agreement – instead, he plans to slaughter every orc, human, and dwarf in Stonemouth to ensure control of the vital transportation hub.
**The Prisoner:**  There is a further complication in the already tangled situation in Stonemouth. In a cage hanging in the village square sits a mallard outsider, captured by the orcs during the revolt. ## LOCATIONS
Stonemouth lies in a valley surrounded by a lush and hilly landscape, embracing the twenty-­meter-­wide Hill Creek and flanked in the north and south by sloping hillsides, either cultivated or filled with grazing cattle.
Inside the three-­meter-­high wall are masonry houses with rarely more than one floor under their moss-­covered wooden roofs. The exceptions are the Sathmog temple (#4), the Copper Tent inn (#6), the citadel (#8), and the mill, which all rise above the other buildings.
Along the river are a number of jetties, and inside the wall there are livestock enclosures as well as a large turnip farm. All buildings not described below are empty, looted by the orcs.
### 1. HILL CREEK
> It may seem calm and pleasant as it flows between the hills

- **Strong Current:**  The river is only twenty meters wide but the current is so strong that anyone who tries to swim in it must make a SWIMMING roll every round to avoid getting pulled down and starting to drown (page 53 of the Rulebook).

- **The Riverbank:**  The walls only extend a handsbreadth into the river, so an agile individual can get around the wall in those places. It requires a successful ACROBAT­ ICS roll with a boon. Failure results in a fall into the rushing water.

### HISTORY
The temple tower of Otag dates back to the time before the Demon Wars, when dragon-­worshiping humans first made contact with the objects of their prayers. According to the local Eledain knights, this is where the alliance against the demon invasion was formed – they claim that Eledain himself used their tower for meetings and negotiations with the primeval dragon Arknarath.
The other buildings were constructed hundreds of years later, when the dust of battle had settled and the Keepers of the Immaculate Flame spread across the world, as one of the places where knights were trained. The focus and scope of their activities have changed over the centuries, though their main task has always been to guard the region against the return of the demons – a task that gave Archpriest Godvindel gray hairs, until Dakoth arrived and made things even worse.

### HISTORY
When Sathmog’s hordes invaded the world a thousand years ago, the elves of Fang-­Forren, led by King Ilvariel, were one of their first targets and most ardent opponents. After heavy fighting in which the shieldmaiden Iskolde and the animist Muriel helped balance the scales of war, Sathmog manipu­ lated Ilvariel’s brother, Fangiel, into stabbing his sibling in the back. As a result, the entire elven kingdom perished and the forest became infested with demonic energies.
Ilvariel had already foreseen his own death and the down­ fall of his people and prepared for his and his family’s afterlife.
When the demon prince’s hordes swept on to new lands, the few surviving elves laid their king and his loved ones to rest inside the rocky island of Is-­Hel. Thanks to rumors of deadly traps, ghosts, and vengeful magical mechanisms, the tomb has remained almost untouched for centuries.
### PIKEWATER
The village of Pikewater had long been a bustling commu­ nity whose inhabitants relied on fishing and trade with the monks of Otag and other villages. But when Dakoth’s undead launched their assault, the village did not stand a chance and most of the survivors fled.
The only ones left are the aged fishermen Otis and Voley, both frog people, the former nearly deaf and the latter equally dim-­sighted. Because of a youthful feud over a frog girl who eventually married a third suitor, there is a deep hatred between them. Both managed to escape the undead by taking refuge in the lake.
Otis has laid claim to the village’s western jetty and the two rowing boats (page 81 of the Rulebook) moored there.
Voley has claimed the eastern jetty and its two sailing boats.
They spend much of their time on these separate jetties, mending fishing nets with their backs to each other. Both are in dire need of coin and supplies.
**Bartering:**  The characters first encounter Otis, who demands an outrageous sum of 100 gold for a rowing boat and information regarding the location of Is-­Hel. Voley hears what they are talking about and shouts from his jetty a few dozen meters away that he can offer a much better “tub” – with sails!
Let the characters control the negotiation. The price starts at 100 gold for both options. A successful BARTER­ ING roll with Otis or Voley lowers that person’s price by 20 gold. The characters can then BARTER with the other frogman to reduce the price by another 20 gold.
This can go on until the price is down to 20 gold. If the characters try BARTERING again after that, Otis and Voley get into a fight and start exchanging blows, and the characters must step in to stop them from beating each other senseless.

## ACROSS THE LAKE
The island of Is-­Hel lies well to the south of Pikewater, and the beautiful lake is full of treacherous rocks, sandbanks, and violent gusts. The journey takes at least two shifts.
For each shift, the person steering the boat must make a SEAMANSHIP roll. On failure, the characters make no progress on their journey this shift.
Traveling by rowing boat grants the player characters a boon on the roll, but another character must also make a STR roll per shift for the boat to move forward. Characters who are not rowing or steering the boat can rest or fish while traveling (page 103 of the Rulebook).
**Random Events:**  The journey across the cold waters of Is-­Anor can also be dangerous. Roll on the adjacent table once per shift that the characters spend on the waves, or simply choose an event you find exciting.

#### WHERE IS THE HEART?
## THE HILLS OF KALIN
he Heart of Darkness is not buried among the Hills of Kalin, as the player characters can find out if they go there. What is there, however, is an army of hundreds of orcs and undead. Dakoth is there as well, in the flesh, unreachable and in an awful mood.
The characters will hardly be able to get to the necro­ mancer since he is surrounded by an armed horde. But they do have a chance to get close enough to eavesdrop on his conversation with the scarred orc chieftain Uhulu-­ Uhurm (the father of the traitor Uhuluk), which may yield important information.
If the characters pass through Ankvarn on their way to the hills, they find the little town completely burned down. A handful of wounded people, mostly mallards, are moving among the rubble, arranging scores of dead in rows in the town square. If the characters engage them in conversation, they have little more to say than that it was Dakoth and his army that wiped out the town and its population.
### HISTORY
The stone circle on the burial mound among the Hills of Kalin was indeed erected in Prince Kalin’s honor, but the story that he is buried there is a tall tale, made up by local merchants to attract pilgrims to the area. There is not even a royal tomb under the hill – just pitiful catacombs for local Sathmog worshipers.
### CURRENT SITUATION
Since losing contact with his minions in the dwarven mines, Dakoth has become interested in other locations where the Heart of Darkness may be hidden. Although his war of conquest against the people of the Eastern Lands must be finished, he has assumed personal command of a large force to investigate a few sites that seem likely, while the army subjugates the locals wherever it goes.
Dakoth arrived at the Hills of Kalin a couple of days before the player characters, and the results of his exca­ vations have left him deeply disappointed. Though he is reluctant to enter the demonic forest of Fang-­Forren, that is where he intends to go next.

### HISTORY
Long before the Demon Wars, an elven kingdom flourished in the heart of the Eastern Lands, in the forest of Lum-­ Forren which at the time stretched all the way down to the southern foothills of Wolf Peaks. Bolstered by a forest imbued with animist magic, the realm was one of the greatest and most influential powers of the day, which likely explains why the demon prince Sathmog chose the elves as the first and most important target of his invasion force.
Sathmog’s army attacked from the south, turning that part of the forest into a battlefield. More and more demons poured into the world to replace the fallen, yet King Ilvariel’s forces had no reinforcements to rely on. Thanks to heroes like the shieldmaiden Iskolde and the sorceress Muriel, the elves held out for many years, but in the end, they were inevitably worn down by the enemy’s relentless attacks. When one of the elven king’s brothers, the gullible Fangiel, in desperation let himself be manipulated into ending the war by stabbing King Ilvariel in the back, the last resistance finally crumbled.
Sathmog’s troops erected a demonic temple tower as a sign that the region had been conquered, but then moved on to quell other threats to their burgeoning demonic empire. They left behind a completely devastated forest, twisted and tormented. The few elves still alive were ban­ ished there by the traitor Fangiel, who also gave that part of the forest its current name – Fang-­Forren.
Only the troll witch Morgasa and her servants remained in the Demon Tower, ordered by Sathmog to watch over the place. Hundreds of years later, the severely aged Prince Kalin found his way there. He came to the Demon Tower to die and to deliver the artifact he carried for safekeeping. Morgasa built a crypt at the top of the tower, and when Kalin had breathed his last, she laid him to rest there. In his coffin she placed the Heart of Darkness, which Morgasa swore to guard until the end of time.
### ROUTES
There are two main routes to the Demon Tower – south­ east through the forest, or northeast along the river Night­ water. Judging by the map, the journey along the river may look easier, but that is not the case. The river is too rough and full of sharp rocks to be navigable. Besides, the forest extends all the way to the riverbank, which means that the player characters still must deal with undergrowth and other obstacles.
The characters might decide to go around Wolf Peaks and try to cross the mountains from the east. But this route is impassable, as an NPC living in the Eastern Lands can explain to them – the mountains are too steep, swarm­ ing with monsters, and treacherous in nature.
### REFUGEES
Whichever route the player characters take, they spot a similar sight near the edge of the forest – a thick column of smoke rising from a village (it can be any of the villages on the map). Coming towards them, away from the village, is a group of refugees hauling their belongings on carts and wagons.

Below is a suggestion on how the confrontation at the top of the demon tower might play out, based on the goals and motives of the NPCs involved. What actually happens will largely depend on the actions of the player characters.
### MEETING MORGASA
The troll witch Morgasa wants to protect the Heart of Darkness at all costs. If the player characters show up on the rooftop terrace, she will first make one last attempt to persuade them to leave. She starts by screaming a hoarse warning:
*“Turn back! This is the tower of Sathmog and I am his* *servant. There is nothing for you here but death and despair,* *pestilence and ruin. If you do not leave at once, you will* *perish in terrible torment!”* It is quite possible to engage Morgasa in combat, but while she herself is not a very challenging opponent, the effects of her witchcraft might make things more difficult for the characters in the battle ahead. The wiser option is to try to trick or persuade her to help them.
Thanks to the ravens, Morgasa knows that another intruder has already arrived at the tower, but she has no idea who he is (Dakoth). The player characters can use what they know as an argument:
- She is going to lose her treasure – the only question is to whom.
- The person following them is a necromancer who is Sathmog’s mortal enemy.

### THE DRAGON RIDER
If the player characters manage to defeat Dakoth, they have just enough time to get their hands on the Heart of Darkness before the next player enters the stage – the dragon knight Karlak Sol-­Tar. If Dakoth was victorious, this happens as the characters lie bleeding at his feet (in his arrogance he does not bother to end their suffering) and the necromancer triumphantly picks up the box.
Suddenly the sky darkens, and all sounds are drowned out by the thunderous beating of dragon wings as Ash-­ Ark-­Afar lands on the roof of the tower. See the image on page 8. If Dakoth prevailed, the dragon opens its huge mouth and obliterates the surprised necromancer with a single devastating avalanche of fire. Only the box containing the Heart of Darkness remains where he stood, smoking with heat but still intact.
Then, or immediately after defeating Dakoth, the characters have a chance to negotiate with Karlak Sol-­Tar.
He explains that he comes from the realm of Immer-­Drak beyond the Sea of Dreams and that his sacred mission is to destroy the Heart of Darkness, whose existence threatens his realm and the rest of the world.
Karlak Sol-­Tar claims to know that the Heart of Dark­ ness can be destroyed by dragon fire, and demands that it be handed over for immediate destruction. If they refuse, they too will bathe in fire!
**Negotiation:**  The player characters may agree to his demand, in which case the curse on the Dead Forest will not be lifted. But more likely they will try to persuade the knight, before or after it turns out that the black heart of Sathmog cannot actually be damaged by the dragon’s fire.
Once again, good arguments are required:
- The only way to break the curse on the Dead Forest is to destroy the Heart of Darkness at a certain location.
- The curse is spreading and will eventually cover the entire world, with apocalyptic consequences.
- If Karlak Sol-­Tar helps break the curse, the dragon riders will be hailed as heroes by people on this side of the sea, instead of being hated and feared.
The characters roll PERSUASION with a boon if all the points above are mentioned, normally if at least one of the points is mentioned, and with a bane if completely different arguments are put forward. On success, the dragon rider is convinced, and not only that – he offers to take the characters to the Temple of Winds on dragonback!
If the roll fails, the characters have another difficult battle ahead of them.

### STORMHAND AND DORIATH
If Stormhand and/or Doriath are still alive, they may be at the temple when the player characters arrive. If so, they are there out of desperation, pounding stubbornly on the temple door. The Lady of the Wind grew tired of their tedious questions days ago, thinking that she has already answered them several times over, and has simply stopped being of service – even if summoned correctly.
**Stormhand:**  If Stormhand is alive, he knows that some­ one has managed to open the Gates of Power – he pesters the Lady of the Wind to find out what happened to them and where they have gone. If it becomes clear to him that the player characters have returned to destroy the artifact he has sought for so long, Stormhand becomes furious and wants to stop them at all costs.
First, he tries to buy the heart by offering non-­existent riches. Failing that, he starts making threats. And if that does not work either, he becomes so desperate and enraged that he attacks, however futile it may seem.
**Doriath:**  Doriath’s reasons for troubling the Lady of the Wind are probably the same as Stormhand’s, except that she also wants information about her brother. Convinced of her own omnipotence, she would like to exchange a few words with the player characters before attacking them with her spells – she wants to know where they have been and grows even more curious if they mention Dakoth. If she learns that they killed her brother, that is the final straw and she attacks them with all her might.
**Old Friends:**  Should both Doriath and Stormhand have survived, it is not inconceivable that the old friends will make a deal to find the heart and save it from destruction – both of them fully aware that they will eventually have to fight each other for the artifact.
