## Overview
This chapter’s all about setup and hooking the player characters, and ends with them setting off in search of the Eastern Express in Chapter 2. If you’re in the middle of an existing *Heart* campaign, prefer to let the players drive the action, or like wallowing in the weird, let them bounce around the various landmarks and NPCs in this section until they trip over one of the connections pointing to the Eastern Express.

For a more direct approach, run *Dead Man With A Gun* (p. 28) to hand the players a plot hook.

#### Felise

There are those who lurk on the edge of the Heart, like creatures who live in the littoral zone along the coast. They endure the waves of chaos that wash in and out, they pretend they live in a stable region. Sometimes, they get to feast on some huge carcass thrown up from the deeps; sometimes, they get eaten by some unimaginable predator. There are those, like the player characters, who march bravely into the unknowable depths to drown. And then there are creatures like Felise, creatures of the shallows. They dip in and out of the water, but never too deep and never out of sight of land**3**.

She fled the City Above to avoid indentured servitude. She’s like an alley cat: wary, mangy, a little pathetic, and sharp-clawed. Above all, Felise is a survivor; whatever desires she had, she walls them away so they cannot lure her deeper into the Heart.

#### Chapter 1: Trainkill

**Dead Man With A Gun:** Pick one of the player characters – ideally, the nicest and mildest of the characters, if those descriptors fit any of them. While that character is going about their nice, mild, inoffensive business, a half-crazed dark elf emerges from the shadows and sticks a gun in their face. This would-be assassin is **Helskir**. He’s travelled all the way from the Amyrran Zone (p. 57), so deep in the Heart that time moves strangely. He’s seen many horrors – but none as terrible as the thing that the player character *might* do in one possible future. So, Helskir intends to pre-emptively murder the unwitting architect of that nightmare.

Helskir knows the player character’s name and a great deal about them (or, to be precise, the Amyrran version of them), but he’s so exhausted and traumatised that his rambling doesn’t make much sense. Right now, all the player needs to know is 1) this elf thinks they’ve done something terrible 2) there’s a gun in their face.

No half measures for Helskir: he fights to the death. If reduced to 0 resistance, he keeps coming until one of the player characters puts him down.

-  A search of the elf ’s corpse reveals a curious talisman with an arcane sigil. Anyone with Discern or **Occult** guesses it’s the sigil of a human sorcerer-sect, which is an odd thing for a dark elf to carry. A successful **Discern+Occult** roll identifies it as the sigil of the defunct Gwynn-Ardasi cabal, a group of retro-engineers who splintered about a century ago. The survivors founded the respected Academy of Gywnn-Enforr in the City Above.
-  Asking about the Gwynn-Ardasi cabal in a place like Coinpit (p. 30) points the delvers towards the legend of the Eastern Express (p. 44).
-  The gun might be the time-warped duplicate of a favoured weapon of one of the other player characters (see **Strange yet Familiar**).

**Strange Yet Familiar:** Browsing in a junk market, picking through the loot after a bloody fight, or perusing the antiques of an eccentric collector, one of the player characters spots an object that’s strangely familiar. The more the character stares at the object, the more uncanny it seems. It has all the same scratches and marks as the one in the character’s possession. It feels the same, looks the same – but it’s *older*. This older item came from the Coinpit (p. 30).

#### Landmarks

**Hired By The University:** Civic-minded delvers might be hired by the Tower Division to search for the missing expedition led by Egrin Weselsmith (p. 110). The expedition’s been missing long enough that they’re presumed dead – but the investigators find a piece of expedition gear in the market at Coinpit (p. 30).

**Ptolemy Bay Stomps A Haven:** While the player characters are in a Haven, forces loyal to Ptolemy Bay show up. Their goal might be to secure clues pointing to the Eastern Express, or just to turn the Haven into one of Bay’s temporary weaponsmanufacturing workhouses. Either way, a bunch of heavily armed bastards (p. 112) arrive, striking at all known exits simultaneously. A rattle of gunfire, then an ultimatum: surrender, or they’ll gas the place. Bay’s operation is well-oiled – they know to pick Havens without strong protectors like Vermissian Knights, and pay off potential obstacles. The presence of any player characters is an unexpected wrinkle.

If all goes according to plan, then the Haven’s converted into a temporary factory, an armed camp used for the processing and distillation of substances gathered deeper in the Heart (like the Red-Litten Caves, p. 95) or high in Spire. More caravans full of Bay’s servants roll into town, carrying disassembled industrial equipment. There’s a fairground feel to the whole affair at first, as weird machines sprout up, looming over the Haven. Then the raw material comes in, and the work begins. Cauldrons of stewed spireblack, vats of protomatter, heartsblood stills, grave dirt from the burial grounds of witches, the offal of godbeasts and the bones of angels, stirred and roasted and ground to dust. Toxic fumes, alchemical run-off and mandrake curses spill into the Haven, blighting the indentured workforce and their home forever.

Bay recruits new soldiers from the healthiest survivors. After all, they have nowhere else to go.

#### Tooth Alley
**Domains:** Haven, Desolate
**Tier:** 0
**Default Stress:** D4
**Haunts:** 

-  ‘Doctor’ Varakin’s Pre-Emptive Dentistry (D8

Tooth Alley’s right on the bleeding edge of the Heart. It’s part of the City Above – for now, anyway. Day by day, little by little – tooth by tooth – it’s being drawn down. It’s called Tooth Alley because, well, the teeth. It’s not that *everything* in Tooth Alley has teeth, just that teeth grow on things that shouldn’t have them. Doorknobs, window frames, books, teacups. Snails and spiders, fingers and eyeballs and the backs of knees, trash on the wind or framed pictures of your grandmother. Teeth. Your grandmother, also teeth. The locals try to ignore the growing profusion of aberrant denticulation, because other than the teeth, it’s a safe neighbourhood, and it’s not like any *people* have been eaten yet.

**Resources:**

#### Mandrake Gardens
**Domains:** Desolate, Cursed
**Tier:** 1 or 2
**Default Stress:** D6
**Haunts:** Campsite of Bitter Vegetables (D6 Echo)

Mandrake Gardens used to be a safe place on the fringes of the Heart. A fragment of someone’s dream made real, maybe, or the memory of someone forced to leave their beloved home and come to Spire: a green valley, hedged in by woodland that thickened and darkened around you until, imperceptibly, you weren’t walking down a tree-lined lane any more, but a stone corridor. There was a little hamlet in the valley, swaddled in mist. It was – still is – a byword for ease and calm. In some circles, they still say “*he’s gone to Mandrake Gardens”* as a euphemism for a gentle, unhurried death.

Living here was like falling asleep. Then came Ptolemy Bay (p. 6). The Haven was razed and repurposed as a weapons factory, the inhabitants forced to labour in the foundries and processing plants. Once the area became uninhabitable, they abandoned the factory, moving onto some other unlucky portion of the Heart.

What remains is a blighted valley surrounded by a dead forest. It’s still swaddled in fog, but the fog has the yellowish tinge of poison gas. There’s still a hamlet, but it’s all empty hovels and the skeletal remnants of derelict industrial equipment.

And it’s still inhabited. Among the Heart-spawned horrors experimented on here by Bay was a form of Wild-aligned vegetable essence, and its residues mixed with the bitter ghosts of those who lived here. Eerie mandrake-creatures – human in shape and size, but made of tangled roots – crawl out of the earth and shamble about. They have no mouths, but with their knothole eyes they plead for revenge.

**Resources:** 

-  Leftover weapon salvage, toxic and/or volatile (D6 Technology)
-  Animal-mimicking vegetable essences (D6 Wild)

**Potential Plots:** 

-  The mandrake-creatures offer to heal a wounded delver (healing up to D10 Blood, Mind or Echo stress), but as part of the healing rite, the delver must consume a poisoned fruit born of their vegetable flesh. Eating the fruit means the delver shares the memory of the assault by Ptolemy Bay, and is consumed with the desire for revenge…
-  Vengeful mandrake-creatures sneak out of Mandrake Gardens in search of Ptolemy Bay, clutching emblems of the weapon-maker’s company. Unable to survive for long outside their home soil, they perish and rot, leaving enigmatic remains for the delvers to discover (and fertile soil for fruit-of-therot monsters, p. 13, to spawn).

#### Coinpit
**Domains:** Occult, Technology
**Tier:** 1, or 2 near the bottom
**Default Stress:** D4
**Haunts:** 

-  Granny Grimgroth’s Sitting Room (D6 Mind or

Body, D10 Supplies – but unless you bring trade goods worth at least D6 Supplies every time you return here, Granny breaks your kneecaps)
-  Slantwise Tower of the Truesquare Geomages (D6 Echo)

It used to be that Coinpit was a regular street – regular-ish. It was always a haunt of back-alley sorcerers, experimental retro-technologists, deviant cults, dealers in antiques and relics of questionable provenance and other disreputable weirdos. A few years ago, a human Junk Mage called Heresh Spellfule obtained a mysterious folder of papers from deep in the Heart. Whatever formula was encoded there doomed Heresh – his shop vanished overnight into a screaming, apparently-bottomless abyss ringed with hellfire. His neighbours shrugged and put up a wooden fence around the howling void (the ‘DO NOT ENTER ABYSS’ sign was added later, after complaints) and went back to fleecing idiots who came seeking arcane power.

In the years since then, the abyss has warped Coinpit. The whole street’s tilting into the hole. It happened slowly enough that people could adapt, but now Coinpit’s a near-vertical pit lined with ladders and rope walkways strung above the howling abyss.

#### The Shuntyard
**Domains:** Desolate, Technology
**Tier:** 1
**Default Stress:** D6
**Haunts:** Abandoned Vermissian cafeteria and storage sheds (D8 Supplies)

A graveyard of train carriages. Stacks of railway sleepers like cryptic monuments. Rusting machinery stored in brickwork sheds. Rain splashing in oily puddles, the only light the distant bobbing lantern of a night watchman.

After the failure of the Vermissian project, the various stations were sealed up and abandoned.

The trains were all supposed to be moved down to the Shuntyard, a storage facility off the Pulse line, for decommissioning, storage and eventual junking. Less than half the trains actually made it here, the rest were stolen, stranded or lost on the lines.

Only the carriages are stored here these days; the actual engines were moved elsewhere, although it’s always possible that one or two got lost amid the carriages. Still, it’s a rich humus of spare parts and salvage, with its endless rows of empty carriages – the cheap passenger cars intended for the Fathom line, the gay colours and peeling posters of the Candle line, the eldritch luxuries and private cars of the Loft.

#### Vermissian Night Watchman

These constructs are born from train engines that got blasted with chaos-energy in the Vermissian Incident. Cyclops-lamp eyes, engine brains, limbs of twisted rails, the night watchmen clank and stalk around the carcasses of their fellow trains. They have some instinct to protect – or mourn – the wreckage of the Vermissian, so the OCM uses the horrors to guard train depots and abandoned stations.

**Names:** The engines are all numbered, although the chaotic influence of the Heart means the numbers aren’t always real. OCM guards give the watchmen nicknames – Greeneye, Old Biter, Little Stomper, the Sergeant Major, Mr. Grumpy

**Motivation:** The Night Watchmen appear to consider the derelict trains to be the honoured dead, and seek to defend the remains of their kinfolk from tomb robbers.
