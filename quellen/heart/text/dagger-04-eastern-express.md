#### Chapter 2: Eastern Express

The aelfir brought in human sorcerers – the Gywnn-Ardasi cabal, of the Magwan Porth arcology – to map the Heart and channel its energies into the Vermissian project. When the Vermissian Incident occurred, some of the cabal tried to battle the contagion. Others tried to flee using their own rapidly-failing creation – the Vermissian line was designed to link disparate places in Spire, so they intended to use a train to travel all the way home to the Eastern Kingdoms. They’d drive their train throught the Heart, and the dream of home would become the reality of home.

They never even left the station. While the human sorcerers prepared to leave, the aelfir masterminds behind the Vermissian project were desperately covering up the disaster, and part of that was containment.

The human cabal could not be allowed to leave and spread work of Spire’s vulnerability to potential enemies – or be permitted to bear witness against the aelfirs’ own mistakes. They had to be silenced. The members who stayed were bribed and folded into Spire society – they’re now part of the Academy of Gywnn-Enforr (p. 126**4**)

The members who tried to leave? Well, the aelfir do love their poisons – the toxin known as ‘*blessed* *forgiveness’* was planted among the belongings of the Gwynn-Ardasi cabal by a gutterkin assassin.

### Legends of the Eastern Express
While the aelfir were successful in covering up the scale of the Vermissian incident when it first occurred, fragments of truth surfaced over time, like bits of a dismembered corpse being dug up by scavengers. The full story of the Eastern Express is known only to a few, but there are lots of rumours.

-  Cursed trains run eternally on the lower lines.They flash through empty stations sometimes, and you can see passengers’ starved faces staring hungrily back, desperate to disembark.
-  The Vermissian project depended on the sacrifice of thirteen human sorcerers. They were entombed in a secret station, but their souls were strung out along the steel tracks.
-  There’s a prokatakos outpost down in the Heart, full of technology for the taking.
-  There’s a prokatakos teleporter down there, and the gnolls intend to use it to invade Spire.
-  When a Vermissian Knight grows too weak to carry the weight of their armour, they’re laid to rest in tomb-carriages and walled away in sidings.
-  A god passed through Spire and promised to bring the faithful to her heaven – and there were so many righteous souls that she hired a train. Now there are corpse-carriages where dead passengers sit, waiting eternally for the journey to start.
-  When the sages of the Vermissian Collective learn secrets that no living creature was ever meant to know, they write them down on special scrolls of oblivion, and load those scrolls onto a train carriage. When the train carriage is full, it’s buried in the depths so no-one can ever find these repositories of forbidden knowledge. So, wanna go dig one up?
-  The Vermissian project was sabotaged by enemies of the aelfir. Those responsible were secretly murdered and entombed deep in the earth, as a warning to others who might challenge the rulers of Spire. Now, the disciples of the drow revolutionary cult treat that tomb as a holy shrine.

## Overview
The delvers learn of the Amyrran Zone, the bleeding portion of the Heart that’s an open wound *and* a short cut to the innermost reaches. And they learn that they’re not the only ones searching for this place.

## Staging
This part of the campaign’s pretty much *Heart-*by-the-numbers: delvers make their way through a series of delves until they reach the Eastern Express. There – assuming they don’t all get eaten by angry ghosts – they learn more about the wound in the Heart and the Delving Machine. Ideally, the expedition to the Eastern Express takes the characters through all the landmarks in this section. For an express route, skip the Perfidious Cathedral and Tollembrood. Drop these scenes in during Delves:

**Lost & Alone:** While travelling through the Heart, the delvers come across a lone traveller. He’s a drow from the City Above, wearing the stained overalls and breathing mask that form the livery of the Office of Closure and Mitigation (p. 19). He’s carrying a heavy Pigsplatter gun (the standard-issue shotgun for sewer workers and Vermissian line maintenance staff) and a cudgel, but the Pigsplatter is out of ammo. The OCM worker is cautious when it comes to the delvers, preferring to observe them for some time before showing himself. If they appear trustworthy, or at least not immediately murderous, he steps out and introduces himself as **Jago Tarte**; he claims (truthfully) that he was working in the Vermissian in the City Above, and got lost. If the player characters could give him directions back to Derelictus, and maybe a bite to eat and some other necessities, he’d be very grateful. Play Tarte as a salt-of-the-earth tosher; he’s a janitor assigned to mop up an atomic meltdown, and he’s doing it with a smile and a cheery whistle. He’s a sympathiser of the Ministry, too.

At minimum, helping Tarte costs D4 **Supplies** stress, but he’s got a much better chance of making it back to safety if the delvers dig a bit deeper (D8 stress). Whether or not the delvers help him, Tarte sets off back to the surface. There, he’s debriefed by his superiors in the OCM. When they realise he’d got lost near the Black Casern, he’s subsequently abducted and interrogated by agents of Aramos Brightness-Sears-The-Eye. What Tarte tells the aelfir depends on his interactions with the heroes.

-  If the characters didn’t help him, Tarte tells Aramos everything he saw: full descriptions of the player characters, their gear, and anything they let slip.
-  If the characters gave him the bare minimum,

Tarte stays quiet. He didn’t see anyone down there, honest.
-  If the characters went above and beyond, Tarte not only tells Aramos nothing, he seeks the player characters out afterwards (possibly through the Ministry, p. 18) to warn them that there’s some aelfir lord asking questions about the region they were delving in, and that they should watch out for trouble.

**Touch of the Hungry Deep:** At a suitable moment during a delve (ideally, when a character has just lost or sacrificed something of value), the nascent god of the Hungry Deep reaches out and touches one of the player characters. In the character’s eyes, the light dims and the air becomes colder, fouler. Mould sprouts on the walls. Metal rusts. The other player characters suddenly seem much, much older. Some might seem to be rotting corpses held together only by their armour. Those who may later undergo a transformation or evolution (Witches/Cleavers) appear bizarrely twisted and inhuman. All things come to nothing – and then the tide of decay retreats, the vision passes – leaving two things in its wake:

-  There’s an immediate boon – some foe or obstacle is blighted by the Hungry Deep. A barrier might be weakened by internal rot, chains rust and break, rocks crumble. A foe might suddenly succumb to a hitherto undiagnosed ailment.
-  The touched character feels a tugging, drawing them towards the nearest outpost of the Hungry Deep (likely the temple at Tollembrood (p. 39), Derelictus (p. 120), or the Perfidious Cathedral (p. 38)).

It’s up to the player whether or not they want to explore this moment of connection to the abyss. Cultivating a connection to the Deep requires embracing hopelessness and decay, and ultimately subordinating your will to The One Who Waits In Ashes**5**.

## Landmarks

#### The Perfidious Cathedral
**Domains:** Wild, Religion
**Tier:** 1
**Default Stress:** D6
**Haunts:** The Hermitage of the Belfry (D6 Mind)

At first glance, the Perfidious Cathedral resembles a temple or church that’s been overtaken by nature. Ivy covers the walls, shrouding the statues of saints and spirits adorning the outer walls (although a close examination reveals that walls and statues alike are made of greyish wood). Inside, fungal growths have devoured the pews and the altar’s an angular ant-hill. The stained-glass windows appear intact at first, until you notice they’re made from thousands of butterflies. Choirs of songbirds and bullfrogs intone sonorous chants. The cathedral is the Wild gone awry and craving order, mimicking the trappings and forms of surface religion.

Why Perfidious? The cathedral parasitises the faith of visitors. If you’re a believer in, say, Damnou, then the Perfidious Cathedral displays the trappings of a temple of the Moon Goddess, with luminous moths and spider-veils. If you’re a devotee of Incarne, then the temple drapes itself in crimson birds that shit piles of golden guano. Any prayers offered here are hijacked and channelled to the Wild God that’s growing under the cathedral.

Canny delvers can abuse the cathedral’s bounty of mimicry – if you can convince it you’re a worshipper of, say, Garrack the Steel-Boned, then the Perfidious Cathedral will have a usable surgical forge. Mention that you worship Our Lady of Vengeance and the cathedral sprouts an armoury of ersatz weapons (but they’ll work here, at least).

-  Cathedral-mimicked religious texts that, when you examine them closely, are actually really clever ants lined up on papery leaves (D8 Occult, Mobile)

Wild, Taboo, and you’ve got to dig for it)

#### Tollembrood
**Domains:** Haven, Religion
**Tier:** 2
**Default Stress:** D6
**Haunts:** 

-  The Gallows Club, a trio of talkative corpses, the previous rulers of the bridge (D6 Mind)
-  The Only Inn (D10 Blood, D12 Supplies – but you’ve got to exchange a resource one step higher, as the prices here are steep)

Tollembrood is a small hamlet at the midpoint of a stone bridge that spans an unfathomable abyss. The abyss is one of those irritating spatial anomalies that give cardio-cartographers headaches – a chasm so vast that both its length and depth appear immeasurable, but there aren’t any other known openings into it – just the Tollembrood path. Unsurprisingly, this literal choke point has attracted a succession of brigands, thieves, warlords, giant hungry monsters and at least one goat-king. It wasn’t until Tollembrood fell under the control of Thystre, a powerful cultist of the Moon Beneath, that the place became something of a Haven.

Thystre believes that the chasm might be the resting place of the Moon Beneath, and is slowly assembling the resources to build a skyship that could descend into the chasm in search of the goddess. She permitted others to settle here, collect tools and provide services to travellers – as long as they pay a cut towards the Church Skyship Fund. The half-finished skyship blooms like an unsightly and luminous fungal growth on the side of the bridge.

To allow her to focus on her divine project, Thystre recruited an acolyte, the drow Gysala – and that’s where the rot set in. Gysala’s a secret adherent of the Church of Absolution, a follower of the Hungry Deep and its prophet The One Who Waits In Ashes. She’s corrupting the Moon church here in Tollembrood, sapping the faith of the townsfolk. Thystre’s so blinded by her quest that she hasn’t noticed, and most travellers only stay a short time before moving on – it’s not like there’s much to do on a bridge.

What Gysala hasn’t realised is that the influence of the Deep is rotting the bridge structure. When you pray to entropy, it answers.

They intended to use the very transportation network they’d been building, and set up all the conditions needed to create a continent spanning wormhole, only they got poisoned before they could depart.

This place was their intended point of departure. Take the desire to return home. Evoke it, concentrate it, focus it, pump it into the reality-warping engine of the Heart, and then drive a train through it, and desire becomes reality. At least, that was the theory.

Over the intervening years, it’s blurred. What’s solid and certain here? #### The Black Casern
**Domains:** Technology, Desolate
**Tier:** 2
**Default Stress:** D8
**Haunts:** None

It’s clear on approach, that this place was not conjured by the Heart. No soul, no matter how broken, would have ever desired this bleak camp of tar-roofed sheds, the skeletal frames of construction yards, this rusting expanse of industrial sorcery and cracked tiles, sigils drawn in rails and thickly insulated power cables. The tunnels leading to this place are blocked by rusted barbed-wire fences and *‘KEEP OUT – BY ORDER OF* *THE COUNCIL’* signs. Once, it was part of Spire, a secret enclave under the city.

The Black Casern was where the Gwynn-Ardasi retro-engineers were housed while they worked on the Vermissian project. The aelfir mistrusted them and feared giving them too much influence over the project, so they kept them in this place – hermitage, workshop and internment camp all at once. During the Vermissian Incident , the aelfir sent in their forces to secure the Casern. Bullet-holes and other damage still scars the concrete.

Only later was the Casern drawn into the Heart, and it’s falling quickly. The chaos-rot has set in: the barbed-wire coils and flexes its rusty claws, and the signs change when you’re not looking at them (*‘HYPERCUBE INFECTOR – OUTLOOKED’*).

### Exploring the Casern
Papers spilled from filing cabinets, rotting in pools of oily water. Ritual chambers, wires trailing from the ceiling, discoloured spots on the floor where large pieces of equipment once stood. Little scraps of blue paper tacked to a wall, remnants of blueprints that were hastily ripped down and rolled up. It’s clear that this place was hastily abandoned, *then* ransacked, then sealed up and left to rot. Judging by the graffiti and other debris, the place used to be closer to the surface: there’s broken bottles, surface newspapers and other junk that could be dated to about thirty years ago, but nothing more recent.

Things of note:

-  There’s a *huge* **construction shed**, as vast as any of the Vermissian stations. You could sail a skyship in here. The entrances were once heavily warded against sorcerous energies; there are remains of titanic dampening-coils and burnt-out sigils of protection. Either they really wanted to ensure that no external influences touched whatever they were making in the shed – or they were trying to *contain* something immensely powerful. The shed’s long since been stripped of anything useful. A close examination, though, reveals that work continued in one corner of the facility after the rest of the place fell into disuse. This was where the Gwynn-Ardasi built the Delving Machine – and the corner’s where they assembled the Eastern Express.
-  There’s a **mural** (Handout 4) that was clearly created to mark the commencement of this project. It’s aelfir art, and depicts a group of human engineers on one side, and masked aelfir on the other. They’re all lifting their hands towards the fruit of their labours – a wound in the Heart. The Vermissian line grows from the wound, sprouting like a miraculous tree to wind around the Spire above. Examining the mural reveals:

B Something depicted as *creating* the wound in the Heart. The mural’s too abstract to tell what it is – it might be a machine, or a weapon. B The masks of the aelfir nobles have been carefully erased from the mural. The rest of the art is quite well preserved, considering its surroundings; the aelfir cherish their own conception of beauty. But the masks have been chipped out, anonymising whoever was once depicted here.
-  There are some **useful documents** (Handouts

5, 6, 8). For true immersive accuracy, soak the handouts in mud and engine oil before you show them to your players.
-  With a successful **Discern+Desolate** roll, the characters discover a little nest in the walls of what was once a cafeteria. It looks like someone was living here for some time – a child, by the size of the pile of rags used as a bed. Ghostly keening can be heard through the walls.

As the delvers approach this landmark, the passageway widens and the walls melt away. They’re entering one of the pocket dimensions within the Heart that give the impression of being outdoors, a mist-shrouded marsh under a blood-red sky. The impression of being outdoors, or that you’re shrapnel embedded in a bloody wound. Take your pick.

The Eastern Express train derailed, its driver dying like all the passengers – choking on aelfir poison. The train, consisting of an engine and three carriages, now lies half-submerged in the gory mire. Even though it crashed a century ago, the train is eerily well preserved. Flickering galvanic light spills from the train windows, making the swirling mists above it look like writhing ghosts.

### The Approach
Call for **Hunt+Cursed** tests as the delvers cross the mire. Those who fail get ambushed by the leecheys lurking in the mud (take D8 **Blood** stress). These horrors are the descendants of people who became lost in the Heart and tried to cling to their humanity instead of embracing the wild magic. They’re humanoid leeches, gelatinous and pale until they feed and flush a tumescent red. The leecheys were drawn here by the train crash, but could never overcome the wardings on the carriages.

### The Eastern Express
Once the delvers have defeated the leecheys, they can get a better look at the half-drowned train. It resembles a Vermissian subway train, the livery of the Pulse line still visible beneath the mud, but it’s been fitted with bizarre arcano-circuitry and protective wards. Opening the door without disarming those wards (**Mend+Technology**) is dangerous (D10 Blood stress). The fact that those wards are still running suggests the delvers are the first people to make it this far.

Peering in through the windows, the Delvers see dozens of corpses, green-tinged skin drawn like parchment across their rictus-grin skulls, all still in their seats in the subway cars like a lich tour group. From the look of them, they were almost all human. The aisles of the cars are crammed with retro-tech components and other baggage – the impression is that of a hasty exodus.

#### Chapter 2: Eastern Express

### The Map of the Heart
Searching the train reveals plenty of obscure technological tracts, sorcerous paraphernalia, arcane jottings and gizmos (D8 Technology and D8 Occult resources), and Handouts 1, 2, 3, and 5 (and throw in Handouts 6-8 if the delvers never visited the Black Casern).

There’s also a heavy crate, marked with aelfir sigils. Inside is a brass kinetoscope, a device for showing moving pictures. Press your eye to the peephole, turn the handle. Come and see.

The Gwynn-Ardasi mapped the pulses of energy, tracing the currents of magic that surge through the Heart. This place is infinite, unmappable, unknowable, but a map is not the territory – you can reduce infinities to a symbol, etch the bleeding edge of the universe as a line on a glass plate.

The Gwynn-Ardasi did not map the Heart, not really – but they created a visual model. Peer into the kinetoscope, and you see those magical fields expand and contract, the beating of a monstrous event horizon. It’s not live – it’s a dozen frames recorded a century ago, looping eternally. Looking at the animation, the delvers see there’s a wound in the Heart, located beyond Gap station. The name of the wounded zone flashes across the last frame of the kinetoscopic animation, burning an after-image into the observer’s eye – AMYRRAN ZONE.

There’s something in the wound, burrowing ever deeper.

And when each delver puts their eye to the kinetoscope, they glimpse their heart’s desire in that beating core.
