## Staging
**Contact with the Authorities:** Agents of the elite Black Guard – the aelfir who serve the ruling Council of Spire – ‘arrange’ a meeting with one of the delvers, using some personal contact as a go-between – or leverage. So, either it’s “*your old university lecturer says* *she’s got a proposal for you”* or “*come to the Ticking Clock* *tavern or we shoot your sister”.* The meeting takes place in the City Above, or in Derelictus (*Heart*, p. 144, also see p. 120) at the deepest – the agents of the City Above don’t go deeper than that.

The emissary from the powers that be is an elderly drow, **Verrine Tabrae.** Tabrae’s a former Vermissian Knight, now in eternal debt to the Office of Closure and Mitigation (p. 19). She stumbled out of a delve, badly wounded, and had to bargain for the train parts she needed to repair her armour’s life-support systems. She’ll die without a regular supply of recirculation filters and engine oil, and the Office uses that dependence to ensure her loyalty.

So, while Verrine’s sympathetic to the delvers – she’s been where they are – she’s here to serve the interests of the OCM. To the Office of Closure and Mitigation, the Amyrran Zone is the ultimate unsolved problem, the key part of the Vermissian that they can’t shut down. The Amyrran Zone manifested around the same time as the Vermissian Incident, so they’re connected. They dare hope that that if the Amyrran Zone is allowed to heal, then the Heart might return to dormancy, and malign energies would no longer threaten the City Above. They’ve sent teams into the Zone before, but they never returned.

The Map of the Heart may be the key to safe entry, so they want the delvers to enter the Zone and destroy whatever’s causing the wound in the Heart (i.e. the Delving Machine).

Verrine plays on whatever she judges might motivate the delvers. She might threaten those they’ve left behind in the City Above, or offer them a suitable boon on completion of the mission (forgiveness of crimes, a government sinecure, a big pile of gold – something desirable but wholly mundane, to contrast with the impossible desires offered by the Heart.

**Contact with the Ministry:** The Ministry of Our Hidden Mistress, the secretive cabal of drow freedom fighters, also have an interest in the Amyrran Zone. They’ve used the Vermissian line to launch covert attacks on the City Above (see the *Heart* sourcebook *Vermissian Black Ops*). In the course of their exploration of the rail network, they’ve brushed against the edges of the Amyrran Zone, and have seen enough to know that the magic of the Zone could be weaponised and turned against the aelfir. They haven’t been able to get any deeper – but if the delvers have a route in, then the Ministry is interested.

*Violently* interested. The Ministry dispatch a team of operatives led by **Meroc Twinne** to make contact with the delvers. Twinne’s a strung-out veteran of the doomed war against the aelfir occupiers, keeping himself going with stimulant mushrooms and paranoia. He’s not a healthy elf, but he’s a competent agent.

## Overview
[K] The Map and the documents discovered in the Black Casern hint at the existence of a wound in the Heart, and that mysterious machine that caused the wound in the first place was part of the Vermissian project. Eager delvers may rush off immediately, following the Map (see **In Search** **of the Amyrran**, p. 58). More cautious groups may prefer to gather more information first.

[K] Factions in the City Above have an interest in the Amyrran Zone – and as the player characters have shown some signs of time-displacement, it’s statistically likely that they’ll find their way there. Therefore, the factions may seek to recruit the delvers as agents and informants. See **Contact with the Authorities/the Ministry/Your Tormentor**, below. Target players whose callings or desires wouldn’t necessarily compel them to travel into the deepest Heart (Penitent and Forced come to mind).

[K] For groups that enjoy intrigue and conflict, have different factions recruit different player characters. If your players have little interest in the affairs of the City Above, skip the entire subplot and concentrate on the race to the Delving Machine.

#### Staging

Their preferred method is to stalk the delvers, waiting for a moment of vulnerability, and then intervening. If most of your delvers are drow, then the Ministry plays on their loyalty to their people; otherwise, they threaten the delvers or frame them for some crime and then offer to clear their names. Much as the Ministry might like to simply kill the delvers and take the Map, they’ve already visited the Mirror of Unreflected Thought (p. 64) and *know* that at least one of the delvers *will* get deeper into the Zone. So, they want to bet on a sure thing.

### Delving with the Map of the Heart
The Map of the Heart works as a piece of Delve equipment, inflicting an impressive D10 stress on any delve in or near the Amyrran Zone (including the pursuit of the Delving Machine, p. 90). It’s got the **Wyld** and **Reload** tags. **Wyld** means it inflicts D6 **Echo** stress on the user if they inflict stress with the Map and roll the maximum amount. **Reload** means that the delvers need to pause and take readings using an etheric barometer every so often to work out their current position. Using a barometer requires a **Discern+Cursed** or **Technology** roll, and presents a perfect opportunity for ambushes and other perils.

**Getting Aramos Involved:** The other two Opposition factions (Ptolemy Bay’s company/the Cult of the Hungry Deep) are active down here in the Heart, and so might clash with the player characters in the course of those sinister schemes. Aramos is another matter; for him to go after the player characters, he needs to first take an interest in them. How does the spymaster finds out about them?

If the players don’t take precautions, then it’s easy – one of Aramos’ informants hears about the bunch of delvers who found some weird Heart technology. Or if the players start asking questions about Aramos based on the Black Casern documents, that works too.

However, if your players are naturally secretive types, then you need to justify Aramos’ knowledge. Have someone in the player character’s orbit betray their confidence – and later, ensure the players find out who betrayed them. (If all else fails, they can find out who sold them out when they visit Aramos’ house, p. 128).

**Contact with your Tormentor:** A Forced character is ordered by their master to seek out the innermost Heart; a Penitent character learns that they can win redemption through the Amyrran. For the Forced, consider who their master is – and who’s really pulling the strings. An aelfir might trade the delver’s indenture contract to Aramos BrightnessSears-The-Eye or a cult leader might fall under the sway of The One Who Waits In Ashes. For Penitent characters, think about how they can redeem themselves. Might they use the time-warping powers of the Amyrran to correct their previous mistake? Or would the secret of the Amyrran Zone constitute a suitable repayment of their debt?

**An Offer You Shouldn’t Refuse:** A different Opposition faction tries honey instead of stabbing. They attempt to contact the delvers with an offer – hand over the Map of the Heart and anything they recovered from the Black Casern, and they’ll be rewarded.

-  **Ptolemy Bay** relies on the Azurite trade-priests as intermediaries, and sends **Deacon Silvereye** (p. 8) to cut a deal with the characters. She claims that a ‘patron of the church’ is interested in technological relics, and offers an honest and divinely endorsed trade: weapons and trade goods to the tune of a D12 Technology resource, in exchange for the map.
-  **Aramos** picks someone from the delvers’ pasts, someone they know. What haunts have they used before? Pick a name they already know as Aramos’ catspaw. The offer is generous (D12 of a suitable resource), but **Discern+Haven** picks up that something’s off about the deal, and questioning the middledrow gets the truth about how they were approached by shadowy figures from the City Above to acquire the map.
-  **The** **One Who Waits In Ashes** takes an even more indirect approach, sending out bleak dreams to a legion of followers of the Church of the Hungry Deep, flooding their minds with images of the player characters. The characters are approached by dozens of despairing wretches who beg for charity or offer the scrapings of the gutter for the Map. Collectively, they can cobble together around D8 of a resource.

#### Staging

**Into the Amyrran Zone:** At some point on their journey through this section once they pass into Tier 2, the delvers cross into the Amyrran Zone. This happens on the far side of the Scabbery if they take that route, or at a dramatic moment during a delve between two of the other landmarks. Characters who have a potent connection to the Heart – those with the Cursed domain, characters with Echo fallout and Witches – can feel the sudden wrongness, the heartstopping lurch of transition and the faint scent of decay that marks the Zone. This part of the Heart is sick. Less attuned characters can detect the edge of the Zone with a **Discern+Cursed** test.

The Amyrran Zone *looks* pretty much the same as the rest of the Heart – it, too, is a chaotic mess of weird architecture, impossible landscapes, bizarre horrors and carnivorous plumbing. Key differences:

-  There’s a general vibe of *rot* to the Zone, of sickness.

This place is an injury, after all. This theme is reflected in many of the landmarks, but work it into your descriptions, too.
-  Time’s strange throughout the Heart, but it’s especially strange here. Describe how the characters hear echoes from adjoining rooms and corridors, half-heard conversations where they can’t make out the words, but recognise the voices as their own. Describe characters they meet as wary – dopplegangers, ghosts and timeshifted duplicates are common here, so everyone’s conscious that the person they’re talking to might not be quite who they seem to be.
-  The Amyrran Zone keeps trying to knit itself back together; the Delving Machine constantly reopens the wound. The push and pull of healing and injury makes time in this region especially distorted – resulting in fun new fallout that exists only within the Amyrran.

**Fallout:** Unsettling Echo. [Minor, Echo]. Time glitches. Events still progress mostly linearly, but you find remnants of another possible timeline where things went differently. For example, you narrowly escape the blast of a thrown grenade – well done you! After the fight, you find a charred corpse where you were standing a few minutes ago – it’s an echo from the timeline where you failed to dodge. Take D4 **Mind** stress from the sight. [Immediate]

**Fallout:** Marked by the Amyrran. [Minor, Echo] The Amyrran lays claim to you, enfolding you in the slow-healing scab of time. You sense, intuitively, that you’re bound to this region and that leaving will be perilous. If you travel to a Tier 0 landmark, gain **Hollow** **Heart** and remove this fallout. [Ongoing]

**Fallout:** Hollow Heart. [Major, Echo] You feel sickened and oddly faded, like you’re a frail copy of your former self. Reduce all your resistances by 1. When you return to the Amyrran Zone, remove this fallout. [Ongoing]

**Fallout:** Claimed by the Amyrran. [Major, Echo] As **Marked by the Amyrran**, but much harder to get rid of. You still get **Hollow Heart** when you leave the Zone, but you don’t get to remove this one. [Ongoing]

**Fallout:** Near-Death Experience [Major, Echo] The next time you take Major fallout, it kills you. An alternate-timeline version of you shows up soon after – you’re playing that delver now, and this fallout is erased. Your new history is mostly the same as your previous character, with two key differences. Obviously, one difference is that this incarnation survived whatever killed the other. [Ongoing]

**Fallout:** Cursed Immortality. [Critical, Echo] You can’t die – but you can’t leave, either. You’re forever stuck in the Amyrran Zone, and can’t ever reach a landmark outside of Tier 2. [Ongoing]

### Losing the Map
The player characters can still reach the Amyrran Zone without the Map – it’ll just be harder to navigate the delves. The campaign won’t be derailed if the Map’s sold or stolen – and they can always steal it back later, anyway.

**Encountering Excalibur Carter:** (In)famous explorer Excalibur Carter’s waiting at the Chaingod (p. 59) for his agent Felice; Carter can’t travel below Tier 2, so he has to rely on others for missions outside the Heart. He believes that removing the Delving Machine may lift his curse, so he’s searching for information about the machine and for timeshifted delvers who have a chance of reaching it.

The player characters might run into Carter at the Chaingod, or he could rescue them from some peril on the way to the Wormery.

**In Search of the Amyrran:** Following the Map (or wild rumours, or Felise’s guidance) leads the delvers into perilous regions. The Scabbery (p. 62) is a moderately well-known landmark, although the only tales of the place are cautionary ones. Little is known of the shifting regions beyond – but the characters must brave the unknown to reach the Delving Machine.

As the delvers proceed through the shifting unspace, they draw close to the Amyrran Zone. Landmarks like the Mirror of Unreflected Thought (p. 64), the Receding Gallery (p. 65) and White Station (p. 66) herald the disruption of time.

## Landmarks

#### The Inkhive
**Domains:** Occult
**Tier:** 1
**Default Stress:** D4
**Haunts:** 

-  The Colour-Garden (D8 Echo, D4 Supplies)
-  Razther’s Morgue (D6 Blood)

The Inkhive is an uneasy collaboration of renegade scholars from the Vermissian Corrective and the eldritch serenities called Deep Apiarists. The main Vermissian Collective uses the tunnels of the Vermissian line to store archives and gather secrets from the shifting Heart, while the Vermissian Corrective believe this effort is ultimately selfdefeating, for even the information obtained from the Heart contains the seeds of chaos. The Corrective fear that the books and records so treasured by their colleagues are subtly changing, distorting their readers’ minds. This belief has proved violently controversial, with the Collective accusing Corrective members of being agents of the aelfir, undermining the fundamental validity of their work by sowing doubt.

The Deep Apiarists provide ink (made from honey) and wax-paper, both infused with the perfect order of the Hive. Documents written with these supplies are surely unchanging – they provide a baseline of certainty. The Corrective seeks to identify true facts that have not been touched by the Heart. They want a correct history, an accurate map, an uncorrupted understanding. These facts can then be preserved with honey-ink.

The Inkhive’s a warren of narrow tunnels, the walls lined with hexagonal chambers for books and scrolls. The scholars here work by candlelight, and have learned to ignore the bees that crawl over every surface. New Deep Apiarists are recruited from among the scholars.

Visitors were once welcome, but the growing schism between Collective and Corrective means they’re suspicious of newcomers. Entry requires a suitable offering (D8 Any, except Cursed) or proof of identity – and what documents or tokens can be trusted in this place?

**Resources:** 

-  Brightly coloured honey, especially delicious if eaten during the season of the matching pulse (D6 Supplies)
-  Documents and records that have been corrupted by the Heart and are marked for incineration (D8 Cursed, Deteriorating)

#### The Chaingod
**Domains:** Cursed, Wild
**Tier:** 2
**Default Stress:** D6
**Haunts:** Hound Checkpoint Cerby, emergency supply depot (D8 Supplies)

As gods go, it’s seen better days. It’s a titanic horror, roughly humanoid, about ten stories tall. Oh, and two-and-a-half heads. Once, it was a ravening monster that roamed the Heart – likely akin to the minotaur (*Heart*, p. 213) – until someone stunned it by blowing up one of its heads. (Forensic theology implies an internal explosion: the blast blew the left head’s jaw off, but also penetrated the soft palate and pulped the divine brain.) The unconscious god was muzzled, bound in enchanted chains and stuffed into a pit, and here it remains – although it’s since woken up with one hell of a headache.

The Hounds maintain an outpost here to keep watch on the chained god and keep its prison in good repair. They also warn travellers to turn back, for the only path onwards from this landmark is via a narrow ledge that runs past the right head – and the muzzle on that head broke a few years ago, so anyone going that way risks getting eaten.

The current commander of the Hound checkpoint is **Giezzel Mordaine**, although she really, really hopes that someone comes along to relieve her soon. She fears the chained god will break free, and would prefer if it happened on someone else’s watch.

Also currently resident at the checkpoint is **Excalibur Carter**.

### Excalibur Carter, I Presume
Waiting here at the Chaingod is a square-jawed, bronze-skinned living legend – the infamous explorer, Excalibur Carter.

At least, there’s something here that thinks it’s Excalibur Carter.

Four hundred years ago, Carter and his associates entered the Heart, and nearly made it through. They had the courage and tenacity required for any challenge, but not the wisdom, and certainly not the protective magic.

When the Delving Machine tore a hole in the Heart and created the Amyrran Zone, Carter’s expedition were caught in its wake**8**. One by one, they were consumed, becoming Cursed creatures. Carter and a few of his comrades have retained some humanity, but are very much part of the Heart – they cannot die here. (More accurately, they’ve got so many echoes and alternate-timeline versions of themselves that *some* incarnation of them always makes it through the danger, and their consciousnesses are smeared across their various selves so they act like there’s a degree of continuity.)

While the Amyrran Zone exists, they’re stuck in this living hell. They’ve tried and failed to make it to the Heart, many times. Carter suspects that the Heart has drained them so dry of inspiration and desire that they can no longer *conceive* of the end of their delve – all they can discover, forever, is more labyrinthine corridors and eldritch horrors. For all his skill and experience, Carter cannot reach the source of the injury. (Carter also doesn’t know the nature or origin of the injury – he’s unaware of the Delving Machine and its connection to the Vermissian project, until he gets access to the Black Casern documents through the player characters or Felise.)

Some external force is needed to break this deadlock. Maybe that force is the player characters – and maybe it’s the Opposition.

**Playing Carter:** Carter’s a potential ally and guide – or an ally for the Opposition. Play him as friendly – even if he does end up fighting against the player characters, he does so with sporting good humour and a lack of malice. At the same time, he’s fundamentally insincere – at this point, he’s died so many times and seen so much that life is like a hollow game to him. Consequences just don’t matter much to him any more.

He tries to ingratiate himself with the player characters by asking them why they’re down here, and then hinting that he’s seen *just* such a place on his travels. Why, if they buy him a drink or two, maybe he’ll remember where.

(Also, obviously, play him as an absurd pulp parody of a Victorian explorer, pip pip!)

#### The Mirror of Unreflected Thought
**Domains:** Cursed
**Tier:** 2
**Default Stress:** D6
**Haunts:** Probably none.

This landmark at the edge of the Amyrran Zone is the first one that’s been shaped by the player characters. Whatever’s here was created as the result of one of their Zenith abilities (or a really dramatic Critical fallout). Now, we’re still in the early portion of the campaign, so the goal here is foreshadowing weirdness and drama to come. We want a mood of eerie strangeness, not shocking confrontation. Ideally, the players should be making confused faces and grumbling about this being a pointless, confusing interlude – and then, *in retrospect*, after encountering more places like this, they realise what was going on.

Whatever you put here, the general appearance of the place is a desolate grassy scrubland, forever night-time, with a ceaseless keening wind that whips up the dust. There are ancient ruins scattered about, so eroded that nothing about them can be discerned. At the heart of the ruins is some echo of one of the player characters – again, so ancient and weathered that it’s virtually unrecognisable. It’s all bargain-basement *Ozymandias*.

Possible options for the centrepiece:

-  The hideous desiccated corpse of a witch who attained her final form. Shrines to the heroes who brought her down – all worn to anonymity – stand eternal vigil over the dreadful wreck of the monster that once ruled here.
-  A marshy region, with the wreckage of a once-beautiful palace to the Drowned Queen. Aeons ago, a Junk Mage ruled here. No sign of them remains, except a lingering feeling of melancholia that haunts the actual Junk Mage PC.
-  The remnants of a Haven the delvers visited in the past, dragged deep into the Heart by catastrophic **Echo** fallout (**Descent**, *Heart* p. 91). The Haven’s so wrecked and old that its true nature is not recognisable to the player characters – at least, not without a lot of archaeology.

**Special Rules:** The character who created (might in the future create) this landmark has troubled dreams if they rest here, to the tune of D6 **Echo** stress.

**Resources:** None. (Even if you have a really good idea for a time-twisted resource. Better to save it for later in the campaign.)

**Potential Plots:** 

-  Archaeologists and scholars from the Inkhive (p. 58) under the supervision of drow scholar **Gerlais Thrake** search the ruins, trying to reconcile the remains with the accepted history of the Spire. In their excavations, they’ve accidentally angered a pack of ghosts.

#### The House of Doors
**Domains:** Occult
**Tier:** 2
**Default Stress:** D8
**Haunts:** None

Tython Ardasi’s a Junk Mage in the employ of Aramos Brightness-Sears-The-Eye. Tython’s obsessed with the Ardasi cabal’s attempts to channel the power of the Heart. And for a mere train network? Nonsense! No, that power can be shaped into magic, into bound gods and servitor spirits. Aramos slipped Tython some of the Gwynn-Ardasi cabal records, and he used those to find this spot where many Heart-currents meet.

It looks like another derelict warren of tunnels and narrow corridors, broken and cracked, but magic seethes through this place, warping dimensions. Lines aren’t straight here; gravity’s twisted here. Ghosts and spirits flicker in and out of existence and unearthly lights glimmer in the recesses as currents of energy blow through the tunnels, the hot air carrying the strange stench of deeper levels.

Tython’s installed dozens of mismatched doors throughout these tunnels, each one marked with a different rune. He can play the flow of currents through this House like an instrument, shaping the magic to his needs as it enters the central chamber of his laboratory. Tython’s warped himself into a thing of sorcery. In outline he’s vaguely human, but with arms that bifurcate again and again (the better to make sigils with elongated fingers), with a skull grown pulsing and transparent (the better to absorb psychic aethers – and he can always wear a hat), with a dozen mouths and a single all-seeing eye.

The drawback is that the Master of the House of Doors cannot ever step through any of them, as he’s grown so dependent on the precisely calibrated magical field of his laboratory. He can adjust the doors to power extremely potent spells, but he can never leave and will never reach the centre of the Heart.

When he needs to communicate with his employer – or anyone else – he uses messengercreatures (rats or piglets, usually) branded with an arcane sigil. When he needs to speak through this messenger, he opens the corresponding door and his words emanate from the animal’s mouth. (The animal spontaneously combusts after a short conversation.)

**Special Rules:** Tython can increase or decrease the stress and Difficulty of any spell cast in his domain by 1 step. Meddling with his precious doors by forcing one open or holding it shut disrupts this.
