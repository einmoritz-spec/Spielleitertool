#### Chapter 4: The Wormery

## Overview
The delvers reach the Wormery, the only Haven in the Amyrran Zone and their base of operations for the second half of the campaign. Many in the Wormery are like Excalibur Carter, unable to leave the Amyrran for long, so the delvers can easily find employment as messengers and mercenaries while they gather information about the course of the Delving Machine.

#### Staging

## Staging
**Finding the Wormery:** The only Haven of any size in the Amyrran is the Wormery, so that’s the next stop for the player characters. Most of the people the characters meet in the Amyrran Zone know of the Wormery.

**A Place to Breathe:** Once the characters gain entry to the Wormery (which may require bartering resources or proving themselves), there’s scope for a break from the main storyline. There are several potential side plots suggested in the Wormery, and your player characters doubtless have their own schemes and complications – now’s a good time to explore them and weave them into the campaign.

**The Precise Nature of the Catastrophe:** The Wormery is also where you should look towards the end of the campaign. The Delving Machine offers a shortcut to the innermost Heart, a way to reach your ultimate desire – but it’s created the aberrant, injured region called the Amyrran. Various nonplayer characters in the Wormery have thoughts on the Delving Machine and what should be done. Use them to provoke debate amongst your players about what their real goal is.

**The Opposition Encroaches (1):** One of the Opposition factions openly advances into the Amyrran Zone, seizing territory and claiming a foothold. Pick *one* of the following:

-  **Ptolemy Bay**’s troops, under the command of the gnoll **Abayghur** **Thunderchaser** (p. 9) march across the Scabbery to the shores of Wormsea, and take over the fishing villages on the shore. They force the fisherfolk to start gathering heartsblood from the Red-Litten Caves (p. 95), and turn the fishing village into one of Bay’s weapons factories. They fortify the village with barbed wire, watch-towers, gun turrets and other defences. Food supplies in the Wormery become scarce.
-  **Aramos Brightness-Sears-The-Eye** dispatches the Knight of Larks (p. 18) to establish a base camp in the Demolished Heaven (p. 76).

Using Aramos’ records of the original Vermissian project, they reactivate the machinery in the bunkers, turning it back into the Advance Staging Area described in the plans. By recreating the initial conditions of the launch of the Delving Machine, Aramos intends to bend time and bring the machine back to its starting point. (In game terms – it’ll reduce the resistance of their attempt to hunt the Delving Machine, as described on p. 90).
-  **The One Who Waits In Ashes** tires of waiting for Second-Sister Maeja (p. 79) to succumb to despair, and instead launches a bloody purge of the temple, murdering any of the drow who refuse to abjure the worship of Damnou. For best effect, wait until the players have visited the temple and befriended Maeja before running this event.

**The Opposition Encroaches (2):** While one of the Opposition factions openly advances, another operates through stealth.

-  **Ptolemy Bay**’s forces ambush trade caravans run by Light-Falls-The-Leaf, trying to drive her into debt. He then sends Jecca Silvereye (p. 8) or another messenger to her, summoning LightFalls-The-Leaf to the Purple House of Incarne (p. 120) in Derelictus where he can force her to serve him, a spiritual hostile takeover.
-  **Aramos** uses his covert influence over the drow

Ministry to have Ministry agent Meroc Twinne (p. 54) dispatched to the Wormery. Aramos instructs Twinne to target player characters who might be sympathetic to the drow cause, hoping to lure them back to Derelictus or up into Spire (where Aramos can capture and interrogate them).
-  **The One Who Waits In Ashes** already has a covert presence in the Amyrran, through the hidden temple at Avallone (p. 78). So, her move is to get some of the player characters injured so they go to Avallone for healing…

Given a sufficiently strong stomach and a sufficiently titanic shovel, you could part the Wormsea and dig down to the seabed.

You would find the underlying terrain of this place to be similar to the Scabbery (p. 62), but where the Scabbery is hard and desiccated, the flesh of the Wormsea bed is soft, wet, red and gently oozing. The worms feed here in incalculable numbers, an ocean of trillions of hungry beasts, devouring each other if they cannot reach the rich bounty of the seabed. The largest worms are the deepest, and the ‘sea’ is made up of their lesser offspring and their slimy secretions. There’s no water in this sea. You can walk across it if you’re brave enough. (Slip into the slime and the worms eat you.)

#### Landmarks

Fisherfolk hunt these worms for their meat and their hides. Worm-hide is needed to maintain and expand the walls of the Wormery (p. 80), and the meat’s… plentiful. Worm-hunters from the village of Istmouth sail out on slime-rafts. They carry hooks attached to cables, and those cables are wound around gigantic capstans on the shore. Hook a worm, drag it out onto the shingles, then butcher it with polearms. It’s backbreaking work, especially since the mechanised steam capstans broke down.

The scholars at the nearby worm research station believe that the worms are related, somehow, to the bees of the Hive. If the Hive is perfect order, these worms might be avatars or incarnations of entropy (and likely connected on some metaphysical level to the Hungry Deep). The researchers seek communion on the same level as the Deep Apiarists, but after a series of disappointing and grotesque failures, they’ve put a temporary hold on any attempts at symbiosis.

**Wormleather Armour:** Honestly, it’s shitty armour – it doesn’t offer any **Blood** protection (although good or excellent-quality leathers can help in combat; see *Heart* p. 95). Its main virtue is that it gives **Echo** protection, increasing the wearer’s **Echo** resistance by 1. Buying wormleather armour costs a D8 resource (D12 for Good quality; Excellent leathers aren’t for sale anywhere).

**Resources:** 

-  Worm-meat (D6 Haven, Deteriorating)
-  Worm-hide (D6 Wild, Deteriorating)
-  Heartsblood from the sea floor (D8 Cursed, Taboo)

**Potential Plots:** 

-  There’s a constant demand for worm-hide in the Wormery, and the best worm-hide comes from the biggest worms. **Gall the Worm-Fisher** tells the tale of the White Worm, the greatest worm to ever wriggle. Slaying the White Worm would win the company renown in the Wormery forever. What Gall doesn’t know – or isn’t saying – is that the White Worm isn’t a worm at all – it’s something that came *up* from the wounded flesh of the Heart.

-  Another tale of the worm-fishers – once, hunters from one of the fabled Gryndel Hunting Clubs came this way, and they sailed a mighty ship of steel and bone. Their vessel has been seen a few times since, apparently intact but adrift on the Wormsea. Who knows what treasure’s still onboard?

#### Demolished Heaven
**Domains:** Desolate, Religion
**Tier:** 3
**Haunts:** None

On the Black Casern documents and the Map, this place is marked as ‘Advance Staging Area’ – it’s where the Delving Machine began its excavation of the Heart. It’s a vast, empty expanse of rough concrete shrouded in mists that still stink of engine oil and other chemicals, an industrial desert. Only a few concrete bunkers break the monotony, and these have long since been stripped of the arcanotechnological machinery they once contained. The Office of Closure and Mitigation was thorough when they shut the Advance Staging Area down.

Before this was the Advance Staging Area, though, it was something very like a heaven. Not one of the major heavens, of course – those still exist, deeper in the Heart – but there are countless lesser cults in Spire, the secret faiths of ghouls and gutterkin, cults of forgotten gods and religions yet to come. One such heaven was demolished, pounded flat and buried in concrete.

But faith endures beyond the limits of reason or hope. There are glimmers of the heaven that was once here, little flickers of beauty. A traveller here glimpses marvellous temples, or feels angels brush past, or feels for an instant the glorious presence of the divine. Someone out there still has faith in the god that dwelt here, and that lingering faith preserves those fragments of the demolished heaven.

If someone could identify the cult that once aspired to this place, if they could restore the faith of the cult and spread the holy name of the god, then maybe this demolished heaven could be restored.

And what’s better than heaven? How about a god owing you a big fat favour?

**Resources:** Discern+Religion gets glimpses of the lost heaven. Interpreting these visions is up to the player characters.

**Resources:** Blessed landfill (D6 Religion, Niche)

**Potential Plots:** 

-  This place is haunted by the souls of the faithful who died *after* their heaven was demolished but *before* the religion faded into obscurity. These desperate, faded ghosts wander the concrete desert, looking for their promised reward. Individually, they’re so thin and frail they barely exist, but as a collective entity they’re dangerous – or helpful, if the player characters can rebuild enough of the demolished heaven for them to find rest, or lead them to a replacement afterlife. (If placated: A bunch of Grateful If Somewhat Confused Souls (D10 Religion).)
-  The Delving Machine started its excavation of the Heart here – which means it’s still connected to this place. Starting a hunt for the Delving Machine from this spot allows each character to roll with mastery on any **Hunt** or **Delve** tests made in pursuit of the machine.

#### The Place You Made
**Domains:** Any
**Tier:** 3
**Haunts:** 

-  The sweetshop that your mother dragged you past, but you remember the wonders in the window (D8 Echo)
-  The den you played in as a child (D8, Fortune)

This landmark was created by an alternate-timeline incarnation of one of the player characters: they used one of their Zenith abilities and died here, long long ago. Before running this landmark, pick the player character and the Zenith ability used. The intended mood here is nostalgia and regret, not full-on horror, so go for one of the more positive Zenith outcomes. Some suggestions:

-  **Cleaver: Legendary Beast.** This is where the

Cleaver slew the Beast. The bones of the Beast lie at the centre of this place.
-  **Deep Apiarist: Dimensional Bastion.** The

Deep Apiarist character saved the rest of the company by shielding them against the chaos of the Heart.
-  **Heretic: Testament of Faith.** There’s a shrine here, decked in fading flowers. Pilgrims come here seeking wisdom and comfort.
-  **Junk Mage: Herald of the Stone Chorus.** There’s a stone door here, carved with the face of the Junk Mage. It’s not opening, ever.

Before the characters encounter the remnants of the Zenith-tier apotheosis, they pass through the outer reaches of this landmark. The landscape’s drawn from the memories of whichever character died here – it’s an echo of some important phase in their life (childhood’s always an illuminating option). So, the characters might find themselves walking through the alleyways and streets of the City Above listening to the distant hymns from the temple, or in some bucolic woodland far from the Heart where the Cleaver first learned to hunt, or in the fading memory of some great library where the Junk Mage spent many happy days. Ask the player to describe their surroundings and to add details.

As they approach the centre of the landmark, tell the player that they know what’s around the next corner: they’re coming to a place where their life changed in some way, where they were set on the path that led them down into the Heart. Maybe the character found a stairway going down into the red depths, or they met an old priest who charged them with finding a lost relic, or they were banished from the library for experimenting with a forbidden text. When they turn the corner, they’re instead confronted with the aftermath of their alternate-self’s Zenith transformation.

Don’t tell the players what’s going on, it’s more fun if they realise the implications for themselves.

No-one else lives in this landmark, although other travellers might pass through.

The Zenith transformation happened so long ago that most of its magic has faded.

**Resources:** D6 Supplies for any Domain other than Cursed, depending on the nature of this landmark.

**Potential Plots:** Eerie Witch-poet Janec writes poems and laments for those Delvers who perished here in the Heart, so that they might become cautionary tales for other travellers below. He knows nothing of the tale of the Delver who died here, though – can the player characters enlighten him, so he can warn others to avoid a similar fate?

#### Avallone
**Domains:** Religion
**Tier:** 3
**Haunts:** 
-  The Chapel of Tenacity (D10 Fortune)
-  The Chapel of Sagacity (D10 Mind )
-  The Chapel of Grace (D10 Blood)
-  Sister Senna’s Tuckshop (D6 Supplies)

The healing magic here is potent enough to remove Major Fallout – with limitations. The magic is here all about accepting the permanence of injury or loss, and then gaining the ability to live without it. They can’t regrow your severed arm, but the goddess of this place can bless you with enhanced fighting ability to make you as deadly as you were before. They cannot take away the memory of horror, but they can numb your soul so you don’t care that you saw such things.

Thick, clinging grey fog shrouds this landmark. Push on through the mists, and you come to the shore of a cold lake. There’s a small rocky island across the water, and on that island is what initially appears to be a derelict temple. Then, the bright Moon Beneath rises, shining through the mists, and the temple is revealed to be intact and glorious – a holy place sacred to Our Glorious Lady.

The moonlight also reveals that there are figures on the far shore – dozens of wretched creatures, pale and thin. These are the Stranded, former delvers trapped in the Amyrran Zone and broken by what they’ve seen and done, down here in the dark.

<!-- S. 79 -->

#### Landmarks

There are two ways to reach the isle. Some of the Stranded have little rowboats and provide passage for a price (D4 of any resource). Alternatively, anyone faithful to Damnou may walk across the moonlit waters, crossing the silver bridge of moonlight.

The temple is divided into Outer and Inner Precincts. Drow clerics tend to the Stranded and injured in the Outer Precinct, all under the leadership of **Second-Sister Maeja**. Maeja’s harried and exhausted, weighed down by the burdens of caring for all the Stranded, but for now she’s got enough faith to keep going. She tries to appear serene and wise as a priestess should, but if the characters get to know her, they see that she’s struggling.

Most of Maeja’s subordinate clerics are former pilgrims who went into the Heart seeking the Moon Beneath, and are happy to have found this holy shrine. For all their diligent effort, the condition of the Stranded never seems to improve, and more broken souls arrive at the Temple every month.

The isle is close to the Wormery, and there’s lots of traffic between the two locations. Avallone is the only place nearby that offers reliable and competent healing.

**The Outer Precinct:** The outer precinct’s divided into six chapels, each one sacred to a different virtue (Community, Fury, Sagacity, Grace, Tenacity, Vigilance). The temple’s healers and carers operate out of the Sagacity, Grace and Tenacity chapels; its few defenders – silent, masked warriors who have the power to become swarms of flies – dwell in the chapels of the other virtues. Their captain is known as **Watchful Eye.**

In each temple, there’s a door leading to the Inner Precinct – but no-one ever goes in or out, and the clerics ignore the doors as if they can’t see them.

**The Inner Precinct:** To enter the Inner Precinct, one must approach the door with the opposite of the virtue in mind (selfishness instead of Community, resignation instead of Tenacity and so on). On the far side is the true temple, the temple that the characters glimpsed when they first arrived – derelict and decaying. The lake is dark and foul, the mists a choking miasma. The sky is empty and the moon is dead.

The true temple is dedicated to the Cult of the Hungry Deep. The Stranded know this unconsciously, and in their wounded hearts they worship the Deep while the earnest clerics of Damnou tend to their bodies. Of the priests, only Second-Sister Maeja knows the truth, but she conceals it from her followers because she values the healing magic of the temple. She fears that if she turns on the Hungry Deep, they’ll cut off the blessings that make the outer-temple viable.

Clerics of the Hungry Deep tend to the Inner Precinct. The defenders of the temple are also Hungry Deep adherents. In here, they wear no masks. Descend into the uttermost depths of the temple, and it becomes a delve leading to the Throne of Ash (p. 107).

**Special Rules:** Note any Fallout that the characters remove here. The One Who Waits In Ashes can re-inflict that Fallout at will, as it’s only held in abeyance by their dark magic.

**Resources:** 

-  Scriptures and relics from the Outer Precinct (D8 Religion)
-  Scavenged Heart-relics that the Stranded no longer care about (D10 Cursed)

**Potential Plots:** 

-  One of the Stranded, a stammering drow named

**Osiren**, claims that he was part of an expedition from the Tower Division of the Gwynn-Enforr Academy. The rest of his expedition got stranded in a “hungry clock” (Grinding Halls, p. 92). He escaped, but he suspects some of his companions are still alive. He doesn’t have the courage to go back and rescue them – but if the player characters do so, then they’ll be rewarded by the Academy in the City Above. (D10 Fortune).
-  Second-Sister Maeja desperately wants to send a message to the Temple of the Moon Beneath (*Heart*, p. 150), but dares not use conventional means in case the Inner Precinct intercepts it. If one of the player characters arrives at Avallone with lots of **Blood** stress or fallout, Maeja arranges for their wounds to be treated – and tattoos her message in secret runes on the character’s wounded flesh. She then demands that the characters perform a pilgrimage to the Temple.

#### The Wormery
**Domains:** Cursed, Warren, Haven
**Tier:** 2
**Haunts:** 

-  The Last Gasp Tavern (Fortune D8)
-  Torsten’s General Store (Supplies D8)
-  Wormery Decontamination (Cursed D8; minimum D4 spend required for entry)

The fecund chaos of the Heart did not birth this place. People made this in their folly.

The Wormery’s an enclave made by intruders into the Amyrran, by people who know they are prisoners of a cruel god and cannot escape. It’s a maze of tunnels lined in worm-hide that dampens the chaos-radiation of the Heart outside, so the whole place feels like a blanket fort made of meat.

All official access to the Wormery is via heavy mechanical airlocks, and there’s a decontamination station in each airlock where caustic chemicals and purifying oils are sprayed at high pressure on each traveller. This is believed to rid visitors of any lingering Heart-taint that might otherwise corrupt the controlled environment of the Haven. Many of the inhabitants of the Wormery desperately avoid further exposure to the Amyrran Zone, as they fear they’ll end up stuck here forever. Bringing in Cursed relics and other sources of Heart energy is forbidden, and damaging the protective wormhide envelope is a serious crime (which means that using knives or firearms in the Wormery is frowned upon; brawling’s fine).

Pass through the airlocks, and you’re into the cramped confines of the tunnels. Privacy is at a premium in the Wormery. At first glance, the place feels like a little like a camp for mining prospectors or mercenaries out on campaign – everything’s an ad hoc mess of tents and lean-tos, most people are here just to restock supplies or blow off some steam, and it’s the last Haven on the edge of the unknown. It’s only when you spend a little time here that you realise there’s another aspect to the Wormery. There’s another community here, made up of those who are stuck here in the Amyrran, and those who want to avoid being stuck here and so obsessively avoid further contamination.

#### Landmarks

These (semi-) permanent inhabitants won’t leave the protected confines of the Wormery, instead using the transients as their agents and messengers. They tell themselves that they’re just waiting for the one big score that’ll make it all worthwhile, that one day soon they’ll be able to walk out and never look back.

### Locations In The Wormery
The Wormery’s centred on the **Base**, the cavern where Excalibur Carter’s expedition encamped all those years ago. That cave’s still the largest open area in the Haven – it’s the market square, city hall and communal sleeping den for those who haven’t claimed their own little nook.

Adjoining tunnels include **Car­** **ter’s Row**, where most of the taverns and trading posts are found, and the **Fume Cave** where the Haven concentrates its forges, chemical works and other noxious industries. The Fume Cave’s cut off from the rest of the complex by another heavy door.

### Who Runs Worm-Town?
The Wormery started off, centuries ago, as a base camp for Excalibur Carter’s expedition. One of Carter’s lieutenants, the formidable **Ysella Blake** is the official leader of the Wormery. Like her former captain, she’s effectively immortal, trapped in the Heart for eternity. Unlike Carter, who believes that curing the Amyrran Zone will free them, Blake’s convinced that they need to atone for their offences against the Heart before they’ll be forgiven.

#### Chapter 4: The Wormery

She maintains the Wormery as a safe place in the Amyrran, a refuge for those trapped here. Blake’s stern and fair, and is chiefly interested in keeping the Wormery secure and active. And by ‘chiefly interested’, we mean ‘clings to the Wormery like a drowning woman’ – her sanity and sense of self are bound up in this Haven, just like Excalibur Carter holds onto a façade of humanity by acting like the square-jawed hero of a pulp story. She’s suspicious of those who threaten the hard-won stability of her little empire.

-  Blake’s determined to maintain her position

– she’ll force a confrontation with the most ambitious or dangerous-looking player character, just so she can prove she’s top dog.
-  She fears that meddling with the deeper Heart might endanger the Wormery. As far as she’s concerned, the only people who might understand the Heart are those who’ve submitted to it, like the Witch-cult of Hallow. She’s opposed to any attempts to reach the Delving Machine – everything is so precarious here that cosmic forces shouldn’t be tampered with.
-  Blake’s in danger of slipping into despair – which would make her an ideal recruit for the Hungry Deep…

Blake maintains her auth-ority through alliances with three other powerful figures.

The first is the witch **Wyllt**, who typically manifests as a young boy of around six years old. Out of a mocking acknowledgement of the Wormery’s taboo against Heart-trait, he wraps himself in wormhide strips. When annoyed, he peels away some of the leather so his pulsing black veins can be seen.

Wyllt’s here to commune with the Heart and discover the true nature of the Amyrran – is it a wound in the Heart, or a birth caul?

-  Wyllt embodies the strangeness of giving into the Heart, of subsuming yourself to the nascent god of this red heaven. Play him as appealing, yet monstrous. Abandon all morality, all sanity, and you can be like him.
-  He’s jealous of other witches – he’s determined to become the Red Queen (*Heart,* p. 75) here in the Amyrran, where the magic of the place will preserve him forever – he’ll be the eternal leader of the cult, instead of ending up in the Red Vaults.
-  The Heart is ever-changing – who is to say that the Vermissian Incident was a *mistake* on the part of the surface folk? Maybe the Heart took the Delving Machine and the Vermissian network, all those trains and tunnels, as *offerings*. Maybe the Heart *chose* to embrace the Delving Machine, maybe it *wanted* to expand into the City Above. Who knows the will of god?

Second is the Cleaver **Hungry Bess**, a gaunt, nervous woman with an impressively distended mouth running down to her collar-bones. Bess recoiled from her destiny; she fears that consuming any more Heart-tainted flesh will destroy her. She took refuge in the Wormery to hide from the Heart’s corruption, and justifies her place here by serving as the community’s enforcer and executioner. If anyone makes trouble in the Wormery, Hungry Bess chops them up with her mighty axe *Sharkspirit* and then eats the remains. Hungry Bess is also the Wormery’s designated bellringer, sounding the muffled bell that hangs above the Base at the start of each ‘day’.

### The Mark of Corruption
The Wormery’s supposed to be a bulwark of purity, so strictly speaking Witches, Cleavers and anyone else irrevocably tainted by the Heart shouldn’t be welcome here. As the Haven’s leadership includes a Witch and a Cleaver, that rule is rarely enforced. As long as you’re not a slavering angel dripping raw heartsblood all over the upholstery, most travellers are allowed in. The rule’s more often used as an excuse to throw unwanted troublemakers out of the Haven.

Some residents, like Wyllt, wear worm-hide masks or armour to ‘contain’ any spillage of Heart energy.

### Other Wormery Residents
The most famous resident, of course, is **Excalibur** **Carter** who can be found propping up the bars in Carter’s Row as he recounts endless tales of his immortal exploration of the Heart. Are these tales contradictory because the Heart’s inherently mutable and maddening, or does Carter forget some bits and invent nonsense to fill in the gaps? Having heard rumours of the Delving Machine, Carter believes that destroying it will free those trapped in the Amyrran Zone. He speaks about it as his next great expedition – why, if you’re lucky, you too might have the honour of a supporting role in the ongoing epic of Excalibur Carter!

Less ostentatious is the Incarnadine priestess **Light-Falls-The-Leaf**. She wears the red robes of her order, but also hides her features behind *the* most magnificent aelfir mask imaginable, a glittering solar face dripping with jewels, precious metals, and potent relics from the Heart. Despite the mask, she’s drow, not aelfir – she demanded the mask as payment for a debt, long before she came down here. Like Carter, she’s unable to return to the surface, but still manages to run a trading empire, running rarities from the Amyrran up to Derelictus. Light-Falls-The-Leaf believes the Amyrran Zone is a blessing – whatever force wounded the Heart stirs up all sorts of potent magic, and there are fortunes to be made in the deeper reaches.

Typical of Light-Falls-The-Leaf ’s servants in the Wormery are the drow siblings **Miquet** and **Neraphine** who they came to the Heart seeking an escape from the oppression and poverty of the City Above. Miquet’s a Vermissian Squire; he’s salvaged a few bits of Vermissian technology that he’s cobbling together into armour, and seeks a knight to serve who might tutor him in the ways of the order. He’s heard tales of the Delving Machine, and secretly believes it may be his destiny as a knight to find it.

His sister Neraphine has no intention of staying down here, but they’re both debt-bonded to Light-Falls-The-Leaf. Neraphine hopes to become a priestess of Damnou, and has been spending time at the Temple of Avallone studying under Second-Sister Maeja.

### Belly of the Beast
The Wormery’s half prison, half mining boomtown. The churning chaos of the Amyrran means that many of the landmarks near the Wormery are rich in resources and magic; it’s easy to find a fortune here – unless the Zone claims you and traps you. Newcomers are preyed upon by those who’ve been here too long.
### Assaults on the Wormery
The Wormery regularly comes under attack by creatures called Flenslings. In their natural form, they’re aquatic insects about half a meter long, pale and spiny things that slip through marshes. They breed in great profusion in the wet parts of the Scabbery and in the Red-Litten Caves. They can inhabit humanoid corpses and animate them, and use them to build nests and provide food for hatchlings – the animated corpses wall themselves up in mud tombs. They see the Wormery as a rich source of food for the young – and they’re intelligent enough to try to sneak in disguised as the freshly deceased.

Flenslings can only inhabit tainted flesh – they’re drawn to those with high **Echo** stress or **Echo** fallout.
