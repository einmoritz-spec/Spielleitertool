#### Chapter 5: The Wounded Realm

## Overview
This part of the campaign describes the perilous region beyond the Wormery, in the wake of the Delving Machine. It’s possible – but only barely – for the delvers to catch the machine. More likely, however, the delvers’ first hunt for the machine ends in defeat and they have to fall back to the Wormery to regroup.

## Staging
**Signs & Portents:** As the delvers explore this region, they run into signs of the Delving Machine. The Machine’s forever pushing into the Heart, but as it’s outside of time, it’s also retracing its path again and again forever, present folding into past and future until they become one timeless moment at the core.

Each landmark in this section has a Wake entry, describing the devastation left by the Machine. Mechanically, whenever the delvers enter a new landmark in this section, roll a D12. On a 10–11, the Delving Machine recently passed nearby, possibly inflicting severe damage on the landscape. On a 12, it’s here right now but moving away. (Also, if you haven’t dropped in Zenith-foreshadowing for all your player characters, then consider the roll of a 1–2 as a prompt for you to do that here.)

**Hunting the Delving Machine:** Mechanically, the Delving Machine works like a delve that operates in parallel to ordinary delves. The machine has its own resistance, and stress is applied to both the Machine’s resistance and that of the delve. Reduce the Delving Machine’s resistance to 0, and you’ve caught it.

**For example:** The Chase the Delving Machine ‘delve’ *currently has a resistance of 25, and the delve between* *the Grinding Halls and the Hotel Amaranthine has a* *resistance of 10. The player characters pass a* *Delve* *test* *and inflict D8 stress on both Resistances, rolling a 6.* *The distance to Hotel Amaranthine drops to resistance* *4; the resistance to catch the Delving Machine, to 19.*

If the pursuers delay to heal and rest or to gather resources, get stalled by the perils of a landmark, stop to help a puppy or similar, then the resistance of the Delving Machine increases. The first delay increases the resistance by the roll of a D4, the next by D6, then D8 and so forth. Past D12, any hope of catching the Delving Machine is lost – at least for this attempt.

The starting resistance depends on how the characters learn of the current location of the Machine:

-  **Rumour:** 30. *The players follow a vague rumour or*

*just set off in search of the Machine without any solid* *clues.*
-  **Recent Signs of its Passage/Magical Divination:** 25.

*Either the players find a landmark recently devastated* *by the Delving Machine, or use an ability or other* *method to gather accurate intelligence about its* *current location.*
-  **Unexpected Encounter in the Wild:** 20. *The*

*characters run into the Delving Machine while* *adventuring (likely when they’re already wounded* *and exhausted and must choose whether to take* *advantage of this opportunity or retreat to safety).*

If it’s dramatically appropriate – and it usually is – then heroic self-sacrifice through a Zenith ability reduces the resistance by D12.

### The Blazing Eye
**Domains:** Occult, Technology
**Tier:** 3
**Haunts:** None
**Wake:** Flashes of green light; Bits of blasted metal litter the landscape as the tower blasts the Delving Machine.

A landscape of blasted rubble beneath a sootstained sky. The tunnels that lead to the rest of the Heart are steep-sided ravines – to cross between them, travellers must clamber up and scurry across the surface, darting from cover to cover.

The only structure visible in this realm is a single tower on a hill. A hideous light – harsh, greenish bolts of static leaping between this cruel beacon and the clouds. The light sweeps across the ground, drawn to motion – and anything caught in the light *burns*. Whoever built that tower intended it to be a firebreak against the Heart’s influence. It was made to draw a line, saying *there is the domain of seething red* *chaos – and not one inch further.* Obviously it failed, and now the tower is at the centre of a barren island surrounded on all sides by seething chaos.

On examination, the tower’s a mix of crudely cut stones piled up to support a much older structure (likely Prokatakos, and every time delvers discover an ancient structure, some sage toddles up, strokes their learned chin, and says *“hmm – likely prokatakos”*.) Inside is a shamble of wiring and arcano-tech – and a silent titan made of sewn-together body parts and mechanical components. This twelve-foot-tall hulking guardian is the custodian of the searchlight above, and does not appreciate interference.

There are signs that many people once dwelt here – and certainly this would make a remarkably secure Haven, if not for the unwelcoming titan and the fact that the light blasts anyone who moves nearby.

#### The Grinding Halls
**Domains:** Warren, Technology
**Tier:** 3
**Haunts:** None
**Wake:** The Delving Machine passes through a Vermissian Tunnel directly beneath the Grinding Halls. The rattle of its passage reveals the location of the hidden tunnel.

The Grinding Halls are audible long before a delver enters them. Within these halls, huge cogs turn endlessly, heavy pendulums swing, cryptic assemblies of gears mesh and tick, carrying out some vast calculation. There’s no solid ground here – every surface is part of the same grand clockwork, although some wheels turn more slowly than others. Like some intricate puzzle, the Halls move into new configurations, unseen hinges and axles turning to move the cogs into position. Finding a route through this vast living clock requires a successful **Delve+Technology** test.

The slowest – and hence, safest – part of the halls is the lower reach, down near the winding mechanism. (Something down there in the shadow winds the clock. Something big.) The higher you climb in the Halls, the more complex and intricate the clockwork becomes, apparently without limit. Certainly, only a few levels up, the cogs are smaller than a fingernail and spin with blurring speed, the air hot and thick with oil and copper filings. Somewhere in the attic of the halls, reality dissolves into perfect mathematical order, the complexity of the clockwork exceeding the limits of crude base matter.

**Fallout:** I Turned Into an Equation and **Sublimed.** [Critical, Fortune] Climbing too far, you discover that everything’s numbers, and you’re asymptotically approaching zero. You will exist forever on the threshold of non-existence. Until someone invents a D0, you’re not dead per se, but functionally out of the game. [Immediate].

Brass crabs maintain the cogs and remove any obstructions. Once, there were countless swarms of these mechanical creatures, but they’ve been harvested for parts and now only a handful of weary crabs survive to maintain the machine.

#### Red-Litten Caves
**Domains:** Warren, Cursed
**Tier:** 3
**Haunts:** 

-  *“Eat of the flesh and drink of the blood”* say the overly friendly Cleavers (D10 Blood)
-  The favour of the Wounded King (D10 Fortune)
**Wake:** The Delving Machine passes through the cave of the Wounded King. It also causes a massive heartsblood gusher, flooding the lower caverns.

Maybe it was the passage of the Delving Machine that created this red hell, wounding the Heart so deeply that its living blood welled up out of the ground. Or maybe this place is older, more primal – an offering from the Heart to its beloved, hungry children. These Red-Litten Caves are a labyrinth of basalt, volcano-vomited lava tubes and twisting scab-caves. The floors are awash in pure heartsblood. Hot red fluid spurts from fissures in the walls or wells up through the mud. It’s so pure here it glows with a ruddy light. In places, it even catches fire, chaosflame blasts exploding through the tunnels. The baying of distant beasts echoes for miles, and it’s hot enough to bake a Vermissian Knight inside their armour.

There are witches here, too – you find them in the larger caverns, floating above pools of bubbling heartsblood, psychic amniotic sacs congealing around them as they birth their future forms. Best not to disturb them.

This place is not safe. This place is the perfect antithesis of ‘safe’. Rampaging heartsblood beasts, cannibal Cleaver tribes, the ever-present risk of explosion or flooding – oh, and just the ambient Heart radiation is enough to warp the meekest up-Spire librarian into a howling cultist, bloodstreaked and naked, worshipping their own incipient divinity.

Despite that, there are plenty of visitors to these Red-Litten Caves. Pure heartsblood is valuable as a trade good or alchemical component. The outer Litten Caves are littered with the shattered remains of drilling derricks, pumps and pipes, testament to attempts to drain the heartsblood. These days, the methods used are more modest. Here’s a metal keg: go fill it up, don’t get eaten, and don’t get too much of the red stuff on your skin.

**The Wounded King:** At the centre of the labyrinth is the throne room of the Wounded King, a creature of the Heart. The King – or at least, half of him – was once a drow mechanic, one of the crew of the Delving Machine on its ill-fated maiden voyage. Beneath the gore, the tatters of a Vermissian engineering uniform can still be seen (his badge is number #4549). This unlucky mechanic fell from the Delving Machine and was sliced in two when it ran over him.

The Heart did not let him die. So, the Wounded King’s right half is that of a drow mechanic. The left half isn’t there – or rather, it’s an ever-changing red mess of tendrils and stringy gobbets of congealing blood connecting the King’s mortal half to the living Heart. Whatever remains of the nameless mechanic is wholly subsumed by his new role as a quasi-avatar of the Heart. Witches come here seeking prophecy; Cleavers bring him choice morsels of flesh. The right offering can compel the King to speak. His ‘visions’ are always him describing in the first person the experiences of the nearest Heart-connected entity to the object of divination. So, if the player characters ask him how to find the Delving Machine, he’ll describe, say, a heartsblood Beast’s view of the machine as it rumbles past. There’s still useful information in there, but it has to be disentangled from the observer’s perspective. (Alarmingly, the King can have visions about the City Above, suggesting that everyone and everything in Spire is at least a little touched by the Heart.)

**Special Rules:** Every hour or so spent in the Red-

Litten Caves gives the delvers D4 **Echo** stress. Deeper caves, heartsblood gushers and the like mean much higher stress levels (up to D12 Brutal in the deepest caves)

#### Castle of Skin
**Domains:** Desolate and any one other
**Tier:** 3
**Default Stress:** D8
**Haunts:** None, technically. But virtually any fallout can be removed here
**Wake:** The Delving Machine rips through the lower level of the castle – causing matching stigmata on the Bonded

This landmark’s a bit of a gimmick – the party ends up ‘inside’ one of the player characters. The castle is a reflection/alternate perspective/ memory palace of one character, who we’ll refer to as the Bonded. Any of the characters can be the Bonded – pick someone whose story would benefit from a little examination, or who hasn’t had the spotlight in a while. (If the characters return to this landmark later, pick a different Bonded or just make returning impossible).

So, the state and décor of the castle reflect the nature of the Bonded. If the Bonded’s an old Vermissian Knight, then it’s a crumbling, dusty old keep with lots of steampunk contraptions and oil paintings of mighty locomotives on quests. If the Bonded’s a young Cleaver, full of piss and vinegar and more alarming and unnatural fluids, then the castle’s more… well, spiky-meaty with vaulted ceilings of bone and red cellars dripping with what one hopes is wine.

The castle may be inhabited by shades from the Bonded’s past. They have no names, referring to each other by fairly generic titles (the Cook, the Astrologer, the Priest and so forth).

Things to note:

-  If the Bonded character has any ongoing fallout or similar conditions, then that manifests within the castle as a monster or other problem. For example, if the Bonded’s got the **Ravening** **Beast** fallout, then there’s a Ravening Beast besieging the castle. If the character’s got the **No Rations** fallout, then the key to the food pantry has been stolen and everyone in the castle’s pointing fingers. (This is an excellent place to spotlight Amyrran-related fallout like **Marked by the Amyrran**, which might manifest as an invasive fungus or an attack by Opposition forces.)

#### The Arch Of Gwynn-Ardasi
**Domains:** Technology, Cursed
**Tier:** 3
**Default Stress:** D8
**Haunts:** None
**Wake:** None. The Delving Machine doesn’t come this way.

The delvers come upon an unlikely railway line, unconnected to the rest of the Vermissian network. The line twists through passageways no train could never follow – maybe it runs along a narrow spur of rock, or snakes through cramped caverns. It leads inexorably to a titanic arch of stone, wrapped in cables and festooned with arcane devices. (It’s very similar to the Nameless Summer Country, p. 40 – but where that was abandoned and melancholy, this looks still active and… angry.) On the far side of the arch is seething red chaos, the terrain – including the arch itself – dissolving into the primal Heart. Currents flow and writhe within this chaos, energy flows mapped and shaped by the machinery.

This is what ‘*we bled god to power our magic train set’* looks like. There were once several of these archways, but others have been absorbed into the Heart. They still function, still connect the City

Above to the chaos below, even though they’ve been consumed and remade in new forms.

Signal-box cultists and other devotees of the Vermissian come here to worship this icon. They offer sacrifices, hurling unlucky victims into the archway. Captured delvers may be brought here for consummation.

However – things thrown into the arch may not be destroyed. There’s a chance that anything flung into the arch gets teleported along the rails and ends up somewhere else in the Vermissian network. Entering the arch means taking D12 Echo stress and putting your fate in the hands of the Gamesmaster.

There aren’t any routes onwards from this point. It’s a dead end.

#### Chapter 5: The Wounded Realm

#### The Last Stop
**Domains:** Warren, Cursed
**Tier:** Fracture
**Default Stress:** D8
**Haunts:** None
**Wake:** Special, see below.

A wasteland in the Amyrran Zone. Rubble and broken concrete litter a maze of narrow tunnels. It’s hard to tell if this was once a Vermissian station, or if it merely echoes the shape of one, as if stone and scab and spore have mimicked polished tile and steel rail. Poke through the crust of debris, and the walls ooze thick, stringy blood. The warren of tunnel mouths snaking off in different directions suggest that there are many ways through this landmark, but the passageways twist back on each other or end abruptly.

Many of the tiles are covered in scrawled notes or arcane formulae left by the one dweller in this ruin: a grey-bearded old delver, a castaway of the Heart. Beneath his tangled hair and the inkscrawls on his skin, beneath his rags, he carries the battered remnants of some scientific instruments – for this sorry fellow is all that remains of **Egrin Weselsmith**, once part of the Tower Division expedition to the Amyrran.

Long ago – back when he remembered sunlight, or happiness, or bathing – Egrin sought the Delving Machine. Drawing on his sister Hesha’s work (p. 9), he determined – correctly – that the Delving Machine will one day pass through this point in the Heart. What he got wrong was the time – he’s been waiting here for decades, recording all his travails and experiences in his diary. When he ran out of paper, he started writing on his skin and on the tunnel walls. (The horrible truth is that the Heart, in its ghastly generosity, gave Egrin exactly what he desired. He sought this place, and he found it.)

Egrin initially assumes the player characters are monsters out of the Heart, and survives the way he has for years – by running into the tunnels and hiding. The delvers can lure him out (**Compel+** **Warren**) so they can question him, or even bring him back to his sister in the City Above, or just grab the journal that contains his tale and leave him to his sorry fate. Optionally, use Egrin to spell out the possible consequences of controlling the Delving Machine. He might tell the characters that only one of them can reach the innermost Heart, or urge them to reject their own obsessions and put the Delving Machine beyond use.

**Resources:** None

**Potential Plots:** 

-  If you haven’t had a Zenith-aftermath encounter for one of the player characters yet, drop one in here. Suggest that destruction of whatever once stood here was caused by that player character taking a Zenith advance. Did they/will they sacrifice themselves to stop the Delving Machine?
-  If the characters haven’t started searching for specialised Delve equipment to chase down the Delving Machine, then this is an excellent place for them to look.
-  Or maybe they just race past this place in pursuit, briefly glimpsing Egrin as they go – a shadow of failed obsession.

### Doors to Elsewhen
The following three landmarks – the Fallen Spire, the Frozen Spire, and the Hollow Heart – depict possible futures where one of the Opposition factions have won. Only use the landmarks for the factions that are a major part of your campaign. In each case, describe the landmark as dreamlike and surreal; as the delvers draw ever closer to the core, desires become manifest.

Ptolemy Bay won. He bargained with the gnolls of Al’Marah, and took his revenge on the city he’d grown up in but never loved. His bargain was blessed by Azur the Trade God, so the gnolls knew they could trust their unlikely ally. The Delving Machine was the final part of his design. Spire shattered. Bay’s treacherous attack destroyed the aelfir district of Amaranth, the holy Solar Basilica, the shining Silver Quarter and the aerial docks. Falling debris crushed other regions of the city. At the same time, the gnoll armies advanced, seizing the initiative.

Now, Spire’s a disaster area. The aelfir have fled and the drow get to rule over the ashes and try to stay neutral as the war laps around the base of their broken city.

#### The Frozen Spire
**Domains:** Haven, Warren
**Tier:** Fracture
**Default Stress:** D8
**Haunts:** Spire Market (D12 Blood or Supplies)
**Wake:** None

While exploring the Heart, the delvers arrive at a long-deserted Vermissian station (maybe Well Station, *Heart* p. 157). While there, they hear the rumble of an approaching train.

The train that arrives is… clean. Well-maintained. Full of commuters. The doors slide open soundlessly, inviting the delvers aboard. Drow obediently shuffle back, making space for the new passengers.

With a merry whistle and a cry of “mind the Heart”, the train starts on its way to the City Above.

Aramos Brightness-Sears-The-Eye won. Not now, but more than a century ago. The Vermissian project was a grand success, and nothing whatsoever went wrong. (He still murdered the Gwynn-Ardasi cabal, but their prognostications of doom came to nothing.) Arcane machinery hums in perfect harmony, channelling the energy of the Heart into the Vermissian and containing it.

Vermissian! The veins and arteries of commerce! All of Spire has been knitted together into a perfect, orderly city under aelfir rule. Every day, tens of thousands of drow obediently troop into the train cars, and are whisked away to their assigned tasks. Every day, tourists and traders arrive at the international terminals, drawn by the wonder and prosperity of Spire. And every day, the aelfir bask in the glory and perfection of Aramos’ creation.

Perfect order. Monitoring posts at every Vermissian station weed out dissidents (and as the network is now the only way to travel between districts in Spire, they’ve thinned out most of the troublemakers). Trains loaded with spireblack and trash dump Spire’s waste far out of sight. The city is now a frozen monument to perfection. The Gnollish Empire is in retreat now that the war effort is supported by Vermissian supply lines and trainmounted bombs.

The characters can wander around the city for a while, stocking up on supplies or visiting old friends (many of whom have likely been executed in Aramos’ Brave New Spire), until they run afoul of the elite Black Guard security who, in this elsewhen, are all reanimated drow dispatched by the aelfir to eliminate traitors.

#### The Hollow Heart
**Domains:** Religion, Desolate
**Tier:** Fracture
**Default Stress:** D8
**Haunts:** None
**Wake:** None.

The delvers enter this elsewhen as they travel through the Heart. The terrain around them changes as they make their way through the tunnels. The fleshy walls, once red-flushed and solid, slowly become greyer and more fragile, until they’re reduced to a crumbling, gritty substance, as if everything’s made out of ash. The travellers come to whatever landmark they were making for, only it’s equally desiccated, drained of all meaning and vitality, a sculpture in dust. People might still dwell in the place, but they’re grey too, all hope and desire taken from them. The whole Heart is like this now. No danger, no monsters, no weirdness, just endless crumbling grey ruins. The delvers can easily travel through this Hollow Heart, but there’s nothing to find here, no treasures or secrets to discover. In places, the caverns of the City Below have collapsed, opening huge open sores in the land, and the characters can see the City Above through these gaps.

Spire’s less damaged than the Heart, but it has lost its gods. All spirits and divinities have fled – the cathedrals and temples are empty now. Only the Hungry Deep remains.

**Refugee Gods & Fugitive Spirits:** If any of the characters are Heretics, Incarnadines, Witches or strongly associated with a particular deity, then congratulations! As the only spark of faith within hundreds of miles of the Heart, you just became the last refuge of whatever divine scoundrel you venerated. Being forcibly connected to a god is worth D10 **Mind** stress *and* a free Major Advance connected to that god.

If none of the player characters are obvious refuges for divinities, then pick whichever character has the clearest, most passionate calling. As almost all desire and ambition has been expunged from this elsewhen, the character is a blazing psychic beacon of hope.

#### Landmarks

The contrast is palpable – qualifying characters seem to glow from within against the grey, empty world. And the thing about being a beacon of belief in a world of decay is that it attracts *attention*.

As the characters explore this broken shade of the Heart, they’re pursued by a monster. It’s all rot and entropy, a thing of maggots and grave dirt. Roughly humanoid in shape but mutable in stature, growing to the size of a giant or shrinking to that of an emaciated infant to slink through small gaps. The monster pursues the delvers relentlessly, its approach heralded by the scuttling of insects and a chill in the air.

At the same time, other player characters – the ones who aren’t shining spiritual beacons – hear the voice of The One Who Waits In Ashes whispering in their minds. The One tells them that they will never achieve their desires in the Heart, but they can safely give up and go home. The world above is still there – diminished, yes, but still there. The spell of fruitless obsession cast by the Heart is broken. They can leave. All they need to do is… nothing. If they choose not to defend their comrades when the monster catches them, then they’ll be spared.

Slaying the Entropic Shade opens a portal back to the regular Heart. The character who inflicts the killing blow on the Shade also gets a psychic glimpse of the location of The One Who Waits In Ashes (pick one – the Isle of Avallone (p. 78), the Church of Absolution in Derelictus (p. 121), the Perfidious Cathedral (p. 38) or the Throne of Ash (p. 107).
