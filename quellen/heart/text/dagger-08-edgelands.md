#### Chapter 6: Edgelands

## Staging
**A Visit Up-Spire:** The Vermissian project and two of the Opposition factions are connected to the City Above, so it’s likely – especially after a failed attempt to catch the Delving Machine – that the player characters pay a visit to Spire in search of more information or on a mission of revenge.

Optionally, if you’ve got access to *Spire*, then instead of your regular *Heart* player characters going up-Spire where they’ll draw a lot of attention, then the players could create temporary *Spire* PCs and contract with the Ministry (p. 54) to obtain the information they need.

Alternatively – and especially if your characters are afflicted with Amyrran fallout – then the best route for the characters could be to find a Vermissian station and backtrack up the line. Or take the shortcut connecting the two versions of the Hotel Amaranthine.

**Last Gasp of the Opposition:** Before the delvers reach the Delving Machine, have at least one and optionally two of the Opposition factions fall by the wayside. If a faction hasn’t played much of a role in the campaign so far, then have it stomped by a more prominent bad guy. (Maybe Ptolemy Bay attacks Aramos’ mansion or stomps the isle of Avallone; a resurgent Aramos has Ptolemy Bay arrested by the Black Guard, or manipulates the Ministry into assassinating The One Who Waits In Ashes.) Consider having a survivor of the smashed faction contact the player characters, offering information and resources out of a desire for revenge.

If you’ve got two prominent factions, then encourage the players to take the chance to inflict serious damage on one group while they’re in the City Above. For example, if the characters sneak into Aramos’ mansion, then run a final confrontation with the fallen aelfir there. If they visit Bay’s Emporium, have it go boom by the end. If they go to the Church of Absolution near Derelictus (p. 121), they can unmask The One Who Waits In Ashes.

If the characters don’t take out the weaker Opposition, then throw a final fight in the delvers’ path as they go after the Delving Machine.

**Hunting the Delving Machine, Take 2:** Several of the locations in this chapter hold highly potent Delve equipment that can aid the characters in their hunt for the Delving Machine. If the characters tried and failed to catch up with the machine, encourage them to seek out new resources and tools (or steal back what was taken from them, if the Opposition stole the Map of the Heart).

#### Derelictus, the City Between
**Domains:** Haven
**Tier:** 0
**Default Stress:** D4
**Haunts:** D10 Any

Derelictus is described on p. 144 of *Heart* – it’s a vast abandoned Vermissian train station, never actually connected to the rest of the line, now inhabited by all sorts of scavengers, pedlars, would-be explorers, exiled occultists and the like. Lost-luggage souls without anywhere else to go come here. All the player characters have almost certainly visited Derelictus before, as nearly every delver comes through here on their way down.

**Potential Plots:** 

-  If the characters bargained with agents of the Ministry/the authorities all the way back in Chapter 3, they can go to their sponsors for aid, trading what they know about the Delving Machine for better gear and supplies. The Ministry can source Delve D8 equipment, and the authorities have access to Delve D10 equipment. All the characters need to do is swear to shut down the Delving Machine safely (for the authorities) or turn the Machine over to the Ministry (for the Ministry). Both will, of course, demand blood oaths, hostages or other security if warranted.
-  Hunting a beast in the Heart? Even a beast of steel and technology? The Gryndel Hunting Club might be interested in such a quest! An experienced hunter, lured by tales of the Delving Machine, counts as Delve D8 equipment.

#### The Purple House
**Domains:** Religion
**Tier:** 0
**Default Stress:** D6
**Haunts:** Redemption for sale (D10 Any)

The Purple House is a temple of Azur the Trade God, run by Deacon Jecca Silvereye. (It’s called purple because it’s also used by devotees of crimson Incarne). It’s quite at odds with the neighbouring buildings – the rest of the City Between is desperate and crumbling, but the Purple House drips with wealth (and has security to match). The house’s prosperity does not have a singular source – Azurites are famously wealthy, Incarnadines are blessed by forcing others into debt, and the combination of security and theological neutrality makes the Purple House a natural destination for dealmakers. Oh, and Ptolemy Bay funnels his donations to the church through this temple.

Bay’s been an Azurite since he was rescued as a child from the Black Casern (p. 42). The Azurites revere the god of the market, holding that the divine speaks not only through prophets or visions, but through subtle signals too – price fluctuations and market forces, all shaped by the invisible hand of the golden god. All things can be traded in the holy marketplace, even life and death. While the Azurites are masters of the somewhat crooked deal, they hold deals and bargains to be holy – so in Bay’s eyes, the aelfir like Aramos Brightness-Sears-The-Eye are doubly damned. Not only did Aramos murder Bay’s parents along with the rest of the Gwynn-Ardasi sorcerers, he broke his contract with the cabal.

While Bay cultivates the reputation of being a devout Azurite**12**, his passions for revenge and high explosives mean he’ll never be inducted into the higher mysteries. His donations to the church – funnelled through Jecca – buy him a measure of spiritual protection. He makes offerings regularly – sometimes, treasures and silver from the City Above, delivered by **Hawks-Flight-At-Dawn** (p. 8), sometimes strange wonders from below, brought by **Abayghur** **Thunderchaser** (p. 9).

#### The Church of Absolution
**Domains:** Religion
**Tier:** 1
**Default Stress:** D6
**Haunts:** None

Located in a derelict cathedral not too far from Derelictus, the Church of Absolution (*Spire,* p. 125) is the centre of the worship of the Hungry Deep in the City Above. An unlikely cross-section of society, from fallen nobles to starving urchins, from magicians driven mad with ambition to those who have had all ambition ground out of their souls, join here to worship the cosmic embodiment of decay.

The congregation of the Hungry Deep is much larger here than in the City Below. The population of Spire is much bigger, and there’s a lot more despair and oppression Above. Yes, down below you might get devoured by some nameless monster, transformed into a hideous mutant, or wander for eternity in a lightless labyrinth made from the broken ruin of a dead heaven – but there’s always the possibility of redemption or even transcendence around the next corner. The Heart kills with hope, not despair.

Religious services are overseen by aelfir priestess **Mother Falling-Ash-On-Snow**, a cleric of the Deep so powerful that entropy congeals in her presence, robbing light of its brightness, colours of their vibrancy, words of their meaning, and matter of all solidity. She descends into the Heart through a gateway beneath the cathedral.

**The Temple Beneath:** The Temple Beneath was carved out of the living Heart by the collective despair of the cult’s adherents. The walls are ashen-grey and fragile. Divine worms and gravegrubs scuttle through the ruins, maintaining the entropic energies and feeding on the rot of the Heart. These avatars of entropy are the incarnation of the anti-faith of the congregation, and a close examination of the grubs reveals that each one has a tiny human or elf face – whenever someone gives up all hope and gives themselves over to the Hungry Deep, another grub spawns here.

The grubs can be stolen (or given with the blessing of the cult) and used as a sort of Delve equipment. They sap the energies of the Heart, reducing the terrain to bitter ash. Mechanically, they’re Delve D10, Limited 2.

Keep descending into this temple, and you end up in the Throne of Ash (p. 107).

**Resources:** 

-  Worms, scuttling centipedes, grubs, foulness in a bag (Wild D4, Mobile)
-  Discarded rags, jewellery and other items that once held value (Haven D6)

**Potential Plots:** 

-  Arguably, Mother Falling-Ash-On-Snow is an even purer embodiment of the Hungry Deep than The One Who Waits In Ashes. The One Who Waits remains capable of hope – she *hopes* to plunge all other living things into such utter despair that they suborn their wills to her, so she can command the Heart. The Mother has lost all hope; the Hungry Deep has consumed her completely. What happens when a follower is truer to the tenets of a cause than the leader?

#### Solstice Station
**Domains:** Religion
**Tier:** 1
**Default Stress:** D4
**Haunts:** None

This Vermissian station is the closest to the Office of Closure and Mitigation office, convenient for day trippers and any delvers climbing up from below. Solstice Station was designed as an occult vent: there’s a brick-lined shaft directly above the main platform, looking up at the sky. Heart energy vomits out of this shaft, relieving the spiritual pressure that builds up on the line. The energy’s usually invisible, but certain celestial conditions like a full moon can react with the vent plume, revealing the lurid, crackling chaos bleeding out into the City Above. More prosaically, the big hole in the ceiling means the station is always cold and damp. The aelfir designers tried to dress it up as an offering to the Moon Goddess (*“we took the ceiling off so She can look* *down on you”*), ringing the shaft with moon-white tiles and putting up some cheap statues of the Six Virtues. A huge, rusting steel grille, installed after the station was decommissioned, blocks easy access to the shaft.

These days, Solstice Station’s purpose is twofold. Firstly, it’s the Office of Closure and Mitigation’s ‘proper’ route into the Vermissian – officially, every other station and tunnel is safely sealed off, so on the rare occasions when some aelfir bureaucrat wants to inspect operations, they’re brought to the top of the shaft and there’s this grand ceremonial unlocking of the security grille (and the bureaucrat peers down at a pool of mucky water, sniffs, and declares they’ve seen enough).

Secondly, the City Guard maintain a fake temple to Damnou here – the temple’s wired with surveillance magic, so any dissidents or would-be Ministry operatives who take refuge here can be monitored and arrested. A damp actor, **Father Cyrus Moon­** **shadow**, sullenly camps here to welcome pilgrims. He’s terrified of the Heart and is privately convinced he’s going to be eaten by monsters from below, so the arrival of the player characters may alarm him.

**Resources:** 

-  Fake religious iconography (D4 Haven)
-  Stashed OCM Gear (D6 Technology)
-  The corpse of that one aelfir bureaucrat who slipped and yes, he’s still wearing his mask (D6 Occult)

#### The Office of Closure and mitigation
**Domains:** Desolate
**Tier:** 0
**Default Stress:** D6
**Haunts:** None

When first established, the Office of Closure and Mitigation was ensconced in a veritable fortress, a bulwark against the encroaching Heart, with artillery emplacements, aetheric wards, and thousands of troops garbed in shielded armour, all with wealth and prestige to match their grand and solemn purpose as defenders of the city. Now, the Office is situated in a dingy warehouse in a rundown part of Spire. Its neighbours are a skywhaleblubber rendering plant and an abandoned bakery inhabited by militant mice.

As the name suggests, the Office handles both the closure of breaches and the mitigation of any fallout. Routine maintenance is the safest work carried out by the Office – work teams check on the seals placed on the Vermissian stations, run places like the Shuntyard (p. 31), take aetheric readings, and generally give the impression that the Vermissian line is thoroughly sealed off. The Office turns a blind eye to intruders like the Vermissian Collective – as long the scavengers and the librarians close the seals behind them, the OCM doesn’t have the energy or resources to go after them.

Closure’s an emergency situation: when there’s a breach in the Vermissian, when something crawls up from below and breaks out, OCM technicians are supposed to rush out, drive back the incursion and seal the breach. In practice, the OCM only rushes to aelfir-controlled districts.

Most of the OCM isn’t open to the public, although there’s a morose clerk called Lykus Notte who dolefully fields all the complaints about heartsblood seeping under some poor drow’s floorboards or noise from the ghost trains.

#### Jacktar Station
**Domains:** Desolate
**Tier:** 1
**Default Stress:** D6
**Haunts:** None.

Further up Spire is Jacktar Station, a convenient stop for Ptolemy Bay’s Emporium, the Azurite Temple, and the Academy of Gwynn-Enforr. Unlike the other Vermissian stations, Jacktar hasn’t been fully sealed-off – it’s also part of the aerial docks where skywhales and giant ravens offload their cargo. Drow stevedores and airlarks work amid the insanely complex web of cranes, nets and other rigging, and pay little attention to the walled-off Vermissian station at the back of the docks. Every day, many tonnes of raven guano are washed into the tunnels, making them an unpleasant route into the Heart at the best of times. The Tower Division of the Academy of Gwynn-Enforr sometimes uses Jacktar for its expeditions below.

Ptolemy Bay runs the Jacktar Skydocks and half the cargo that comes through here consists of weapons shipments or raw materials. He also controls a side tunnel to the Vermissian that was supposed to connect Jacktar and Agate Stations. The tunnel was never activated, but Bay was able to use Gwynn-Ardasi technology to open it – giving him a route to the City Below that isn’t on any records. He maintains this route through superior firepower, and uses it to smuggle weapons up-Spire.

**Resources:** 

-  Megacorvid guano (D4 Wild, Niche)
-  Pet megajackdaw (D6 Wild, Mobile)
-  Stolen weapons (D8 Technology, Volatile)

**Potential Plots:** Shutting down Jacktar inflicts a serious setback on Ptolemy Bay’s operations. The docks are well guarded by Bay’s goons, but the thing about smuggling lots of illegal explo*sives* is that it’s easy to cause an explo*sion –* assuming you don’t care about those poor dockworkers.

#### The Academy of Gwynn-Enforr
**Domains:** Technology
**Tier:** 0
**Default Stress:** D4
**Haunts:** None

The Academy (*Spire,* p. 97) is a human-run enclave, devoted to the study of prokatakos technology and related phenomena. It’s very human – noisy, bright and crowded, with lots of open-air spaces and sunshine between lecture theatres and laboratories. The rulers of Spire have adopted a hands-off policy towards the Academy – humans are strange, cramming so much chaotic business into their short lives, that it’s better not to get involved and just skim off any useful technologies that bubble up from the academic stew.

**The Tower Division** is the Academy’s department of Heart research. Enthusiastic, wide-eyed graduate students go in; bitter, twisted husks maddened by revelation and torment stumble out. In this, it’s not so different from other academic institutions. Among the researchers in the Tower Division is **Hesha Weselsmith**, Ptolemy Bay’s pet researcher.

**The Lost Expedition:** One of Tower Division’s expeditions ended up in the Amyrran Zone and got stuck there. If the delvers rescue any of the trapped explorers (Egrin Weselsmith, Merelack, Osiren or Heconia) and escort them back to safety, then a grateful Professor Arkwright offers to aid the delvers (pick from: Delve D8 equipment for all, or upgrading one other piece of Delve equipment by one die step, or adding an additional use of a One-Shot or Limited item. All options come with extensive arcano-babble and dire warnings about the protean nature of the Heart.)

**Hesha’s Office:** Hesha Weselsmith rarely leaves her cramped little office in the basement of the Tower. Here, she goes through the records and journals left by dozens of expeditions into the Heart, sifting through accounts of madness, mutation and mayhem and winkling out nuggets of useful data. One diary describes an expedition being dismembered by cold fog that spilled from a cave mouth, another mentions passing a titanic stone head that exhaled white mist – what’s the correlation? Other Division researchers already went through all these records and spotted the obvious links, but the realm below is so *subjective*, so shaped by the secret, unspoken thoughts of those who travel there, that Hesha believes that only a deep, obsessive reading of the texts can unlock the true connections.

Her office, then, resembles a wasp’s nest crossed with the Map of the Heart. Journals, transcripts, reports and other documents are arranged in a rough pattern mimicking the regions they detail. In places, she’s torn pages out to serve as connections, even chewed them into papier-mache hyperlinks to trace spatially impossible links. Everyone else in the Tower Division thinks Hesha’s mad (and this is a university department dedicated to exploration of an alien unspace – that’s a high bar), but her research brings in private funding, so they tolerate her presence. Her brother **Egrin** (p. 110), by contrast, is one of the shining stars of the Tower’s exploratory program – or was until he vanished. Hesha idolises her older brother, and his disappearance has filled her with renewed horror of the dangers of the Heart..

Ptolemy Bay funds Hesha’s research in the hopes that she’ll be able to create a reliable replacement for the lost Map of the Heart. Hesha has no idea that her patron, friend and occasional collaborator is an arms dealer – she’s so focussed on the mysteries of the Heart that she pays no attention to the surface world. He keeps close tabs on her; her office and home are under surveillance by his mercenaries. Delvers who blunder in here get ambushed. Alternatively, Bay might decide to cut his losses and kill Hesha and his rivals with a single explosion.

The delvers might obtain Hesha’s work by:

-  Breaking in and stealing it
-  Forcing Hesha to hand it over at gunpoint
-  Convincing Hesha to turn on Bay
-  Returning with Egrin

On its own, Hesha’s Notes count as (Delve D8, One-Shot) equipment when pursuing the Delving Machine. However, if the characters give her access to any of the following sources, she can integrate them into her work, improving her understanding of the problem.

This station straddles the Spiral Line – the twisting spine of the sprawling Vermissian network – and the exclusive Loft line that was intended for the use of the aelfir nobility, and circles the topmost portion of Spire like a halo. The station’s architecture reflects its dual nature. Spire trains would arrive at the Lower Junction, a vast concourse of wrought-iron and polished stone, and disgorge their passengers, who would be efficiently routed through one of a dozen exits. Like worker ants, they would then swarm to their designated purpose.

The Upper Junction is a fanciful castle of faery, a citadel of ice that hangs upside-down from the ceiling. Loft trains approached on suspended rails, like some sort of angelic public transport. Access to the Upper Junction was via a single icicle-tower that reached down to the concourse floor, and was restricted to only to the masked aelfir. They could look down from the citadel above and see their subjects moving in accordance with Aramos Brightness-Sears-The-Eye’s concept of perfect order.
