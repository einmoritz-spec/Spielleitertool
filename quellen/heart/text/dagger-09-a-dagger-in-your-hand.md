#### Chapter 7: A Dagger In Your Hand

## Overview
In this climax, the player characters finally board the

Delving Machine – and must decide what to do with it.
## Staging
**Rival Hunters:** If you have two Opposition forces left in play, then one of them makes a final pursuit of the Delving Machine, and runs into the player characters along the way. This confrontation happens when the Delving Machine’s Hunt resistance is nearly depleted, in one of the landmarks described in Chapter 5.

As time flows differently in the Heart, the rival hunters have been down here for *decades* on this hopeless quest. They’re desperate, ragged, exhausted – and murderous.

-  **Ptolemy Bay:** Bay leads his own expedition of heavily armed cut-throats. After years of chasing the Machine, his final gambit is to cripple the Machine with explosive mines, and then rebuild it after it grinds to a stop.
-  **Aramos Brightness-Sears-The-Eye:** Aramos would never stoop to getting his own hands dirty – his final force consists of whatever agents the player characters have not yet eliminated. He might draw on Ministry drow from the Church of Eight Righteous Knives, the Knight of Larks, Tython Ardasi, or anyone else he can manipulate. Even now, he tries a final gambit – he entrusts a map to his agents that purports to show the route to the Heart’s core. The map resembles the readings taken a century ago by the Gwynn-Ardasi cabal, and could be used as Delve D10 equipment when following the Machine – but it’s a forgery, and a trap. If the characters use the map, it leads them into the Labyrinth of the minotaur (*Heart*, p. 147) where they will doubtless perish. A **Discern+Warren** test spots the forgery.
-  **The One Who Waits:** The One Who Waits waits no longer – she arises and leads a huge pilgrimage into the Heart. Her followers are the wretched and the despairing, the lost and the damned. Many perish on the way to the Amyrran, but each death is an offering to the

Deep, and only stiffens the faith of the host.

How do the player characters deal with such a tide of fanatics?

**Boarding the Delving Machine:** When it was built, the Delving Machine was only a machine. True, it was simultaneously a masterwork and a hasty bodge job of arcano-engineering, an alarming fusion of untried techniques and incomprehensible sorcery, and a monstrous burrowing tick of blackened steel. Now, after aeons of cutting through the Heart, it’s something else. Parts of it orbit around the central mass. Parts are no longer metal, but warped into chitin, or meat, or seething protomatter. It doesn’t bore into the rock, it slips through space and time, constantly hurtling forward. It’s a violation, a nightmare intrusion.

And the player characters need to get on board. The terrain around the machine changes constantly. Its spiritual velocity is faster than the Heart can respond, so it passes through new landmarks before they can fully form. Getting close to the Machine means navigating the chaos around it.

Ask each player character privately what sort of terrain they expect to encounter. Mash them all together. Then call for **Delve/Endure/Discern/Evade** tests as appropriate to navigate this madness. If successful, the characters manage to grab onto the hurtling Machine and pull themselves on board. Those who fail take D10 stress. They can keep trying, but the difficulty increases by one degree per attempt. Those who give up can still follow along behind the Machine, but can’t affect its course.

#### The Delving Machine
**Domains:** Technology
**Tier:** Fracture

**Default Stress:** D12
**Haunts:** None

The Delving Machine was built on the chassis of a Vermissian train engine. While it might resemble a conventional tunnelboring machine at first glance, it doesn’t drill through the meat of the Heart – it burns through realities, searing a path like a hot wire through potential, cauterising as it goes. The original crew of the Delving Machine are gone, sublimed into the Heart as the machine plunged ever deeper. The abandoned crew quarters are in a tender attached at the rear of the machine, while the controls are in the driver’s cab.

#### The Last Obstacle

## The Last Obstacle

Once the player characters are in control of the

Delving Machine then the Opposition tries one final attack.

**Aramos’ Offer:** Aramos contacts the delvers through an aetheric telegraph built into the Delving Machine. A monitor on the Machine’s control console flickers into life, showing the aelfir’s broken mask. It’s hard to see through the static and ghost images, but Aramos is in the Vermissian Operations Centre (p. 102).

And he’s not alone. He’s got a hostage or bargaining chip connected to one of the delvers. Ideally, you’re resolving the central plot of one of the player characters here (“*it’s your mother, the* *woman who vanished into the Heart years ago and you’ve* *been searching for ever since*”) – failing that, pick the NPC that one of the characters cares most about.

Aramos initially tries persuasion: the errors of the past can be corrected. The Vermissian can be perfected, the City Above made orderly, just as he envisioned all those years ago, and the player characters can share in this bounty. If they help him stop the Vermissian Incident and put Spire back on the ‘right track’, then, in this new order, he’ll ensure that they have places of wealth and honour. They don’t need to go into the spiritual abyss of the Heart to achieve their desires when he offers them all the temporal rewards they could possibly want.

And if that isn’t enough motivation, the hostage’s life also hangs in the balance. Aramos has magical protection against the raging fury of the Heart, but the hostage is in the Vermissian Operations Centre – they’re getting blasted with the full force of the magical radiation. The only way to save them from a fate worse than death is to undo the Vermissian Incident.

**Bay’s Weapon:** Ptolemy Bay takes the direct approach.

He burns all his credit with Azur, a lifetime of profit and devotion expended in an instant, his mounting debt to Incarne wreathing him in streamers of red fire. Everything he owns – his weapons factories, his mansion, his indentured servants,

*everything*

– burns with magical fire, a desperate offering to the powers.

The first signs the player characters see of this sacrifice are fires burning in the tunnel ahead of the Delving

Machine. As they rush past these flames, they see the ‘fires’ are pillars of red flame bursting from the bodies of Bay’s minions.

[K] **The One Who Falls:** The headlong rush of the Delving Machine slows. The flickering chaos of the tunnels around it ceases, the eldritch light guttering out and fading to a miserable grey. The very flesh of the Heart rots around the machine as it comes almost to a stop. (Almost, but not quite – the Machine can be dragged out of this spiritual sargasso with a successful **Mend+Technology** test.)

[K] While the machine’s stalled, The One Who Waits In Ashes approaches – accompanied by failed versions of the player characters, alternate-timeline shadows of the PCs who either sacrificed themselves along the way or who never made it. They’re united in defeat; they’ll never reach their heart’s desire. (As The One Who Waits is *also* a shadow-version of a player character, there’s going to be a notable absence in the group of shadows.)

[K] The One Who Waits says that the Delving Machine is an aberration, a shortcut. The Heart rejects it – that’s what the Amyrran Zone is. At most, one person might be able to ride the Delving Machine to transcendence, but not a whole group. The dreams and desires of most of the characters will inevitably come to nothing. Hope is a trap. Better to do as these shadows did, and surrender to the Hungry Deep. Let The One Who Waits ride the Machine to godhood.

[K] If they refuse, then The One Who Waits orders the shadow versions of the player characters to attack (use the same stats as the player characters).

Then they meet Bay himself. The sacrifice was just enough to get him here. He clambers onto the Delving

Machine, his massive frame laden with weapons and explosives. His plan is to seize control of the Machine and rig it to explode in the Heart, sending occult shrapnel flying in all directions. The blast will make the Vermissian Incident look like a minor oil spill.

I’ll destroy the Spire. It’s not the profitable end that

Bay wanted (The Fallen Spire, p. 111

), but the player characters have ruined him – all that’s left is revenge on everyone. (Bay’s combat statistics are on p. 7

.)

#### Chapter 7: A Dagger In Your Hand

## Last Stop
The Delving Machine’s current course is an asymptotic curve. It drives ever closer to the centre of the Heart, but will never actually reach it. Now that the player characters are at the controls of the machine, they can change that course. What do they do?

## Afterwards
Optionally, the next time you run *Heart*, use the events of this campaign as backstory. How has the Heart – and the City Above – changed? If the Delving Machine was destroyed, the Amyrran Zone is (presumably) starting to heal and the chaos-energy that suffuses the Vermissian network is receding like a tide. What does it leave in its wake? Does the Heart become less perilous as it heals – or does it now beat stronger and more savagely?

If a player character succeeded in reaching the innermost Heart, what became of them? Did they find what they sought in the deep Heart’s core? Does a new god grant blessings in the City Above?

**Putting the Machine Beyond Reach:** To preserve the status quo and the Amyrran Zone, the characters can pilot the machine into a portion of the Heart where no-one else can get to it. The Machine will be lodged there forever, allowing energy to leak out into the ruins of the Vermissian. If this isn’t the end of your campaign, the next challenge for the player characters is finding their way back to the Wormery.

**Destroying the Delving Machine:** Destroying the Machine will end the Amyrran Zone, and may also put an end to the Vermissian, restoring the Heart to what it was before it was injured. To destroy the Delving Machine, the characters must pilot it to a landmark with the power to smash it. The Slumbering Warheads (p. 108) or the Grinding Halls (p. 92) might work, or they could drive it over the edge of the Abyss in the Aetherstorm (p. 100). Alternatively, a Zenith ability could work – smashing the Last Train into the Machine in a head-on collision, or having a swarm of alien space bees encase it in imperishable crystal for eternity.

**Into the Unknown**
**Realm:** If you want to continue your *Heart* campaign from here, the player characters can use the Delving Machine to drive into regions of the underworld hitherto unexplored. Maybe they cut a route to the Source (*Heart*, p. 182), creating a bridge between the Spire and the central ziggurat of Al’Marah.

**Subterrene Pirates:** Alternatively, they just take the Delving Machine and go adventuring through the Heart at the controls of a marvellous route-carving machine. With the Delving Machine, they can link any two landmarks by boring (relatively) safe reality-tunnels between them – or go marauding, raiding otherwise unassailable fortresses of the underworld.

**Into The Heart’s Core:** Finally, the characters could nudge the Delving

Machine’s course by a fraction of a degree, pushing it over the event horizon of reality so that they plunge into the Heart’s core. As

The One Who Waits warned, a traveller must pass the ultimate threshold of the Heart alone, for everyone’s desire is unique to them.

Therefore, either the players have to agree on which of them will win transcendence – or it’s last delver standing.
