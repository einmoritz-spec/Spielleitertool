### 69 {farbe=#494344}
# TAKING
# SOME TIME
A montage is a special type of scene, a long period of
time condensed down into a single moment in spotlight
for each bug, a way of skipping over the boring (but
necessary) bits and getting right back into scenes and
journeys.
You might take a montage...
	» Before you set out on a long journey
	» When you get to a new settlement
	» When you need to go shopping
	» When you want to moult, and improve your bug
	» When you're camping in the middle of a journey
	» When you need to heal up after some tough times
## TASKS
The long action you'll take during a montage is known as
a task. It's just as freeform as a normal action - there are
some special types, but most of the time you can pretty
much describe what you're trying to achieve and, if it's
difficult, dangerous, or dramatic, you'll have to roll.
## MONTAGE RULES
There are a few special rules that apply to montages
that mostly come into play when you're doing a specific
type of task. We'll cover those over the next few pages.
The most important rule is just there to keep everyone
equal - in a montage, your bug can only take one
task.

### 70 {farbe=#494344}
# FREEFORM TASKS
*“They say a diamond is a piece of coal that stuck to the job. Unfortunately I don't know who 'they' are, or what a*
*diamond is, or where I'd find coal. But it sounds good, right? Sounds really motivational.”*
When your group takes a montage, all bugs can perform a single task. Some of these tasks are defined - recovering,
scrounging, and mysteryizing all have their own special rules and results shown over the next few pages. But there
are *loads*  of other tasks a character can take part in, from chatting with other bugs to get information to tending to
a small beast, from wandering around the nearby environment to explore to poring over maps and charts to learn a
little bit more about the world.
These freeform tasks usually give a benefit or outcome decided by the conversation, or the Weaver, and they only
require a roll if they're difficult, dangerous, or dramatic.
## GOING FREEFORM
Here are a few examples of what a freeform task might achieve when you're taking a montage...
	» Carlosa wants to find more information on the problems this new settlement his crew have arrived at is facing, so
he uses a task to wander off and find some kind of bounty board, or a knowledgeable NPC that can clue him in.
	» Maka wants to get a better idea of their surroundings, so they use a task to climb up a nearby tree for a
commanding view. This might make the next leg of their journey easier, or reveal a new landmark on the horizon.
	» Elsbeth wants to hire a picotitan for an upcoming journey, so she spends her task searching for a nearby stable.

# RECOVERING {farbe=#3b413f}
Bugs are pretty good at bouncing back from various
maladies, anything from a damaged scuttler to being
partially eaten by a fox. They can rely on their natural
healing, which is slow and steady, or get a bit of a bonus
from medicine, repairwork, or hearty food, which gets
a poorly bug back up and out into the world a lot faster,
but with a bit more of a variance in efficacy.
## NATURAL HEALING
Sometimes, slow and steady really does win the race.
You can use a task to sleep, which pulls your mood
positive. Alternatively, you can use a task to heal,
clearing one mark of damage on any of your aspect
tracks (part, tool, or buddy). This represents a lazy time
in the sun, tinkering with your gear, or ministering to
your even-tinier-than-you companion.
*Example: Sasha took a couple of knocks in her last*
*encounter, so she uses a task to recover. She clears a*
*mark of damage from her shiny shell, buffing it until it's*
*almost as good as new.*
### HEALING IN SETTLEMENTS
If you're in a settlement, you can remove burns just
as easily as marks (whether through natural healing or
by rolling, as shown below). But burns can't usually be
removed out on the road, or in the wilds.
## ROLLING FOR RECOVERY
There's also the option of speeding the process by
using a bit (to heal a part or buddy) or a bob (to heal
a tool). If you choose to consume a bit or bob, you can
use a task to roll on the table at the top right of this
page (the Care and Make skills might help out here).
This is riskier - a bad roll might not have you heal at all,
but a good roll can get you several hearts cleared at
once. Luckily though, using the required bit or bob does
give advantage on the roll!
*Example: Pinto has a delicious bit of cucumber he's*
*been saving in his bits and bobs slots, and he uses it*
*to try to heal faster. He uses the Make skill to prepare it*
*and rolls on the table, 2d6 for the skill and 1d6 for using*
*the delicious bit. He gets a conflict with a bump, healing*
*two marks at once.*
### DOCTOR DOCTOR
You can also use a task to make this roll for another
bug, if you're a doctoring type. You need that bug's
permission though, and any benefits you roll apply to
that bug rather than you.
## RECOVERY ROLL RESULTS
### TRIUMPH
You're feeling much better! Clear two marks
of damage from one of your parts, gear, or
buddies, OR clear one mark of damage and
pull your mood to positive.
### CONFLICT
It could have been better, but nothing
went dramatically wrong. Clear one mark
of damage from one of your parts, gear, or
buddies, OR pull your mood to positive.
### DISASTER
Which bit goes where again? Eh, at least I
got to sit down. You don't clear any marks,
but you do pull your mood to neutral.
### TWIST or BUMP
You can treat a twist while recovering as
normal, where something unexpected
happens. Alternatively, you can add a bump
to the healing, either clearing one more mark
of damage from any part, gear, or buddy
(even if it wasn't the target of the Recovery
Roll), or pulling your mood another step
toward positive.

# SCROUNGING {farbe=#3b413f}
*"Just remember what Mary used to say, before that unfortunate umbrella-related accident - a spoonful of sugar is*
*extremely hard to carry, even if you've got particularly large pockets."*
Bits and bobs are easy to come by if you know where to look, but the *right*  bits and bobs might be tougher to find in
a pinch. Using a task to go scrounging for resources might cover rustling around in nearby grass, fishing in a pond,
ransacking cupboards, or visiting market-stall traders, and the fact that you're taking your time to do it actually
makes it a little more reliable than gathering during a scene.
## WHAT DO YOU WANT?
When scrounging for something, the first thing you do is state what *kind*  of thing you're looking for - for example,
'something spicy', 'something sweet', or 'something hard'. If what you're looking for is easily found, you'll likely get
it without needing to roll just by using a task. You'll still need some empty carry slots to put the resulting find in
though, so don't just go hunting for stuff that you don't have room to carry (though you could always give it to
another bug in your band to hold onto in a pinch).
*Example: Clay, a chefly bug, wants a new ingredient. She's looking around a pantry and states that she wants*
*something sticky. The Weaver informs her that there's honey there, and that she can take a glob if she likes. She*
*does, and adds this to one of her carry slots.*
There's no approved list you need to pick from when specifying the kind of thing you're after, but be warned - if
you make your request too narrow, you're almost definitely going to have to roll thanks to the difficulty. It's
possible, for example, to look for 'something diamondy', but how likely are you to actually find that? *Unless you're in*
*some kind of abandoned jewelry store, in which case looking for 'something diamondy' is actually oddly appropriate.*
### TRIUMPH
Well, would you look at that! You find exactly
what you were looking for, a bit or bob that
relates directly to your stated wants.
### CONFLICT
Hey, I can probably use this! You find
something close to, but not exactly, what
you want, OR you find exactly what you
want, but it has a negative tag chosen by
the Weaver.
### DISASTER
*I'm going to pretend to know what this is.*
You find a bit or bob, but it's nothing to do
with what you were looking for, and might
even be the complete opposite.
### TWIST or ADD TAG
You can treat a twist while scrounging
as normal, where something unexpected
happens. Alternatively, you can add a
positive tag to whatever bit or bob you find.

### SCROUNGE OR GATHER?
Scrounging is a lot like gathering (found on page 52) in
that it gives a bug a nice new bit or bob if it goes well,
but there is an important difference. When scrounging,
you don't have a clear idea of what you want - just a
notion of the kind of thing it might be. When gathering,
you have a precise goal in mind; you want that thing
right there.
Both are useful in different situations, but scrounging
gives the Weaver more freedom to supply you with
something unexpected.

### 74 {farbe=#494344}
# MYSTERYIZING {farbe=#3b413f}
Some mysteries are just too juicy to be left alone! A bug can use a task to go searching for something that might be
relevant to the mystery they're currently investigating, hoping to make a small discovery that brings them closer to
the truth.
## LOOKING FOR A SIGN
While slowly uncovering mysteries happens naturally through play (usually during scenes and journeys), mysteryizing
during a montage helps speed up this process dramatically. Because of this, mysteryizing in this way always
requires a roll, and uses a task.
It's up to the Weaver to supply the situation or moment that helps a bug get closer to solving their mystery, but it's
up to the player to interpret it and draw whatever conclusions they wish - solving mysteries is a personal endeavour,
after all. *The Weaver might have to check the bug's current mystery before they give the situation, and that's fine too.*
### ROLLING FOR MYSTERYIZING
The table on the right shows specific results for mysteryizing during a montage. Because solving mysteries doesn't
usually require a roll, the skill used to aid the roll should suit the situation presented by the Weaver - it might be Think,
for considering what's seen, one of the moving skills for finding whatever might be revealing, or even a friending skill
to have a companion of NPC drop a hint that leads to a small discovery.
## MYSTERYIZE ROLL RESULTS
### TRIUMPH
A revelation! You find something that spurs
a small discovery for you that relates to
your current mystery, and can immediately
announce that small discovery.
### CONFLICT
A revelation, but not for me! You find
something that doesn't speak to you, but
it might well speak to one of your allies.
Another bug in your crew can make a small
discovery based on what you find, if they
wish.
### DISASTER
A false alarm. You thought there might be
something there, but there's not - you don't
make any small discoveries, and neither do
your allies.
### TWIST or RABBIT HOLE
You can treat a twist while mysteryizing
as normal, where something unexpected
happens. Alternatively, you can choose to
go down the rabbit hole, allowing the Weaver
to announce a sudden small new truth
about the world that your own bug has just
realized.

# COOKING
*“A few mushrooms, a dash of pepper, a strip of cured badger, and a garnish of tooth-onions. Now that's a dish.”*
Nothing beats a home-cooked meal. Luckily for adventuring bugs, a campfire feast is a close second.
When a bug uses a task to cook, they consume two (or more) bits with a roll of the dice to create a meal, a special
consumable that offers one of the following benefits when a bug decides to eat it...
	» Temporary Resistance: Gives a bug resistance to a particular damage type related to the ingredients
used, which halves the damage they take from that type (rounded down), as detailed on page 63. An excellent
momentary defence!
	» Temporary Skillification: Gives a bug +1 rank to a particular skill related to the ingredients used. This can't take
a skill above rank 3, but it's great for accessing skills a bug wouldn't normally have or giving their current spread a
bit of a bonus. You'll find the skills overview on page 144.
	» Temporary Undercut: Gives a bug the ability to reduce the amount of a particular type of cut by one, related
to the ingredients used (either Armour Cut, Speed Cut, Camo Cut, or Complex Cut, as described on page 38). A
meal can't reduce the amount of Tough Cut a bug faces though, and this reduction doesn't apply to cut a bug
chooses to take - only cut that's imposed by the Weaver.
## CHOOSING BENEFITS
The effects of a meal or portion should have
something to do with the bits used as ingredients.
Look at the tags a bit has, or think about their
flavour and properties - maybe a spicy soup would
give resistance to Sizzle, or a medley of scramble-
mushrooms allow a bug to reduce Speed Cut. *Work with*
*the Weaver if you're not sure what effects might be*
*suitable.*
## ROLLING TO COOK
A lot can go wrong during meal prep - because of this,
cooking always requires a roll, and uses a task. *The*
*Make and Care skills will likely be useful here.*
You can use the table on the right to find out what
results a meal will have once cooked, but there's
another important question to answer - *who gets to eat*
*what you make?*
## MEALS & PORTIONS
A meal made with two bits is enough for one bug,
but for every bit past the initial two that you add, you
gain an extra portion of that meal with exactly the
same benefits.
Once the Cook Roll has been made, you decide who gets
a portion of the resulting meal - maximum one per bug!
*Meals* /*Portions are added to the Creations section on*
*the Character Sheet.*
## COOK ROLL RESULTS
### TRIUMPH
A culinary masterpiece! Choose either
a temporary resistance, skillification, or
undercut. A portion of this meal grants this
benefit on all relevant actions and reactions
for the rest of the scene in which a bug
consumes it.
### CONFLICT
Useful and edible! Choose either a temporary
resistance, skillification, or undercut. A
portion of this meal grants this benefit on
the next action or reaction it would affect
within the scene a bug consumes it.
### DISASTER
Should have used a recipe. The resulting
meal is edible, technically, but it doesn't give
any benefits when consumed.
### TWIST or DEPTH
You can treat a twist while cooking as
normal, where something unexpected
happens. Alternatively, you can add a depth
of flavour that even a master chef would
be proud of - with a conflict or triumph,
choose an additional temporary resistance,
skillification or undercut for portions of this
meal to grant when consumed, OR have
each pull mood positive when consumed.

# CRAFTING {farbe=#3b413f}
*"My new invention is guaranteed to fail, but what a spectacular failure it's going to be!"*
Crafting follows the same kind of structure as cooking, but with a very different outcome. When crafting, a bug
consumes two (or more) bobs with a roll of the dice to create a contraption, a temporary creation that mimics the
benefits and drawbacks of an aspect the user wouldn't normally have access to.
A contraption can mimic any aspect from the aspects section of this book - parts, gear, or buddies, all can be
mimicked by some temporary mechanical marvel if the right bobs are combined.
## CHOOSING AN ASPECT
The aspect a contraption mimics is up to the inventor, but it should have something to do with the bobs used
as components. Think about the physical properties of your bobs, and what they might be repurposed to do - an
alligator clip might serve to help create the Mandibles part from page 108, or a bolt of fabric help to make some
Dronewings from page 124. *Work with the Weaver and the rest of the table if you're not sure what aspects might be*
*craftable with the bobs you have to hand.*
## ROLLING TO CRAFT
Tinkering with bobs is rarely a smooth process - because of this, crafting always requires a roll, and uses a task.
*The Make, Think, and Rummage skills will likely be useful here.*
The table below and to the right shows what results an invention will have once pieced together, but there's another
important question to answer - *which lucky bug gets to be a test subject once the contraption is up and running?*
## CRAFT ROLL RESULTS
### TRIUMPH
A singular work! Choose one aspect to
mimic. A prototype gives a copy of this
aspect that works from the first time it's
used for a benefit of some kind until the end
of that scene.
### CONFLICT
Serviceable! Choose one aspect to mimic.
A prototype gives a copy of this aspect that
works... once, and then falls apart.
### DISASTER
Great advances require great failures from
time to time. Your bobs are ruined and you
gain nothing from the process.
### TWIST or AUGMENT
You can treat a twist while crafting as
normal, where something unexpected
happens. Alternatively (alongside a conflict
or triumph), you can add an augment to your
mimicked aspect, giving it an extra benefit
taken from any of the augments listed in
chapter 6.

## CONTRAPTIONS & COPIES
A contraption made with two bobs is enough for one bug to use, but adding more bobs to the crafting process allows
you to make additional copies to hand out to your (probably quite nervous) comrades.
Crafting with two bobs gives you one copy of the resulting contraption. For every bob past the initial two that you
add, you gain an extra copy. Once the Craft Roll has been made, you decide who gets a copy of your contraption
to use! *A bug can only hold one contraption at a time, and contraptions are added to the Creations section on the*
*Character Sheet. They can even be used to give 1d6 to the aspect section of an Action Roll!*
### WHERE ARE ALL THE TRACKS?
Contraptions differ in one important way from the aspects they mimic - they don't have tracks. If a bug's
contraption needs to be marked or burned in order to give a benefit, it works once and then immediately falls
apart. Same goes for if a bug wants to use it to absorb damage - it'll eat one hit of any size without harming the bug
holding it, and then collapse into unusable pieces.
Naturally this means that some aspects work better as contraptions than others. We recommend experimenting as
you play to find which work for you and your bugly allies. And who knows, if a bug likes a contraption enough, they
might even grab a permanent version from the aspects chapter when they next moult!

# MOULTING
*“It's a new dawn, it's a new day, I'm a new bug... And I'm feeling good!”*
A very special task that only becomes useful for longer-term play, moulting allows a bug to ingest some of
the Nectar they've found (PICO's character creation and advancement resource), spin a little cocoon around
themselves, and then emerge at the end of the task with a shiny new feature to show off! They might come out
with new skill ranks, or a new aspect, or an extra augment on one of their existing aspects. Isn't nature wonderful?
There are no dice rolls to make while moulting, and there's no risk of failure - it's a 'good stuff only' kind of situation.
## WHEN TO MOULT?
There are three criteria you need to meet in order to moult, which we'll run through below.
	» 1: You need a safe space. Most montages are taken in reasonably safe locations, but not all - if you're using a
montage to explore some kind of hostile territory, splitting up and leaving one bug behind to moult is a pretty
terrible idea. There are some situations, like when you're camping during a journey, that might have a potential
element of danger to them. Moulting here is fine, and even if your camp is raided by bugbarians, the fiction should
assume that the moulting bug completes the process just as they arrive. They might be a little groggy, but they'll
be fine.
	» 2: You need enough Nectar to afford a character advancement. Moulting essentially allows you to purchase
new ranks in a skill, or a new aspect or augment, mid-game. You'll find the cost for these different types of
advancement both on page 102 (which also tells you more about Nectar) and on the very next page, at the top left.
But if you haven't accrued enough Nectar to choose one of those options as you moult, you can't even spin the
cocoon to start the process.
	» 3: You need to know what you want. This is a simple but important rule: moulting shouldn't slow the game down
more than any other task. Don't choose to moult and then leaf through the book, deciding which options might
appeal - that'll be boring for everybody. A better way is to plan out roughly what you're going to take in advance,
so that when you get the chance to moult you can add it to your Character Sheet as the other bugs in your band
perform their own tasks.

### ADVANCEMENT COSTS AND OPTIONS
When you moult, you'll have the chance of purchasing
a skill rank, an aspect, or an augment. *You'll find more*
*information on each of these character features*
*starting on page 100.*
The costs, in Nectar, are as follows...
	» Add an Aspect: 3 Nectar
	» Add an Augment: 2 Nectar
	» Increase a Skill Rank: Nectar equal to the rank you're
attaining (with a rank 1 skill costing 1 Nectar, an
upgrade from rank 1 to rank 2 costing 2 Nectar, and
an upgrade from rank 2 to rank 3 costing 3 Nectar).
If you have a *lot*  of Nectar to spend, you can choose
multiple options at the same time, but you can't
choose the same option multiple times during one
moult (so you can increase one skill rank, add one
aspect, and add one augment with a single task).
