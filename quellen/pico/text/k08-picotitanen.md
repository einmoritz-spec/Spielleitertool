### 171 {farbe=#494344}
## WHAT ARE PICOTITANS?
That chicken with a bakery on its back, scratching dirt
just outside of Craniopolis? The munchkin cat with
goggles and racing stripes, zooming along the garden
path? The golden-furred fox with a watchtower on its
tail and a gondola nestled under its chin, having its coat
brushed by adoring passengers?
Those are all picotitans - massive beasts that form
long-lasting bonds with bugs, acting as helper, vehicle,
and roving home.
They're kind of a big deal.
## SIZE & TRAINING
Picotitans are much bigger than bugs.
This might seem like it goes without saying, but the
concept of relative size is an important one here -
they're bigger than bugs, but *smaller than other titans* .
In terms of the official size chart on pages 12 and 13,
they sit somewhere between huge and titan size.
This is because picotitans are most often the runts
of a particular titan litter, pushed from the nest or
abandoned on the forest-fringe when young. Bugs
might be friendly, on the whole, but nature is cruel - it
happens more than you might think.
Enterprising titan-trainers seek out these abandoned
animals (usually not too hard, given the amount of noise
they can make), rescue them, and raise them, with most
of their efforts going in the direction of instilling a young
picotitan with a singular important idea - that bugs are
friends you can *trust* , rather than friends you can *eat* .
And as far as anybody knows, titan-trainers have a
100% success rate in such matters. You certainly never
hear back from any that have failed.
## TITAN-DENS
As fun as it might sound, hanging off of the fur or
feathers of a titan as it races across the landscape
isn't the easiest way to travel. Because of this, bugly
bands often build a titan-den on their picotitan - a
comfortable backpack-type settlement with a few
useful conveniences and a storage-place for the
animal's favourite treats. *We'll delve more into building a*
*titan-den for your picotitan on page 184.*

# THE ROAMING SHEET
*“Everything in its right place.”*
Below we have a quick breakdown of the Roaming Sheet, at least the bits of it that relate to picotitans.
1   NAME: Some picotitans are named by their bugs, while others name themselves, but they all have a name!
2   ANIMAL: A base animal that best represents your picotitan (options on page 180 to page 183).
3   FAVOUR: A numerical measure of your picotitan's trust, spent to activate picotitan actions.
4   GAINING FAVOUR: Ways bugs can spend their tasks on improving their picotitan's Favour.
5   AURA: This allows bugs to reduce Tough Cut on actions using certain skills when near the picotitan.
6   GIGASKILLS: A representation of your picotitan's innate skills, which can be used by spending Favour.
7   UNIQUE BENEFITS: A special benefit unique to your animal type.
8   TITAN-DEN: A section for adding structures to your picotitan's back using chunks.
9   BITS, BOBS, CHUNKS, & NOTES: A space to record anything your picotitan picks up or stores.
10 TRUTHS: Known truths about the world, revealed through grand discoveries.

### TRADING
If you're wealthy sorts you might want to seek out a
picotitan trainer (sometimes found in the wild, but more
often at picotitan stables in larger settlements). What's
the price of a trained picotitan? Invaluable, really, but
trainers will likely let one go for a huge hoard of bits
and bobs.
How many bits and bobs to a hoard? Hoo boy, that's a
tough one... It's hard to put a number on it, but let's go
with... Twenty? Fifty? Thirty-ish? *It's really just up to the*
*Weaver, but should depend on the rarity of the bits and*
*bobs being delivered - a trainer might accept 10 pre-*
*Mysteriocalypse relics, or 30 delicious bits, or 5 bobs*
*with a spring...*
### EARNING
Possessions are great, but hard work is better. Trainers
are just like anyone else - they often need things done
that they might not want to do themselves. A trainer
might gift a picotitan to the first band of bugs that can
clear some froglin punks out of a local pond, or retrieve
the mysterious ticking amulet on Watchmaker's Hill, or
steal into the mustachioed mayor's house in the dead of
night and shave his whiskers as a prank.
An impressive adventure nets an impressive creature!
*Earning a picotitan usually slots it easily into an*
*ongoing campaign or adventure, or can even be the*
*impetus behind one!*
### RAISING
If you want to take the hardcore route, there's nothing
stopping you from finding your own titan runt and
raising them, over time, to be a loyal friend.
... Is what we *would*  say, if it were that easy. It's definitely
not. But if that's still something that interest you, it's
there as an option!
*When bugs want to raise a picotitan, they first need to*
*find a suitable creature and then slowly earn its trust,*
*likely by filling a Progress Track set by the Weaver over*
*a longer period of time (you'll find the rules for Progress*
*Tracks on page 32). Don't expect a firm friend after a*
*single visit, though - training is playing the long game.*
### INHERITING
Almost as easy as just starting the game with a
picotitan of your own!
Picotitans tend to live longer than the bugs that ride
them, and can be excellent longstanding family friends.
A picotitan ridden by multiple generations of the same
bugs is usually the happiest sort, both loved and cared
for like a pet and venerated like a wise old uncle.
*The journey to inherit a picotitan from a family member*
*in a distant location is an excellent start to a campaign*
*of PICO, an adventure with a tangible and exciting goal.*
# OBTAINING A PICOTITAN
Some bugly bands might be lucky enough to start their adventure with a picotitan as part of their group. If this is the
case for you, you can skip straight to the next page and start learning about what a picotitan actually *does* .
But for other groups (especially those that are playing PICO for a longer, campaign-style experience rather than a
one-shot), obtaining a picotitan is the first step to creating your mobile home-away-from-home.
But how do bugs go about obtaining a friend that's twenty times their size? There are a couple of options...
### STEALING {farbe=#d43415}
The questions '*can*  you steal a picotitan?' and '*should*  you steal a picotitan?' have very different answers.
You could certainly *try*  to steal a picotitan - they're often left in stables outside of settlements, or to rest under trees
in the shade, or at the entrances of warrens and burrows too small for them to fit into. And picotitans are, by default,
trained not to eat bugs, and to avoid conflict at almost all costs.
But... You know...
We'll leave the ethics and morals of this situation up to you and your group, but as a general rule, we advise against
starting off a picotitan friendship with theft/kidnapping. The consequences tend to follow one around.

### 174 {farbe=#494344}
# WHAT CAN PICOTITANS DO?
*“The arrogance of some bugs is in thinking that picotitans are in their control, and not the other way around.”*
Once a band of bugs has a picotitan to call their own, it would be the height of silliness not to bring its best qualities
into the limelight every now and then. But what can a picotitan actually do during a game of PICO, and to what
extent are they under the control of the players? Let's jump right in and answer some questions with some handy
informational text boxes!
## ACT INDEPENDENTLY
No matter how strong your bond with a picotitan is, it's
a creature with free will and its own wants; picotitans
are controlled by the Weaver, not directly by any other
player at the table.
But that doesn't mean you can't influence a picotitan's
actions or behaviour, or have them help out. Narratively
you can treat them like an NPC in that regard, but
there's also a mechanical element to proceedings:
Favour. This is a resource earned by feeding, playing
with, and grooming your oversized companion, and can
be spent on various useful abilities (such as helping you
with an Action Roll in a sticky situation). *You'll find rules*
*for gaining and spending Favour on page 176.*
## HELP OUT!
There are many ways a picotitan's presence can be
beneficial for a band of bugs, both mechanically and
narratively. First off, every picotitan has an aura of
competency around them, allowing for easier rolls of
particular skills. Secondly, Favour can be spent for a
variety of benefits depending on the type of scene
you're currently in - and that's not even mentioning a
picotitan's own unique benefits, special things that
each species of picotitan can do to change the rules of
the game. They even have their own innate gigaskills!
But as useful as these things are, the ability to ride a
picotitan gives one additional important benefit while
travelling from place to place - speed. The strides you
cover with a travel action are doubled when riding
on a picotitan. Longer legs cover more ground!
And while mechanical upsides are good, there's also
the basic narrative benefit you get from having a
friendly goliath on side!
## CARRY A TITAN-DEN
One of a picotitan's most useful features is the ability
to lug around bug-sized structures with ease. These
titan-dens are somewhere between treehouse, driver's
cab, and palanquin, and are often built up and out by
bugs over the course of many journeys.
Building a titan-den isn't easy, though; it requires a
special type of resource, chunks (explained on page
160), and a significant time investment. They're worth it
though, especially if you're on the road a lot. *There are*
*rules for creating a titan-den on page 184.*
## BE PRESENT... ISH
If every moment of the game had your band of bugs
strolling around with a giant, well-meaning friend in
tow, things would get complicated fast. As a rule of
thumb, picotitans don't enter settlements - they
can wait outside or, in fancier places, be settled in a
stable for the duration of your visit.
Outside of settlements a picotitan's level of
involvement very much depends on the situation.
They won't typically join rumbles, as they're trained
explicitly to avoid causing harm in almost all situations,
but they might nose around in the background
of a scene or montage while you smaller folk do
characterish things, and can often help out merely by
being a big, solid, reassuring presence.
By contrast, journeys are where a picotitan *shines* .
Though still under the control of the Weaver, expect
a picotitan to pretty much do what you need it
to do on a journey without much fuss (unless your
table agrees that having a more wilful companion is
something you're all intrigued by). *You'll find rules for*
*using picotitans during travel on page 87.*

## ... AND WHAT CAN'T THEY DO?
Restrictions are important! They set boundaries and expectations, and make sure that everyone is on the same page.
Picotitans are pretty powerful compared to the average bug, and if they were allowed to run amok through the world
of PICO we'd be playing a very different game in a lot of situations, so there's one rule concerning picotitans that
overrides all others...
PICOTITANS CAN'T SOLVE ALL OF YOUR PROBLEMS ALL OF THE TIME
... But solving *some*  of your problems *some*  of the time is definitely within their wheelhouse.
### THE TABLE SETS THE BOUNDARIES
Ultimately, your own group will decide how to handle picotitans, and the power they offer, based on the rules and
suggestions in this chapter. This is the kind of thing that's worth talking about during a session zero (if you start
with a picotitan) or part-way through a campaign (if it looks like a band of bugs is going to obtain one). *You'll find*
*suggestions for a session zero on page 192.*

### 176 {farbe=#494344}
# FAVOUR
*“Vast, Polyphemus-like, and adorable.”*
This is a measure of how a picotitan feels about the band of bugs riding on its back, and a good indicator of its
general mood. The higher a picotitan's Favour, the more helpful it is.
### EARNING FAVOUR
Each picotitan has several options for earning its Favour
recorded in its statblock, ways of making it happy (like
feeding or grooming it). These are performed as tasks,
meaning that the best time to do them is during a
montage, but be warned - a picotitan can only have
its Favour increased once per scene.
Some of these tasks also have additional costs, like
finding the right kind of food or taking some damage,
but don't worry: these methods of earning favour
all represent treats, ways to go above or beyond. It's
assumed that a picotitan will be able to find enough
food and water to survive, or that your bugs are giving it
a comfortable enough existence 'off-camera'.
*Example: Borku is a canine picotitan, a yappy little sort*
*with a well-stocked kitchen balanced precariously on*
*his back. The bugs he carries feed him bits that don't*
*make it to the pot to gain favour most of the time, but*
*every now and then they take advantage of his 'luxury*
*feeding' Favour option and give him a fully prepared*
*meal as a special treat. They also groom him, which*
*takes two tasks rather than the usual one (so normally*
*just has two bugs from the band working together to*
*accomplish it), but doesn't require any bits or bobs to be*
*consumed in the process.*
### ADDITIONAL FAVOUR
The Weaver can break the usual 'one Favour per scene'
rule by awarding Favour for narrative developments
that please a picotitan. They might also remove a
point of Favour if the bugs do something that puts their
picotitan in an unusually dangerous or scary situation
though - it does go both ways!
*Example: The band of bugs that rides Borku are making*
*their way through an abandoned pre-M playground,*
*full of rusted climbing frames and faded plastics. But*
*what's that poking out of the dirt over there? Could it*
*be... a rubber bone? Borku is overjoyed, and the Weaver*
*lets them know that he's barely holding himself back.*
*The bugs dismount and let their canine friend play with*
*its new toy for a while, sacrificing some time on their*
*journey but being rewarded by several points of Favour*
*at once - Borku's a happy pup!*
### SPENDING FAVOUR
Once a picotitan has at least one point of favour, the
bugs can spend it on picotitan actions from the box
below. Each picotitan action costs a single point of
favour, and some picotitans have special actions that
are unique to them.
*Example: The bugs riding Borku spend a point of favour*
*as they travel to use the Titanwatch action, allowing*
*their faithful hound to sniff out a trove of treasures*
*nearby without needing to roll in the lookout phase.*
## PICOTITAN ACTIONS
### TITANIC AID {farbe=#83946a}
Used in scenes, this allows a picotitan to briefly rush in
and aid a bug as they perform an action. If this requires
a roll, make it as normal but substitute the bug's usual
skill for one of the picotitan's inherent gigaskills.
### TITANOTASK {farbe=#83946a}
Used in a montage, this allows the picotitan to perform
a single task independent of any bugs (as long as it
aligns with one of the picotitan's gigaskills). If this task
is particularly boisterous, it might impact bugs who are
trying to use elements of the titan-den for their own
tasks.
### TITANWATCH {farbe=#83946a}
Used on a journey, specifically while making Lookout
Rolls, this allows the titan to spot something that the
bugs might not notice on their own. Spending Favour
on this action doesn't replace any of the usual bugly
Lookout Rolls, and doesn't require a roll of its own either;
instead, simply choose one of the lookout results as a
group without any element of randomness.
### TITANOSPEED {farbe=#83946a}
Used on journeys for a simple but effective outcome;
while riding a picotitan doubles the strides a travel
action covers, spending a point of Favour triples these
lengths for a single action instead.

## THE LIMITS OF FAVOUR
Good feelings can only get you so far. The following rules govern a few of the ways picotitans should and shouldn't
act within a game of PICO, no matter how much Favour is accrued or spent.
### DOWNTIME, ANYTIME
Because a picotitan is under the control of the Weaver, they won't spend the entirety of their time helping bugs
outside of journeys. What if there's an exciting smell to follow, or a cool bit of branch to play with? How can they be
expected to be on the clock the whole time? That would just be unreasonable.
At the end of the day, picotitans *aren't bugs* . They don't see the world in the same way, and they don't share the same
priorities.
*Example: Borku's bugly friends are struggling to replace a piece of piping on the side of an old farmhouse, working*
*against the clock before an approaching storm floods a drain-hugging settlement. Borku spends most of this*
*scene playing with a bit of the old piping, off to the side of things. It's not that he won't help - in fact, one of the*
*bugs spends a point of Favour on Titanic Aid to have Borku saunter in and hold the pipe in place for a while with his*
*beastly strength. It's just that without Favour being spent on some direct commands, he just doesn't understand the*
*urgency of the situation, or what the bugs are trying to achieve, or why. Dogs aren't renowned for their mastery of*
*plumbing, after all.*
Instead, picotitans will usually do their own thing unless they're directly engaged in the story. Certain scenes might
lead to the Weaver having a picotitan take an active role, even helping out without any Favour being spent at all, but
this should be reserved for only when it's narratively appropriate, not just when it would be helpful.
### THE GOODEST TITANS
A huge part of raising and training a picotitan is instilling a general pacifism into it that most beasts lack. Picotitans
don't have Heart Tracks, as they shouldn't be in protracted encounters as an active participant - they're raised as
friends, not fighters. They will, if Favour is spent, leap into a melee when Titanic Aid is used for an Attack or Defend
Roll, but not if this would cause direct harm to bugs (even hostile bugs). Other things that don't have the bugly
keyword? They're fair game.
*If you want a picotitan to be able to take a few hits in a rumble, you can decrease their Favour by one each time they*
*get hit instead of dealing any damage - but discuss this alternate rule with your table first to make sure it fits the tone*
*of your game.*
### - PICOTITAN FAVOUR EXAMPLE -
Weaver:	The cluckatrice in the yard isn't 'yours' yet, though in truth, picotitan ownership is a bit of a misnomer.
Swift:
It's more like a partnership, right?
Weaver:	Exactly. Anyway, it isn't part of your little band yet, but you've made it clear that you're angling for it to join
up, so you can actually start building Favour with it now if you like - it'll help progress the Friendship Track
I've set for you, and if you do manage to fill it you'll start with a bit of Favour from the get go.
Swift:
Nice, that sounds like a good idea. And on that very same subject, I think we've got something that might
be right up this feathered friend's alley...
Layla:
Oh, that's right! I've been collecting some bits that it might want, I've got three husks of corn in my
backpack I've been saving.
Weaver:	Well then, let's slip into a montage. Basic feeding for a cluckatrice takes two bits, and gives one point of
Favour - I'll mark the Friendship Track once for that as well, so you're one step closer to having a massive
friend of your very own! Layla, cross those bits off of your sheet as the cluckatrice pecks at them.

# AURA
Perhaps the simplest benefit of having a picotitan
around is their Aura, a general boost to morale and
capability for any bugs lucky enough to have a massive
friend on side.
All picotitans have one of four Auras, each affecting a
different category of skills...
	» Friendly: Reduces Tough Cut by one on any action
using one of the friending skills.
	» Bouncy: Reduces Tough Cut by one on any action
using one of the moving skills.
	» Feisty: Reduces Tough Cut by one on any action
using one of the rumbling skills.
	» Inquisitive: Reduces Tough Cut by one on any action
using one of the finding skills.
... And it really is as simple as that! Being in the vicinity
of your picotitan (so probably close enough to see
them, and for them to see you) gives you an easy way
to reduce Tough Cut on your rolls, without needing to
spend Favour or fulfill any other special requirements.
*Example: Curtis is trying to negotiate with a trader out*
*on the road. The trader drives a hard bargain, and the*
*Weaver adds a Tough Cut of 2 to the roll, but Borko is*
*close by - that Tough Cut is knocked down to one by the*
*friendly pup's Aura.*
# GIGASKILLS
Though it often takes a lot of effort or teamwork for
a bug to access giga impact, picotitans are just *built*
*different*  - with size on their side, feats of herculean
strength and skill come easily.
A bug can spend Favour to access 'Titanic Aid',
allowing them to use a picotitan's gigaskill for a
single action by replacing the dice they'd usually add
for their own skill ranks. The resulting action has giga
impact by default, and narratively involves the picotitan
itself stepping in to physically help out.
All picotitans start with two gigaskills at rank 3 and
two gigaskills at rank 2.
### POTENTIAL LIMITS
For some tables, it may be worth limiting the number
of times a picotitan's gigaskills can be used in a scene
(on top of the usual limits that Favour imposes). We
recommend discussing this during session zero if it
seems like something your table might benefit from.
*Example: Kylliant, a brilliantly chitinous alchemist, is*
*looking for some subterranean bits as part of a shroom-*
*based concoction they're throwing together. The*
*ground is hard and stony, and they're not exactly an*
*expert on digging in the best of situations... But Borko*
*is! One point of spent Favour later and stones and soil*
*go flying, revealing a mycelial bounty of just the*
*right kind.*

# UNIQUE BENEFITS
Each picotitan comes with at least one inherent benefit, themed around the kind of animal that the picotitan
represents. Some can deal damage, others add positive tags to certain bits you find... There are as many unique
benefits as there are types of titan!
*Example: Oskari, a millipede adventurer, has been riding Borko for as long as he can remember. Though a naturally*
*surly type, Borko's unique benefit allows bugs that groom him to raise their own mood in the process; Oskari's player*
*uses this as often as possible to access his positive mood and keep Borko's Favour topped up for the rest of his band*
*to use.*
It doesn't cost Favour to use a unique benefit unless the text of the benefit explicitly calls for it (the foxfriend's
Binbreaker ability, for example, requires you to spend a point of Favour to access it, but the Catling's Predator Instinct
triggers every time you spend a montage without any associated spend). The only additional rule here is that unless
otherwise specified, unique benefits (like Auras) require you to be in the vicinity of your picotitan in order to use them.
### - PICOTITAN BENEFITS EXAMPLE -
Weaver:	Percival cocks his head sideways, looking at
the four of you shivering down there on the
snowy ground. There's a long, dangerous
moment as his avian mind runs through
potential scenarios.
Layla:
Can we subtly remind him that we gave him all
that seed last week?
Weaver:	I don't think chickens are known for being
subtle...
Layla:
Good point. I take out one of my seeds and
wave it around, shouting "Come on man,
there's more where that came from!"
Weaver:	Oddly enough, that actually seems to work.
With the promise of a more seed-based future,
the titanic chicken ruffles his plumage before
lowering his tail-feathers down to the ground.
He's inviting you on board.
Dunf:
Well, it took a lot of work to get here, but I think
that means we have a picotitan of our very
own. For as long as we keep him fed, anyway.
Weaver:	It does seem that way! Do you climb up?
Bicco:
Definitely!
Weaver:	Then it's time to reap your first reward - up
amongst Percival's feathers, the winter winds
don't seem to cut through you anywhere near
as much. While you're riding your new picotitan,
his Incubation benefit means you won't take
any negatives from cold weather.
Dunf:
No more frozen antennae? I'll take that win!
## TITAN-DEN BENEFITS
Bugs that take the time to construct a titan-den can
access many more unique benefits, each related to the
kinds of structure being built. Other than the additional
cost of time and materials to build these structures,
titan-den benefits work exactly the same way as any
other benefits a picotitan might give. *You'll find rules for*
*constructing your own titan-den on page 184.*
*Example: Kylliant's experiments have a habit of blowing*
*up in his face, so he and his group decided to add a*
*bunkroom to the titan-den upon Borko's back. The*
*bunkroom's starting benefit, A Place To Rest Your Head,*
*allows him to spend Favour during a montage to heal*
*entire aspects in one go - useful for the kind of bug that*
*always seems to find themselves in the middle of an*
*explosion!*
## CARRYING CHUNKS
In addition to all of the good stuff a picotitan can get
from their unique benefits and titan-den, all picotitans
also have the ability to hold a single chunk at a time
(in their mouths, or paws, or maybe balanced on their
tail). Because chunks are large enough that most bugs
can't move them, let alone carry them, this additional
innate benefit is almost essential for the building and
upgrading of titan-den structures.
*Example: When the bugs riding Borko happen upon an*
*entire box of pre-Mysteriocalypse matches, they could*
*split them up between the various members of the band*
*and continue as normal. But they have their sights set*
*on a kitchen, and the matches will provide a useful fuel*
*source - they have Borko pick up the entire box in his*
*mouth as a chunk, keeping it for a construction project*
*later on down the line.*

# CHOOSING
# A PICOTITAN
*“The colour of the fur is peerless. None could find*
*the words to describe it, although one said it was*
*reminiscent of the deepest ink...”*
Choosing the picotitan that's right for you is a big deal
- you need to think not only of the aesthetics, but the
mechanics as well! As shown on page 172, all picotitans
come with a few mechanical benefits that bugs can
use to their advantage, which are then added to over
time by constructing a platform of useful rooms and
equipment for your particular band of bugs. Each
picotitan entry below contains a name and description,
as well as information on how to gain the titan's Favour,
the gigaskills they can use, the Aura they give off, the
speed bonus they offer during travel, and the unique
benefit they give to all friendly bugs in their presence.
*Choosing a picotitan should be a group choice - if*
*you're going to be riding and caring for the thing for*
*the foreseeable future, might as well choose one that*
*makes everybody at the table happy!*
## CATLING (picotitan) {farbe=#9f2320}
A smaller than average cat with only a tinge of the
divine presence most larger felines possess, socialized
around bugs to the extent that their wicked claws stay
sheathed... For the most part. *Can carry one chunk.*
### GAINING FAVOUR {farbe=#9f2320}
	» Grooming: 2 tasks
	» Playful Hunt: 1 task, deals a mark of Slash damage
	» Basic Feeding: 3 meat-based bits
	» Luxury Feeding: 1 meat-based meal
### GIGASKILLS {farbe=#9f2320}
	» Rank 3: Gigaslink, Gigahunt
	» Rank 2: Gigaslash, Gigadance
### AURA {farbe=#9f2320}
Feisty: Reduce Tough Cut by 1 on rumbling actions
### UNIQUE BENEFITS {farbe=#9f2320}
Predator Instinct: It doesn't matter how cute a catling
appears, it's still an opportunistic hunter at heart. Your
band gains a free bit whenever you take a montage,

# CREATING A TITAN-DEN
*“If you build it, they will come. And they'll take their shoes off too, if they know what's good for them.”*
Once you have a picotitan that's friendly enough toward you to let you ride it, it's time to build your titan-den!
A titan-den is a collection of odds and ends adapted to fit the needs of travelling bugs, junk and treasure in equal
amounts turned to rooms, quarters, and useful equipment - features. One picotitan might carry a fully functional
kitchen alongside a luxurious set of sleeping quarters, another a cluttered workshop abutted by a bar and a smithy.
The possibilities aren't endless... But there are quite a few of them to go around.
### MATERIAL NEEDS & IMPROVISATION
All features of a titan-den require chunks, a much larger version of the bits and bobs that bugs are used to lugging
around. A kitchen, for example, requires a tin can and a matchbox, the first to act as a flame-resistant room and the
second to give a source of heat for cooking.
But... What if you can't find a tin can, or a matchbox?
*Improvise* .
The chunks listed in a feature's entry are kind of a 'best case' scenario, describing the typical presentation of a
particular feature. If you don't have a tin can but you do manage to find a broken milk bottle, that'll probably do. No
box of matches, but some firework fuses? Stick them in there instead! Using alternate chunks to those suggested will
make your titan-den just that little bit more unique, and we heavily advise doing it when you can.
### SETTING UP A FEATURE
Once you have the chunks you need (or something
that's close enough to them, anyway), it's time to get
building! When adding a new feature to your picotitan,
you'll need to...
	» Begin a montage
	» Consume the required chunks (or stand-ins)
	» Use a task to construct the feature
	» Spend a point from your picotitan's Favour reserve
... And that's it, you're done! Add the feature to your
roaming sheet, along with any special benefits it gives,
and you're in business!
### UPGRADING A FEATURE
Once you have a feature in place, you can continue to
upgrade it by spending additional chunks. Each feature
comes with a set of possible upgrades, and a list of
the chunks needed for each one. Upgrading a feature
works just like setting one up - start montage, consume
chunks, use a task, spend Favour - and the chunks are
just as mutable if you've got clever ideas to replace
them.
*Try to make sure that everyone's happy with a feature*
*before adding it - many of the features offer team*
*benefits, but a few of them are themed around skills or*
*activities that are more specialized.*
