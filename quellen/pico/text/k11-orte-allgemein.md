## REALMS
The world of PICO is big.
Really, really big.
Part of this, as one might expect, is due to the inherent
smallosity of bugs and the relative largification of just
about everything else they encounter. The scuttling
masses are tiny types, and it's extremely obvious that
the majority of the world wasn't constructed with them
in mind.
There's also a wider issue of scale at play - even the
longest lived, most energetic bugly traveller could
spend their whole life on the move and never see but a
fraction of the wonders that the post-Mysteriocalypse
has to offer. There's just too much ground to cover, and
that's not even factoring in the trials and dangers an
explorer would encounter along the way.
But is the inability to experience *everything*  a good
reason to sit at home, twiddle your thumbs, and accept
a life of seeing nothing at all?
No, say adventuring bugs. Not in the slightest.
### FROM REALM TO REALM
This chapter contains several pre-made realms, large
locations with their own landmarks, factions, NPCs,
and hazards. A band of bugs might spend their entire
lifetimes in a single realm, or travel from place to place
throughout their adventure, or maybe never see a single
one of these pre-made realms at all - it's all up to you,
the Weaver, to decide where the game you're running
is set. *Though it might be a good idea to ask the other*
*players at your table if they have any thoughts on the*
*matter too - seems like the polite thing to do.*
Each realm on offer here has a distinct tone, from the
cold darkness of the Shadow House to the verdant
expanse of the Hogwild, but these aren't set in stone - if
a few changes to the way a realm is presented would
make it a better fit for your group, we say go for it.
And there's always the option, if you have a bit of time
on your hands and a penchant for creativity, of making a
realm of your very own - more about that on page 271.

# THE REALMS ON OFFER
*"Exploration is really the essence of the scuttling spirit."*
There are five pre-made realms lovingly spread over the next fifrty or so pages, each of them with their own unique
tone. Here's a quick primer for what's on offer...
### THE HOGWILD  (page 272) {farbe=#8a948d}
A place of vegetative mystery, a vast forest shared by groundbreaker hogs, unstable alchemists, and mercenaries
ready to work for whoever has the shiniest bobs.
General Tone: Dark and mysterious, with infrequent but impressive explosions.
### THE SHADOW HOUSE  (page 286) {farbe=#8a948d}
A creaking mansion with altogether too many rooms, the interior so rarely kissed by the sun that ghosts consistently
vote it their most comfortable holiday destination.
General Tone: Dilapidated, quiet, and 100% haunted.
### THE SKYGLASS DOME  (page 300) {farbe=#8a948d}
A world within a world, deserts and jungles enclosed by ancient glass-and-iron defences. The Dome is a breeding
ground for conflict and beauty in equal measures.
General Tone: Bright and dangerous, an ever-shifting vibrancy.
### THE NEKOPOLIS  (page 312) {farbe=#8a948d}
A city of claw and fur, an urban sprawl ruled over by hissing pseudo-deities on an endless, joyful hunt. The neon-lit
streets of the Nekopolis are inherently hostile, but that hasn't stopped a thriving insectile society from forming.
General Tone: Punky and desperate, challenging at every turn.
### THE WESTGATE MALL  (page 324) {farbe=#8a948d}
All flourescent lights and mercantile memory, the bright colours and air-conditioned environment hide some
surprisingly potent dangers. There are spadefuls of corporate-approved fun to be had!
General Tone: Cheerfully colourful, but somehow still subtly worrying.
## USING PRE-MADE REALMS
Each realm presented in this chapter follows a specific formula, designed (hopefully) for it to be as easy as possible
for a Weaver to dip into the pages, grab something that appeals, and slap it into play at a moment's notice. Witin each
realm write-up, you'll find...
An Opening Splash: A large piece of art you can show
players to help get everyone in the same tonal headspace.
An Overview: General info with a section specifically on
the vibes of the place.
A Set of Particulars: With optional rules, pre-written
mysteries for bugs to pursue, page references for local
hazards that might turn up, and a pre-made list of bits and
bobs that are common to the area.
Some Local Factions: And some NPC suggestions for
each, great for adding goals and drama.
A Travel Guide: A set of three or four fully fleshed out
locations, each with notable features, landmarks, and
bugly settlements for a band to explore. Each location
also comes with a short set of random tables for the
Weaver to roll on, providing inspiration for landmarks,
NPCs, and events the bugs might run into as they travel.

## CREATING A NEW REALM
Oh, *we*  get it - none of the options presented here are perfect for your table? A real failure on our part, that one.
We're joking, of course. One of the deepest joys of tabletop RPGs is seeing what a book offers, mulling it over, and
then leaping off into left field to make your own stuff. Maybe you have an idea that you just can't contain, or you know
the tastes of your table after many years of gaming together, or perhaps you want a load of bugs tromping around
some other established fantasy world and trekking mud all over their nice clean carpets. Whatever the reason, if you
want to make a new realm for your players to explore, you have our complete and total blessings. And also our help!
Below are a few 'best practices' for creating a realm from scratch, questions you should answer for yourself as you
develop your own little slice of the post-Mysteriocalypse.
### WHAT'S THE CENTRAL THEME?
A strong core theme gives you some solid boundaries to
work within while creating factions and NPCs, and also
allows you to throw in some spice by adding elements
that run counter to that theme from time to time.
### WHAT'S THE LOCAL CONFLICT?
Wouldn't it be nice if everyone was nice? No, it would be
deathly boring. A conflict (between factions, attitudes,
or traditions) doesn't need to be deeply divisive, and
certainly does't need to be represented by violence, but
should give points of friction that you can exploit while
running the game.
### WHAT'S ABUNDANT?
Knowing what an area has an excess (or at least a good
supply) of should inform the cultures residing there,
as well as the local art and architecture. Living in a
sandpit? Probably going to build some stuff out of sand!
### WHAT'S IN SHORT SUPPLY?
The world of PICO has enough resources for all, but they
may not always be distributed in the right way. Don't
feel like you need to limit yourself to physical resources
either, especially if the bugs in your table's adventuring
band show strong leanings toward certain types of
behaviour that locals to your realm might lack.
