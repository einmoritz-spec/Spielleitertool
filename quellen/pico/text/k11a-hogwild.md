### 273 {farbe=#494344}
# OVERVIEW
There are dangers in the darkened depths of the
Hogwild, dangers that the average bug dare not speak
the name of.
Oh wait, yes they do. Sorry, I was thinking of somewhere
else entirely.
There are dangers in the darkened depths of the
Hogwild, and just about *every*  local could give an
itemized list of them. There are feral cats, terrifying
mercenaries, golden-horned vipers and, worst of all, the
enormous hungry hogs that give the woods their name.
But even without those thinking things, the Hogwild
would be a tough place to travel. Sudden rains bring
difficult-to-escape deluges of freezing water, sticky-
leafed plants snag at legs and antennae, and spores
drift lazily in the early morning breeze. It’s not a place
for easy living, attracting the kind of bugs that don’t get
too unsettled when life gives them a few knocks, or their
grandmother gets eaten by something.
But there’s treasure there too, down among the roots.
... Probably, anyway.
## VIBES
Overgrown: It should probably go without saying, but
there really isn't much going on in the way of forest
maintenance post-Mysteriocalypse. The hogs leave
their own paths (which you might follow, if you're
very hurried or very brave), but a lot of travel is going
to involve pushing through undergrowth that towers
above the head of the average bug.
Secret-Filled: The deeper into the wilds you get, or the
farther away from established settlements, the more
mysteries of nature and history you're likely to run into.
Musky: There are smells in the forest like you wouldn't
believe, smells that tell stories of porcine wars and
dragonfly dances and fresh flowing water.
Serene: Yes, there are scary things in the thickest part
of the wilds, and yes, you run the risk of being snuffled
up by a hog if you stray too far off of the path... But
there's such a sense of peace when things *aren't*  trying
to eat you.

## OPTIONAL RULES {farbe=#405435}
When bugs are going on tiny adventures within the realm of the Hogwild, consider bringing the following special
rules into play. They're not entirely necessary, especially for newer groups, but they should bring some spice into
proceedings for more experienced explorers of the world of PICO.
### DAPPLING (optional rule) {farbe=#405435}
The soft play of sunbeams through branches, the brief slips of darkness as leaves move in the breeze - the eaves of
the Hogwild are an ever-changing tapestry of light and shadow. *When in areas with a thick layer of moving leaves*
*above them, light and shadow get pretty muddled. When a bug takes an action in this kind of area, they can choose*
*whether they're in light or darkness (if it matters for their aspects).*
### LANGUAGE BARRIERS (optional rule) {farbe=#405435}
If a beast can speak, it's assumed to be able to speak the same language that the bugs are speaking (whatever that
is, probably something exceedingly cute or chittery). Nothing saying that has to be the case though! *Beast-type*
*hazards from the deeper areas of the Hogwild may not speak the same language as the player characters (or other*
*NPCs from nearer the edge of the wilds), making verbal communication impossible, but mime, gesture, and intention*
*all the more important. Maybe smells could help here too?*
### NUTFALL (optional rule) {farbe=#405435}
Wind pushes, raindrops sting, and a leaf on the breeze might as well be a storm-tossed trampoline... But nothing
compares to the sudden, explosive impact of a hognut fallen from one of the region's towering trees. *Strong winds or*
*scuffles that shake a tree or branch might also bring nutfall, a scatter of bug-crushing boulders falling at random.*
*Nutfall impacts should deal Gigabash Damage, and are a great opportunity for the Weaver to call for Luck Rolls to*
*see who gets hit (details on page 36). Remember, falling nuts can damage hazards too!*
### LOST OR HIDING? (optional rule) {farbe=#405435}
With long grass and fallen branches, mouldering leaves and scattered wildflowers, the Hogwild is a great place to
get lost in - and a great place to hunker down and hide! *Trying to find paths through the Hogwild without a suitable*
*vantage point always comes with a Complex Cut of 1, and actions taken to hide or conceal yourself ignore cut.*
## HIGH OR LOW WEIRDNESS?
The Hogwild is a pretty normal place by the standards of PICO, a large forested area with a few signs of old humanity
left there to be overgrown and wondered about. Even so, you've got a few different options when deciding on the
level of local weirdness you want to exhibit.
For High Weirdness: Those leftovers of the old world? They're still powered, even without a power source. A
tree's hollow might contain a working arcade machine, or a stream be fouled brown (and delicious) by drips from a
suspiciously coffee-maker-shaped island. Maybe the hogs even know how some of these old machines work...
For Medium Weirdness: Bring the beasts of the Hogwild into the story as much as possible, showing off that their
ways and cultures, while understandable with some practice, may differ greatly from those of the average bug.
For Low Weirdness: Everyone knows what a forest is like, even if you don't spend much time wandering around one
in your real life. To get that authentic forest feeling, throw in lots of local bugs that are entirely happy living the way
they live, and they don't need any meddling outsiders coming in with big city ideas, thank you very much!
# HOGGISH PARTICULARS
What makes the Hogwild so special? Let's find out together... *These pages cover optional rules, notes on tone, and*
*resources for the Weaver to use in a pinch.*

# LOCAL FACTIONS
Wide, deep, dark, and fecund, the wilds are the perfect breeding ground for innocent misunderstandings and petty
squabbles. Can you even lay claim to a tree, or a bit of old gorse? Some seem to think so. *The following pages detail*
*the main factions of the Hogwild, the Two-In-Ones and the Itsy Bitsy Mercenaries.*
## THE TWO-IN-ONES {farbe=#9f2320}
General Mood: Overly enthusiastic, slightly singed
Home Turf: Fringewood
What could one do with the limitless resources of the wider world on one side of them and a steady stream of oddities
from the depths of the wood on the other? Well, turns out the answer is 'just about anything, if they put their mind to
it'. The Two-In-Ones are self-styled (and self-taught, and often self-defeating) alchemists.
### THE UNEXPLODED COLLEGE (landmark) {farbe=#9f2320}
Pride of the Two-In-One elders, the college is safely
contained within a mud-sunk wellington boot. *Maybe...*
	» Only those that can go a full year without exploding
are invited to further their studies at the college. They
have a reputation to uphold, after all.
	» If the complaints of nearby neighbours are anything
to go by, the name might not be as applicable as one
might hope.
	» The boot has been reinforced with additional rubber
from a tyre-pile within the Hoggish Depths, but more
is always needed.
### IN WITH A BOOM (feature) {farbe=#9f2320}
Membership of the Two-In-Ones is earned through
alchemical experimentation. Note that this is different
from alchemical *prowess*  - you don't actually have to
be very good at what you do to join, you just have to
show the right level of shell-searing, antennae-burning,
potentially explosive eagerness. *Maybe* ...
	» The Two-In-Ones are on a constant search for
ingredients taken from the Philisopher's Tree, said to
grow at the very centre of the Hogwild, a mighty oak
with leaves of pure gold and bark of darkest lead.
	» No Two-In-One accepts payment of any kind for
the concoctions they create, but they also don't
guarantee results - at least, they don't guarantee
desired results. Most have waivers for you to sign,
scrawled out on the back of old shopping lists*.*
	» Cogtooth Carlisle is the most experienced
alchemist around, a caterpillar that's refusing to turn
into a butterfly until they''ve perfected a serum that
will let them fly without wings.
### SIZZLIN' SAM'S (landmark) {farbe=#9f2320}
The ancient script above Sam’s little outpost reads ‘F R
WOR S’, and it’s definitely a bit of truth in advertising; if
you’re not the kind of bug that enjoys being blown up in
a shower of coloured stars as you eat, a stop at Sizzlin’
Sam’s is indeed far worse than continuing on your way.
But if you’ve got a spark of pyrotechnic joy in your eyes
and a hunger in your belly, there’s no better place to
light the blue touch paper and run for cover. *Maybe...*
	» Most of the employees are waiting for the return of
the eponymous owner, last seen rising above the
treeline strapped to a rocket of her own design. They
don't get paid until she's back, either.
	» Quite a few of the Two-In-Ones get their start
working in the kitchens at Sizzlin' Sam's - if they can
survive that, they can survive anything!
	» Sam's is kind of a 'restaurant of Theseus' at this point,
having burnt down and been rebuilt more times than
anyone can remember.
### AN INTRIGUING MIX (npcs) {farbe=#9f2320}
It's not that there aren't members of the Two-In-Ones
that can brew up an impressive potion, because there
definitely are. They're simply outnumbered by the
number of adherents that just want to make weird
liquids go boom.
	» Mah-Mah, a ladybird chemist with a focus on healing
salves made from carnivorous plant extracts.
	» Libette, a water skimmer who prefers to conduct her
experiments in the middle of a nearby lake.
	» Skavamander, a self-styled 'revenantologist' who's
sure to make a breakthrough in the field of insect
zombification any day now.

mended.

# A WILD TRAVEL GUIDE
Visiting the Hogwild, are you? Well that's certainly a choice.
Though the edges of the wood, where trees falter and fields take over, are easy enough for a bug to traverse, heading
into the deeper areas takes a strong heart and an even stronger sense of direction. It's easy to get turned around
under the canopy, and predatory plants aren't exactly known for giving the best directions. *The following entries*
*all represent distinct areas of the Hogwild that a band of bugs might stumble upon as they travel, as well as the*
*features and landmarks they could encounter.*
### ROADS FROM FIELD TO FOREST (feature) {farbe=#eba400}
There are many ways to enter the Hogwild, but the
roads leading to, and through, Fringewood are the
safest bet. *Maybe...*
	» The roads are never empty - adventurous types sing
as they head toward the woods, rickety wagons haul
goods to and fro, and winged bugs crisscross the
skies above as they carry messages of greeting and
market manipulation from convoy to convoy.
	» A set of flags on poles have been erected at the side
of the main Fringewood road, with the colour of the
flags changing to indicate the growing danger before
they're swallowed by the depths beyond the fringe.
	» It's not one road, as you might have heard, but a
collection of smaller paths all twining together from
various parts of the wider world.
### TEKSO QUAILHEART (titan) {farbe=#eba400}
Foxes are known for cunning rather than bravery. It's an
old trope, and perhaps that's why it doesn't really apply
to Tekso - he's a fox that's neither cunning nor brave.
*Maybe...*
	» Tekso lost a chunk of his bushy tail somewhere in the
forest, but is too scared to retrieve it himself despite
his size. If any bugs are going that way, they might
consider bringing it back for him?
	» This fox loves stories, and will happily trade tales of
the deeper wood with travellers intending to enter.
Whether his words ring true is up for you to decide*.*
	» He has the capacity for bravery, he just hasn't grown
into it yet. Maybe he's stronger than he knows?
### STUMP LAKE (landmark) {farbe=#eba400}
Straightforward in its naming, Stump Lake is a... well, I'll
let you work that out for yourself. *Maybe...*
	» The stump at the centre of the lake holds an ancient
hatchet, buried deep into the wood. Whosoever frees
this weapon from its resting place is theoretically
crowned king of the lake, though they'll first need to
work out how to lift something fifty times their size.
	» Enjoyers of watery pursuits tend to steer clear of the
lake, unless they have an understanding with the
local froglins.
	» Cassius Carvexo, a silver-backed carp living within
the lake, only ever surfaces to watch the night of
colours - but last week was notably absent.
### THE LAST BAZAAR (landmark) {farbe=#eba400}
Clustered around the base of the very first tree one
comes to on their way into the wilds (if you take the rut-
scarred trader's path, anyway), the bugs running the
Last Bazaar are in it for the loot, not the glory. *Maybe...*
	» A betting pool is set up whenever any particularly
tough-looking adventurers head into the forest. Just
how long will *this*  lot last, do you think?
	» There's so much stuff piled on various tables that
a bit of jostling in the wrong place can start an
extremely expensive cascade of glories*.*
	» At least half of the stalls sell some kind of 100%
guarantee hog defence - an amulet, a spray, a
special dance. Not many of them have endorsements
from happy customers, though.
## FRINGEWOOD {farbe=#eba400}
Stepping into Fringewood means two things above all. One, that the vast, deep expanse of the inner Hogwild is only
a stone's throw away (if you're a very big person with a very large stone, which is highly unlikely) - that's why there's
such a scrum of dwellings and traders around the place, supplying travellers heading under the leafy sky and buying
wonders off of those who manage to make it out again.
And two, there's still time to turn back.

### A NIGHT OF COLOURS (feature) {farbe=#eba400}
Fringewood isn't all trading and preparation -
sometimes the locals like to let their hair down
(metaphorically, of course) and engage in some good
old-fashioned celebration. Once a week, to be precise!
The Night of Colours is a Fringewood festival hosted by
the Two-In-Ones (page 276), usually centred around
Sizzlin' Sam's and the nearby Stump Lake. It's a time
for revelry and explosions, fireworks and sparklers, nut
wine and roasted truffles. And there are hardly ever any
deaths! *Maybe* ...
	» The central attraction of the Night of Colours is an
alchemical contest, open to all who bring their own
ingredients. The goal? Craft a new colour; the more
vibrant, the better!
	» Bugs come from far and wide for the festival, partly
for the enjoyment but also for the temporary safety.
Local beasts keep clear of the celebratory noise, but
return with a vengeance the next morning*.*
	» The truffles consumed during the Night of Colours
are traditionally of the highest quality, and obviously
come from deep within the Hoggish Depths... But
nobody ever sees them being delivered.
### ONE MORE TRY! (landmark) {farbe=#eba400}
A fallen disposable camera is a good place to build a
little shelter, but a *great*  place to set up a photo studio.
The bugs in charge move mirrors around on a tiny crane
to help the skyward lens capture something other than
clouds, but only when paid in tasty tasty bits. *Maybe...*
	» The mirror used to angle One More Try's shots
was stolen from a titan living deep within the
Hogwild, and they want it back.
	» One More Try usually charges for pictures, but it'll
take one for free of every adventuring group that
makes it back from the Hoggish Depths with a
bounty of truffles to share.
	» The camera's film was spoiled long before
the bugs got ahold of it. That doesn't stop an
enterprising businessbug though, and One
More Try employs local artists of variable skill to
paint what they imagine the lens might
be seeing. The results are often
inaccurate, but always cute.

## THE HOGGISH DEPTHS {farbe=#405435}
A thicket of dense brush and towering trees, which local bugs and visiting explorers share with both the
groundbreaker hogs and a huge variety of predatory plantlife. It's not the easiest place to make a home (at least not
for those that want a good, long life), but the depths are chock-full of opportunities for a brave traveller. There are
secrets in the scents, if you have the olfactory nous to sniff them out, and the overgrown forest floor hides relics of a
bygone age half-swallowed by centuries of verdant growth.
### GREAT, GREEN, & DARK (feature) {farbe=#405435}
The road peters out. The traders' shouts are forgotten.
The light dims, and the air cools. There are other bugs
here, and predatory beasts, and hungry plants, but
they're all hidden behind a veil of tangled green that
stretches out in all directions. *Maybe...*
	» Smell is a better way of navigating through the
Hoggish Depths than sight, by far. It's how the pigs
and moles do it, and they seem to know what's going
on around here.
	» Local bugs have built small watchtowers peeking out
of the brush, rising through bracken and jutting out
of long, tangled grass. They let a bug get the lay of
the land, but they also make one very, *very*  visible.
	» The canopy of leaves above throws the depths into
near-permanent shadow, save for deific rays of
sunlight piercing the foliage in unexpected bursts.
### WATCH WHERE YOU STEP (feature) {farbe=#405435}
The rich loam of the depths is the perfect base for a
variety of bug-eating plants. *Maybe...*
	» Globs of sticky sugar water turn out to be the eyes of
a rustling predator rooted below.
	» Ambulatory flytraps scurry about the densest areas,
ravenous and toothy.
	» The Itsy Bitsy Mercenaries have a standing contract
with local explorers to deal with predatory plants
however they see fit.
### TUSKING ROUTES (landmark) {farbe=#405435}
A relief from the thick undergrowth, these natural paths
are carved by hogs as they snuffle. They're walkable, but
bugs won't be the only ones walking there. *Maybe...*
	» The paths are deeply churned and muddy, spotted
with bits of bark and mushroom from where the hogs
thrash their tusks around as they search.
	» Each path leads, if you follow it for long enough, to a
truffle-rich burrow*.*
	» The routes are jealously guarded by elder hogs,
shocks of white tufting around their shroom-stained
jowls and indignant rage in their piggish eyes.
### EVERY BUG FOR THEMSELVES (feature) {farbe=#405435}
Territory is hard to carve out here, and even harder to
defend from the manifold dangers of the deep wood.
A dose of healthy suspicion is a sensible survival trait
when you're on the menu. *Maybe...*
	» Small families of bugs guard their little lands with
vigour, throwing nuts and twiggish spears at any
interlopers that might come to steal their secrets.
	» It's oddly quiet for a place that must be thronging
with so much life. Everyone just keeps themselves to
themselves out here.
	» Local bugs gather for only two reasons this deep into
the wood - festivals and funerals. Both are raucous,
and both may also involve drinking pitcher-nectar
straight from the source.
### GROUNDBREAKER HOGS (titan)
Bugs are pretty literal when it comes to naming things,
and perhaps nowhere is this more true than with the
groundbreaker hogs. These porcine terrors stalk the
tangles on the Hogwild, grunting and squealing battle
cries the moment they see a delicious-looking edible.
And while those that can fly or swim might be able to
outclass them if they’re quick enough, natural diggers
are out of luck - a groundbreaker hog’s tusks are as
potent a tool as they are a weapon, gouging great
gouts of earth away in an instant to reveal tunnels and
warrens once thought safe (you can use the entry on
page 234 to run a hog as an active hazard). *Maybe...*
	» Sometimes an injured hogling can be nursed back
to health and made a friend to bugs, but the average
piggly beast is more interested in eating than
greeting.
	» Hogs are actually less interested in bugs than
rumours would have you believe, partly because the
average bug is small enough that they barely register
as a blur to a hog's tiny eyes, but mostly because the
search for truffles is all-consuming*.*
	» Some hogs bear palanquins upon their backs,
transporting contracted digging bugs that aid them
in their search for truffles below.

## KING'S RIVER {farbe=#008a99}
Though the Hogwild tends to hide its dangers behind curtains of long grass and thickets of thorns, King's River
stands proud and deadly without the need for obfuscation; a winding blade of water that slices the forest in two,
rapids strong enough that even the hogs don't dare swim them.
One might expect bridges to have been built, and in truth there are a couple of crossing points (a fallen tree that's
half-submerged, a beaver dam with rickety scaffolds). Even so, for the majority of bugs the most reliable way to cross
the churn is with the aid of the local dragonfly kingdom. Safety doesn't come cheap, though...
### DRAGONFLY RAFTS (feature) {farbe=#008a99}
