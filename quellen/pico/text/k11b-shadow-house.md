### 287
# OVERVIEW
What possible purpose might the Shadow House have
fulfilled, before the Mysteriocalypse? Chamber after
vast chamber, an interconnected layer of obstacles and
detritus hidden from the sun by thick wooden walls and
billowing curtains. One might spend a lifetime exploring
arcane cable-paths beneath the floorboards, or simply
sit and wile away the hours in the glow of the great and
shining mountain.
## VIBES
Abandoned: Despite the presence of both bugs and
beasts within the walls of the Shadow House, visitors
and residents alike agree that, of all of the places in the
known world, it feels the most like it was meant to be
something else. There isn't just darkness, but a keen
absence of light - not just silence, but the sense that
sound is on an extended and unplanned vacation. Was
this place simply a casualty of the Mysteriocalypse, or is
something stranger at play here?
Haunted: Whether or not you personally believe that
ghosts exist, the house certainly does. Creaks and
unexplained groans cut the omnipresent quiet from
time to time, and there are footsteps where no feet can
be seen to fall.
Gloomy: If one could but open the tattered curtains...
The interior of the Shadow House is, for the most part,
locked in an eternal night. There are places in the attic
where the sun peeps through, if you believe the tales of
travellers, but such spots are few and far between.
Complex: Room after room filled with shroud-covered
furniture, ransacked cupboards, mouldering desks.
It's not just the clutter that makes navigation difficult
though, but the layout of the house itself. Some
stairways seem to lead up further than they have any
right to, other corridors to connect to rooms that surely
couldn't take up that amount of physical space. It would
be tempting to say it was a trick of the light, if there was
any light around to trick you with.

### 288
When bugs are going on tiny adventures within the realm of the Shadow House, consider bringing the following
special rules into play. They're not entirely necessary, especially for newer groups, but they should bring some spice
into proceedings for more experienced explorers of the world of PICO.
### ALL IN DARKNESS (optional rule)
As you might expect, the interior of the Shadow House sits in a near-perpetual state of gloom, broken only by the
brief shift of a curtain or the flickering light of an ancient screen. *While shafts of sunlight and televisual glows can*
*count as light for various bugly aspects, most areas within the Shadow House are counted as being in a state of*
*permanent darkness. It is in the name, after all.*
### SCAVENGER'S WONDERLAND (optional rule)
A house built for human-sized inhabitants is titanic to a bug, and the junk and old furniture filling the ancient rooms
acts as obstacle, homestead, trap, and treasure trove all at once. Bobs of mysterious purpose are often found lying
around the shadow house, but bits are harder to come by unless one finds a larder, kitchen, or long-abandoned plate.
*Add a Tough Cut or Camo Cut of 1 to all actions taken to locate or gather bits, and consider giving out positive tags*
*along with any bobs that are found as well.*
### WEATHER REPORT (optional rule)
Draughts, burst pipes, and malfunctioning radiators all combine to create a sort of 'internal weather system' for the
Shadow House, but it's far more predictable than the weather out in the wilds. *Weather-based events within the*
*house are more locational than time-based - that fireplace is going to keep pumping out heat whether you're there*
*or not.*
### THEY SAY IT'S HAUNTED FOR A REASON (optional rule)
Bugs don't tend to dwell on issues of the soul, but anyone that spends enough time in the Shadow House will find
their minds drawn to the subject. It's hard to brush off fleeting pale shapes and phantasmal chittering as 'a trick of
the light' when there's so little light to begin with. *Page 298 gives a special set of rules for a specific 'Manifestation'*
*track, one that would suit bugs and Weavers wanting to explore beyond the veil or play up the potential horror*
*elements of the area - feel free to use it in any part of the Shadow House for a spookier feel!*
## HIGH OR LOW WEIRDNESS?
The Shadow House is going to be an odd sort of place to explore no matter the tone of your game, but the level of
weirdness is still easy enough to adjust to fit the way your group sees the world.
For High Weirdness: Play up the haunted elements of the location, and bring them into the physical building as well.
Some corridors are cavernously large, others barely big enough for a bug to fit through, yet others seeming to twist
against gravity as bugs traverse them. The house is a labyrinth, a lovecraftian structure that follows abstract rules.
For Medium Weirdness: Include as many 'ancient relics' as you can. The Shining Mountain (page 293) and Winter's
Tower (page 297) are both a good start, but think of the trouble you could cause with a malfunctioning microwave, a
remote controlled car gone haywire, or a laser pointer.
For Low Weirdness: The house is big, sure, but it can still be understood easily enough. Mouseholes lead to spaces
within the walls that are perfect for bugs to crawl around in, the flooded basement is a good source of water, and you
can even head up to the attic if you want a dose of healthy sunlight through the crumbling roof.

# LOCAL FACTIONS
Vast and sprawling, the bright points of life scattered among the Shadow House's rooms stand out like beacons in
the dark (sometimes literally). *The following pages detail the main factions of the Shadow House, the Indomitables*
*and the Sparkchompers.*
## THE INDOMITABLES {farbe=#979f32}
General Mood: Brave and fearful in equal amounts
Home Turf: The Hundred Chambers
Few actually see ghosts traversing the Hundred Chambers, but if legends are to be believed, the chance is always
there. The Indomitables (and yes, they named themselves, and yes, they were being very serious) are a group of
treasure-hunting bugs that search the rooms of Shadow House for mysterious goodies. This is made more difficult
than one might expect due to each of them wearing something to cover their eyes at all times, ranging from a
blindfold to a bag over the head. Remember, if you can't see a ghost, it can't see you either. That's *science* , that is.
### A HOUSE WITHIN A HOUSE (landmark) {farbe=#979f32}
What better place for the Indomitables to call home
than a smaller version of the structure they spend their
lives exploring? While some high-ranking Indomitables
have bases and outposts of their own, most start their
training in the House Within a House - a doll's house left
on a table within the Hundred Chambers that's almost
the perfect size for bugly inhabitants. *Maybe...*
	» The known ghosts of the wider house (if they exist)
are represented in candlewax miniature within this
tiny reclaimed replica. They move on their own, too...
	» Some say that the layout of the Shadow House
changes over time. If it does so, the same must surely
be true of the Indomitables' seat of power. But would
they even notice?
	» The Indomitables are actually pretty brave, in their
own way - they may have an irrational fear of spirits,
but they can take on every other danger of the house
even with their eyes closed (literally). A lot of this is
thanks to the rigorous training offered at the House
Within a House.
### SIGHT BEYOND SIGHT (npcs) {farbe=#979f32}
The newest Indomitables stay close to the doll's house
they call home, marking their paths with string to avoid
getting lost. The most experienced, though, are a
different breed entirely.
	» Olgenfrold, a scorpion who keeps their stinger in
front of their eyes at all times. Not known for their
directional savvy, but unexpectedly wise in the
history of the pre-Mysteriocalypse.
	» Pease Pottage, a wise and snailish sage that styles
themselves the leader of the Indomitables, hasn't
emerged from her shell in over a thousand years. *This*
*one's almost definitely not true, just to be clear* .
	» Li Cheng is the House's number one expert in ghosts
and hauntings, though - as you might expect - he's
never actually seen a ghost himself. He's sure felt
their presence though, and with bell and matches
sent them plunging right back into the deeper
shadows beyond (or so he says).
	» Cassandra, the only member of the Indomitables
who shuns their eye-covering practices.
### TREASURE HUNTING (feature) {farbe=#979f32}
There's a rich history behind the wanderings of various Indomitable groups - with enough fumbling, you're bound to
find something of great interest! *Maybe* ...
	» The House Within A House holds a small museum where the Indomitables display their greatest finds to visitors.
If they weren't wearing eye coverings, they'd probably have realized that half of the display cases are empty -
doubtless the work of an experienced cat burglar with little regard for tradition.
	» There's a whole city of Indomitables inside a broken drawer in one of the chambers nearest the old front door,
found by one of the first wanderers. Why leave the doll's house for a drawer? So they don't accidentally fall off the
edge of anything, most likely*.*
	» Every now and then, an Indomitable stumbles upon an exit to the Shadow House. The relics they bring back from
the world outside are some of the most highly prized of all.

## THE SPARKCHOMPERS {farbe=#979f32}
General Mood: Hungry!
Home Turf: The Cable Maze
Bugly delicacies are as varied as bugs themselves. Some chow down on roasted mushrooms, where others prefer
a well-aged acorn brew. Plastic might not be the tastiest thing in the world, but you can bet some bugs go mad for
the stuff. To put it simply, whenever there's something that could theoretically *be*  eaten, you'll find a bug somewhere
that's evolved *to*  eat it. That said, Sparkchompers are still regarded as a little odd by most other bugs. "It's so spicy"
they say, and "you don't know what you're missing" - but creating an entire society around the desire to chow down
on things that make you go *bzzzt*  is still quite an odd thing to do. It takes all sorts to make a world, I suppose!

# A HAUNTED TRAVEL GUIDE
A jaunt through the Shadow House? Not for the faint of heart, I tell you that.
There are, some say, two Shadow Houses. The physical one, that's solid but creaking, damp and ancient, dark as a
tomb and almost as quiet? That's the one that's easy enough to traverse, if you take advice from the locals and steer
clear of rifts in the floor. But the other house, the one that's somehow more shaded, more spectral? That's the one
you might wander into if you go chasing ghosts, or follow a noise you can't quite place... And it's far harder to escape.
*The entries below all represent distinct areas of the Shadow House that a band of bugs might stumble upon as they*
*travel, as well as the features and landmarks they could encounter.*
### AFTERNOON TEA (landmark) {farbe=#185d7e}
A tabletop town built up around a dusty teapot and
a terminally chipped set of china. The inhabitants of
Afternoon Tea are friendly to visitors, as long as they
bring news of the bright outside. *Maybe...*
	» The teapot's interior is still warm even after all these
years, and every week a festival is held where locals
and visitors alike can drink the elixir within.
	» The mayor, one Cassius Crumpet, has wrapped
themselves up in a ceremonial doily and refuses to
take it off*.*
	» The town is in shambles after the theft of a silver
teaspoon, one of their most prized possessions. No
single bug could be strong enough to lift it - is there
a gang of thieves at work on the streets of Afternoon
Tea, or is the problem of a more titanic nature?
### UNSTABLE FOUNDATIONS (feature) {farbe=#185d7e}
Though the House is grand and imposing from the
outside, the decrepit state of its interior rooms shows a
slow but sure slide toward enduring entropy. *Maybe...*
	» Rotten planks and gaping holes spoil the hardwood
floor, leading down to a sloshing, basement-level sea
(see page 297 for details). Either that or ancient shag
carpet, a fibrous jungle taller than a bug's antennae.
	» Dust-coated furniture, some partially covered in old
sheets like ghostly billowing monuments. Who knows
what terrors, or mysteries, lie in the deeper darkness
beneath them?
	» The planks creak like an old ship in a storm... Most of
the time, at least. In moments of unexpected silence,
all of the local bugs run for higher ground.
## THE HUNDRED CHAMBERS {farbe=#185d7e}
Bugs are, as I think we've previously mentioned, not exactly natural mathematicians - accurate counting just isn't in
their wheelhouse. They're also very small, tend to be easily impressed, and are extremely prone to exaggeration.
I want you to keep these facts in mind when I tell you that the Hundred Chambers (or so bugs call the interconnected
rooms, closet, stairways, and cupboards of the Shadow House) really are very, *very*  large, and far more numerous
than most tiny explorers imagine.
### THE SILKEN RAILS (feature) {farbe=#185d7e}
With the chamber floors clogged with detritus and often less than perfectly preserved against rot, many bugs choose
to travel by sideboard and tabletop as they venture from room to room. For those in a rush, or that want to travel in
style, a network of silken rail lines run by local spider engineers (suspended between the various items of furniture
scattered around) is a godsend. *Maybe* ...
	» The spiders don't need trains to carry passengers, preferring to load up spinneret palanquins instead.
	» The rail lines are technically owned by the Indomitables (see next column), and the arachnid engineers that put
them together did so while blindfolded - with interesting results*.*
	» You can ride the rails without buying a ticket, but few that try to do so ever reach their destination. They definitely
end up somewhere, though...

## THE CABLE MAZE {farbe=#c86f00}
Found within the partially hollow walls of the Shadow House, the maze is a vertical tangle of old electrical cables,
sparking machinery, and exposed nails. While the interior of the walls is larger than one might expect (for a bug, at
least), there are still places that are a mighty tight squeeze for even the smallest explorer.
The upside of these small spaces is a lack of titan-sized predators, but unfortunately the almost comical dangers of a
space full of arcing discharge and choking dust render that point partially moot.

## THE MIST-MANSE {farbe=#5a676e}
If you've ended up here, something has gone terribly wrong.
The Mist-Manse doesn't sit within the crumbling walls of the Shadow-House, not exactly - in fact, some bugs would

### MANIFESTATIONS (feature) {farbe=#c7c4be}
A chill in the air, a breath on your shoulder, a knock at the door but there's nobody there... That's a haunting, no doubt
about it.
When bugs are exploring the Mist-Manse (or perhaps even the least-frequented areas of the Shadow House proper),
the Weaver might set a 'Manifestation' track of 2 to 4 boxes. This track is marked whenever a player mentions the
mist or darkness, or how their bug deals with it, and when full the group are subject to a *ghostly happening*  (though
whether it's genuine spiritual activity is very much up to the tone of the group).
Take the list below as examples - don't be afraid to ask a player what their bug experiences when the last box fills, or
even to give different information to different players via notes or whispers to stoke up the fear of the great spiritual
unknown. *And remember, ghostly happenings are a great way to pull a bug's mood toward the negative, but relief*
*after realizing something isn't ghostly might just push it positive instead.*
	» A sudden chill - or just a breeze.
	» A hellish creak... Of settling floorboards. Or perhaps of a scavenjar's limbs!
	» A skittering shape caught on the fringe of your senses. Not actually a ghost, more likely a bug.
	» Dark laughter, that sounds on closer inspection like water gurgling through an old pipe.
	» A knock at the door. A good jumpscare moment, but really usually just means someone wants to come in.
	» A real, actual ghost! Oh wait, it's just dust catching the light.
