# OVERVIEW
The Mysteriocalypse has been termed, by some
bugly historians, as the 'end of an age of order', and
perhaps nowhere is this more evident than within the
transparent confines of the Skyglass Dome. There are
echoes of a less chaotic time - workbenches stacked
with rusting tools, sacks of sand that would have
dwarfed even a picotitan before they split and spilled,
the remnants of a fine iron cage standing stark against
the outer sky. But that old world is... Well, not gone, not
entirely, but certainly thoroughly *reclaimed* .
The Skyglass Dome now stands as an ecological
triumph, a contradiction and a living chaos. Desert
dunes stretch between lush, fertile flowerbeds, cacti
grow in the shade of fruiting vines. The glass panelling
that once kept the outside world at bay is bloom-
covered, stem-choked, and in some places missing
entirely.
Some of the Dome's denizens work to maintain the new
order, spiky sorts that revel in the spilled sands and
the heat of a glass-focused sun. Others want to speed
the spread of vegetative chaos, hatching elaborate
schemes to break panes and twist metal, and sowing
ever more seeds wherever they'll take. One of these
sides may even triumph, one day.
But for now, perhaps it's better not to choose a side.
## VIBES
Glorious: From curling iron and shining glass to
hyperchromatic flora and succulent fruits, the Dome is
the closest thing to a paradise most bugs would ever be
able to find. If only it was a little bit cooler...
Rowdy: The clash between the Dome's opposing
factions is a constant and inescapable fact of life, and
even the most peacefully intentioned visitors have
a tendency to become embroiled in skirmishes and
disagreements if they're not extremely careful.
Tropical: They say there are only two kinds of weather
within the realm of the Skyglass, the raging rains and
the searing sun. Neither is without benefits, but both
carry their own risks. *Staying out on the Compass*
*Dunes after dark lets a bug experience the freezing cold*
*too, but why would anyone want to do that?*
Colourful: Even more so than usual for the world of
PICO, given the vast array of dyes and inks that are
drawn from blooming petals and oversized fruit.

## OPTIONAL RULES {farbe=#c86f00}
When bugs are going on tiny adventures within the realm of the Skyglass Dome, consider bringing the following
special rules into play. They're not entirely necessary, especially for newer groups, but they should bring some spice
into proceedings for more experienced explorers of the world of PICO.
### SCORCH & FREEZE (optional rule) {farbe=#c86f00}
The weather within the Skyglass Dome trends toward the extreme, even on the calmest days - if bugs aren't
contending with amplified sunlight, they're likely either huddling together for warmth during the freezing nights that
hit the realm's central desert or wading through sudden deluges caused by the dome's still-functioning sprinkler
system. *While within the Dome, the default weather throughout the daylight hours is Heatwave, and during the*
*night it's Cold Snap (page 95). Plus, while the fringes of the Dome protect its dwellers from outside weather like*
*sporenadoes and gales, the activation of the sprinkler system brings a sudden and powerful Rainstorm without fail,*
*to the extent that some of the overgrown tropical areas become easier to swim than wade through.*
### HOT HOT HEAT (optional rule) {farbe=#c86f00}
As long as the sun is shining, there's no such thing as 'pleasantly warm' among the central sands and outer jungles
of the Dome. *When in areas touched by sunlight that's been focused through the Dome's glass panelling, all Sizzle*
*Damage bugs deal gains increased impact, and all Sizzle Damage bugs take is increased by 2 marks. It's also harder*
*to heal and rest without shade - bugs can't use a task to benefit from Natural Healing unless they've found a spot*
*out of the sun's glare.*
### MIRAGES (optional rule) {farbe=#c86f00}
What's that in the distance? To be honest, I really couldn't tell you! *Mirages and distortions are an everyday part of*
*life while the sun is shining, and it's almost impossible to accurately identify distant objects, structures, or hazards*
*if they're in direct sunlight. Mechanically, the Weaver might add a Camo Cut of 1 to actions requiring clarity of vision*
*over distance, but should also bring this shimmering uncertainty into the narrative as well - judging dimensions*
*accurately is better done at night, and some things glimpsed on the horizon might not even be real at all!*
## HIGH OR LOW WEIRDNESS?
The Skyglass Dome is an easy place to crank up the weirdness; you've got wild weather, scuffles between factions,
and the inability to trust one's own eyes to play with. That being said, weirdness is still optional - the suggestions
below should help you find some appropriate ways to highlight the level of strange your very own version of the
Skyglass Dome exhibits.
For High Weirdness: Mirages may not have substance, but that doesn't preclude them from having *intent* . With high
weirdness, treat the mirages of the Skyglass Dome's desert as something of a third faction, a band of semi-living
illusions with unknown goals and mysterious powers. Do they help or hinder visiting bugs? Where do they go when
the sun hides below the horizon? And, perhaps most importantly, are they *hungry* ?
For Medium Weirdness: Focusing on the oddities of weather is a great way to set a consistent tone of 'weird, but not
too weird'. Play up the sweaty closeness of the daylight air and the isolating cold of the nights, and use the Dome's
sprinkler system to change the terrain mid-scene for additional challenges.
For Low Weirdness: Whether a hothouse or greenhouse, the Dome was once a prime location for the growth of
plants that wouldn't fit the surrounding climate. For the least weird game possible, remove the sentient cactoids and
instead treat spiky plants as an immobile, rather than roving, hazard.

# LOCAL FACTIONS
Some characterize the constant back-and-forth of the Dome as a war; petal versus sand, spine against claw. In
actuality it's more like a dance, a tango of order and chaos that swells with the dawn and subsides each night. *The*
*following pages detail the main factions of the Skyglass Dome, the Needle-Nobles and the Bringers of Bloom.*
## THE NEEDLE-NOBLES {farbe=#979f32}
General Mood: Patient, prideful yet forgiving
Home Turf: The Compass Dunes
Bugs may consider themselves the inheritors of the post-M world, but among the vast deserts of the Compass
Dunes, it's cactoid life that flourishes best. The Needle-Nobles are the spiky green protectors of calm, seeing the
golden sands of the Dome's central expanse as a haven of serenity amid the lush madness of the outer grow beds.
### AN ANCIENT LINEAGE (feature) {farbe=#979f32}
*Those are just spiky plants* , I hear you cry, *and plants*
*aren't walking around types!*  Well, that may have been
true in the days before the Mysteriocalypse, but the
times? They are a-changin'. *Maybe...*
	» The Needle-Nobles consider themselves architects,
but very rarely build lasting structures. Instead they
throw together temporary dwellings wherever they
stop to sleep, specifically engineered to hold the
last vestiges of the sun's warmth as the cold sets
in. They're even willing to let wandering bugs take
shelter with them for a night, in exchange for some
good campfire conversation.
	» Many Needle-Nobles claim to remember the pre-M
years like they were yesterday, giving confusing
reports of gloves and tongs and huge, fleshy titans
that were too afraid to touch them.
	» If you meet one of the Needle-Nobles, you should
ask them about the temperature - it's their favourite
topic of conversation. Don't expect a speedy reply
though, as they're famed for experiencing life at a
slightly slower pace than the average bug. *Maybe*
*bring a book?*
	» Many cactoids view the incessant seed-planting
behaviours exhibited by the Bringers of Bloom with a
kind of exasperated amusement. The dunes are not
a place for weaker plants, they argue - it's almost a
cruelty to expect specimens from the grow-beds to
take root in the desert's harsh climate. Some even
take it upon themselves to dig such seeds back
up from the sand, returning them to the borders
of the Shatterpot Scree (where they're inevitably
found, rescued, and re-sown in an endless cycle of
frustrated expansion).
### THE LONG WALK (feature) {farbe=#979f32}
There are few traditions common to the Needle-Nobles,
as most of them lead solitary, nomadic lives that only
rarely intersect with each other. But one that many
view as worthwhile is the 'long walk', a journey around
the perimeter of the Compass Dunes that lets them see
every other clime the Dome has to offer. *Maybe...*
	» The value of the long walk is in learning to appreciate
what you have. Once one has experienced the
rampant tangle of the Verdant Fringe, the glass-and-
metal maze of Stormtotter, and the endless industry
of Shatterpot Scree, the calm of the desert is hard to
see as anything other than a stoic source of stability.
	» Some of the Nobles take the long walk to the
extremes, leaving the Dome entirely to wander under
the open sky. Most return, but not all.
### PRICKLY, BUT NOT IN NATURE (npcs) {farbe=#979f32}
Needle-Nobles have a certain reputation for fairness
and patience, and are typically respected by the other
dwellers of the Skyglass Dome (even in the face of
ideological differences).
	» Hobie and Heral, a pair of Needle-Nobles who met
during their long walk and never went their separate
ways. Utterly devoted to each other, always smiling
(or at least making expressions that pass for a smile
on a cactus).
	» Patah, a towering arbiter of sandy justice. Has a
courthouse in the Understar Oasis, which she visits
on a schedule none can quite fathom.
	» Karabet, a wandering musician with a thick-stringed
guitar. Though he seems as trustworthy as any other
Noble, he'll gleefully trick, mislead, and even rob bugs
that he comes across on his travels.

### SOWING THE SEEDS (feature) {farbe=#ed6b6c}
While the bugs of this faction can be found engaging
in dangerous pursuits all across the realm, they're all
ultimately in service of a singular goal - to spread life,
colour, and growth to every inch of the great glass dome
they call home. *Maybe...*
	» Some Bringers venture into the Fruiting Jungle,
braving both predatory plants and scaled foes to lay
claim to the strongest seeds they can find.
	» Others climb the gantries, pipes, and cables of
Stormtotter, collecting water and stealing scrap from
the dizzying heights.
	» And, at some point, every Bringer is expected to head
out into the deserts and sow their seeds. It's the
one thing that they seem to have in common with
the Needle-Nobles, their own botanically motivated
version of the long walk - a calling to plant and
nurture, even in the face of almost certain failure.
### NEVER GONNA GIVE YOU UP (npcs) {farbe=#ed6b6c}
Most bugs belonging to the Bringers of Bloom can be
found in their unofficial stronghold, an
overgrown workbench nestled among
the detritus of Shatterpot Scree...
but only when recovering between
ill-advised excursions.
	» Makori Spatz, a water-skimmer
determined to adapt to a life of
surfing the desert sands with seeds in
tow. An excellent flautist, too.
	» Bea, a large scorpion with a delicate touch,
something of a quality controller over which hard-
won seeds are released for planting.
	» Frisk, a spiny green beetle chosen as a go-between
when deals need to be made with the Nobles. Would
much rather be climbing the wet, high places of the

## THE BRINGERS OF BLOOM {farbe=#ed6b6c}
General Mood: Optimistic, looking to a greener future
Home Turf: Shatterpot Scree
Standing in stark contrast to the sedate existence preferred by the Dome's more cactoid residents, the Bringers of
Bloom are a bugly alliance that relish change, chaos, and progress. Every day is a joyful struggle; against the weather,
against convention, against scale and danger and sense. The Bringers believe (broadly speaking, as they're not the
kind of bugs that hold their tenets as sacred) that if it's not difficult to achieve, it's not worth achieving in the first
place - and what could be more difficult than riding the wild flow of nature itself?
### CREATIVE OUTLETS (feature) {farbe=#ed6b6c}
Sometimes it's nice to tackle tasks that may actually
come to fruition. Whenever one of the Bringers gets
frustrated at a lack of progress toward their ultimate
goal, they turn to art, music, and crafting until the
feeling subsides. *Maybe...*
	» They attack such tasks with uncommon vigour - no
sense in slowing down, no time to truly rest!
	» Their products aren't always beautiful, but no one
could ever accuse them of a lack of creativity.
	» Some of the faction's bugs become so skilled in their
chosen outlets that they leave the business of seed-
sowing to others, earning them respect and ire in
equal measure.

# A SWEAT-DRENCHED TRAVEL GUIDE
The Skyglass Dome hosts some of the most extreme weather conditions of the post-M world, and with them a
generous scatter of different biomes too; deserts at the Dome's centre, jungles sprouting up from long-untended
growbeds at the outer edge, and ever-misty walkways rigged up around the sprinkler system high above. And if
you've ever had a hankering for cactoid encounters, you've definitely come to the right place.
*The entries below all represent distinct areas of the Skyglass Dome that a band of bugs might stumble upon as they*
*travel, as well as the features and landmarks they could encounter.*
### ENDLESS SANDS (landmark) {farbe=#d98d62}
Okay, they're obviously *not*  endless - the Compass
Dunes are encircled by a tangle of jungle on all sides.
But when you're out there, with the sun beating down
on you and the sand scouring your little scuttlers, they
certainly *feel*  endless. *Maybe...*
	» There are stones among the sands, and sometimes
even discarded junk from the pre-M times. These are
few and far between, but are excellent at blocking
the sun for a spell.
	» A striding cactus is easy to spot among the sands,
but hard to catch up to.
	» Distance is difficult to judge at the best of times, but
when the sun is at its strongest, mirages and tricks
of the light make it almost impossible to accurately
get one's bearings.
### THE MERCILESS HEAT (feature) {farbe=#d98d62}
While the Dunes are cold by night (and occasionally
made briefly wet by the malfunctioning sprinklers in the
Dome's upper recesses), it's the long, hot days that stick
in the mind. *Maybe...*
	» Shadow is such a rare find that stumbling upon some
lifts moods dramatically. Just don't tarry for too long,
as the biting chill of night won't be quite the relief
from the elements that you might expect.
	» Predatory plants from the jungle areas at the edges
of the desert sometimes venture into the Compass
Dunes, but don't have the staying power of the local
cactesque sorts*.*
	» If there are any permanent sources of water outside
of the Understar Oasis, they haven't been found
yet. *Or perhaps they have, and someone's jealously*
*guarding them in secret?*
### THUNDER-NEVER-SLEEPS (titan) {farbe=#d98d62}
A hekatantonopede with a distinctive red and black
carapace, this grand scuttler only ever appears after
a deluge from the Stormtotter sprinkler systems
drenches the desert sands. *Maybe...*
	» The titan's course is impossible to anticipate, but
always takes it from one edge of the Dunes to
another. It's logical to assume that it must reside
somewhere in the Verdant Fringe the rest of the time,
but no bugs local to the area ever report stumbling
across it amongst the Fruiting Jungle.
	» The appearance of Thunder-Never-Sleeps marks
a kind of local festival, somewhere between sport
and celebration; daredevils, both spiky and shelled,
try to intercept and mount the titan as it steamrolls
through the sands. *Not an easy task.*
### ALL THAT GLITTERS (feature) {farbe=#d98d62}
If you've never seen a mirage, it can be hard to
understand just how *real*  they seem. The longer a bug
stays in the desert, the more they come to realize that
heat plays tricks on more than just the eyes. *Maybe...*
	» A sandcastle in the distance reveals itself to be just
another hump among the landscape as travellers
draw nearer, but... Wait, what about that one over
there? Or there? *Or there?*
	» Ophilian Halfcut is a flightless dragonfly explorer
who's been travelling the Dunes for as long as he can
remember. You could even walk with him for a spell,
share a meal and a drink, but the moment he's out
of your sight it's like he was never there at all*. He's*
*convinced he's real, but he's not so sure that you are.*
## THE COMPASS DUNES {farbe=#d98d62}
Make sure to pack a waterskin if you're heading out among the sands, and a new set of travelling clothes wouldn't go
amiss either; shells may be a great built-in defence for most bugs, but they do tend to heat up to an uncomfortable
degree under the glass-magnified glare from above.

### THE UNDERSTAR OASIS (landmark) {farbe=#d98d62}
Possibly the only permanent landmark among the
endless sands of the Compass Dunes, a haven of blue
and green among the rolling gold.
The oasis is fed by a pre-M artefact, a half-buried hose
that, if followed, would lead one all the way to the edge
of the Dome and beyond. The volume of water the hose
dispenses changes throughout the year, gushing during
the summer months and slowing to a trickle as the
world outside fades to winter, but it never stops entirely.
It's this single important feature that makes the
Understar Oasis the perfect meeting place for both
Needle-Nobles and Bringers of Bloom, an area of
common ground where the desert's peace still reigns
but where seeds can also sprout. Nobles stop here
every now and then to suck up as much water as they
can before resuming their wanderings, and Bringers are
permitted to sow seeds along the oasis' edge to make
life a little more delicious for local bugs. *Maybe* ...
	» Bugs love a good meal, and nobody thirsts quite like
a cactus. The local chefs use the water-blessed fruits
of the oasis to cater to both, providing flesh and rind
creations for their bugly customers and energizingly
pulpy juices for travel-weary cacti.
	» The oasis is named for a peculiarity of the skies
above, where a missing pane of glass allows a
breathtakingly clear view of the night sky. Some
local businesses rent out blankets and fluffy coats to
visitors, allowing them to stay up all night despite the
desert cold and stargaze to their heart's content*.*
	» The green-and-copper hose that feeds the oasis
is marked with ancient text, but neither bug nor
Needle-Noble can make heads or tails of what the
writing might mean (if it means anything at all).
	» Patah's Courthouse is only open when Patah
herself passes through, allowing her to sit in
judgement of any caught breaking the laws of the
Dunes. Exactly what these laws are is rarely talked
about, and her schedule for holding sessions is, if
anything, even more nebulous.

### THE SPRINKLER SYSTEM (landmark) {farbe=#cde7ea}
Spreading throughout the mists of the Dome's tip,
this creaking, gushing, rattling mechanism irregularly
dumps massive amounts of water on the realm below in
the form of artificial storms. *Maybe...*
	» While there's no schedule for these downpours, there
is a natural early warning system in the form of a
growing gurgle from among the pipes.
	» The electrical cables feeding the sprinkler system are
worn through by the elements, sparking dramatically
each time the 'rains' come.
	» Most of the deluge is directed toward the jungles at
the rim, but even the Compass Dunes gets a quick
wash from time to time.
	» The walkways between the sprinklers are enduringly
slippery, even on the hottest of days. More than one
bug has put a tiny foot wrong without thinking and
disappeared over the edge, swallowed by the mists.
	» As dangerous as they are, the tubes that feed the
sprinkler hold fresh, clean water.
## STORMTOTTER {farbe=#cde7ea}
The dizzying heights of the Dome are a tangle of old tubing, rusted ironwork, invasive mosses, bug-built gantries,
and heat-trapping glass. it's a different world up there, a miniature ecosystem of steam and mists that has more in
common with the average sauna that the oven-like deserts below.
It's also unexpectedly *planty* , thanks partly to the overgrowth of the Fruiting Jungle that climbs the Skyglass frame
and partly to the efforts of the Bringers of Bloom, frequent visitors to this sweltering realm who find it hard to keep
their seed-sowing impulses in check.
Watch your footing, and watch your back - you're not alone up here.

## SHATTERPOT SCREE {farbe=#c86f00}
A small but busy haven near the doorway to the Skyglass Dome, usually the first place stumbled upon by visitors
from the outside. The heat is less oppressive here thanks to the breeze from the outside, though it still hits like a
hammer when the sun is at its highest.
### THE GRAND WORKBENCH (landmark) {farbe=#c86f00}

## THE FRUITING JUNGLE {farbe=#405435}
A verdant fringe around the rim of the great glass dome, where scaly types frolic and hekatantonopedes thrash. The
mulch that makes up the jungle's flooring is artificially placed, remnants of pre-M growbeds that once hosted orderly
lines of twine-wrapped tomato plant, cucumber patches, and bright red pepper plants. Things have taken a turn
since the world changed though, and while those plants remain, it's in a vastly different form - as a thick, dark tangle
of thorn and heat that's inherently hostile to anything bug-sized.
Hostile, but *delicious*  - there's no better place to find succulent fruits than in the Dome's wild outer jungles.
Unfortunately for the culinary ambitions of those passing through, you're far more likely to end up *as*  dinner than with
it - the jungle's lizardish locals and voracious ambulatory plantlife will see to that.
