# OVERVIEW
An urban environment somehow more wild than the
deepest woods, the Nekopolis is a monster of concrete
and neon, a haven for fur and claw. Cats rule this
labyrinth of shadowed alleyways, holding court between
crumbling tenement blocks, hunting and lounging and
politicking without fear.
But how do bugs fit into this world of feline titans? By
taking the spaces too cramped to host an invasion by all
but the smallest of kittens, that's how. Where there are
sewer pipes, metal gutters, and basement rooms, the
scuttling masses endure.
## VIBES
Soaked With Light: How can a place so shadowed be
so bright? The neon signs of the Nekopolis provide a
constant light, even during the deepest, darkest nights.
The only places that experience true darkness are the
rooftops, once the sun's gone down, and the deepest
recesses of building and sewer.
Ruined: Crossing the Nekopolis is as much a climb as
it is a hike, with rubble-choked streets and collapsed
walls requiring a zig-zag pathway from even the most
hardcore trailblazers. Slipping beneath the city allows
for straighter, if damper, progress, but comes with
additional perils of its own; the cats may be a danger,
but at least they're a *known*  danger.
Hostile: The bugs of this realm still outnumber the cats
ten to one, but when you think about it, that's actually
a really low ratio of scuttler to prowler. More cats make
their homes in the Nekopolis than you could shake a
stick at, even if you found a really shakeable stick.
Glorious: Adventuring bands that roam the Nekopolis
talk, when they're safely ensconced within sewerside
bars or basement settlements, of a worrying pull at the
mind. Bugs weren't designed to spend so much time
around the sleek, playful, murderous godlings of the
streets above, and some of them get a bit *weird*  about it
all. Listen for the jingling of bells, they say, and move the
other way.

## OPTIONAL RULES {farbe=#c86f00}
When bugs are going on tiny adventures within the realm of the Nekopolis, consider bringing the following special
rules into play. They're not entirely necessary, especially for newer groups, but they should bring some spice into
proceedings for more experienced explorers of the world of PICO.
### NEON SUNS (optional rule) {farbe=#c86f00}
There are plenty of dark areas for a bug to slink around in throughout the Nekopolis, but very few of those are out
in the open. Between the daytime sun and the flicker of nightly neon, the streets manage to be at least partially
illuminated at all times. *When out above ground during the day, the streets of the Nekopolis are treated like any other*
*area - most of them will be bathed with light, with a few pools of shadow acting as darkness. But the ever-glowing*
*neon signs play tricks on the eyes at night - areas lit by neon once the sun goes down count as being in light AND in*
*darkness at the same time, a confusing state for some that pass through them.*
### CATELEPATHY (optional rule) {farbe=#c86f00}
Cats aren't gods, we made that clear all the way back on page 24. But the Nekopolis is very much their domain, and
feline inhabitants do seem, in some way, to draw power and knowledge from this home turf. *Cats of all shapes and*
*sizes seem to be able to share information without the need for words (or yowls) while prowling the streets, allowing*
*them a kind of feline telepathy. This uncanny ability doesn't seem to work throughout the sewers though, which bugs*
*might be able to use to their advantage.*
### SCRAPS AND SCRAPS (optional rule) {farbe=#c86f00}
What used to happen here, before the big M? No bug seems to know, and if the cats are aware, they're not keen on
telling. But, whatever it was, it left a veritable bounty of both bits and bobs for sticky-fingered bugs to collect. *The*
*first time each player rolls a set of doubles while Gathering or Scrounging during a scene, they can take both the*
*twist and the special outcome rather than having to choose one or the other. Additional rolls to Gather or Scrounge*
*are treated as normal, though.*
## HIGH OR LOW WEIRDNESS?
While the cats are the defining feature of the Nekopolis, they're plenty strange already. To tune the weirdness of the
realm to a level that suits your group, it's better to focus on the properties of the city itself - the dimensions, the
lighting, and the overall feel of the place.
For High Weirdness: The glow never dies! Neon signs fizzle but never go out, merry music plays from disused
shopfronts, and street-cleaning robots scatter grumpy cats before them as they try to keep some semblance of
order. The streets seem to turn too many times, spiralling in on themselves, and the sewers run deeper than any city
would have need of.
For Medium Weirdness: The towering buildings won't stay that way forever. Every storm sends cats and bugs alike
into hiding, and falling air conditioners and aerials crack streets that will never be fixed. The supply of fish heads
from various pre-M butcheries will, eventually, run dry.
For Low Weirdness: The giants that inhabited the crumbling city are long gone, and the cats rule in their place.
Though some are feral, a few remember and still long for their once-domesticated lives. Cat trees, jingly balls, and
food bowls have been dragged out into the alleys to make it a bit more homely for these unfortunates, but the city's
on a timer - more and more neon is failing, and the power grid threatens to short out any day now.

# LOCAL... FACTION?
With the streets overrun with titanic individuals of a decidedly feline persuasion, there are no warring factions within
the boundaries of the neon-lit city. What bugs deign to live there realized long ago that survival was the only metric
by which one should measure success. *The following pages detail the singular bugly faction of the Nekopolis, the*
*Ding-Dong-Ditchers.*
## THE DING-DONG-DITCHERS {farbe=#008a99}
General Mood: Perpetually anxious
Home Turf: Boltholes all across the city
Most commonly referred to as 'Ditchers', 'D3s', or '3Ds', the Ding-Dong-Ditchers are a loose coalition of survivalist
bugs from different communities that, rather than cower and hide from their feline overlords, have instead developed
proven strategies for staying alive.
Well, one strategy.
Sort of proven.
### BELL-BEARING (feature) {farbe=#008a99}
No Ditcher stepping out of one of their safely isolated communities would
dream of doing so without a bell on their person.
These bells are muffled by default, the clappers inside producing nary
a jangle until a ditcher feels that they've become the target of a
titan's hunt. It's at that point that they earn their namesake -
they unmuffle the bell and make a run for it, dashing for the
nearest area too small for a pursuing cat to squeeze fully
into while clanging and clonking with every step to draw
as much attention as possible. Then, at the last second,
they ditch the bell and dart for safety, relying on a cat's
inherently distractable nature to see them through. *Maybe...*
	» Some Ditchers, the fastest and bravest, are used as
roving distractions that keep particular areas of the
Nekopolis clear of kitties so larger groups of bugs
can scavenge and trade for a time. These purposeful
attention-grabbers often carry multiple bells at once,
ready to scatter them in all directions if needed to
distract an entire clawed horde.
	» The bells are worn as a kind of helmet, offering not
only the benefits of a sound-based lure to keep
others safe, but a second chance at life if one is
caught up in a set of titanic jaws - many a cat has
ended up with nothing but a mouthful of metal after
what they thought was a successful chase, the bug
they caught shedding its 'head' before scampering
away to safety.
	» A bell and a piece of muffling ribbon don't do much in
the hands of an amateur, but an experienced Ditcher
can herd cats on command. All they need is a high
place, a few bits of tasty bait, and a well-planned
escape route.

### THE PILGRIMAGE (feature) {farbe=#008a99}
Bells don't come cheap! Every Ding-Dong-Ditcher is
expected to make a journey to the Palace of Jingly
Things at some point, to choose a bell of their very
own (and to hopefully grab a few spares to replace the
recently discarded). But the journey isn't easy - the
palace is guarded by some of the most terrible, and
eagerly playful, feline titans that the Nekopolis has to
offer. *Maybe...*
	» The palace is what remains of an old pre-M pet
store, and many of the bells inside were specifically
designed to appeal to cats. The place is under guard
not by the feral cats that own the streets (who are far
too haughty to show interest in such a location), but
the descendants of the kittens once sold there.
	» It's said that a bug can't just choose any old bell.
When a D3 enters the palace, they hear the ringing of
a chime, a singular object that's somehow made to
be the perfect weight, balance, and timbre for them.
	» One large cat is planning a heist, a theft of every toy,
chew, and bell the palace has to offer... and any good
heist needs an unlikely crew.
### THE MOST DANGEROUS GAME (npcs) {farbe=#008a99}
Though most Ditchers see their scampering journeys
as a necessity, there are a few that have risen to
prominence for treating the extreme danger of
streetside travel like a sport. For them, the titans are
sparring partners, respected opponents distinct enough
to name. Some even swear that a particular cat will seek
them out for a chase, night after night. *Maybe...*
	» Swiftflight McLeggerton, a slinky-framed tabby
with a kink in his tail. McLeggerton spends most of
his free time lounging on an old parasol with a good
view of several streets, which also conveniently gives
him an excellent pseudo-springboard for the start of
his chases.
	» Dicey is sympathetic to the Ditchers, but she's still
a cat - there are appearances one has to maintain.
It's a matter of feline decorum to give chase if a small
thing runs, but Dicey makes sure to let just enough
go free so as to not rouse the ire of her compatriots.
	» Big Mog is the kind of cat that other cats give a
wide berth, a titan large enough to almost need a
new descriptive word for its size. Mog can barely fit
through an open window, and is more likely to smash
through a fence than climb it.
### CHIME TOWER (landmark) {farbe=#008a99}
A leaning monument slung with neon signs and old
timepieces, any bug that's caught and consumed while
ditching is laid to rest within its hallowed halls. *Maybe...*
	» Most 'burials' are more symbolic than anything,
as catling titans aren't known for leaving leftovers
after catching an edible little bug. The bells are what
gets buried, often dented and toothmarked, but
decorated with painted narratives describing the
bearer's greatest achievements.
	» Chime Tower must have been *for*  something once,
but what that might be has been lost to time. Cats
stay clear of it once the sun goes down, birds won't
land on it no matter the time of day, and even the
sewer beneath it is oddly free of enthusiastically
bitey fish. When the Chime Tower clocks sound, the
echoes take too long to fade.
	» If the palace ever runs out of bells, the Ditchers
have an emergency plan in place that involves
requisitioning bells from the Tower and bringing them
back into service. Nobody wants to be the one to go
and get them, though - much better to get a band of
outsiders to tangle with whatever ghosts might be
hanging around a place like that.

# A CLAW-MARKED TRAVEL GUIDE
Most bands of bugs avoid treading the illuminated streets of the Nekopolis, but you're obviously made of a different
kind of chitin!
From the dusty stillness of old apartments to the cat-patrolled streets, from the darkness of the sewers to the
moonlight-bathed rooftops, and beyond; one could spend an entire life exploring the Nekopolis and never see
everything it has to offer. *The entries below all represent distinct areas of the Nekopolis that a band of bugs might*
*stumble upon as they travel, as well as the features and landmarks they could encounter.*
### PREY OR PLAY? (feature) {farbe=#185d7e}
Cats are mercurial at the best of times, creatures of
indiscernible mood and twitching tails. One encounter
might end in a frantic chase, another in a contest
with arcane rules, and a third in a furred titan offering
friendship and aid - perhaps the same one that
attempted to eat your companion on a previous night.
*Maybe...*
	» The Nekopolis cats keep their actual predation of the
local bugs as low as possible, worried about scaring
them off for good if they're too aggressive. And what
would they have to amuse themselves then?
	» Some visitors, more used to cats from the wider
wilds, believe that carrying a complement of gifts to
give out will keep them safe. While it's not the worst
idea, it's hardly a foolproof plan.
	» The local titans have a mysterious etiquette about
them, some unspoken rules of engagement to keep
things interesting. A bug might even learn some of
these rules if they're a keen observer, and use them
to their advantage for safer travels.
### THE BOLTHOLES (landmark) {farbe=#185d7e}
A series of closed metal guttering and abandoned
mouse-made mazes running through the walls of
various Nekopolis structures, used as a (theoretically)
safe way to move around without catching the
attention of a titan. Travel through the boltholes can
be a tight squeeze sometimes, even for a bug, but the
narrow passageways ensure that nothing larger than a
questing paw can fit. *Maybe...*
	» The mice that made the vast majority of the boltholes
have long since fled the upper city, pushed out by
the ever-growing population of cats. At least, bugs
hope they fled - the alternative is rather grisly, and
not something you should dwell on for too long.
	» The bolthole network actually runs far deeper into
the city than even the local cats know, with carved
tunnels connecting to both pipes from the old sewer
system and sunken cable-channels for the failing
electrical grid.
	» Mice still guard and patrol the lower boltholes,
extorting heavy tolls from travelling bugs.
## THE TWISTING STREETS {farbe=#185d7e}
Every alleyway an ambush, every intersection a courtroom; the cats of the Nekopolis are in total control of the
surface-level streets.
But still, bugs endure. Ditchers scamper from cover to cover as merchant salvagers rifle through spilled trash cans,
and adventuring bands scuttle through the gutters with a hundred feline eyes tracking their every move. To be a bug
in the Nekopolis is to live life on the edge, perpetually.
### THE THREE FATES (titans) {farbe=#185d7e}
The undisputed rulers of the Nekopolis, each of the three fates counts a different part of the city as their kingdom. As
far as anyone can tell, the three only gather on nights where the neon burns low and the moon shines full. *Maybe...*
	» The Fate of High Rooftops is a climber by nature, spending as little time at ground level as possible. They lure
visitors higher with dangled yarn, fishing for excitement and company when they're not lounging under the stars.
	» The Fate of Dusty Places slips from room to disused room like a shadow, and is said to know the layout of every
apartment, the number of steps in every stairwell, and the contents of every cupboard*.*
	» The Fate of Every Crossroad rarely moves when observed, but always seems to be waiting at a nearby
intersection when they're needed. Legend has it that they've *never*  given a straightforward answer.

### HACKLEBACK JUNCTION (landmark) {farbe=#185d7e}
While the furry denizens of the Nekopolis like to pretend
that each of them is tsar of their own domain and could
get along just fine without anybody else, thank you very
much, there's actually a surprising amount of trade and
logistics that goes into keeping the city functioning.
Fish heads need harvesting from old markets and
distributing to less piscine areas, feline safety officers
string ribbons up around broken streets and broken
sewer grates, and a whole cadre of kittens keep water
bowls filled until they can be trusted with more exciting
work.
Hackleback Junction is where goods are exchanged
for promises and services, and is important enough to
operate under an amnesty-style treaty; the cats run
the market, but it's open to four-leggers and six-leggers
alike. *Maybe* ...
	» Titan entrepreneurs sell bits and bobs out of open
biscuit tins, shrewdly price-gouging all who browse
for too long before buying.
	» Visiting bugs walk the junction with a kind of reverent
awe, surrounded on all sides by titans that have
decided, just this once, to at least pretend to treat
them as equals*.*
	» Goldfeather is one of the Junction's most esteemed
figures, a brewmaster pigeon who has achieved the
lofty honour of being regarded as an honorary cat by
all in attendance.
	» An oft-frequented stall selling forbidden pre-M
literature, mostly picture books too large for a bug to
carry that depict bright, sunny city scenes bearing
little resemblance to the neon-blanched streets
outside of the junction.

## NEON RISE {farbe=#8a3679}
The higher you climb in the Nekopolis, the more colourful it gets. The walls of almost every building are draped in
glowing signage (and the electrical cables that feed it), and the space over each street slung with overburdened
washing lines. Greenery, scarce on the harshly lit ground, takes hold as the altitude increases - fire escapes and
rooftops, once barren, now host sprawling gardens growing wild under the stars.
### THE CLANGING GARDENS (feature) {farbe=#8a3679}
There's very little earth for plants to grow from up on
the rooftops, but as a famous scientist once said 'Life,
uh, finds a way'. The vines and bushes of the upper
Nekopolis take root in whatever they can, breaking
through metal sheeting and cracking concrete with a
kind of silent, vegetative glee. *Maybe...*
	» One species of flower native to the city has learned
to feed almost entirely on metal, rusting whatever it
grows on to the point of dangerous fragility.
	» Locklin's Grove is a bugly settlement with its own
feline protector, an unusual set-up that flies in the
face of the city's usual power dynamics. Could the
local bugs have found a way to mesmerize their titan
guardian, or is it simply an oversized picotitan that
they've raised from kitling to catling?
	» A colony of pigeons has taken up residence on top of
the city's station, purposefully sowing the seeds of
plants that cats find particularly distasteful. Visiting
bugs often approach these birds as potential allies...
a dangerous assumption to make.
### BRIGHTSIDE (landmark) {farbe=#8a3679}
A buggish stronghold built into the interior of a movie
theatre advertisement, each glowing bulb acting as a
miniature home. *Maybe...*
	» The advertisement that contains Brightside acts as
an interesting repository of pre-M history, proudly
displaying the names of the movies that were on
offer at the time of the world's great change. What
deep truths may have been revealed by 'The Great
Hand Returns', 'Entomoloko', or 'Catch Me If You
Flange'?
	» Living inside of a lit bulb is far from comfortable, but
the locals see it as better than the alternative. When
they can see at all, that is - spending half their time
inches away from a white-hot filament requires some
heavy-duty eye coverings if one wants to get any
decent rest*.*
	» Several times a week, bugs from Brightside head into
the abandoned theatre below them on scavenging
runs. The most desired material on these trips is
popcorn, kernels so large that they take two bugs to
haul back home, and so buttery that another three
are required to help the first two stabilize their prize.
### LINE-SURFING (feature) {farbe=#8a3679}
A local alternative to ditching that requires 100% fewer
bells, line-surfers rush down washing lines and use
pegs as distracting percussion to enrage and titillate
the titans below. *Maybe...*
	» Ditchers are brave, but they're not exactly *cool* .
Line-surfers tend to corner the market where
bodaciousness is concerned.
	» Some clever kitties actually encourage line-surfing in
their territory - the unexpected bursts of movement
and noise keep them sharp for any genuine titanic
threats that might show up, and if a line-surfer
chooses the wrong washing line (one that's a little
too close to the street, for example) then they've
essentially made a delicious, bug-based sushi
conveyor by mistake.
	» Wenningham Hax is the most famous line-surfer of
all time, a stick insect who can slip through the eyelet
of a peg without slowing down.

## THE SEWER SYSTEM {farbe=#405435}
There are few genuinely safe places for bugs within the Nekopolis, and the sewer system... Well, it doesn't *quite*
qualify as one of them. Don't get us wrong, the cloying dark of slime-coated tunnels are safer (if smellier) than the
neon-lit streets up above, and most cats wouldn't be caught dead dipping a paw into the waters down there (even
with multiple lives to spare). Unfortunately for the locals, 'most' is not the same as 'all'.
### 99% CAT FREE (feature) {farbe=#405435}
Drips, splashes, and weird, echoing clanks - the sewers carry a very different
ambience to the chorus of hisses and yowls up above. It
would be tempting to think that the ever-present
darkness down there was entirely free of
tooth and claw, but life within the Nekopolis
boundaries is rarely so kind. *Maybe...*
	» While the deeper tunnels are relatively
safe, anywhere accessible by grate or
manhole is a potential hunting ground
for fisher-kittens, younger cats immune
to the social stigma of clinging dankness.
	» A particular brand of D3s call the sewer home,
dressing as bright, scale-flashing fish to draw attention
away from the less combative local scuttlers.
	» Cats are the least of your worries - the deeper you head, the more
likely you are to find the sewer's own brand of undermice, who have
traded their usual clove soda for worryingly glowy goo they scrape off of
