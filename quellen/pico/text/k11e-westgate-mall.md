# OVERVIEW
They say this place was once a market for the gods
- sparkling, reverberant, a cathedral stretching for
countless meters. Where tall, sticky-fingered titans went
to shop, to play, to walk around and eat a pretzel.
Those original titans are gone now, along with most of
the pretzels. The escalators are frozen. The signs are
faded. The lights are on, but dimly.
But the Westgate Mall isn’t dead. If anything, it’s more
alive than ever. Lichen speckles the walls. Duckweed
spreads across the waters of flooded shops. Birds nest
and frogs sing and wild grass grows from the tile.
And bugs live here. By the thousands. Bugs
whose ancestors lived in the walls since their
construction. Bugs whose forebears arrived after the
Mysteriocalypse, drawn by promises of food, shelter,
and dead things to start a family inside.
And bugs like you.
Drawn by the promise of adventure. Of glory. Of
knowledge. Of money. Drawn by tales of drowned
relics in the green waters of the dragonfly empire, or
the crowded bazaars of the Food Court’s slums, or the
storied battlegrounds of Buggy’s Funzone. Whatever
your journey, this is the destination.
I mean, you can get anything at the mall, right?
## VIBES
Teeming: Civilization blooms like algae in the comfort
and prosperity of this place. An oasis that turns
settlements into cities, and armies into empires.
Empty: And yet, this place is hollow, built too large for
this age. Endless halls that will never crowd, massive
chambers that will never fill, and dizzying heights visited
only by echoes.
Bustling: Everyone has big plans and lots to do. Big
dreams and endless energy. Bugs here are industrious
and easily bored. Art and commerce, war and crime and
feasting - life goes hard here. Keep up.
Still: But apart from its inhabitants, this land has no
pulse. A dead place without wind, nor seasons. The
ground does not breathe, and Gaia does not dwell here.

## OPTIONAL RULES {farbe=#af8fc2}
When bugs are going on tiny adventures within the realm of the Westgate Mall, consider bringing the following special
rules into play. They're not entirely necessary, especially for newer groups, but they should bring some spice into
proceedings for more experienced explorers of the world of PICO.
### AIR CONDITIONING (optional rule) {farbe=#af8fc2}
Even with the walls beginning to crumble and the glass roof broken, the Mall's confines are gloriously resistant to
the weather of the outside world. Huge ventilation units, running on ancient generators in the basement, provide a
cool flow of filtered air at all times. *The area within the mall is treated as having Calm Skies at all times... until the air*
*conditioning malfunctions. In such periods, the weather may change to Gale or Cold Snap with very little warning,*
*and stay that way for days at a time (or even until the generators in the basement have been tinkered with, resetting*
*the central air control units and providing another long period of cool, placid calm).*
### EVERYTHING HAS A PRICE (optional rule) {farbe=#af8fc2}
If there's one thing the bugs within the Westgate Mall understand, it's commerce. How could they not? There are
examples all around them, tantalizing in their faded glitziness. Very little is given for free, especially not when there's
a profit to be made. *Whenever bugs are trading bits and bobs within the Mall, they can expect to get a slightly lower*
*value piece in return for whatever they give away in all but the most exceptional circumstances. This might come in*
*the form of giving two bits and bobs away in exchange for one, giving a tag-less bit or bob away in exchange for one*
*with a negative tag, or having a trade rely on some kind of additional service being performed (even if it's a simple*
*one that can be wrapped up with a single rolled action). Even when using this rule, feel free to ignore it if the bugs*
*form some genuine connections - friends try to stay fair, even if it means breaking the habits of a lifetime.*
### CLOSING TIME (optional rule) {farbe=#af8fc2}
An ancient machine ritual, the 'locking up', is still going strong within the fluorescent-flavoured halls of the Mall. Be
careful how you wander around at dusk, unless you don't mind making camp for the night in a temporary prison. *Old*
*security systems still close shutters and lock doors to various shopfronts when the mall's internal clock deems it*
*time, usually as the sun sets outside. Some might have spaces large enough for a bug to slip through - others might*
*be uncomfortably airtight.*
## HIGH OR LOW WEIRDNESS?
With such high human traffic in the pre-M times, and so many colourfully confusing oddities left behind on dusty
shelves, the area is *ripe*  for high weirdness. But does it *have*  to be that way? No, there are always other options -
especially at the Westgate Mall!
For High Weirdness: Bugs still practice rituals handed down from the times before the Mysteriocalypse, informed
by sleepy, long-lived titans and frazzled machines. The Mall is more awake, more vibrant, more *active*  than it's ever
been before, but you'd be wrong to think of it as more 'alive'.
For Medium Weirdness: The Mall is a place of malfunctioning technology, dying batteries, and old, yearning dreams.
Local pre-M machines are reliably unreliable, just like the power grid.
For Low Weirdness: If there was ever a spirit here, it's gone now. The locals keep up appearances, and there are still
gaudy decorations on display, but neither quite distract from the creeping dissolution the place is experiencing.

# LOCAL FACTIONS
The Mall could hold a billion bugs, but space is still (somehow) a concern. Where there's commerce, there's product,
after all, and where there's product, there's scarcity - even if it is artifical. *The following pages detail the main factions*
*of the  Mall, the Merchant's Guild and the Shining Empire.*
## THE MERCHANT'S GUILD {farbe=#ed6b6c}
General Mood: Hedonistic, transactional, pragmatic
Home Turf: The Food Court
This place was built for commerce. The ghost of trade haunts these halls. Sale stickers fade in display windows.
Murals line the Concourse, depicting beautiful gods in half-price jeans. The gods may be gone, but the spirit remains,
stewarded by an aggressive merchant guild that governs the city of Manna and controls nearly all mall trade. If you
sell something in the Mall, the guild will know and be happy to do business with you... or else.
### MONEY IS POWER (feature) {farbe=#ed6b6c}
The Merchant’s Guild has controlled the Mall’s economy
since the founding of Manna, and the larger that
economy grows, the more power they have. *Maybe...*
	» The Guild has a long-standing claim on Dog, as much
as anyone can do such a thing. No one and no thing
comes aboard without being tallied and taxed by
a certified quartermaster familiar with the rules of
canine-transported commerce.
	» Beyond trade routes, The Merchants have also taxed,
levied, purchased, and loaned their way into so much
crucial infrastructure that Maridane Kilsher could
collapse Fountain Plaza’s entire economy with the
stroke of her tiny pen.
	» Suspecting the Shining Empire to be meddling
in the mini-golf war, the Merchants have started
sending financial aid to The Purple Ants. What the
empire stands to gain is unclear, but their actions are
undoubtedly a threat to business.
	» Rumour has it that Long Lickerton, a caterpillar
trader from the draughty tunnels of the Mall's air
conditioning system, traded a substantial amount
of electronic components for the rights to place the
apostrophe in 'Merchant's Guild' exactly where he
wanted it. *No take-backs.*
### TYCOONS AND HUSTLERS (npcs) {farbe=#ed6b6c}
While the Guild offers (demands) membership from
anyone selling anything, only the richest members have
any power over lasting business decisions. *Maybe...*
	» Maridane Kilsher, a decorated orb-weaver spider
who holds the position of guildmaster. She used
her silver tongue, golden touch, and (supposedly)
platinum spinneret to not only survive Manna’s
downfall, but amass huge amounts of wealth doing
so. She cares not for actual wealth, of course, only
the power it buys... And she’s bought it all.
	» The Fassle Twins, Maridane’s right (and left) hand
bugs, a caterpillar and centipede respectively. Tomas
Fassle knows numbers and markets. Timothy Fassle
knows people and politics. A pair of geniuses who
have made only a single error - believing Maridane to
be their puppet.
	» Gideon Kilsher, Maridane’s drunken, overly entitled
son, interested only in decadence. Often found
tangled in his own webs.
	» Pal Horden, a small-time cloth merchant with a
pushcart, a house filled with grubs, a garage filled
with inventory, and a head filled with dreams of
having his own trade route.

### A STRONG ARMY (feature) {farbe=#ed6b6c}
In order to survive in the deadly waters of Fountain
Plaza, the Empire has spent generations cultivating a
nation of warriors and culture of battle. Of honour and
glory. Of steel and stinger. They arrived prey, but the
flowing waters of the fountain have carved them into a
legion of dragonfly heroes. *Maybe...*
	» The Emperor leads through pure strength where
possible. The bugs of Fountain Plaza rely on a
scuttling army to protect them from frogs and fish
and brigands. They may fear the Emperor’s soldiers,
but they fear their absence moreso.
	» The Empire’s army is larger than it ever has been,
well-armed and at the ready. With a word, the
Emperor could send a swarm of soldiers to casually
occupy half of Manna before dinner.
	» Suspecting The Merchants to be meddling in the
mini-golf war, the Empire has started sending
military aid to The Green Ants. What the Guild stands
to gain is unclear, but their actions are *undoubtedly*  a
threat to the shining seat of Varian's power.
## THE SHINING EMPIRE {farbe=#ed6b6c}
General Mood: Arrogant, militaristic, principled
Home Turf: Fountain Plaza
When famine struck the city of Manna, a band of dragonflies led an exodus of hungry citizens out of the merchant-
controlled Food Court and into Fountain Plaza, where they spawned a marshland empire.
### A BRITTLE THRONE (npcs) {farbe=#ed6b6c}
This empire was built on the shoulders of strong
leadership and steadfast council... Neither of which
remain today. *Maybe...*
	» Emperor Varian, recent inheritor to the throne.
Varian is young, squeamish, and nothing like his
venerated dragonfly father. He is paralyzed by even
small decisions and is sure of only one thing - he’s
always wanted to be a painter.
	» General Nebrixis, is an advisor as impatient as he
is ruthless. Despite increasing stress-molting, the
General is loyal to the throne and determined to
guide Varian to a victory befitting an emperor.
	» Magister Cassian, a challenger to the throne.
Cassian thinks the Emperor is weak, thinks the

deserves better. *He’s not going to say that out loud,*
*of course.*
	» Domina Marice, sister of Imperator Justice, a
commander exiled to the Concourse for disagreeing
with the previous emperor.

# A CONSUMER'S TRAVEL GUIDE
Ever had the urge for ice cream, inoffensive elevator music, and endless tiled floors? Then you're heading in the right
direction, little bugs!
The Westgate Mall has seen better days, from a certain perspective, but the spirit of mercantile endeavour is stronger
than any Mysteriocalyptic shake-up. One thing's for sure: there's no better place to hunt for a life-defining bargain.
*The entries below all represent distinct areas of the Mall that a band of bugs might stumble upon as they travel, as*
*well as the features and landmarks they could encounter.*
### THE CHICKADEES (npcs) {farbe=#5a3582}
Named for their battlecry, this clan of small black and
white birds has occupied this territory since before the
fall, when two-legged herd animals would leave them
seed offerings. *Maybe...*
	» The chickadees perch on shop signs and hop along
handrails during the day, looking for prey. At night
they nest higher up, near the skylights.
	» They may be songbirds, but they hunt like raptors.
Though smaller and weaker than the birds of the
greater wilds beyond the Mall's walls, chickadees
have been known to fend off large predators through
sheer numbers.
	» The Concourse is their land, and they'll defend their
home to the last, but the chickadees have little
interest in the other parts of the Mall. Food has
dwindled since the development of the Undertile
network, however, and some younger members of
the clan have their eyes set on new territory.
### SUGAR HAVEN CLIP JOINT (landmark) {farbe=#5a3582}
Whether you travel above the tile or below, on foot or
by canine express, you are likely to stop at the toppled
soda machine known as Sugar Haven, a clip joint
designed to wring your purse dry in exchange for brief
shelter, crummy supplies, bad food, worse company,
and as much high-proof fermented soda as you can
handle. Keep your wits about you. *Maybe...*
	» Beds, toilets, entertainment, water - nothing here is
free except the soda. You pay for your table and then
for your chair. You pay to get in. You usually pay even
more to leave.
	» Dasani is a glasswing butterfly who dances after
dinner, chats briefly with (paying) guests, then
hurries out through a secret corridor before the
stroke of midnight.
	» The owner, Sierra Mist, is as charming as she is
devious. She’s always accompanied by her favorite
bodyguard, a snail who goes by the name of Crush.
## THE CONCOURSE {farbe=#5a3582}
The Plaza, Food Court, and Funzone are separated from each other by Westgate Mall’s main shopping area, a
quartermile of white porcelain tile, gleaming aluminium benches, and rows of shuttered boutiques. A wide expanse
where any brave bug can roam free (though they're likely to be instantly eaten by birds), this 'Concourse' is a
desolate, haunted place.
### A DESERT OF PORCELAIN (feature) {farbe=#5a3582}
Crossing the Concourse is very difficult, and not something the average bugly citizen would ever consider. What few
expeditions set out that way are the stuff of fables, the travellers that make them up typically falling into one of two
camps: the exceptional, or the desperate. *Maybe...*
	» Traveling on foot out in the open is a bugly death sentence. Over a dozen chickadees nest here, living on a steady
diet of wanderers and fools. Travelers without some kind of cover, concealment, or existing agreement with the
birds will be targeted within a few minutes of venturing out across the tile.
	» So why not take the Undertiles? A hidden network of tunnels, vents, and crawl spaces is rumoured to run beneath
the Concourse, tightly controlled by scam artists and rough-shelled gangs.
	» The safest, most reliable way across the concourse is on Dog, who visits each area once per day as part of his
routine. Passage on Dog requires payment to both the Merchant's Guild *and*  the beast's native flea population.

### DOG (npc) {farbe=#5a3582}
Dog is big and fluffy. Dog wears a neat vest. Dog waits
happily for his long-absent owner to return, trotting
around the mall in a steady routine, completely oblivious
to his central role in the bug transport and shipping
economy. *Maybe...*
	» Dog starts each day by peeing against the side of the
Clip Joint, during which desperate travelers may find
a brief window to hop aboard.
	» Next he heads to Fountain Plaza to swim, drink,
and bark at frogs. Cargo and passengers exit into
the water and are retrieved by boat. New goods are
loaded by a specialized fleet of dragonflies, loyal to
the Shining Empire.
	» Soon Dog gets hungry and walks to the Food Court,
where his breakfast has been left in a special bowl
near the Bazaar’s freight yard to facilitate loading and
unloading while he's distracted.
	» In the afternoon, Dog makes his way to Buggy’s
Funzone to chew on golf balls and nap in Soft Play.
Teamsters wait near a well-worn indent on his
napping mat.
	» After his nap, he heads back to the Food Court for
dinner, where freight workers load the day’s final
cargo and passengers prepare for overnight travel.
	» Finally, Dog returns home to the Concourse, where
he looks wistfully at the moon and falls asleep on his
owner’s coat.
	» *There's a good chance that, given Dog's general lack*
*of observational prowess, he may not even have*
*noticed the coming of the Mysteriocalypse, and*
*the world-shaking changes it heralded. This has*
*worked out well for the bugs of the Westgate Mall,*
*and there's a general agreement between all parties*
*that talk of the Mysteriocalypse anywhere near Dog's*
*shaggy ears is strictly forbidden - just in case.*

## THE FOOD COURT {farbe=#eba400}
Legends say the gods left behind a feast. Mountains of food heaped on plastic trays, warming in metal basins, and
siloed away in deep garbage bins. From that bounty, the glorious city of Manna was born. Word of its comfort and
prosperity drew bugs from all over. The city boomed - population surging and purses growing fat. It was a time of
decadence, a time of hubris, a time that could not last.
When the food ran low, Manna collapsed under its own weight. Many left, more starved, and a lucky few became
very, very rich. Today, the city is a sprawling slum, entire neighborhoods shuttered and necrotic. A wealthy merchant
class rules from their luxurious countertop villas, the rest of the citizens scratch out lives for themselves below, every
morsel hard-won and twice-stolen.
### THE BAZAAR (landmark) {farbe=#eba400}
The pride of Manna. Humming with wingflutter, ringing
with crowdcall, and smelling of everything - spices
and nectar, mulch and wasp paper, brass polish and
raw meat. A pulsing marketplace five tabletops across,
connected with crowded bridges, and surrounded by
the villas and estates of the rich above, and the slums
and shanty towns of the poor below. *Maybe...*
	» Looking to buy? The Bazaar has anything you need,
plus everything you could ever want. Fried bread and
stickmeats and fresh waterlilly juice. Shellshines and
foot rubs and pan flute lessons. Batteries. Lighter
fluid. Overpriced imports from Book & Coffee. It’s all
there, from bits to bobs to bolts of spidersilk.
	» Looking to sell? You can apply with the Merchant’s
Guild to receive trade papers and a market stall. Or
throw down a blanket, lay out your wares, and take
your chances! Colorful items sell well here, as do
flavours from far-flung locales.
	» Looking to go somewhere? Twice per day, the
Dogport opens. You can take the afternoon ride to
Buggy’s Funzone for a handful of bits and bobs, or
the overnight to Fountain Plaza for a significantly
larger handful of *shinier*  bits and bobs. If you aren’t
keen on dogs or need to avoid The Merchant’s Guild,
you may find Undertile routes across the Concourse
by asking around The Bins.
### THE BINS (landmark) {farbe=#eba400}
It's just like all the locals say: if you want to see the
*real*  Manna, head along down to The Bins. Where the
alleys smell like garbage. Where the street food can’t
be beat. Where music breeds and laughter spills. Where
hope takes effort and dreams are for sleep. Where the
Guild takes your dinner and the gangs lick your plate.
Where life is what it is. Where things are what they are.
Where clotheslines cross and walkways sway. Where a
stranger is equally likely to be mugged or invited in for
tea. *Maybe...*
	» It is hard to go unnoticed here. Visitors are easily
recognized and closely observed. Be prepared for
pickpockets, territorial gangs, and swarms of curious
school-grubs asking you questions.
	» The Merchant Guild’s lenders routinely come through
town to shakedown residents, sometimes clashing
with gangs who were in the middle of doing a less
veiled version of the same.
	» On Feast Days, any stairway will eventually lead to
what must be the best party in town, where you're
sure to be welcomed in as long as you know how to
have a good time. Even in the off-season you can
usually find some action by following the music or
asking the right person.

### TEMPLE KITCHEN (landmark) {farbe=#eba400}
Temple Kitchen is operated by an order of cockroaches
that called the Food Court home well before the
Mysteriocalypse. They guard their secrets tightly, work
new hires to the bone (metaphorically speaking), and
spend every hour of every day preparing for the next
feast. *Maybe...*
	» The Temple is always hiring. Scullions and spicers,
greaslings and mop-bugs - the work is unpleasant,
but at least it pays bad.
	» The most dangerous job is, of course, frost mining,
where brave fools are sent into walk-in freezers with
picks and saws to harvest chunks of frozen food.
	» Although Temple staff guard their ingredients
closely, there are usually enough scraps, spills, and
unattended pans for stealthy chefs to do a bit of
unsanctioned cooking.
### FEAST DAY (feature) {farbe=#eba400}
Manna was founded in a time of plenty. And while the
cornucopia is little more than legend, each month the
residents still do their best to pretend, celebrating their
history with an elaborate ritual feast. *Maybe...*
	» In the days leading up to Feast Day, people busy
themselves with party preparation. Decorating,
cooking, planning games and rehearsing plays.
A friendly spirit of competition arises as Manna’s
citizens all try to throw the best party.
	» On Feast Day, everyone skips breakfast to attend
a very solemn, sacred blessing ceremony which
naturally could go wrong in any number of ways
considering the amount of candles and robes.
	» After the ceremony, the feast begins. Celebrants
go from house to house, party to party, meal to
meal, eating and drinking their way through their
neighborhood and partying until daybreak.
