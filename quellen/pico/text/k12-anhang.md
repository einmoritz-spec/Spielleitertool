### 346 {farbe=#494344}
## GENERAL STUFF
Just useful to know!
### THE WEAVER Pg 191
PICO's GM figure, shortened from 'Weaver of Tiny
Stories'. They run the game, and keep things moving.
### GAME STRUCTURE Pg 30
A game of PICO is split into scenes, montages, and
journeys. Each of these different modes of play have
a core of similar rules to follow, but also unique rules
of their own. We'll devote some space to each of these
types of play within this rules summary.
### SPOTLIGHT Pg 30
Moves from character to character naturally through
play, but can be directed by the Weaver. Can be visibly
tracked in tense moments, using A to record actions
and R reactions. Can be hijacked by certain moves/
abilities, but should usually be given back to the
individual it was hijacked from when that move is over.
SIZE Pg 12
Characters can be Nano, Small, or Large. The effects are
mostly narrative, but some aspect and hazard rules take
size into account. Bugs are Small unless otherwise
indicated.
### TRACKS Pg 32
A series of linked boxes that can be used in a variety of
situations to measure progress, time, or damage.
Tracks can be marked with a / or burned with a X (a
burn usually representing a more lasting mark that's
hard to clear). When a track fills, something changes.
Tracks can also have breaks, points on the track that
signal a change before it fully completes.
Most character actions fill one box on a track. Actions
with giga impact fill all boxes up until the end of a track,
or until the next track break.
Tracks can also be open (out for everyone to see) or
hidden (visible only to the Weaver).
# RULES SUMMARY
While not exactly jam-packed with rules, PICO does deviate from the classic Wild Words Engine in a few important
ways. The next few pages give a quick-and-dirty breakdown of the rules of PICO, as well as page references for when
you need to see them in context.
### ACTIONS Pg 34
Taken when a player wants their character to do
something. If an action is difficult, dangerous, or
dramatic, they'll usually require a roll of the dice. You
can get dice for this roll from your character's Skills
(1-3d6), Aspects (1d6), and Advantage (1-2d6, from
environmental, teamwork, or bits and bobs usage).
When rolling for an action, look for the highest result
and if there are any doubles. *Scenes, Montages, and*
*Journeys may require different types of rolls.*
The standard action roll gives...
	» A Triumph on a 6, which shows things have gone
really well. If you're affecting a track, this lets you
mark or clear a box.
	» A Conflict on a 5 or 4, which shows things have
gone alright, but not perfectly (or that there's an
equal mix of bad and good). If you're affecting a track
this *might*  let you mark or clear a box at the cost of
some associated drawback.
	» A Disaster on a 3, 2, or 1, which shows things have
gone awfully wrong. If you're affecting a track, you
don't get to mark or clear a box. There will definitely
be some kind of drawback.
	» A Twist on a double, where any other player at the
table can suggest a small extra detail (either bad or
good) that comes about as a result of the action.
Twists can give information, a change of fictional
position or situation, a bit or a bob... Anything really,
as long as it's not too game-altering.
When a character takes an action, they naturally take
the spotlight. If another character has the spotlight,
don't break in and do an action of your own until
they're done!
### REACTIONS Pg 36
Like actions, but instead of being decided by the player
they're prompted by the Weaver. They work like actions
in terms of building a dice pool, rolling, and reading
the dice, and usually have the same results spread
as shown above, but some reactions have their own
special results (more on that later).

CUT Pg 38
Removes a number of results after rolling dice, equal to
the cut amount (starting with the highest, to represent
an increase in difficulty). There are five different types...
	» Tough Cut: For a general increase in difficulty
	» Armour Cut: For rolls made against well-defended
targets
	» Speed Cut: For rolls made against swift targets, or to
attempt to complete an action with great speed
	» Camo Cut: For rolls made against targets that are
difficult to see, or in murky conditions
	» Complex Cut: For rolls made against unpredictable
targets, or in extremely complex situations
Different kinds of cut stack together - a Tough Cut of 1
and a Speed Cut of 2 on a roll gives a total cut of 3.
### IMPACT Pg 40
A measure of the impact an action or event has on the
world. Impact is split into four different levels...
	» No Impact: For an action that has no effect on the
world. Doesn't mark boxes when affecting a track.
	» Low Impact: For an action that has minimal effect
on the world. Marks one box fewer than usual when
affecting a track.
	» Normal Impact: For an action that has the expected
effect. Marks the usual one box when affecting a
track.
	» Giga Impact: For an action that has massive, often
impressive effect on the world. Marks all boxes on a
track, or until a track break is reached.
The basic impact of most actions is normal.
### TRUTHS Pg 44
Established by solving mysteries, recorded on the
Roaming Sheet. Truths are used to gain environmental
advantage on a roll if relevant, and can also be called on
for a free twist by each player once per session.
### COMBOS AND FINISHERS Pg 46
A combo track is a three box track set by the Weaver.
When taking an action, a bug can burn a box on one of
their aspects - if their action is a Conflict or Triumph,
this also marks a box on the combo track. When
the combo track is filled, a bug can clear it to either
increase their next action's impact by one step, or to
ignore all cut on their next action.

### ATTACKING Pg 58
Used to attack a hazard. Any skill can be used to attack,
and the damage an attack deals is equal to the action's
impact. The dice pool is made as normal, but the results
are read as follows...
	» A Triumph on a 6 lets you deal damage and add an
effect.
	» A Conflict on a 5 or 4 lets you deal damage or add
an effect (your choice), OR deal both damage and
add an effect but also take some damage or an effect
yourself.
	» A Disaster on a 3, 2, or 1 means you've missed, and
take both damage and an effect from your target
instead.
	» A Twist on a double can be treated as usual, giving a
small unexpected benefit, or can be used as a critical
to deal an extra mark of damage.*If you're dealing*
*giga damage that gets stopped by a break, a critical*
*marks the next box after the break.*
### DEFENDING Pg 59
Used when a hazard attacks you. Any skill can be used
to defend, and it requires a reaction rather than an
action. The dice pool is made as normal, but the results
are read as follows...
	» A Triumph on a 6 lets you avoid all incoming damage
and effects.
	» A Conflict on a 5 or 4 lets the Weaver choose to hit
you with either damage or an effect.
	» A Disaster on a 3, 2, or 1 means you take both the
damage and effect.
	» A Twist on a double can be treated as usual, giving
a small unexpected benefit, or can be used as a
counter to deal normal impact damage to the hazard
attacking you.
DEATH Pg 66
... Is always a player choice. If you fully burn every box
on your aspects, you can curl up and hide until the end
of the scene. At the start of the next scene, completely
clear a single heart of your choice for free and you're
back in the action.
## SCENE STUFF
The most common mode of play, with few special
rules to take into account outside of those for having a
rumble with dangerous hazards.
### POINTS OF INTEREST Pg 50
These are useful or important elements of a location,
mentioned by the Weaver as they describe a scene or
elicited by player questions. POIs are useful for gaining
environmental advantage, for gathering bits and bobs,
and for allowing a bug to do something that wouldn't be
possible with only their own body and equipment.
### GATHERING Pg 52
A special type of roll used to gather bits and bobs from
the environment. The dice pool is made as normal, but
the results are read as follows...
	» A Triumph on a 6 gives you exactly what you want.
	» A Conflict on a 5 or 4 gives you what you want, but
it's not as nice as you expected (may come with a
negative tag) or there's a downside to acquiring it.
	» A Disaster on a 3, 2, or 1 means whatever you were
trying to gather is completely unfit for purpose or
entirely unobtainable, and you may take damage
trying to get it.
	» A Twist on a double can be treated as usual, giving
a small unexpected benefit, or can be used to add a
tag of your choice to whatever you acquire.
### RUMBLE TRACKS Pg 56
When a rumble begins, new tracks might be used. These
are...
	» Spotlight Tracks: Kept by the Weaver to track
who has acted in a scene, and marked with an A to
represent an action and an R to represent a reaction.
*Hazards may be added to a spotlight track if the*
*Weaver wishes, but this is up to personal choice.*
	» Heart Tracks: Kept by the Weaver to track the
damage a hazard takes, or the progress toward
the end of a rumble (as a rumble can be won with
damage, without damage, or with a mixture of both
approaches).
	» Combo Tracks: Kept by the Weaver but visible to
every player, these can be filled and then cleared for
massive damage or the ability to ignore cut.

### DEALING DAMAGE Pg 60
Damage doesn't have to be physical - actions that
trap, demoralize, or scare hazards all still deal damage.
Hazards can also be engaged through non-combat
means, solving their problems or befriending/
negotiating with them - it all goes on the same heart
track, and ultimately counts as damage, so everyone
can contribute no matter their skills.
	» Weaknesses: When you hit a hazard weakness,
increase your impact by one step.
	» Resistances: When you hit a hazard resistance,
decrease your impact by one step.
	» Immunities: When you hit a hazard immunity, it
won't take any damage (but can still take an effect).
### TAKING DAMAGE Pg 62
Damage is represented by marks made on a character's
aspect track, with the player choosing the most relevant
track when they take the damage. Hazards don't use the
same levels of impact as bugs, and instead deal more
granular damage (usually between 1 and 6 marks).
Breaks on an aspect stop damage, allowing for tactical
defence. If a bug takes more damage than their aspect
track can hold, it wraps around and starts converting
marks to burns. You can't split damage from one source
over multiple aspects.
If a bug has resistance to damage, it takes half the
usual marks. If it has a weakness, it takes double.
### EFFECTS Pg 64
Attacks and actions can also have effects. These are
mostly freeform, but some are common enough to have
a specific name and some simple rules...
	» Push and Pull: These move a hazard around the
environment.
	» Stun: These prevent a hazard from taking the
spotlight next. *If a player character is stunned, they*
*also can't take the spotlight next and any hazard*
*that attacks them triggers an auto-disaster due to*
*their inability to react.*
	» Grab: Entangles two individuals. The larger individual
can easily move or throw the smaller, and the smaller
individual may be able to climb around or ride on the
larger.
	» Swallow: Allows a hazard to trap a character until
they find a way to escape.

## MONTAGE STUFF
A mode of play used to pass time quickly, while getting
useful things done. Every bug in a montage gets a task
to use, which can either be freeform or can be used for...
### MOULTING Pg 80
This allows a bug to spend Nectar and gain a new
aspect, augment, or skill rank, but requires a safe place
to moult.
Aspects cost 3 Nectar, Augments 2 Nectar, and Skill
Ranks an amount of Nectar equal to the rank you're
gaining.
### RECOVERING Pg 71
You can use a task to sleep, pulling your mood to
positive, or to heal, clearing one mark from any of your
aspects. If you're in a settlement, you can clear burns as
well as marks. If you choose to roll instead, consume a
bit and use the results below...
	» A Triumph on a 6 lets you clear two marks, or clear
one mark and pull your mood positive.
	» A Conflict on a 5 or 4 lets you either clear one mark
or pull your mood to positive.
	» A Disaster on a 3, 2, or 1 means youdon't clear any
marks, but you pull your mood to neutral.
	» A Twist on a double can be treated as usual, giving a
small unexpected benefit, or can be used as a bump
on your healing, clearing an extra mark or moving
your mood an extra step.
### SCROUNGING Pg 72
This works like gathering, gaining you a bit or a bob, but
you get a greater degree of control over the type (you
can specify what you want, like 'something shiny' or
'something sticky') thanks to using a task. Roll the dice
and use the results below...
	» A Triumph on a 6 lets you find exactly what you were
looking for.
	» A Conflict on a 5 or 4 lets you find something close
to what you want, or something that's exactly what
you want but it also has a negative tag supplied by
the Weaver.
	» A Disaster on a 3, 2, or 1 lets you find a bit or bob
that has nothing to do with what you wanted.
	» A Twist on a double can be treated as usual, giving
a small unexpected benefit, or can be used to add a
positive tag to what you find.
### MYSTERYIZING Pg 74
This allows you to use a task to potentially make a small
discovery, bringing you closer to solving a mystery. Roll
the dice and use the results below...
	» A Triumph on a 6 lets you make a small discovery.
	» A Conflict on a 5 or 4 lets you find something that
allows an ally to make a small discovery.
	» A Disaster on a 3, 2, or 1 means you find nothing,
and nobody gets to make a small discovery.
	» A Twist on a double can be treated as usual, giving a
small unexpected benefit, or can be used to go down
the rabbit hole, allowing the Weaver to add a new
truth to the world.
### COOKING Pg 76
Using a task to cook requires two bits, and creates a
meal with benefits based on the bits used (adding an
extra bit allows for an extra portion of the meal to be
made). A meal can offer resistance to a damage type,
+1 skill rank, or a reduction of one type of cut by 1.
Each meal or portion is enough for one bug. Roll the dice
and use the results below...
	» A Triumph on a 6 has the meal/portion grant
benefits for the rest of the scene.
	» A Conflict on a 5 or 4 has the meal/portion grant
benefits on the next relevant action or reaction.
	» A Disaster on a 3, 2, or 1 gives no benefit.
	» A Twist on a double can be treated as usual, giving
a small unexpected benefit, or can be used to add
depth of flavour with a second benefit, or can have
each meal/portion pull mood positive when eaten.
### CRAFTING Pg 78
Using a task to craft requires two bobs, and creates a
contraption that mimics a part, gear, or buddy (adding
an extra bob allows for an extra copy of the contraption
to be made). Each contraption or copy is enough for one
bug, and if its nonexistent track would ever be marked,
it works once and then falls apart. Roll the dice and use
the results below...
	» A Triumph on a 6 has the contraption/copy grant
the mimic aspect for the rest of the scene.
	» A Conflict on a 5 or 4 has the contraption/copy
grant the mimic aspect once, before it breaks.
	» A Disaster on a 3, 2, or 1 gives no benefit.
	» A Twist on a double can be treated as usual, giving
a small unexpected benefit, or can be used to add
an augment to the aspect your contraption/copy is
mimicking.

### LOOKOUTS AND LOOKOUT ROLLS Pg 88
Each bug that isn't the leader is on lookout, and gets
to roll 2d6 to see if they spot anything while travelling.
For this lookout roll, check the results table below (if
you don't get any of the results below, you've spotted
nothing interesting).
	» Double 6: An unexpected landmark.
	» Double 5: A trove of stuff.
	» Double 4: An event in progress.
	» Double 3: A change in the weather.
	» Double 2: An NPC.
	» Double 1: A looming hazard.
The Weaver can add secret or random results to
increase the chance of certain lookout outcomes, or can
introduce an encounter or choice of their own making
regardless of the dice rolled.
### ENCOUNTERS AND CHOICES Pg 90
Whether found with a lookout roll or put in the path
of the bugs by the Weaver, encounters and choices
represent an interesting and potentially dangerous
point along a journey.
Choices simply require the bugs to make a decision
between two or more options. Encounters usually begin
a small scene, taking longer but offering more potential
rewards.
### SURPRISE Pg 91
Players usually decide whether to engage with a choice
or encounter, and can often avoid it if they wish. But
if they're surprised, they can't avoid it - they have to
engage because they're caught off guard or blunder
right into the middle of things.
If the encounter quickly becomes a rumble, this state of
surprise lasts for a single action (typically the first action
taken).
### WEATHER AND LIGHT Pg 94
Can change due to a lookout roll, a microclimate, or the
Weaver's decision. Each weather type outside of the
usual Calm Skies (Rainstorm, Cold Snap, Sporenado,
Heatwave, Gale) has possible special rules a Weaver can
choose to apply.
Light conditions are either described as Daylight or
Darkness. Bugs can see fine in both, but some hazards
and aspects interact with one or the other in special
ways.
## JOURNEY STUFF
Journeys let a band of bugs get from one place to
another, finding adventure along the way (if they're
lucky).
### THE JOURNEY TRACK Pg 84
Set by the Weaver, a special kind of track that has both
boxes (to measure the amount of strides it takes to get
to a destination) and a ticking clock (to represent the
time that passes, and keep track of night and day).
Journey track lengths are determined by the Weaver.
Ticking clocks are always eight segments, with the
upper 4 being day and the lower 4 night.
### LEADER AND TRAVEL ACTIONS Pg 86
For each part of the journey, the players should choose
one character to be the leader. The leader decides
which travel action to take, chosen from the list below...
Ramble: Your group advances at a gentle pace.
*Lookout Rolls: Yes*
*Surprise!: No*
*Distance Covered: 1 stride*
*Time Taken: 1 tick*
March: You move swiftly and seriously.
*Lookout Rolls: Yes*
*Surprise!: Yes*
*Distance Covered: 2 strides*
*Time Taken: 1 tick*
Wait: You stay in one place, alert.
*Lookout Rolls: Yes*
*Surprise!: No*
*Distance Covered: None*
*Time Taken: 1 tick*
Camp: Your group takes a breather and sets up camp,
starting a montage. Tasks can be used to go on the
lookout as well as usual montage actions; if nobody is
on lookout, the band can be surprised.
*Lookout Rolls: Maybe*
*Surprise!: Maybe*
*Distance Covered: None*
*Time Taken: 2 ticks*
If you're riding a picotitan on a journey, double the
amount of marks made with each travel action.

SKILLS Pg 144
Add dice to a roll that relates to the skill equal to the
number of ranks a character has, either 1, 2, or 3. Skills
are split into four categories: Moving, Friending, Finding,
and Rumbling.
MOVING SKILLS
	» Dash: For sprinting and leaping
	» Climb: For scaling ropes and flatter surfaces
	» Dig: For burrowing into the ground
	» Slink: For keeping a low profile
	» Fly: For taking to the skies
	» Swim: For cutting through liquids with speed
FRIENDING SKILLS
	» Yap: For talking, joking, and finding information
	» Swagger: For throwing your weight around
	» Dazzle: For impressing and surprising
	» Scare: For leveraging fear and shock
	» Care: For tending to plants and injuries
	» Tame: For putting creatures at ease without words
FINDING SKILLS
	» Sense: For learning about the world around you
	» Hunt: For tracking beasts and finding food
	» Think: For processing information and deducing
	» Rummage: For finding lost things and useful trash
	» Make: For building and tinkering
	» Chart: For finding paths and making maps
RUMBLING SKILLS
	» Bash: For hitting and barging things
	» Bite: For sinking your teeth in
	» Slash: For deft cuts and spiky assaults
	» Sizzle: For using flames or acid
	» Dance: For moving with rhythm and flow
	» Shield: For taking hits like a champ
Skills are flexible by design. Each skill can always do
what it describes, but can also do other things that may
only be tangentially related if the situation feels right
(so Swim will always let a bug Swim, but a bug without
Swim might try to move through water using something
like Swagger or Dance if it fits the situation).
Skills also double as damage types - when a bug deals
or takes damage, it's always of a type that matches
one of PICO's skills. Some bugs, and some hazards, are
resistant, weak, or immune to various skills.
## CHARACTER STUFF
Characters are bugs, and bugs can be complex!
NECTAR Pg 102
A character creation and advancement resource, given
by the Weaver at the beginning of character creation
and also as a bug learns and grows through play. 25
Nectar gives a good starter bug, and 50 gives a more
experienced bug.
Nectar can be stored indefinitely, and can be used
during a montage to Moult and advance a character's
skills or aspects.
NAME Pg 168
Bugs have two names, a Common Name and a
Pseudoscientific Name. The pseudoscientific name
is taken from a combination of a bug's aspects and
augments, and the common name is decided entirely by
the player.
### ASPECTS Pg 104
Maximum 6 per bug. Describe the special elements of
your bug. Each aspect has a Name, a Track, a Type (Part,
Gear, or Buddy), and some Benefits and/or Drawbacks.
When a track takes damage, represent that with a / for a
mark or a X for a burn. Fully marked/burned tracks don't
give you their Benefit, but they do still give Drawbacks.
Aspects can also have Breaks in their tracks. If marking
or burning multiple boxes on an aspect track at once,
stop when you hit a break. If you need to mark a track
but it's fully marked, convert existing marks to burns
instead.
	» Part aspects describe something biological, a special
feature of your bug.
	» Gear aspects describe something important that
your bug has found or made, that they'd never be
seen without.
	» Buddy aspects describe small friends, pets, or
companions that are devoted to your bug, and stick
by them at all times.
### AUGMENTS Pg 106
Added to aspects to give them longer tracks and
additional benefits. An aspect can have a maximum of
two augments.
*Aspects and augments also give an optional*
*pseudoscientific name component.*

### BITS AND BOBS Pg 160
Every bug has a number of carry slots (usually starting
at six, but this can be added to by a bug's aspects). Each
carry slot can hold a bit or bob.
Bits are organic things that can usually be eaten, like a
drop of syrup or an acorn. Bobs are synthetic, inorganic
things, and usually can't be eaten, like a paperclip or a
rusted nail.*Each bug begins the game with two carry*
*slots full of bits and bobs, but can gain more as they*
*adventure.*
Bits and bobs are used to add dice to rolls, to create
things during a montage, or to trade with NPCs. They
also often have Tags, special words like 'sweet' or
'sticky' that give you more information about them, and
might suggest interesting ways they might be used.
*Chunks are massive versions of a bit or bob, like an*
*entire orange versus a single orange segment. A band*
*of bugs can only carry one chunk around with them at*
*a time.*
MOODS Pg 156
Each bug has three moods placed on a five-box
track. OK, the middle mood that covers the second,
third, and fourth box of the track, is neutral - it has no
special rules. The first box of the track is given over to
a negative mood, and the fifth box to a positive mood
(these are chosen at character creation).
The mood track holds a single mark that begins at
OK, and pulls negative or positive depending on world
events, such as...
	» The Weather (either direction)
	» Winning a Rumble (either direction)
	» Making a Grand Discovery (either direction)
	» Finding an Impressive Location (either direction)
	» Finishing a Tiny Story (either direction)
	» Using a Task to Recover or Sleep (which always
pulls the mood marker toward OK).
### MYSTERIES Pg 164
Each bug chooses a mystery at character creation,
which can hold three small discoveries. Small
discoveries can be made when a bug notices something
that might be relevant to their mystery, and calls it out.
When three small discoveries are made, a bug can make
a grand discovery that adds a new truth to the world
of PICO, maybe even changing reality as it does so.
## PICOTITAN STUFF
Sometimes, having a big friend around is really, really
helpful - a picotitan can be acquired through trading,
earning, raising, inheriting, or even stealing (if you're an
amoral sort).
### SPECIAL RULES Pg 174
	» Picotitans are controlled by the Weaver, but bugs can
spend Favour to have them perform specific actions.
	» Picotitans have an aura of competency that makes
certain skills used in their presence more effective.
	» Picotitans have their own gigaskills and unique
benefits.
	» Picotitans can be ridden during journeys, doubling
the amount of strides covered with a travel action.
	» Picotitans don't enter settlements and won't typically
join rumbles.
	» Picotitans can carry a titan-den, built with chunks,
which gives additional benefits. A picotitan can carry
one chunk at a time.
### SPENDING FAVOUR Pg 176
Picotitan favour can be spent on the following actions...
	» TITANIC AID: Allows a bug to subsitute their usual
skill for one of the picotitan's inherent gigaskills.
	» TITANOTASK: Allows the picotitan to perform a
single task independent of any bugs.
	» TITANWATCH: This allows the titan to spot
something on a journey (gain a free lookout result).
	» TITANOSPEED: Triples the distance bugs travel with
a single travel action rather than the usual doubling.
### TITAN-DENS Pg 184
Miniature settlements built upon a picotitan, like a
travelling base. When adding a feature to a titan-den,
bugs need to begin a montage, consume the required
chunks (listed in each feature's entry in the picotitan
chapter), use one task to complete the construction,
and spend a point of picotitan favour.
