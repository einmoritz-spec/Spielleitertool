## A local multiverse

## Tearing Spacetime
Spacetime permeates every world and keeps it from doing illogical things, like sloshing into neighbouring universes. A field of math covering everything, thicker in some places, thinner in others. Piling up in drifts like snow. Collecting in pools like slush. Flowing in undulating waves and able to boil and froth.
Nth gear hoverboards are designed to ride these curves, and innovative slugblasters have learned to plunge through spacetime like a jet ski breaking the surface tension of the water, exiting this world and leaving a portal behind them.
- Most spacetime portals are temporary, usually lasting no longer than a few seconds before they cool, shrink, and smooth back over.
- Creating a portal generally requires three things: an action roll, a hoverboard or backup portal device, and something called a thin-zone.

## thin-Zones
You can’t make a portal just anywhere. You need a spot where the spacetime is thin enough. These thin-zones (sometimes called portal-zones) are scattered across the multiverse and hidden throughout town, each linked to a specific neighbouring world. There’s a zone to Vastiche in the park. Operaeblum has a mid-air outie at the end of The Dropline. And you heard Sita fell out of The Golden Jungle right in Ryan Paulson’s bedroom.
Some thin-zones are undiscovered, hidden, secret, guarded, buried or blocked in some way. A spacetime ampimeter can measure nearby spacetime, and experienced hoverboarders say they can feel out spacetime density by the way it affects their ride. Unlocking one of these valuable spots might be a run itself. And of course, some thin-zones are just not as thin as they should be.
These are what sluggers call “doubles” and “triples,” and require special tech or skill to use.

## planar Hopscotch
You can’t reach every universe from yours, just the universes that border yours, dimensionally speaking. You might travel through a few worlds to get to your final destination, and then a few more to get home. To get from Thennis Spar to Popularia, you’ll cut through The Waking Pits, zagging from thin-zone to thin-zone.
Sometimes these zones are close together, and your stay in a connecting world is very brief (kids say there’s a shortcut through Desnine short enough to swim without an oxygen mask). Other times you’ll find yourself skating a kilometre through the jungle pursued by mathpanthers just to get to your next thin-zone.
A lot of slugblasters find all the 5-dimensional logistics and dynamic route-planning their favourite part of the sport. You’ll often hear sluggers discussing paths and routes in impenetrable jargon and slang, geeking out about the kid from Thennis who did a 5-burn P2Q slide cycle in under 3 minutes.
## one-way gates
Most of the portals you create in these thin-zones are one-way. Yes, you can go to Vastiche from the park, but you need to find another way back once you’re there—a specific portal-zone that goes to Null.
One-way portals are gnarly. When you create one, you see nothing but a perfect, vantablack hole in space. No sound, light, or matter can come back through from the portal’s destination. If you put your hand in, you can’t pull it back out. You can’t even feel it, because your brain can’t receive signals from your fingertips. Step through quickly so you don’t black out!
Once you’re through, that same portal looks very different, like an open window into the world you just left, but a window you can’t re-enter. Your hand passes through it like it’s a hologram, a mirage of home.

## Peelback
Just as spacetime tends to sew itself back up, the cosmic immune systems of these other worlds tend to spit out foreign objects. If you go too far or stay out too long eventually you’re forced back to your homeworld through a traumatic process called “peeling back.” On the one hand, this means that it’s unlikely you’re ever stuck anywhere forever. On the other hand, peeling back is so unbearably intense that many teenagers hang up their boards after experiencing it. Without special equipment, adults rarely survive at all.
Much is still unknown about peelback. Why does stress seem to trigger it? Why does it only seem to affect life, and not that keychain from Popularia? Why did Nicole’s brother get flung into the astral plane for three weeks and why can he read minds now?
## Hostile environments
Because of quantum waveform probability echoing, the topography of some worlds may seem familiar, but almost everything else is likely way different.
Sure, it might have a *field*  and a *sky* , just like Hillview does, but here it’s a field of black glass and pink flame, with a white sky so hot your bodily fluids expand quicker than your skin. It may even be so different that your atoms separate, or a third of your dimensions blink out, or your existence itself becomes utter nonsense under the alien quantum laws of this new hellscape you decided to pop your head into just for the lols.
These worlds come in a variety of hospitalities and may require you to have masks (for toxic atmospheres or deficient oxygen levels), hazwear suits (for extreme temperatures, pressures, and radiation), or logic binders (for worlds with different physical laws than your own).
