## faction rules
There are four kinds of factions in Slugblaster: crews, sponsors, authorities, and crowds. Each faction has things they like and things they don’t. How you treat them, what you post, and what they hear about you can change how they view you, and how they treat you back.
You can track your faction relationships using the following symbols.
**Friendly.** Agreeable, interested, encouraging.
**Tight.** Helpful, close, caring, enthusiastic.
**Ally.** Ride or Die. Should trigger a perk or event, such as *Diehard Fans* , *Collab* , or *Swag* .
**Unfriendly.** Annoyed, skeptical, or wary.
**Rivals.** Resentful, angry, threatened, petty.
**Enemy.** Let me at ‘em. Should trigger an event like *Hunted*  or *Smear Tactics* .
**Unstable.** The relationship is on the verge of changing dramatically. Anything could tip it.
(Useful for betrayals, which have the potential to turn *allies*  into *enemies*  quickly, for example.)

## Sponsors
Getting sponsored is the nearly-multiversal goal of all slugblasters, ever since the very first hoverboard shop gave a free t-shirt to some kid they liked. However, a few years ago, a candy company called Wilkie’s transformed the scene by injecting big money into it, offering massive rockstar sponsorship deals to promising slugblasters.
- **Miper.** A sleek, idealistic big-tech company.
- **Hardecker.** A clueless blue-collar brand that’s just been made cool.
- **Wilkie’s.** Candy, toys, energy drinks, x-treme sports.
- **Scram Cat.** The original indie slugblasting shop, in the process of becoming a Miper subsidiary.
- **FUST.** Last real indie sponsor, owned/operated by total a-holes.
## Crowds
The slugblaster scene is a 4-dimensional Venn diagram of complex sub-scenes and overlapping political, philosophical, and aesthetic in-groups. But in general, it can be divided into five main clusters. These are your potential fans—while your fame level determines how well-known you are, your relationship with these various crowds determines who actually likes you and thinks you’re cool. Cool is relative, after all.
- **The Thrashers.** The partiers, hipsters, and artists.
They value authenticity, aesthetic, and attitude.
- **The Heads.** The nerds, athletes, and aficionados.
They value skill and innovation.
- **The Mavens.** The activists, punks, and diehards.
They value community and standing for something.
- **The Freaks.** The ravers, crusties, and oddballs. They value originality and expression.
- **The Normies.** The mainstreamers, pre-teens, and basics. They value fun.
## CREWS
You’re not the first group of slugblasters to give themselves a name and matching hats, and you won’t be the last. Many crews have come and gone, formed and reformed, risen to stardom, and washed up just as quickly.
- **BRB.** A crew of straight-laced, athletic, and super competitive laserbladers.
- **Jet Collective.** A large crew of inclusive activists with an addiction to petty drama.
- **Null Range.** An obnoxious, anti-authoritarian crew of older kids from Hillview.
- **Lazy Bunny.** Super mainstream, super broish, super nice live-streamers having their moment.
- **The Wicks.** A crew of young, rowdy, disenfranchised up-and-comers.
## Authorities
There is more to this world than slugblasting—there are also people who want to stop slugblasting, control slugblasting, and use slugblasters to help them commit crimes! Authorities are the various powerful groups of adults you have to deal with whether you want to or not. These authorities’ motives are complex, mysterious, and often-changing, and you tend to see different sides of them depending on your relationship. They can be your biggest ally or your greatest enemy, and each entry details how.
- **DARA.** Big, mysterious research facility. Its Rescue division acts as Hillview’s portal cops.
- **The Arborists.** An ancient sect of planeswalkers from Thennis Spar.
- **Shimmer.** Transversal criminal syndicate.
- **Doorways.** Concerned parent group from Hillview.
- **The Old Guard.** Promoters, magazine editors, and retired slugblasters.
