PRISONERS
“Something bad is happening, but
who cares if a few of us die?”
There are hundreds of prisoners in Brygidki,
many of them accused of serious crimes. The
player characters will notice at first glance
that the prison is overpopulated. There are
many more prisoners in each cell than there
are beds – some of them sleep on thin mattresses laid on the cold floor.
Most of the prisoners are in poor health,
rarely getting to walk outside their cells, and
are treated harshly by the prison’s staff. They
are afraid of what could happen to them and
feel that no one in this prison will defend
their lives. Many prisoners believe in the
Angel of Justice – Father Hryhorii’s explanation for what is happening. They try to convince the guards to let them attend a sermon
so that they can confess and repent for their
sins to protect themselves from God’s wrath.
If player characters ask any prisoners
about their crimes, many of them will deny
having done anything wrong and say that they
got here accidentally. With an OBSERVATION
roll, it is evident when a prisoner is lying,
perhaps to the point that they have started
to believe their own lies.

I VA N FR A NKO
“Do you have a pencil to spare, by any chance?”
Ivan Franko is a poet and a political prisoner,
falsely accused of being involved in an international socialist organization and sentenced
to nine months in Brygidki. A young writer,
he is an activist for the rights of Ukrainians
in the Austro-­Hungarian Empire. Ivan is
well-­educated and has good manners and
bright ideas about conscious society, and
despite his young age, he is respected by his
cellmates.
He does not believe in the “Angel of Justice” explanation of what is happening and
will go into lengthy disputes about the freedoms of body and soul. Ivan is concerned
about his inability to continue his studies at
university and complains about the prison
guards who took everything from his cell,
even his pencil and paper.
