GR AV EYA RD
The graveyard is located in a small yard inside the
prison walls. Although it is possible to see the sky from
there, the feeling of being trapped gets even worse.
The walls feel crushing, and even a breath of what is
supposed to be fresh air does not help.
A single old tree stands here, as sick as all the
involuntary inhabitants of this place. Its branches
are covered with many large growths of mistletoe,
like tumors seeping the life from its host. The graves
under the tree look ancient with their crosses and
weeping angels.
Another set of graves, off to the side, seems to
have been dug later. They are all the same – simple
tombstones, as gray as the sky. Two graves from this set
are fresh. There is not a single blade of grass around,
and the ground seems darker than black.
One other grave does not seem to belong to these
two groups. It looks older than the second group, but
not as old as the first. It is separated from both groups
and located at the very corner of the yard, far from
the shade of the tree.

Some of these volumes are so thick that it feels
almost impossible to hold them in one’s hands; some
are so old that a strong breath in their direction could
cause their destruction. But all the books and shelves
look well-­kept and cared for, despite their condition.
Several benches are placed beside the shelves, in
front of icons on the wall. The icons look as old as
the chapel, or even older. The paint has darkened
over the years, and figuring out what is painted there
takes time and effort.
The chapel is not as full of people as it would be outside the prison walls. However, a few off-­duty guards
are present here. One sits quietly on the bench, staring
at an icon, another walks back and forth along the bookshelves, and a third talks to the chaplain in low tones.
A large, winding staircase leads to the second floor,
where Father Hryhorii lives in the rectory. The chapel’s bell tower is on the third floor.

CH A PEL
Stepping inside the chapel feels like traveling back in
time. If the rest of the prison shows some signs of a
modern influence, this place looks as if time ignores it
entirely. Perhaps this is because of the dust in the corners of the room, or the scent of numerous books lining the walls – so many that it is difficult to see the walls
behind shelves overburdened with the heavy tomes.
