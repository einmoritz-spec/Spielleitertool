GOLEM LEGENDS
There are several versions of the golem story. According to most of them, the creature was magically created (or summoned) by Rabbi Judah Loew ben Bezalel.
It was supposed to protect the Jewish community from
persecution in the 16th century. The building material
for the beast was clay (or silt) from the Vltava River,
and the creature was supposed to be brought to life
by magic, which the rabbi was said to have mastered.
According to one of the most frequently repeated
versions of the legend, the golem had to be activated
by placing a piece of paper with an incantation in

its mouth (there are also versions in which the paper
was affixed to the golem’s head or put in the place
of its heart). Removal of the paper resulted in immediate deactivation.
The golem had no feelings or will, but due to an
error, it went out of control and began a frenzy of
random killing. Judah Loew ben Bezalel destroyed his
creation by changing the spell on the golem’s forehead – the word Emet ‫“( אמת‬truth”) was changed
by erasing the first letter, so it became the word
Met ‫“( מת‬death”).

CHURCH OF OUR L A DY
OF THE SNOWS
One of Prague’s oldest and longest-­built churches.
Construction started in the 13th century in the Gothic
style, but it wasn’t fully completed until the 17th century. The Franciscans now use the church – however,
the old parish priest Vojtěch Jirásek has remained
there. There are numerous buildings around the
church, most of which have a monastic function.
Underneath the side aisles from the 12th century,
there are two large crypts for church dignitaries and
wealthy Prague citizens (including the founders of
the nearly 400-year-­long construction).
