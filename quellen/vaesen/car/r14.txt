Lemberg is considered the most important city in
Austrian (and later Austro-­Hungarian) Galicia. This
large city has a mixture of Slavic (Poles and Ukrainians) and Jewish populations. Since 1661, one of the
most significant universities for the Slavs has operated here, where in the 19th century studies could
be conducted in national languages (the permitted
faculties were language-­dependent, as the “Franciscan University” educated “both Poles and Ruthenians,” under which Ukrainians and Byelorussians
were considered). The Pravda Society had a branch
in Potocki Palace for years.

KR A K AU (KR A KÓW)
For centuries the capital of Poland, Krakau is one
of the most significant centers of Polish culture, art,
and education. The Austrians heavily fortified the
city. The Wawel Castle, the seat of Polish kings, was
made into one of the most extensive barracks in the
east of the Empire. The city’s university, founded in
1364, is one of the oldest universities in the world.

SEIFFERSH AU (KOPA NIEC)
Among such a respectable group of metropolises significant to the Slavs, the presence of a small Sudetenland village may be a surprise. In the pre-­Christian
period, however, it was an essential place of worship.
In modern times, for the Prague branch of the Pravda
Society it is a kind of a “gateway to the north,” located
in a mountain valley called the “Dark Ravine.” The
increased activity of vaesen in the area is astonishing.
