OLMÜTZ (OLOMOUC)
The historic capital of Moravia until the 17th century.
In historic Bohemia, Olmütz served as the second
most important city after Prague. It all ended in 1642
when the Swedish army occupied the city that had
been turned into a fortress, and destroyed it. Due to
its good location, the city, like the fortress, was raised
from the ruins, but its role was never as important
again. In the 19th century it served as a strategically
placed garrison, a “gateway to Moravia.” Because of
this, it was also significantly Germanized – the German population was three times larger than the Czech
population. The situation of the Jewish people there
was unusual – Jews had been expelled from the city in
1454, and the ban on settlement was maintained in
Olomouc until 1848. Since then, their community
has grown steadily.
The city walls stifled the city’s development, so
the villages around Olmütz were heavily urbanized
and inhabited mostly by Czechs.

Weaving industry developed in the city starting in
the late 18th century. Wise investments, combined
with rapid industrialization and many technical innovations, quickly made Brno one of the most important
industrial centers of the Empire, often compared to
Manchester. In 1814, the Empire’s first steam engine
was installed here. Then, in 1846, gas lanterns were
put in place to light up the city after dark, and a year
later Brno gained a telegraph connection to the capital. In 1869 trams (initially horse-­drawn) appeared
on the streets, and the city was being prepared for
electrification.

DAILY LIFE
There is a great deal of social stratification in Bohemia
in the 19th century, which is typically encountered
in places where, in a multinational state, no form
of assimilation was pursued for centuries but only
forced conversion to the language and culture of the
dominant nation. Including the origin or a particular
social stratum in the background of a character from
these areas may significantly impact gameplay, and
especially relationships with NPCs. It is an optional
choice that can enrich a character’s story.

The decline of Olomouc’s importance benefited Brno,
which became the most significant city in Moravia.
Initially, nothing foreshadowed this – Brno began
the 19th century as a fortress city. After the nearby
Battle of Austerlitz, Napoleon ordered the demolition of some of the fortifications, depriving the city
of its defensive capabilities. Fragments of the fortifications survived for another four decades, but after
1848 the remains of the city walls were demolished
and replaced by a green belt. Of the entire series of
fortifications with seven gates, only one gate and the
Špilberk Castle survived by the second half of the 19th
century, with the castle serving as a prison until 1853.

Austrian aristocracy is exclusively German-­speaking
and sometimes descends from families related to the
Habsburgs. Its members are the wealthiest and most
influential citizens, with access to the country’s highest offices. They often live in lavish palaces in city
centers. Politicians, ministers, and generals come
from this stratum.
