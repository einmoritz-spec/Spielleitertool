THE CARPATHIAN
SOCIETY
This chapter describes the Carpathian Society,
locally called Pravda (which means “truth” in every
Slavic language) – an organization based in Prague,
a territory which at this time belongs to the Austrian
Empire, under the rule of the Habsburg dynasty.
The Pravda Society has long been engaged in
research and, when necessary, expulsion of vaesen,
playing a key role in the lands from the Urals to
the Oder, from Estonia to the south of the Balkans.
Such a vast space meant a huge variety of vaesen,
but the low population density, especially in the
east, allowed people of many ethnicities to coexist
with them.
However, these lands were eventually divided
between three empires that rejected traditions and
old knowledge to focus on building a modern power
of their own – imperialism. These three were the
Kingdom of Prussia (later known as the German
Empire or Second Reich), the Austrian Empire (later
the Austro-­Hungarian Empire), and the Russian
Empire. Vaesen were relegated to the realm of superstition and legend.
It is thanks to you, Thursday’s Children, that Pravda will regain its meaning. You and your allies will
form a group managing its new premises – the newly
renovated and rebuilt Powder Gate (Prašná brána),
on the border of the old and new town.

PRAVDA
The full name of Pravda – “The Society for the Integration and Adaptation of the Carpathian Tradition,
Based in Prague” – was registered at the Prague City
Hall on April 11, 1808, under number IX/203/1808.
By then, the Society had already existed informally
for more than 600 years. Since relocating to Prague,
it has been operating as a science and research society,
fitting into the fashionable trend of Romanticism.

DA ILY ACTI V ITIES
According to the application (and the state permission granted), the organization is focused on studying
local history and culture. It also works for integration with the German-­speaking part of society. That
is the official front, which occupies the lower level of
the Gate. Pravda does not conduct external activities,
so it is unlikely to have visitors. If any should appear,
however, two clerks, formally employed members of
the Society, handle possible services for them.
Officially, the upper rooms are intended to serve
as employee accommodations, workspaces and document repositories, and are only available to members
of the Society. In practice, Pravda carries out all kinds
of activities there, adapting the premises as it sees fit.
