THE ELDERS
The Pravda organization has evolved over the centuries – initially, it was a closed, independently
operating circle of insiders. The Council of Representatives, meeting once every five years, was
responsible for coordination and key decisions.
At the peak of Pravda’s development, two groups
emerged – the ordinary members, who had the
essential knowledge and skills to perform basic
tasks (procurement, including unusual resources,
maintenance of the headquarters, intelligence,
and verification of information), and the elders
who, in addition to ensuring the preservation and
transmission of the knowledge of the “forefathers,”
developed new methods of dealing with the vaesen
and performed expulsions in the case of particularly dangerous beings.

STRUCTURE
OF THE SOCIET Y
Members of the Society. Formally, the structure
is flat – the Society is headed by the President, who
serves for life or until their resignation. Ordinary members of the Pravda Society do office work,
including assisting with official correspondence
between Slavs and the German-­speaking administration. They also work with universities, especially with historians, archaeologists, anthropologists, and art historians. These members have basic
knowledge of the vaesen, but they have not had any
contact with them, seeing them as “legends with a
grain of truth.”
Council Members. They are members of the
Society just like the others, with slightly more legal
responsibility. They also make decisions regarding
Pravda. The Council does not have a set number of
members – in prosperous times, there were ten members, now there are only five. To become a Council member, one must have been in the Society for
at least five years. These members are already fully
initiated into the subject of vaesen and have various
specializations predisposing them to membership

in the Council. Their skills are those necessary for
particular tasks, so there is a chief librarian, chief
logistician, chief liquidator, etc.
Sons/Daughters of the Ancients. Informal leaders of the Pravda Society. They are outstanding vaesen
experts, well versed in the history of the known vaesen
and their contacts with people, and responsible for
passing on knowledge between generations.

JOINING PR AV DA
The simplest way to introduce characters to the Society is to have them already be members of it – whether
longstanding members or ones who have just been
promoted and are being introduced to the real challenges that Pravda faces. The Society is not, after all,
completely secret – it can use consultants and has
links with the scientific world. Perhaps there has been
a situation where they are looking for support in a
case connected with vaesen. Note that members of
the Society don’t have to be of Slavic origin. Everyone
is welcome as long as they can provide knowledge or
skills to the organization.

HISTORY
The Pravda Society was founded in the 12th century.
Most Slavic peoples had already adopted Christianity at that time, and old beliefs and knowledge were
becoming very dangerous to followers and researchers. The transmission of knowledge was not easy,
either – the first Slavic script was Cyrillic, created
at the beginning of the 10th century and not widespread until the 11th and 12th centuries. The script
was known mainly to the clergy.
The aim of the Pravda Society, however, was never
religion – it was to gain ever more knowledge of the
vaesen and, if necessary, to drive them out. They also
referred to the wisdom of the “forefathers” (ancient
ancestors), which had been forgotten for centuries.
