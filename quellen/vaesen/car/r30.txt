THE SOCIET Y RESURGENT

The situation improved somewhat at the end of the
18th century, when many researchers of ancient history found their way into the aristocracy – wealthy
representatives of the Schaffgottsch, Gersdorf, and
Hohberg families were willing to spend considerable
sums on research into fields that were poorly known
(such as the anthropology of Slavic peoples).
Under the guise of this research, Pravda could
slowly revive itself while its members carefully dispensed knowledge that could see the light of day.
Thanks to the favor of its patron, Adolf Traugott von
Gersdorf, the Society gained its first official headquarters in years – the Mon Plaisir tower, which was
the highest astronomical observatory in the world at
the time, and to which only Gersdorf (plus his guests)
and Pravda members had access. The tower was in
Wigandstahl, in the Western Sudeten Mountains.
After Gersdorf’s death in 1807, the Society moved to
Prague and purchased property in the Powder Gate
thanks to the funds it received.

It was not until the middle of the 17th century that
the construction was provisionally completed, and
the city gunpowder magazine was placed there. The
gate was then named Prašná brána (“Powder Gate”).
It served as a gunpowder magazine for over a century
but was severely damaged by enemy artillery during
the Prussian siege of Prague in 1757. It was thoroughly
repaired in 1799, with all the damaged decorations
stripped and the structure reinforced. However, a
military inspection showed that such a tall building
was unsuitable for a modern powder magazine. The
building was handed over to the city of Prague, from
which Pravda acquired the premises at a good price,
with an obligation to look after the condition of the
building and to pay an annual city tax.
The Gate gained its current, most iconic appearance during its reconstruction in the second half of
the 19th century. Josef Mocker supervised the work,
aiming to recreate the Gothic design. The new form
outgrew the original by more than 20 meters – the
characteristic towering roof was only added during
this reconstruction. The Gate grew from the original 42 meters to 65 meters. The distinctive clock
was removed, and much of the Gothic detailing was
restored.

POWDER GATE

NEIGHBORHOOD

The Powder Gate is still located on the border between
the Old Town and the New Town, forming part of
the fortifications of the Royal Court – the seat of
the ruler of Bohemia. Construction of the Gothic
city gate began in 1475. Before that, the Gate of St.
Ambrose, with its basic shape, had stood there for
almost a century and a half. The first construction

The Powder Gate is located near the former Royal
Court (Royal Palace), built on a triangular plan, which,
after being abandoned by the sovereign, became an
archbishop’s palace, a seminary, and eventually a
Jesuit college. The area began to decline after the
dissolution of the order and its possessions in the
1870s. An important factor was the proximity to the
densely built-­up Jewish ghetto in Josefov and parts
of the New Town – regarded as quite dangerous for
outsiders.
