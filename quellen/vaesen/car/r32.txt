The premises of the Gate, the Jesuit college, and
the adjacent church were handed over to the army,
which did not carry out any significant work there
for several years, apart from securing the buildings
(and repairing the Gate). The premises were used
as warehouses, workshops, and stables. In 1869, the
Cadet School (called “Kadetka” by Prague residents)
was opened there, which made the area much safer.
Several minor and more extensive renovations were
also started, including the second renovation of the
Gate and construction of a square that could serve
as a parade ground. That means there will always be
soldiers and sentries hanging around the Gate. On
the other hand, an old Slavic proverb says that “the
darkest place is under the lamp” – the authorities
consider the area around Kadetka to be completely
safe and will not disturb the Pravda headquarters
unless absolutely necessary.
A large inn with the small hotel Café Français is in
close proximity, and there are numerous workshops
and service businesses in both the Jewish Quarter
and the Old Town.

A BOUT THE GATE
The Gate has a large passage for carts and pedestrians
at its base. Celetna Street goes through the gateway.
On either side of the Gate are spiral staircases leading
two levels up. The actual headquarters of the Pravda
Society is thus located at the level of the upper floor
of surrounding townhouses. A long corridor runs
through the entire floor, connecting the two staircases. On both sides of this hall are tall rooms, each
with a single large window. These were used as gunpowder storerooms, so they are well-­ventilated, and
despite the thick walls, there are no traces of dampness.
However, they were not heated – only recently were
wood stoves put in. The rooms have nicely preserved
Gothic vaulted ceilings.
Both staircases lead further up, to the second
level of the building. Originally, there were magazines there as well; the layout is now identical to the
first floor. There are also remains of Gothic vaulted

ceilings here – unfortunately, they were severely damaged in the 18th century. Attention is drawn to the
much sturdier doors than the level below – these
rooms are only accessible to Pravda members. There
is an additional, third spiral staircase in one of the
rooms in the western part.
The double staircases on the sides lead on to the
third floor, the last one that is accessible from the
street. This level has no windows. It was originally
an attic, with ventilation openings to dry the rooms
below. The ceiling here is much lower than on the
previous levels – the rooms are just over two meters
tall. The layout is similar to that of the lower floors
(a corridor and series of rooms to the east and west),
but the rooms are more numerous and therefore
much smaller.
The side staircases lead to the viewing gallery and
end there. The gallery can be closed with solid tin
flaps. The “internal” staircase reaches the fourth
floor – a modern apartment with small windows to
the east and west, built on the plan of a hexagon,
with two longer “side” walls facing east and west. The
apartment is approximately 60 square meters, with
slightly sloping walls. A steep staircase leads to the
fifth floor, which also has two windows, identical to
the level below. It is a wedge-­shaped room sloping
steeply upwards. The walls conceal the structural
elements of the roof, which significantly limits the
space. This floor can be used as a small storage room
or a two-­bed bedroom. Paraffin lamps are used to
light the building.

UPGRADES
This section includes a number of new Upgrades for
the Pravda Society’s headquarters. These can be used
in addition to the Upgrades listed in chapter 6 of the
core rulebook. However, due to the limited space in
the Powder Gate, two limitations apply:
No more than two Facilities can be added to each of
the four floors of the headquarters – eight in total.
To add more, existing Facilities need to be removed.
