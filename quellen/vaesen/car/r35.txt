Baba Yaga i insije baby
(Baba Yaga and Other Hags)
Author: Unknown.
Edition: A treatise written in 1480 in Kyiv.
Language: Old East Slavic, with annotations in
Latin.
Contents: The book describes vaesen and creatures (probably legendary) inhabiting the territory of Ruthenia.

Language: Polish.

with them. Everything is described in the form

Extra fines mentis

·
·
·
·

(Beyond the Limits of the Mind)
Author: Markus Antonius Herzog.
Edition: Published in Vienna in 1766.
Language: Latin.
Contents: The book describes the author’s
encounters with vaesen and attempts to explain
their presence rationally. The attempts seem quite
successful since, as the encyclopedia notes proclaim, the renowned zoologist M.A. Herzog died
in Graz at the age of 93, utterly sane.

(Slavic Bestiary)
Author: Unknown, it is probably a collective work.
Edition: Basel edition, dating from 1523.
Language: Latin.
Contents: Descriptions of vaesen found in eastern and southern Europe. Some appear to exist
only in the authors’ imagination.

(Hammer for Witches)
Author: An unknown inquisitor.
Edition: A treatise written at the beginning of the
15th century but with numerous different reprints
from the 16th and early 17th centuries.
Language: First published in Latin, with more
recent editions in German and Italian.
Contents: It contains descriptions of popular
magical practices, as well as information on how
to recognize and counteract them. Basic information about vaesen can be gleaned from it.
