Naši Bratři

·

(Our Brothers)
Author: Jiri “Rezak”.
Edition: A manuscript completed in 1441
in Prague.
Language: Czech.
Contents: The book was handwritten in
1403–1441 by a member of the Society, one
of the Sons of the Ancients. In it, Rezak wrote
about the necessity for humans and vaesen
to coexist peacefully without expulsions, and
the need to respect the places where “non-­
human” beings live. It is said that Rezak died
while trying to come to an agreement with
a not-­quite-­peaceful vaesen, which proves
the moderate effectiveness of his methods.

Shedim ve’Dybbukim

·
·
·
·

(Demons and Dybbuks)
Author: Rabbi Abraham ben Zohar.
Edition: A treatise from 1505, published
in Prague.
Language: Hebrew.
Contents: This book contains basic information on Kabbalah and beings related to Jewish
magic – both physical and spiritual (mainly
dybbuks). It is a very valuable and rare item.

Stvorenja južnih zemalja

·
·
·
·

(Creatures of the Southern Lands)
Author: Milorad Todorovic.
Edition: Published in Belgrade in 1780 in a
tiny edition of 100 copies.
Language: Serbian.
Contents: The book describes encounters
with creatures that cannot be found in any
bestiary or encyclopedia (obviously vaesen),
covering the period from antiquity (translations of writings from ancient Greek) to
the author’s contemporary times. The book
was treated as a curiosity in its time.

THREATS
The following are constant issues the Pravda Society
must reckon with, some unique to them and others
to any dwelling in the region in this time. The Game­
master may use these for any mystery.
The state administration carries out biased checks
of any institution run by the Slavs.
Unrest related to Czech liberation movements
from Habsburg rule may trigger a state of emergency in the city (the Powder Gate is near the
military Cadet Academy).
Unrest is directed against the Jews (the Gate is
located near the Jewish Quarter in Josefov).
Journalists seek to know all the activities of the
Pravda Society.
City architects want to demolish the Powder Gate
as part of significant reconstruction.
The military plans to extend the Cadet Academy
into the premises of the Gate.
The Spiritualist Society wishes to possess the
­Pravda Society’s book collection.
A group of drunk cadets from the Academy drops
in for a “neighborly visit.”
A priest who wants to fight “barbaric superstition”
targets the Pravda Society.
A scientific society would like to examine Pravda’s
materials for their suitability at the university.
One or more vaesen nest in the sewers near the
Gate.
One or more vaesen have chosen the Gate as their
ideal habitat.
An official intends to cancel the contract to purchase the Gate’s premises.
An influential and eccentric Habsburg nobleman would like to convert the Powder Gate into
a municipal dovecote.
