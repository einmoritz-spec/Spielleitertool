Babai is an evil night spirit. He is the embodiment of
children’s fears. Living in a lair in the forest’s depths,
he comes to the villages at night. He searches for the
houses where children live. As soon as he finds one,
he sneaks closer to a door or a window and listens
carefully.
What interests him is the behavior of children. If
he hears that a child is well-­behaved and calmly goes
to sleep, Babai has no interest in them. But if a child
is naughty, doesn’t want to go to bed, screams, and
argues with their parents – that child becomes Babai’s
target. Babai waits for all the adults to fall asleep,
sneaks into the house, and steals the naughty child,
putting them in his big bag. To prevent the victim

from screaming or calling for help, Babai licks the
child – his spit immobilizes people. Nobody knows
what happens to the stolen children.
Babai looks like a tall but crooked old man. He
has a gray beard that is knee-­length or even longer,
and hair hangs down just as low. His eyes glow like
embers. Furthermore, Babai always carries a big old
bag, in which he puts the stolen children.
