When I passed through the Serbian villages in the northern Balkans, I heard about a
terrible monster that had recently s ettled in this region and attacked travelers and villagers.
All its victims were found bloodless. Having just heard this story, I was astonished to s ee a
tall man with a crossbow and a long knife on his belt enter the inn on a cold autumn evening.
The locals said that he was a hunter, and he humbly asked for my blessing before going into
the woods. While reading the prayer, I couldn’t take my eyes off the floor where his shadow
was suppos ed to be, but there was none. To bless him, I shook his gray hand, which appeared
highly elastic, and he went into the night. When we were preparing to leave in the morning,
he returned—dirty, with leaves and dried grass in his long black hair and his clothes covered
with traces of a dark liquid that res embled blood. He looked as if he had been in a terrible fight,
but without receiving a single scratch in the tumult. Despite his appearance, the locals greeted
him kindly and respectfully, inviting him to the table.
“That beast won’t bother you anymore,” said the hunter and gave the innkeeper a dirty dark
bag with something round inside.
We s et off, and I am still trying to understand what kind of trophy and from which beast
that strange hunter carried in the bag.

Sometimes, vampires seduce mortal women, resulting
in the birth of dhampirs. They usually die at birth,
but those who survive are neither human nor vampire
but half-­breeds of both species. Most of the children
are male, although from time to time, dhampir girls
are born as well.
Dhampirs inherit traits from both parents. They
are much stronger and more agile than humans, and
they regenerate faster. However, despite having an
immortal parent, the child’s lifespan is even shorter
than that of a human. At the same time, they are
not afraid of the sunlight and can eat regular food,
although human blood enhances their abilities. The
main talents of dhampirs are their sight, which allows
them to see vampires invisible to humans, and innate
sorcery, making them natural vampire hunters.
However, a vampire lineage influences not only
their talents but also their appearance. They usually
have gray skin that appears to be dirty, large noses
and ears, and black hair. Their bodies are extremely
flexible and resilient due to the complete or partial
absence of bones, and they cast no shadow. As descendants of a vampire, dhampirs are often treated with

suspicion and are not generally accepted by society.
However, being vampire hunters allows dhampirs to
gain proper respect among humans.

RITUA L
Dhampirs do not inherit immortality from their vampire parent. It is possible to kill them, although it’s
difficult. However, regardless of whether the dhampir
was killed or died naturally, it will return as a vampire. To prevent it, the body of the dhampir must be
decapitated the same day it dies.
