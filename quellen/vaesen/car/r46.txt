DREKAVAC
A couple of children wound up at the monastery’s doorstep—tired, dirty, and hungry.

two days away from here. After they were fed and had a good night’s sleep, I inquired more

—Antoine Augustin Calmet, August 1720
A drekavac is a malicious being attracted to instances
of great tragedies and cruel injustice. Once drawn
to a place, a drekavac causes a chain of deaths –
starting with livestock and culminating in people
or even entire families. Each death is often preceded by a hair-­raising shriek that gave the creature its name.
Drekavaci live in solitude, in the wilderness, and
can only be seen at night, especially the long dark
nights between Christmas Eve and Epiphany. They

are described as gangly figures with long arms, taloned hands, and oversized heads carried on almost
comically spindly necks. Occasionally, they are covered in thick fur and can turn into different types
of animals.
Drekavaci are especially drawn to the pain caused
by the loss of a child. For that reason, and their terrible shriek, drekavaci are often mistaken for either
mylings or banshees.
