In Jewish folklore a golem is an artificial humanoid
created by a rabbi. The word “golem” means something akin to “raw material” in Hebrew, a fitting name
for a being shaped from earth or clay and brought
to life through mystical means. A rabbi’s creation of
the golem mirrors the Biblical story of Adam, who
was also formed from clay by God himself. However,
golems have a similar flaw to those created before
Adam: even if they are conscious, they lack the soul
and feelings to become real humans.
In rabbinic tradition, a golem was typically used
as a protector of Jewish communities during times of

persecution, or to help with simple or routine tasks.
The creator may shape a golem as they wish, depending
on their skill, time, and the golem’s specific purpose.
However, golems are not always reliable and could
become difficult to control. While the creation of a
golem is deeply rooted in Jewish mysticism, similar
creatures could be formed by those who gained the
sacred knowledge of the rabbis. These magical beings,
though differing in origin, share the golem’s essence
as artificial constructs made to serve their creators.
