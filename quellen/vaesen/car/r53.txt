KHOKHA-YAROKHA
“That house wasn’t made by human hands, that’s for sure. There is hardly a man or a woman
capable of doing such delicate and tiny work. There is hardly a man or a woman who would
create a hous e and all its furniture out of teeth. Children’s teeth. Of cours e, this discovery
attracted curiosity. Of cours e, I was among thos e who decided to go deep underground to
s ee that in person. What a mistake it was! Now I shake every time I hear rustling and
scraping in dark corners. They will find me. I know they will. They will come at night. They
don’t like humans invading their home. They are going to take revenge. Oh my God, I’m sure I
can hear them under the floor right now. I’m sure I can. They are coming for me!”
Here the diary ends. The piece of paper looks as if somebody or something has bitten the rest
of it with small but sharp teeth.

—Antoine Augustin Calmet, February 11, 1749

The khokha-­yarokha is a tiny vaesen with a lizard-­like
body, covered with scales. However, its head does not
resemble that of a snake but rather a rat or a mouse
with long black whiskers and piercing sharp teeth.
Some may also have small wings.
The khokha-­yarokha collects children’s baby teeth.
When a child loses a baby tooth, they need to leave it
on the floor for the night or drop it into a mouse hole
saying “Khokha-­yarokha, take this bone tooth and bring me a gold
one.” Some children also leave a piece of bread or pie
near their lost tooth. If this ritual is not performed, the
new tooth may not grow, or it will emerge sick and black.
The khokha-­yarokha comes through the mouse
hole at night and picks up the tooth. It is said that
this vaesen’s house deep underground, as well as all
the furniture inside it, is built out of children’s teeth.

RITUA L

Khokha-­yarokhas can be talked to and convinced or
just physically killed. Anything that can kill a human
(other than fire) can kill a khokha-­yarokha.
