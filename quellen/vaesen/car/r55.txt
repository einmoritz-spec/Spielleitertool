· Vaesen of Carpathia ·

NACHZEHRER
This happened in the summer when I visited
Silesia to gather materials for my book.
The city of Wrocław had recently suffered
from the Great Plague, and there were many
fresh graves at the local cemetery. Tadeusz,
the cemetery keeper, was accompanying
me when I heard terrible chewing sounds
coming from one of the graves. When I
looked down, I saw a coffin with a hole in
it and the horrifying face of a monster who
was loudly devouring its funeral shroud.
Terrified, we fled the cemetery and reported it
to the Church.
—Antoine Augustin Calmet, August 1739
A nachzehrer is a German version of the vampire; its
name consists of two parts: “nach,” meaning “after,”
and “zehrer,” meaning “to eat” or “subsist on.” Insatiable appetite characterizes this undead. Its cause of
death is often suicide or plague, and it was believed
that when a plague swept a village, the first to succumb to it would become a nachzehrer.
The nachzehrer is driven by gluttonous hunger
and primarily attacks its family. At night, it leaves the
grave as a spirit and sucks the life out of its relatives.
Its hunger is so great that it chews loudly even
when lying in its coffin, which makes its grave easy to
identify in the cemetery. Other signs of a nachzehrer’s grave are a hole bitten through the shroud and
the body’s posture – it lies with one eye opened and
holds its thumb firmly in the other hand. A nachzehrer can haunt its relatives only after it has consumed
its entire burial shroud.
Occasionally, the monster assumes its true
physical form, either to devour the flesh of
the corpses or to climb into the church
tower and ring the bell. Anyone who
hears it is doomed to die before
long. When a nachzehrer wants
to move unnoticed, it assumes
the form of a pig.
