VAESEN IN THIS MYSTERY

BACKGROUND
Bralnic Castle was built at the end of the 14th century
as an outpost on an important trade route between
Transylvanian, Moldavian, and Genoese colonies
on the Black Sea. Unfortunately, a century later, a
great earthquake caused a landslide that made the
trade route no longer accessible, isolating Bralnic
Castle and the village in the valley from the rest of
the world, with only a single dangerous mountain
pass remaining as an access route.
A few months after the landslide, the village suffered
numerous attacks from an unknown beast. Many fell
victim to it, including the local knez (a Slavic term for
prince or duke) who initiated a hunt for the beast – a
wolf-­like vârcolac (page 63). The last survivor among
the knez’s hunters and warriors, his champion, swore to
defeat the beast. The warrior lured the vârcolac into the
dungeons of Bralnic Castle and sealed it there, before
returning to the people and claiming his victory.
During the fight, the warrior was severely injured,
but on his deathbed, he was chosen by the people
to become the new knez. They signed a treaty with
him under which he was to live to protect the valley’s inhabitants. In return, the people of the valley
would pay tribute to him and his descendants. The
next night after the treaty was signed, the new knez
awoke miraculously healed, which people accepted
as a sign from God. Unbeknownst to the villagers,
however, he had been bitten by the vârcolac and died,
but returned as a vampir (page 60).
THE VA MPIRE KNEZ

Becoming a vampire granted the new knez great power,
but he still could not kill the vârcolac despite his many
attempts. So the knez ruled the valley for centuries,
with the imprisoned vârcolac still held in the dungeon. To keep his identity hidden, the vampire knez
slightly changed his appearance from generation to
generation and kept himself isolated from the valley’s peasants, proclaiming that he had sent his son
outside the valley to obtain an education. Then, the
“son” would return to the valley to inherit the name
and title “Knez Vlad,” as written in the treaty.

Dhampir: The daughter of the vampire, she seeks
to change the status quo. The dhampir represents
a creature trapped halfway between undeath and
humanity. An outsider who does not fit properly
within either of the two worlds, this vaesen has
conflict built into her nature. She is written as a
creature players can more easily sympathize with.
Vârcolac: A wolf-­like creature once imprisoned
beneath the vampire’s castle. A force of nature
that doesn’t care about the status quo. It was
released and used by the dhampir’s mother to
instigate conflict between the people of the
region and the vampire. This is an ancient and
obscure folklore creature, adding an unexpected,
unknown element to the story and helping evoke
the proper atmosphere of the mystery.

The locals got to live in greater peace and safety in
those centuries, crafting, farming, and shepherding,
and rarely communicated with the rest of the world.
They continued to pay tribute to their knez by regularly
providing cattle and every few years giving the knez
a young villager to serve in the castle. The vampire
knez used these servants for feeding and hid the signs
with his vampiric powers. Other than that, he never
abused these servants in any way or killed any of them.
He treated them as family and gave them a good education before releasing them some years later. Some
were granted land in the valley, others were sent away
from the valley to represent the knez in the outer world.
A few times throughout the centuries, the vampire had relations with women in the village. Very
rarely, dhampir children (page 39) were born
from these unions.
