PREPA R ATIONS
The letter includes train ticket for the first leg of the
journey, from Prague to Temesvár via Vienna and
Budapest. Before leaving Prague, the player characters
can prepare for the journey by purchasing equipment
(page 73 of the rulebook) or using Upgrades at Powder Gate (page 28 of this book). If they decide to
look for some additional information, let each player
make a LEARNING test. For each success rolled (in total),
they gain one of the following clues:
The valley is named after Bralnic Castle, built in
the 14th century to protect an important trade
route through the Carpathians. An earthquake
caused a landslide in the 15th century, which closed
the route and isolated the valley, leaving only one
mountain road.
The letter’s waxen seal refers to a story of a brave
warrior killing a horrible beast and becoming
the first Knez Vlad of the Bralnic Valley. After
being severely wounded by the beast, the knez
also defeated death and went on to rule and protect the valley.
The paper on which the letter was written has
watermarks of the French paper mill that made
paper for monarchs, mainly Louis 14th, and was
closed almost a century ago, at the start of the
French Revolution.
The Pravda Society did indeed visit the Bralnic Valley almost a century earlier, invited by Knez Vlad
the Eighth to hunt down a rogue vampire. The
vampire was eventually killed, along with one member of the Society – the young priest Karel Benes.
This time of year, weather conditions in Transylvania are dangerous, with heavy snowfalls and
low temperatures being common.

THE JOURNEY
Following the knez’s itinerary, the player characters
depart from Prague by train, passing Vienna and Budapest before reaching Temesvár. There, they are met on
the platform by a well-­dressed young man, presenting
himself as Lucian, son of Knez Vlad. He informs them

that a carriage is prepared to take them to Brașov, the
main town in Transylvania, the next morning. They may
spend the night in a comfortable hotel, already paid for.
Brașov (Kronstadt) has a population of around
25,000 and is one of the biggest cities in Romania.
Meanwhile, winter arrives in full strength, so the
journey to Brașov takes almost a week. There, another
young man greets the player characters, presenting
himself as Darius, son of Knez Vlad. He informs them
that another carriage will take them north along a
small road to Bralnic Valley, far up in the Carpathian
Mountains (not shown on the large map of Mythic
Carpathia). This final leg of the journey takes two days.
It is late December, and roads are covered in snow,
which makes traveling through the Carpathians very
difficult. For each hour that passes, the snow becomes
heavier, and any locals encountered are curious about
the Pravda Society’s purpose, as they see no reason
for outsiders to visit that isolated valley and the village located there.
You can play out the journey in detail or just handle it summarily, letting each player briefly describe
what their character does. As usual, the characters
can gain an Advantage during the journey (page 182
of the rulebook).

A RRI VA L
Alone in a rickety carriage on a dangerous mountain
road, the player characters travel through the Carpathians. The snowfall becomes heavier, before the
coachman suddenly stops in the afternoon of the
second day after setting out from Brașov. He declares
that they have arrived and asks the passengers to disembark with their baggage, before he hurriedly turns
the carriage around and leaves.
The player characters are alone at the gates to
Bralnic Valley, between the ruins of two stone watchtowers that collapsed during the earthquake centuries ago. They rise above the road and are covered in
snow. From there, the road splits in two. The right
path goes down to the valley and the village, while the
left goes up, leading to Bralnic Castle (page 81).
