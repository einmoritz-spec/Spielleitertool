3. After the hunt and the death of the two hunters, they are brought to the church for burial and
another meeting is held – this time in the church.
Eve again agitates the villagers and asks them
why no one protects them from the beast. Why has
the knez failed his duty? This sparks an argument
between Eve and Father Iacob (page 90), who
pleads for patience. The villagers are torn but
eventually agree to send delegates to Knez Vlad
with a petition.
The tone of the message depends on who wins
the argument. If Father Iacob gets the upper
hand, it will be a humble petition, asking the
ruler for protection. If Eve is more convincing,
the message will be an ultimatum, demanding
action under threat of the treaty being broken.
If player characters are present and decide to
intervene, they can sway the villagers in one way
or the other with a successful INSPIRATION test. If
the player characters are absent or choose not to
interfere, you can make an opposed INSPIRATION
roll between Eve and Father Iacob, or simply
choose an outcome as desired.
4. Led by Eve, the villagers march to the castle after
dark, carrying torches. The knez comes out to
meet the delegation. The knez claims to have the
answer to the problem with the beast: the player
characters! They are experts at this sort of thing
and will soon thwart the beast, the knez claims.
The situation is tense, and the player characters
may need to roll for INSPIRATION or MANIPULATION
to intervene to avoid violence.
During the meeting, the knez sees Eve and
immediately realizes that she’s a dhampir – and his
daughter. If questioned by the player characters,
he may reveal this fact and tell them the story about
Maria – only leaving out that he killed her parents.
5. In the night, the celebrations start, and crowds of
people dressed in wolf and bear skins dance and
sing in the streets. The vârcolac, controlled by
Maria, sneaks through the crowds, looking for
the next victim. This leads directly to the Confrontation – see page 95.

THE V ILL AGERS’ CATASTROPHE

If Eve breaks the treaty and the knez is killed and the
vârcolac’s catastrophe (below) does not occur, the
villagers cheer Eve and choose her as their new knez,
signing a new treaty, already prepared by Maria. The
villagers receive new freedoms, but Eve eventually
dies and becomes a vampire.
Unless the family of the former knez, especially
those abroad, can be convinced to support the new
knez by learning of Vlad’s past crimes, the valley lacks
the resources to maintain itself. Most people leave the
valley to look for a better life, and only a few stay, living
in a small community that can maintain itself but is destined to become extinct, leaving a hungry vampire alone.

At the beginning of the mystery, the vârcolac looks like
a pale, skinny man with thick black hair and feral eyes.
The vârcolac restores its powers bit by bit with each
killed and drained victim, becoming more bestial, until
it looks like a giant wolf. Maria’s spindle of yarn makes
the vârcolac obedient and even stronger. Information
about the vârcolac’s powers and how it is controlled
can be found within the knez’s library (page 85), or
even with a successful LEARNING check if the players are
struggling to understand what they are dealing with.
1. The first three victims the vârcolac kills before the
beginning of the mystery restore its general powers,
making its appearance something between man
and wolf. At that stage, it can communicate and
express coherent thoughts.
2. The killing of two more hunters (step #2 in the
villagers’ countdown) grants it more power (giving
it +1 to MIGHT ) but affects its personality so that it
can express only simple thoughts that are barely
understandable amidst its roaring words.
3. The sixth killing restores almost all of its powers
(another +1 to MIGHT ), leaving no place for conscience and making it a bloodthirsty predator.
