BRALNIC CASTLE
The locations in this mystery are divided between
those inside Bralnic Castle and those down in the
village (page 88).
Bralnic Castle was built five centuries ago to protect a trade route through the Southern Carpathians
and was named after the valley where it stands. The
castle was damaged after an earthquake in the 15th
century and lost its purpose after the trade route
collapsed. After the vârcolac was defeated and sealed
within the dungeon, Knez Vlad has resided in the
castle for around four centuries.
The castle itself was built in Romanesque style as
a fortification with massive walls and small windows.
After the earthquake, it underwent a few reconstructions, including those the knez made to adapt the
castle to his unlife. Some rooms were hidden, corridors redirected, and secret passages were created
that only those who lived there could navigate easily.
Only the knez knew all of the castle’s secrets.
Six of the knez’s adopted “children” (page 83)
maintain the building and protect the knez in the daytime. Despite its cold stone appearance, the castle feels
safe from the harsh winter outside. The walls in the
rooms are covered with old tapestries, and the floors
with carpets. Although it might look cozy and safe

DREAMS & VISIONS
The old walls of the castle have witnessed many
rituals and are suffused with the vampire’s
magic. At your discretion, while staying at the
castle, player characters may have dreams or
see visions of the annual village celebrations
(page 89). They can be used to add more
details to the mystery, direct or mislead characters, strengthen the atmosphere, or remind
the player characters about the countdowns.
Try to make the dreams vague or twisted
to keep the player characters intrigued and
to build atmosphere – you may use scenes of
beasts leaving houses or the knez leading torch-­
bearing cavalry descending rapidly from the
castle towards the village, for example.

INNER COURT YA RD
The player characters enter here when they arrive. The
gates are typically open. Tall walls surround the inner
courtyard, with areas designated for chopping wood,
butchering, and skinning. There are a few different
entrances into the castle: the main doors – carved,
wooden, three meters tall – and a few smaller entrances
used by the knez’s “children” that lead to storage areas,
the kitchen, and corridors within the castle. There is
also a wooden door set into the mountainside, leading
to a root cellar and the dungeon (page 87).
