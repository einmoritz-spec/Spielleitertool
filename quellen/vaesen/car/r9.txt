MYTHIC CARPATHIA
Welcome to Mythic Carpathia – a Vaesen version
of Central and Eastern Europe. In the 19th century, this region was torn by wars and dominated
by three powerful empires – Austro-­Hungary, the
Kingdom of Prussia and the Russian Empire. They
kept a tight grip on the many nations under their
rule. Among those nations were countless people
from various ethnic groups who had lived on these
territories for centuries. They knew what strange
creatures populated the night, and they learned,
often the hard way, to live with them.
On these pages, you will find historical information about the Slavs and other peoples in the
region. You will also learn about one of the most
important cities of that time, the wonderful Prague,
where the Pravda Society – the local branch of the
Society – has its headquarters. From there, brave
men and women travel to distant regions of Mythic
Carpathia to help people and find ways to deal with
the strange creatures of these expansive lands. Some
vaesen are native to this region, some came with new
settlers, and some grew out of the beliefs of religious people. It’s a vast and strange territory, full
of dangers and wonders.
As always in the Vaesen roleplaying game, you should
adjust technology and history to your style of play and
chosen time. Pick whatever you need and find useful,
and leave the rest behind. Travel with us to Prague,
Transylvania, and Ukraine, and then explore even
further. Mythic Carpathia hides many secrets waiting to be uncovered.

A QUEST FOR FREEDOM
The 19th century was not a good time for the Slavic and
Baltic peoples. Most of them lived in states that limited
or completely removed their autonomy. The situation was very complex and changed with the evolving
political situation in Europe. Some nations had lost
their statehood in the previous century, when powerful empires formed, while others only just started to
seek recognition of their right to self-­determination.

RUSSI A N EMPIRE
Virtually all East Slavs and Baltic peoples were under
the rule of the Tsars of Russia – the Romanovs. The
Tsar’s subjects included Poles, Ukrainians, Belarusians, Lithuanians, Latvians, and Estonians. In
addition to these, there were citizens of many other
nations. The Tsar conducted a program of intensive
Russification – publication in national languages,
education in the mother tongue, and religious practices other than Orthodoxy were made much more
difficult or, in time, impossible.
