CELEBRATIONS

·

Every year on the day before Christmas, Bralnic
Valley celebrates the beast’s defeat, the signing of
the treaty by Knez Vlad the First, and his miraculous recovery the next day. It is another element of
religion twisted by the knez to sanctify his authority.
This year, celebrations start the evening after the
player characters’ arrival at the village.
The valley’s residents prepare for the celebration weeks in advance. They clean houses, build a
fairground on the main square, and decorate everything with red ribbons that symbolize the blood
spilled by the first knez while protecting them. They
also make a giant scarecrow of the wolf-­like beast
out of hay and wooden sticks to burn at the end of
celebrations.
Another essential element of the festival is the
night beast dances. People of the valley choose the
best pelts they have, usually those from wolves or
bears, craft terrifying heads for them, and make
beast costumes. They also twist red cords and bind
them in tassels that they adorn with small bronze
bells, to decorate their costumes with.
When the first star appears in the night sky, all
the villagers dressed in beast costumes leave their
homes singing and dancing, making as much noise
with their bells as possible. This colorful and loud
mass flows to the main square, where they celebrate, dance, eat, and drink all night. Thus, they
are scaring evil spirits that come during the longest
night to steal and eat the stars and the moon. The
noise of bells, drums, and music must never stop so
the spirits cannot sneak into the village during a
moment of silence. People usually sing and show off

their best dances on stage with families or groups
of friends, participating in a few performances that
night. When the first rays of the sun hit the peaks of
the mountains, people bring out the beast-­shaped
scarecrow to the center of the square, just as the
church bells start to ring.
The knez and his family, dressed in ancient ceremonial armor, descend from the castle to the village
mounted on horses and carrying lit torches. While
the cavalry moves to the main square, the villagers
make as much noise as possible to greet the knez and
his family. Still mounted, the knez makes three circles
around the scarecrow and then sets it on fire. While
it is burning, the villagers dance and celebrate the
knez’s victory. He dismounts and walks among people,
greeting them and giving them presents.
Only after the flames consume the scarecrow do
people remove their masks and see with whom they
have been dancing all night. After the church bell
rings for the last time that morning, Father Iacob
(page 90) starts a service out in the open, in the
light of the sunrise, celebrating the victory of Light
over Darkness, praising the brave people of the
valley and especially Knez Vlad, who defeated the
beast. The knez remains present until the end of the
service despite the daytime starting, since he is an
old, well-­fed vampire and can tolerate such minor
inconveniences.
Throughout the mystery, the player characters
may observe or even participate in preparations for
the celebration. It is unlikely that celebrations will
happen as planned because of the events in both
