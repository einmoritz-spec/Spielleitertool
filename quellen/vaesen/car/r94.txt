THE TAV ERN

FATHER I ACOB
“God sends us these difficult trials to remind us that we
should be grateful and not take anything for granted.”
Father Iacob is a thick-­set man in his forties
with a long, pointy black beard, black priest
robes, a black Romanian skufia (clerical cap)
on his head, and a silver crescent on his chest.
He looks calm and wise, as is often required
for a priest, but he is depressed because of the
killings. He believes that the traveling theater has brought more unrest than joy. His
deep and powerful voice sounds impressive
during the church service.
Iacob belongs to the knez’s family – he
lived in the castle in his youth, was sent to a
European seminary, and then replaced the
former priest in the village. Because of this,
Father Iacob is loyal to the knez and believes
in him and in the treaty as much as he believes
in God. He often refers to the knez and the
treaty during his sermons, sharing his faith
with the villagers.
If Father Iacob learns of the knez’s past
crimes against Maria and her parents, he will
be bitterly disappointed and demand that
the knez be banished from the valley forever.

OBSERVATION

The local tavern called “The Last Warrior” is a circular, well-­insulated wooden building that stands
opposite the church on the main square. It is named
after the local legend who received the title of Knez
Vlad for defeating the beast and protecting the valley.
The tavern has one big, rounded hall, a kitchen on
the opposite side of the entrance, and wooden stairs
on the perimeter that lead to a few rooms for guests
on the second floor.
The Last Warrior, with its big fireplace in the
middle, delicious and nourishing food, and homemade drinks, is a favorite place for the villagers, especially hunters, who gather here to warm up and share
rumors and stories from their hunts. Its keeper, Radu
Harnicu, is also preparing for the celebration and
decorating the tavern with tree branches and red ribbons under the ceiling. The kitchen works without
breaks, preparing meals for the celebrations.
