AFTERMATH
The death of August T. Lysander marks the end of
this mystery and this campaign. The Neck withdraws
from Stockholm, for now, and a temporary calm settles over the city. The poet Erik Johan Stagnelius is
never seen again at the Golden Peace.
Although the unrest in the streets was stirred by
the Neck, the calls for justice, equality and better
working conditions persist – a struggle that will shape
society for decades to come. One day, many years in
the future, universal and equal suffrage will be introduced in the country, including for women. But that
is another story.
Now there is little left for the characters to do but
lick their wounds and return to Upsala, hopefully
having learned a great deal – perhaps after saying
goodbye to friends and acquaintances old and new,
such as Commissioner Utterson, archivist Sigrid
Holgersdotter, and painter Vilma af Zimt. Reward
the characters with plenty of Experience Points and
Development Points for their headquarters – they
have certainly earned it!
But one question remains – what should be done
with the ancient and powerful artifact that is the
Neck’s lyre? Perhaps the safest course is to bring it
to Castle Gyllencreutz in Upsala, where the Society
can keep it safe. But the Neck has not forgotten, nor
forgiven, and still dreams of one day reclaiming the
beautiful lyre he lost so long ago.
And Linnaeus’s ghost? He could make a final
appearance to offer the characters a reward for their
great efforts: the Botanical Garden upgrade for their

headquarters (page 91 of the core rulebook), without
the prerequisite of a Gardener. Read or play out the
following epilogue.

EPILOGUE
It is night, and you have just returned to Cas­
tle Gyllencreutz after a philosophical soiree
at Upsala University. Moonlight shines softly
on the wall surrounding the garden. The air is
still. Suddenly a figure appears in front of you
– it is the ghost of Carl Linnaeus. He leans into
the moonlight.
“How wonderful to be back in my beloved
Upsala. Now that my library is in your safe
hands, I’m no longer bound to the capital. I
still have work to do – so many species left to
discover, understand, classify, and name in this
universe. Both magical and mundane. By the
way, did you know that my very first garden
once grew here?”
He points to a door covered with thick ivy.
There is a large rusty key in the lock. The old door
creaks open to reveal a hidden garden untouched
by time. You see neat beds of herbs and medicinal
plants, all marked with handwritten signs. Some
of the plants can heal your wounds.
“You’ll find my flora in the stone chest in
the center of the garden. This is my gift to you.
Thank you, truly, for helping me protect the
knowledge of the unseen world!”
