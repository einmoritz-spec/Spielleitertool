In this fourth and final part of the campaign, the
story comes together around August T. Lysander.
The player characters are once again summoned to
the increasingly turbulent capital, this time by an
unexpected new acquaintance who brings them into
contact with the ghost of Carl Linnaeus. The ghost
knows that someone is after the lyre of the Neck, which
has been in royal possession for over a century.
Through their investigations, the characters eventually realize that their friend and patron Lysander
intends to steal the lyre from the Royal Palace and
return it to the Neck, hoping that this powerful vaesen
will help break the spertus’ curse and save his soul
from eternal damnation.
It becomes clear to the characters that with this
powerful artifact, the Neck would unleash unprecedented chaos and bloodshed in the city and truly turn
Stockholm into a city of nightmares. Lysander must
be stopped. But can the cork magnate escape his fate?

STOCKHOLM
City of My Nightmares takes place in Stockholm during a
time of growing unrest in the Swedish capital. Revolutionary sentiment simmers beneath the surface,
challenging the king’s authority. In the city’s bustling
streets and markets, people are whispering of a different
Sweden. Lately, the unrest has descended into violence,
with fights breaking out in taverns and public squares,

PLAYING IN A DIFFERENT CITY?
This campaign is set in Stockholm, but with a
little bit of effort it can be adapted to any other
major European city such as London or Prague,
as described in the Mythic Britain & Ireland
and Mythic Carpathia expansions, respectively.

KEY LOCATIONS
The Train Station: Stockholm’s newly opened Central Station is a symbol of development and progress,
built to meet the capital’s need for efficient transportation. The station serves as a hub for Sweden’s
rapidly growing railway network, bringing together
travelers and goods from all corners of the country.
The Royal Palace: This magnificent baroque palace
was designed in the style of a Roman palace by Nicodemus Tessin the Younger. It has seven floors and over
six hundred rooms, including the royal residence,
state rooms, the treasury, and a historical armory.
Shipside Street: This street along the quay facing the
Salt Sea is a key location for the city’s shipping. Many
sailing ships dock here to load and unload cargo from
near and far. The street is lined with customs houses,
warehouses, and magnificent office buildings.
The Police Headquarters: The newly constructed
police station is located in the northern part of the
City Between the Bridges, overlooking the Stockholm
Stream. The sturdy stone building houses a courtroom, a 100-cell detention center, and a morgue, in
addition to staff quarters for the police force.
Fire Mill: Located at Knights’ Bay in central Stockholm, this large steam-­powered mill is used to grind
large quantities of grain and is essential to the city’s
food supply. The mill is viewed as a technological marvel but has recently become a stronghold for workers
fighting for better working conditions.
White Hills: A poor rural area in the eastern part of
Southside in Stockholm, characterized by steep paths
and small shacks. In this rundown shantytown, workers, craftsmen, servants, and paupers live in cramped
conditions but share a strong sense of community.
