The Seraphim Hospital: Stockholm’s main hospital and the first of its kind in Sweden. It receives
patients from across the capital, including the poor
who cannot afford home care. It also trains doctors
and acts as a center for medical research.
The Royal Swedish Opera: A grand opera house that
serves as the heart of the city’s opera scene. Its majestic
building on Gustav Adolf’s Square is adorned with
elegant architectural details. Here, prominent opera
singers and musicians perform both classical and
contemporary works for the capital’s elite.
Berns’ Salon: This newly built entertainment venue
in Berzelii Park has quickly become a hub for music,
theater, and social life. The lavish interior with chandeliers, mirrors, and ornaments reflects its continental influences. Both Swedish and foreign artists
perform here, and the salon attracts bourgeoisie
and cultural figures alike. Its decadent Red Room is
a favored haunt of the country’s most famous author,
August Strindberg.
The City Gazette: A politically conservative daily
newspaper aimed at an educated middle-­class readership. It covers current events in politics, culture,
business, and social affairs. With its polished language
and balanced reporting, it is regarded by many as a
respected voice in public discourse.
The Evening Post: This liberal and popular daily
newspaper is known for defending freedom of the
press. It supports the opposition to the monarchy and
is a thorn in the side of those in power. The Evening
Post has had its publishing license revoked several
times but has always returned.

NOTA BLE RESIDENTS
August Strindberg: A writer and painter who has
become one of the country’s most famous cultural
figures. His personal life is tumultuous, marked
by inner conflict and constant clashes with society.
Strindberg can often be found in the Red Room at
the Berns’ Salon, discussing art and politics with
other radical bohemians.
Jenny Lind: A world-­famous opera singer known
as the “Swedish Nightingale.” She was appointed
court singer after her sensational debut at the Royal
Swedish Opera, and her career has taken her far
beyond the borders of Sweden. Her clear voice now
captivates audiences throughout Europe and the
United States.
Erik Johan Stagnelius: A poet and playwright celebrated for his romantic and mystical poetry, but also
known for his excessive use of alcohol and opium.
Stagnelius can often be found at the Golden Peace
tavern in the City Between the Bridges, where he
rents a room on an indefinite basis.
Vilma af Zimt: A young artist and spiritualist known
for her symbolist paintings. Besides her painting,
she has a strong interest in the occult and esoteric.
She has made a name for herself in Stockholm’s
bohemian circles by hosting séances and tarot
readings.
Fredrika Bremer: A pioneer of the Swedish suffragette movement and a groundbreaking author of
international success. Fredrika Bremer is known for
her novels in which she depicts the lives and societal
roles of women and advocates for women’s rights and
personal freedom.

The National Archives: One of Sweden’s oldest
public institutions, tasked with preserving and
maintaining the nation’s most important records
and documents. Its impressive new building on
Knights’ Isle attracts researchers, scholars, and
officials alike.

Alfred Nobel: Inventor, chemist, and entrepreneur
known for his studies of explosives, particularly nitroglycerine. Alfred Nobel has recently returned from
years of exile and financial trouble in Russia and has
now re-­established himself in Stockholm.
