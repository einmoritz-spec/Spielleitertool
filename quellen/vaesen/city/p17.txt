PREPA R ATIONS
As usual, the player characters can prepare for the
journey in Upsala by purchasing equipment (page 73
of the rulebook) or using Upgrades at Castle Gyllencreutz (page 89 of the rulebook). They can look for
information about Lysander and, with a successful
LEARNING roll, find out about his career as a businessman and that he is a member of the Freemasons
as well as several spiritualist societies in Stockholm
(peripheral clue). They find no information about
the murders beyond what is written in the newspapers (see The Journey below).

At the time of this mystery, the Stockholm Police
Department has recently been reorganized. The
capital’s previous law enforcement organization, the
City Watch, had failed to curb the riots that raged
a few years earlier. Crimes remained unsolved and
thefts increased dramatically. The press openly criti­
cized the City Watch, claiming that its officials were
themselves committing criminal acts.
In response to this criticism, a new civilian police
force was established. Stockholm was divided into three
districts, each with its own police station: Northside,
Southside, and the Town Between the Bridges.
To become a police officer, a recruit needs to be at
least 5 feet 7 inches tall, 24–35 years old, and literate.
It also requires “sound judgment”, a calm disposition,
good health, and a strong physique. A new uniform
has been introduced for the police force, consisting
of a top hat and a double-­breasted long coat, and
officers are armed with a truncheon and saber.

THE JOURNEY
The player characters will likely take the newly opened
steam train from Upsala Central Station to Stockholm.
During the journey they receive today’s issue of the
City Gazette – see handout 1C included as a separate
print in this book, and available for download on the
Free League website. The newspaper contains many
stories of interest to the players. The articles about
von Essen’s murder are a central clue. As usual the
characters can gain an Advantage during the journey
(page 182 of the rulebook).
