POLICE HEA DQUA RTERS
The newly built police headquarters is located in the
northern part of the Town Between the Bridges, overlooking the Stream of Stockholm. The sturdy stone
building has four floors and, in addition to offices
for the police force, houses a detention center complete with a hundred cells and a morgue where Ester
Volk works. The third floor is where Commissioner
Utterson has his office. All visitors must report to
the doorman before they are escorted through the
narrow corridors and the throng of victims, thieves,
constables, vagrants, detectives, and judges.

Previous Attacks: Utterson has a list of times and
locations of the murders – see handout 1D on page
107, also available for download on the Free
League website. No names are listed – the police
didn’t bother as all of the victims were criminals.
Perceptive players may notice that a month passed
between each murder. At the National Archives
(page 31) – or by consulting a calendar – the
characters can learn that all murders occurred
around the time of a full moon.
Insider? If pressed (MANIPULATION ), Utterson
admits that he suspects someone in the justice
system may be guilty, as the victims were all criminals. In fact, Sverker von Essen was a suspect in
the death of conductor Mortimer Cronstedt a few
weeks before his own demise. Cronstedt, who also
worked at the Opera, died after eating turtle soup
at the Golden Peace (page 22). There were
rumors of a feud between von Essen and Cronstedt, but no evidence could be found.
Unrest: As if the murders were not enough for
Utterson to deal with, the streets are rife with
insurgents seeking to overthrow the royal family,
inspired by the revolutions on the continent. The
new police force must prove that it can maintain
law and order – people must not be allowed to
take the law into their own hands.
The Sickness: If the characters ask about the disease spreading through the slums, Utterson or
Ester Volk can inform them that several people
in the While Hills slums in Southside have died
of severe infections in recent weeks. They had all
had an arm or leg amputated and some of them
had had the lost limb replaced by a body part from
a corpse. The police’s interrogations have led
nowhere, as mistrust is high in the slums.
