THE GOLDEN PEACE
The tavern and hotel of the Golden Peace is located
in a centuries-­old stone building on East Longstreet, a stone’s throw from the main water pump
in Iron Square.

COMMISSONER
GA BRIEL UTTERSON
“Man is a wolf to man.”
Gabriel Utterson is a 40-year-­old man with
sharp hazel eyes, strong features, and a stern
look on his face. After many years on the
streets, his eyes are constantly searching for
signs of trouble or danger, his hand often
fiddling with the handle of his truncheon or
the hilt of his saber.
He was recently promoted to police commissioner, but as a proud member of the first
generation of the new police force, he wears
the official uniform of top hat and double-­
breasted longcoat. He takes very seriously
the task of fighting crime in his district – the
Town Between the Bridges.
With his promotion, Utterson was welcomed into the city’s elite circles, and for the
past couple of years he has been a member of the
same Masonic order as August T. Lysander. He
does not share the Society’s belief in the supernatural and is convinced that the murderer is
human. But now he is at his wits’ end and has
reluctantly accepted Lysander’s offer of help.

The Tavern: The ground floor and basement of the
building houses the old tavern, commonly known
as the Peace Cellar. Its clientele includes aristocrats
and customs officials from Shipside Street as well as
poets and composers. The dining room with windows
facing East Longstreet is hung with beautiful lamps.
Ceramic stoves and tealights provide a lovely warmth
in the autumn chill. Guests are sitting in armchairs
and at tables set with white tablecloths.
The Hotel: A crooked wooden staircase leads to the
second floor which holds a number of simple guest
rooms. Here August Lysander has booked accommodation for the player characters in separate little rooms, drafty but clean. The ceiling is painted
with medieval patterns that are visible between the
thick rafters.

A SHADOW IN THE NIGHT
In order to ramp up the pace and suspense of
the adventure, you can give the player char­
acters a glimpse of the werewolf. Have them
make a VIGILANCE roll – those who succeed
feel like they are being watched and notice a
dark and menacing shadow in an alley. If they
go after it, they can make an AGILITY roll to
catch a glimpse of a shaggy creature. However,
they cannot confront the werewolf during such
encounters, so use them sparingly.
