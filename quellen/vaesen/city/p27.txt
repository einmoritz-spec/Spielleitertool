CLUES

The Feud: The emotional Stagnelius says he was
a close friend of the late Sverker von Essen, “who
was a true artist and always smelled pleasantly of lavender.”
He claims the singer had a feud with Mortimer
Cronstedt, conductor of the Opera, and that
von Essen’s eyes sometimes went dark when he
talked about Cronstedt’s plans for the spring
repertoire.
In response to a direct question, Stagnelius
admits that von Essen was at the tavern the night
Cron­stedt dropped dead in his turtle soup.
Laudanum: If the characters ask Stagnelius about
his bottle of drops, he explains that he takes laudanum for his nerves. Anyone who passes a MEDICINE roll knows that laudanum is deadly in high
doses (which Volk can confirm).
If the characters ask Stagnelius if von Essen
may have taken some of his laudanum, it takes a
successful MANIPULATION roll for him to admit that
this is the case. On failure, Stagnelius angrily
accuses them of “smearing his dead friend.”

ERIK JOH A N STAGNELIUS
“Putrefaction! Hasten, oh beloved bride! To ready
our lonely lover’s couch! By the world rejected,
by God set aside. Thou art my only hope, I vouch.”
The poet Erik Johan Stagnelius is renting a
room at the Golden Peace for an indefinite
period. He is highly educated and speaks
quickly and excitedly on all sorts of topics. In
the evenings he entertains the hotel guests by
reciting poems he has written, gesticulating
animatedly until he is so out of breath that
he needs to sit down for a while. A recurring
theme in Stagnelius’s poems is alienation,
his dissatisfaction with life, and his longing
for death. He writes about yearning for a
love that is only realized in the imagination.
Stagnelius is a short man with blonde hair
and a pointed nose. His head looks disproportionately heavy on his thin body and is
always tilted to one side. He is prone to mood
swings and can suddenly become introverted
and melancholic. He is constantly plagued
by his poor health.
