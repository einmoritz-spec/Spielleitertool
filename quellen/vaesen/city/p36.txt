SA INT GERTRUDE’S CHURCH
This church, also known as the German Church, is one
of the tallest buildings in Stockholm. It got its current
look in the 17th century but dates back to the 1300s. The
church is dedicated to Saint Gertrude, the patron saint
of sailors. Next to the church is a small parish house.
The player characters have no reason to come here
until after step 1 of the Countdown (page 16), when
church organist Friedrich Baum is murdered. They
are assumed to arrive here shortly thereafter. Should
they visit this location before the murder, there are
no clues to be found.

CH A LLENGES

The Choir: Frightened members of the church choir
have gathered outside the church. It takes a MANIPULATION roll to get any information out of them (see
Clues). Characters who pass a VIGILANCE roll notice
that one of the church windows has been smashed
and shards of stained glass litter the streets outside.
Police: A police constable is guarding the door. If
the characters are accompanied by another constable or announce that they are working for Commissioner Utterson, they are immediately allowed
to enter. Otherwise, it takes a MANIPULATION roll to
be granted entry or a STEALTH roll to sneak inside.
The Victim: In the staircase leading up to the
gilded king’s gallery lies a mutilated body, face
down. The stairs are covered in blood that has run
down onto the floor. The characters can examine
the body with MEDICINE and the crime scene with
INVESTIGATION (see Clues).
The Priest: Inside the church is the priest Rudolf
Schiller. He is in shock but can talk to the characters if they first calm him down with a successful
INSPIRATION roll. If the characters were called here
by the police, there is also a constable present, but
he has not been able to get a word out of the priest.

CLUES

The Injuries: Anyone who examines the body
with MEDICINE will find that the chest and abdomen have been slashed open and the intestines

torn out. Several internal organs appear to be
missing, including the heart.
The Notebook: With a successful INVESTIGATION
roll, the characters find a small notebook on the
stairs. It seems to have fallen out of the organist’s
pocket. The book contains detailed facial portraits of young women drawn in charcoal. Some
of the women look lifeless. This is a central clue
– if the characters do not find the notebook, an
NPC should.
The Woman: If the player characters have visited the police morgue (page 18), they note that
one of the drawings in the notebook bears a striking resemblance to the woman examined by the
coroner (handout 1E, on page 107, also available
for download on the Free League website). If the
characters have not been to the morgue, Utterson
makes the connection when he sees the notebook.
The Window: A church window has been shattered with great force. All the broken glass is on
the outside, none on the inside. Characters who
pass another INVESTIGATION roll notice a small tuft
of gray fur, sticky with blood, on one of the sharp
edges of jagged glass.
The Organist: The priest Rudolf Schiller can tell
them that the dead body is that of the church organist Friedrich Baum. He was found by members of
the church choir who came here for a rehearsal of
Mozart’s Requiem. No one saw or heard the actual
killing or the escape of the murderer.
Baum’s Room: The priest says that Baum came to
the parish a few years ago and studied organ at the
Royal Academy of Music. He was quiet and kept to
himself. If asked, the priest adds that Baum lived
in a room in the parish house and offers to take
the characters there.
Trophies: Baum’s room is simply furnished.
Under the bed there is a loose floorboard (can
be found if the players look in that exact spot or
pass an INVESTIGATION roll). In a small space under
the floorboard is a box containing five locks of
hair, tied together with silk ribbons. Baum has
taken them from his victims.
