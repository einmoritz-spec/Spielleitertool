BACKGROUND
Seven years ago, Vesper Stiernkors met Lucius
Lysander, son of August, at a Royal Palace ball. It
was love at first sight – for her. Lucius’s father, August,
saw an opportunity to establish himself in Stock­
holm’s high society by marrying his son to a baroness.
At his request, Lucius courted Vesper and the couple
began a romance.
Vesper fell out of favor with her family when she
told them that she was engaged to the nouveau riche
Lucius. The Stiernkors family is one of the oldest
noble families in Sweden, dating back to the 15th century, and they were not pleased that Vesper intended
to marry a vulgar upstart. She was disowned by her
father but was allowed to keep her allowance.
Lucius, who was actually in love with the maid
Hedwig, regretted his proposal but could not back out
of the wedding, as it would bring dishonor to both
Vesper and the Lysander family. Only Vesper’s sister,
the writer and women’s rights activist Sophie Stiernkors, attended the wedding. The ceremony took place
on Big Goose Island in the Stock­holm Archipelago,
where the Lysanders have their summer residence.
On their wedding night, Lucius confessed that
he loved Hedwig and that she was pregnant. Vesper was devastated and asked Lucius to annul their
marriage. She could not bear the thought of Lucius
loving another woman and having a child with her.

Heartbroken, Vesper ran off into the night to Little
Goose Island, a small islet connected to the main island
by a low embankment. Lucius followed and tried to
placate her – a divorce so soon after the wedding would
not go down well with the Stock­holm elite his family
had fought so hard to become part of. Vesper would
not budge, and in desperation Lucius drowned her. He
tied stones to her feet and lowered her body into the sea.
In the morning, Lucius told the wedding guests
that Vesper had disappeared after the wedding feast.
He said that he had searched the entire island and
found her on Little Goose Island. He claimed that
she had waded straight into the water and that he
arrived too late, just as she disappeared below the
surface. Vesper’s body was never recovered.
In the years after the wedding, people in the area
began to talk about a female figure being seen in the
waters around Goose Island. Fishermen claimed to have
noticed a pale face staring at them from under the water.
One day a young man mysteriously drowned in calm
waters near the island. He was the first of several male
youths to perish in the dark waters off Goose Island.
The truth is that after her violent death, Vesper
turned into a rusalka – an undead female water spirit
found mainly in Eastern Europe. The rusalka resembles the lake wives of the North, but is driven by a
furious, insatiable desire for revenge. This creature
is a new addition to the game and is described in more
detail on page 60.
Over the years, Lucius has grown increasingly
tormented by Vesper’s death, and the subsequent
drownings have led him to suspect that he has been
cursed. He now attends his father’s conclave with keen
interest as he seeks a way to lift the curse.
Fellow participant Florian Schiller has known
Lucius since their student days. Early in the mystery
he links the deaths to something supernatural and
suspects it has something to do with the Lysander
family. He finds his old friend changed, his countenance grim and tense.
