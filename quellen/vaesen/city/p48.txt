HEDW IG LYSA NDER
“The Lord is my strength and song.”
Hedwig Lysander has long, blonde,
pinned-­up hair and kind blue eyes. She
wears a light summer dress and pearls around
her neck. She is well-­liked by the servants
because she treats them well, unlike many
other upper-­class families they have worked
for. She is also proof that even someone from
humble origins can succeed in life.
Hedwig came to live with the Lysanders
when she was 17, after her parents died of a
fever. She was literate and good at sewing,
so her aunt arranged for her to be a maid in
Stock­holm instead of working at the spinning
mills of Norrköping where her aunt lived.
Hedwig and Lucius got to know each other
after his mother died. It was love at first sight,
and they corresponded frequently while he
was studying in Leipzig. When he returned
home after four years at university, they professed their love for each other. They asked
for permission to marry, but August refused,
determined that his son would marry into a
fine Stock­holm family.
Things suddenly changed when Vesper died
and it became known that Hedwig was pregnant.
August saw no alternative but to let them marry.
Hedwig does not know that Lucius murdered
Vesper, but feels guilty as she suspects that Vesper drowned herself because of her relationship with Lucius. This guilt is exacerbated by
Hedwig’s deep faith, and she visits the chapel
(page 56) every day to ask for forgiveness.
Hedwig’s son Alexander is the apple of
her eye. Because of the drownings in recent
years, she is terrified that the boy will go too
close to the water.

LUCIUS LYSA NDER
“Family honor above all.”
Lucius Lysander is just over 30 years old and
works as a lawyer in Stock­holm. He has fair hair
with long bangs, blue eyes, and clean-­shaven
cheeks. Lucius wants nothing more than to
please his father and for their newly rich family
to be accepted into Stock­holm’s high society.
August sent Lucius to the University of
Leipzig for a classical education. During his
studies, he became close friends with Florian
Schiller as they were members of the same student organization, the Korps Saxonia Leipzig.
When August urged Lucius to court Vesper Stiernkors, he naturally did his duty to
the family. But he was burdened with guilt
toward Hedwig, whom he had known and
loved since they were teens. When Vesper
threatened to divorce him, Lucius snapped.
He barely remembers what happened and is
deeply troubled by his unpremeditated murder of Vesper, but he is determined to keep
it together so as not to disgrace the family
name. He does long sessions of gymnastics
in the mornings to alleviate his anxiety.
