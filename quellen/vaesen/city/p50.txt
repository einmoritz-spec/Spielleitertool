CATASTROPHE

If the player characters fail to uncover Lucius Lysander’s secret and banish the rusalka, it becomes increasingly aggressive and attacks anyone who goes near the
island’s shores. Boats approaching or attempting
to leave the island are inexplicably wrecked, killing
everyone on board. The only exception is August
Lysander’s steamer, since he is protected by his spertus (see the Leaving the Island? sidebar). In the end there
are no living people left on the islet. The beautiful
Goose Island becomes a deserted island that the locals
will avoid for decades to come, and the stunning villa
deteriorates into an eerie haunted house.

LEAVING THE ISLAND?
It is impossible to leave Goose Island during
the mystery, as the rusalka attacks anyone who
tries. Any boat that leaves the island capsizes in
a sudden gust of wind and everyone on board
ends up in the water, with one or more passen­
gers falling victim to the rusalka. The only per­
son who can actually leave the island is August
Lysander (and those in his company), who is
protected by his spertus (page 6). However,
he refuses to leave before the Catastrophe
occurs (if it does), as he is determined to stay
and defend his island.

“To prevent a child from accidentally wandering into
the realm of the fairies, spin a woolen thread soaked
in mint or tobacco and tie it around the child’s wrist.”
Nuala Fitzgerald is a 45-year-­old woman with
curly dark brown hair and a searching gaze.
She wears simple, practical clothes. Nuala
lives in Vermont, USA, where her sister runs
an orchard supplying fruit to nearby factories. Their harvests are always more plentiful than others in the area, and the fruit is
of the highest quality.
Nuala has roots in Ireland and her family has been making deals with the fairies
on their land for generations. They have
developed a language of little rituals through
which favors can be requested in exchange for
certain items. The rituals are passed down
from generation to generation. Nuala has
gathered knowledge about the rituals from
her own family as well as other families in the
area around her ancestral home, and now
travels around Europe giving talks about her
book Deals with Fairies and Little People – a Handbook.
Nuala sees the rituals as a language
through which humans can communicate
with vaesen without meeting them and speaking to them directly, which would be very
dangerous. Fairies and little people have their
own view of the world and what constitutes
a fair trade. They also have their own way of
interpreting what is said, and a single word
out of place can have terrible consequences.
