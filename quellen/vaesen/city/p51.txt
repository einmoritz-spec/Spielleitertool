THE TALE OF FAUST
The legend of Faust dates back to the Middle
Ages. Popularized by Christopher Marlowe
and others in the 17th century, it was one of the
first depictions of a pact with the Devil. Johann
Wolfgang von Goethe’s play was inspired by
Marlowe’s work and consists of two parts. The
first part is a theatrical classic and has also
been made into an opera.
It tells the story of Dr. Heinrich Faust, a
philosopher and metaphysician in search of
truth and knowledge, who has now grown old
and disillusioned. In desperation, Faust makes
a pact with the Devil, Mephistopheles, who
promises him boundless knowledge and plea­
sures in exchange for his soul. Faust experiences
both great success and personal disaster, and
his relationships, especially with the innocent
Gretchen, lead to tragic consequences.
The second part of Faust, published in the
year of Goethe’s death, focuses on the meta­
physical and mythological planes. In the first
verses of the book, Faust wakes in a field where
fairies dance in a circle. The work depicts a
new cycle of supernatural adventures in which
Faust encounters divine beings.
The final verses refer to rituals described in
the Mysteries of Isis. These rites were believed
to guarantee that the initiate’s soul would be
transported to a blissful afterlife with the help
of the goddess. In the ritual, the initiate under­
goes an elaborate purification process before
descending into the innermost part of the Tem­
ple of Isis, where he or she goes through a
symbolic death and rebirth and has an intense
religious experience, seeing the gods in person.

FLORI A N SCHILLER
“Salvation lies in the pursuit of transcendence
– that is the crux of the matter.”
Florian Schiller is a poet and literary historian who studied with Lucius Lysander at
Leipzig University. Florian has curly dark
hair and intense light gray eyes in a handsome
face. He is thin and wears the latest fashion.
His purple leather-­bound notebook, full of
jotted-­down thoughts and ideas for poems,
is always resting on his lap.
Florian has been a close friend of Lucius
Lysander since they were students. He has a
deep interest in the supernatural and spiritual. The primary focus of Florian’s studies
has been stories about Dr. Faust, who was said
to have sold his soul to the Devil. The German writer Goethe drew inspiration from
the story when he wrote his play Faust (see
adjacent sidebar).
