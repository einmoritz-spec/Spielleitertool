RUSALKA
The first time I met a rusalka was in the area around the Shatsky Lakes. It was a hot
summer day and I saw a girl in a simple white dress sitting by the lake, combing her long
green hair. As I approached to greet her, she turned to me. Suddenly I realized she wasn’t
human—she had bluish skin and her eyes blazed with green fire. I reached frantically for my
garlic, which I always carried with me on my travels in Eastern Europe. But the woman just
smiled at me, jumped gracefully into the lake, and disappeared without a trace.

—Antoine Augustin Calmet, July 1746

A rusalka is a Slavic undead water nymph that appears
when young women die in or near water – sometimes
murdered and left in the water, but more often they
drowned themselves. Another theory is that they are
the souls of unbaptized children.
Rusalki are young and beautiful, with pale blue skin
and large black or green eyes. Their green, knee-­length
hair is always down. They like to keep it wet and combed,
decorated with wreaths made of rushes or weeping willow twigs. Their feet are webbed, like those of a duck,
which allows them to recognize their tracks if they are
scared and do not have time to hide them. They are usually naked, but sometimes they wear long white shirts.
Rusalki can also appear as the drowned, fish-­eaten
corpses they really are. They do not remember their
names and have no memories of their lives as mortals.
Rusalki sleep through the winter and awaken in the
spring. They usually cannot leave the water. Rusalki
contribute to the fertility of nearby fields, protecting
them from flooding and bringing rain when needed.
But they can also do serious harm by drowning people
or tickling them to death, tearing fishing nets, and
overturning fishing boats.
Rusalki are described in more detail in the Mythic
Carpathia expansion for the game.
