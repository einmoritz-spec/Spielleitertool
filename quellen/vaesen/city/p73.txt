THE CELL A R VAULTS
Behind a low wooden door on the ground floor of
the National Archives, an ancient stone staircase spirals down into the deep vaults beneath the building.
It is pitch black and the player characters will need
a light source. Sigrid has a lantern, but is reluctant
to lend it to them unless she has already given them
permission to enter the tunnels.
CH A LLENGES

The cellar vaults are dark and cold with water dripping from the walls. The player characters’ lantern
only lets them see a few meters ahead. The map found
in the Esoteric Section provides an overview of the
tunnels, and the GM version of the map (previous
page) shows a possible route through the maze. There
are several ways for the characters to find the correct
path to the Chamber of Riddles.
The Map: The characters can use the map from
the Esoteric Section.
The Symbols: At each inter­section, a small ouroboros in a triquetra – the Society’s own symbol – is
carved into the wall at the correct entrance. These
can be discovered with an INVESTIGATION test if the
characters examine the walls of the vaults. Once
they have found a symbol, they can easily notice
more of them and find the correct path.
Trial and Error: The player characters can wander aimlessly through the maze until they stumble

·

upon the correct path. However, note that there
are several tunnels leading out of the map. If the
characters follow one of them, after a long walk
through narrow passageways, they will suddenly
emerge through a door in the Knights’ Isle Church
or in an alley in the Town Between the Bridges.
Hauntings: The characters should experience
one or more hauntings (see Countdown on page
65) on their way through the vaults.

THE CH A MBER OF RIDDLES
If the player characters find the correct path through
the labyrinthine passages beneath the National
Archives, they eventually reach a large circular chamber with a high, vaulted stone ceiling. On the walls
are gilded candelabras with candles. If the characters
light the candles, they see the room in all its splendor.
The ceiling is engraved with Latin words in gold
lettering, and on the floor is a large ouroboros in a
triquetra carved in a circle – once again the Society’s
own symbol, but now surrounded by nine smaller
symbols. Finally, the room has three identical arched
doors leading in different directions, each decorated
with a woodcarving of a beautiful tree.
