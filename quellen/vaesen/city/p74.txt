CH A LLENGES

The text on the ceilingreads “Nomina si nescis, perit
et cognitio rerum.” A character who passes a LEARNING
test knows that it means “Who knows not the names,
knows not the subject” and that the quote is attributed
to Carl Linnaeus.
The floor reliefis illustrated on this page (also available as handout 3C on page 114 and for download
on the Free League website). Anyone who examines it realizes that the nine smaller symbols surrounding the Society’s symbol can be pressed down.
A successful INVESTIGATION test reveals that the
ouroboros in the symbol – the snake biting its
own tail – is a separate round slab with a small gap
between it and the rest of the floor. A character
who passes a STEALTH test notices that there seems
to be a hidden mechanism that could open a large
hatch in the floor. The slab with the ouroboros
is actually the door to Linnaeus’s secret library
and the endpoint of the mystery (page 79).
The Secret Passage: If the three correct symbols
in the floor relief – and only those symbols – are
pressed simultaneously, there is a loud rumble as
the slab sinks about a meter into the floor, revealing a dark, narrow staircase that leads to the secret

library. The symbols that must be pressed to open
the secret passage are the sheaf, the bellows, and the
knife. If the wrong three symbols are pressed at the
same time, the mechanism is locked for an hour
and the symbols cannot be pressed at all. Then
the players will likely realize that they must pass
through the three doors in the room to figure out
the solution. If not, Linnaeus’s ghost can point
this out to them (see the sidebar on page 77).
Each doorin the room is made of light wood and
has a beautiful woodcarving of a large tree in the
center. The door frame is carved into a wreath of
seeds, sprouts, flowers, and leaves. See the image
on page 69 (handout 3D on page 115, also available for download on the Free League website).
A character who passes a LEARNING test realizes that
the carvings represent a linden tree and its phases
of growth. A successful FORCE test reveals that the
doors themselves are also made of this light softwood.
The doors – facing east, west, and north – have
brass handles and open easily. On the other side
it is dark and damp, and a cold wind sweeps into
the room. See the sidebar on the next page for
more information about the doors and where
they lead.
