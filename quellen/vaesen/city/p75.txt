Door 1:
LINNAEUS’S CHILDHOOD HOME

THE DOORS TO LINNAEUS’S MEMORIES

When the player characters pass through the door,
they are swallowed by darkness. As their eyes adjust,
they realize they are standing under a large linden
tree at the edge of a garden next to a small, thatched
cottage. It is evening and quiet. The moon illuminates the garden beds of flowers and vegetables. Tall
birch trees with wiry branches cast long shadows
across the yard.
The characters realize that they are child versions
of themselves, perhaps eight or nine years old. They
are wearing simple, old-­fashioned peasant clothing and carry no equipment. Their stats are intact
except for their Physique, which is temporarily halved
(rounded up). Let the characters react to all this and
think about where they are.
Before they can do anything else, they see a small
shadow moving across the yard from the stable. It
is a boy of the same age as them, dressed in similar
clothes. He stops, takes a small wooden box out of
his pocket, and seems to be talking to it.

The three linden wood doors in the Chamber of
Riddles are part of an ancient locking mech­
anism created by Linnaeus and his disciple
Hedda von der Burg, who had deep esoteric
knowledge, to preserve the secrets of vaesen
he discovered during his expeditions to Lap­
land and Dalecarlia.
For an ordinary person, the doors lead to
empty stone chambers. But for a person with
the Sight, they lead into Linnaeus’s memories.
Each door leads to a specific memory and a
challenge, which in turn provides a clue to one
of the three symbols that must be pressed in
the Chamber of Riddles to open the passage
to the secret library.
As they pass through the doors, the player
characters’ age, appearance, and equipment
change and adapt to the environment in the
memory on the other side. Each challenge
involves a confrontation with a vaesen. The chal­
lenges can be solved through cunning or violence.
Let each character decide whether to enter
a door, and if so, which one. A person who
enters a door disappears from sight (includ­
ing any light source) and does not respond if
called. The player is temporarily out of the
game until all characters have entered a door
(or chosen not to).
No matter which door the characters
choose, the first leads to Linnaeus’s childhood
home (this page), the second to Reuterholm’s
residence (page 74), and the third to the
Dance of Death (page 77). If the characters
enter different doors, play each challenge
in the order above with the characters that
are present.
Once the characters have passed through a
door, it disappears behind them and they can­
not return to the Chamber of Riddles until the
challenge is completed, one way or the other.

CH A LLENGES

The Boy: If the player characters make themselves
known, the boy puts the box away. He speaks in a
Smolandian dialect that sounds strangely archaic.
He says his name is Carl Nilsson Linnaeus and that
this is his family’s house in Råshult in the parish
of Stenbrohult in Smolandia. Carl is eight years
old and interested in all things in nature. If they
ask him what year it is, he is puzzled. “The year of
our Lord 1715 of course, don’t you know?” His parents are
not at home – only he and his little sister Anna
Maria are here.
The Cottage: If the characters gain young Linnaeus’s trust with an INSPIRATION test, he lets them
into the cottage and asks them for help. The cottage consists of a vestibule, a main room with a
fireplace, a kitchen area, and narrow beds for the
adults, and an inner room where the children
sleep. Inside, sheaves of barley are hanging from
the rafters to dry after the autumn harvest.
