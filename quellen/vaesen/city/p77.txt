The Sister: Carl’s sister Anna Maria, who is in
the cottage, has been having nightmares for the
past few weeks and wakes up screaming every night.
She says it feels like she is suffocating. Carl adds
that he has seen an eerie black smoke creeping in
through a knothole at night. He is determined to
save his sister. If Carl has not let the characters
into the cottage, they will instead hear Anna Maria
screaming in terror later that night.
Research: If the players have access to the Vaesen
art book or the Codex Occultum booklet (included
in the game’s starter set), they can figure out for
themselves that they are likely dealing with a
mare and get tips on how to banish it. Otherwise,
you can have them make a LEARNING test to recall
clues (see below). The knowledgeable young Carl
can also make suggestions – he has heard stories
about mares.
The Ride of the Mare: Anna Maria eventually
falls asleep in the children’s bedroom, and each
player character must pass an OBSERVATION test not
to do the same. Later that night, the mare (page
142 of the core rulebook) attacks. A dark mist
sweeps toward the cottage like oily black smoke
and enters the children’s bedroom through a
knothole. The smoke gathers around Anna and
forms a hideous female figure on her chest. If the
characters show up or are already there, the figure turns to them, baring its sharp teeth before
launching a furious attack. Sleeping characters
miss the first round of combat.
Banishing the Mare: The Mare can be banished
by force or cunning (see Clues below). It takes
damage from physical attacks, but remember that
the characters’ Physique scores are halved and that
they have no weapons with them. However, they
can find an axe, a knife, and several chairs in the
cottage that can be used as weapons. If all player

CLUES

Mare’s Brooms: The birch trees around the house
are full of mare’s brooms – twigs growing in dense,
sheaf-­like clusters. A mare’s broom can be used
to lure a mare (fast action). The mare must then
make a MIGHT test – on failure, the mare must stop
what it is doing and attack the character holding
the mare’s broom on its next turn.
Mare Cross: Drawing a mare cross – a five- or
six-­pointed star – on someone’s forehead protects that person from the mare’s attack. Doing
so during combat counts as a slow action for both
the drawer and the person being drawn on. If
everyone around mare has a mare’s cross on their
forehead, the creature flees, howling with rage.
Counting Grains: The mare suffers from obsessive thoughts, which can be used to trick it – if
someone throws grains or seeds in front of it (long
action), the mare must pass a MIGHT test to resist
counting them all, which consumes its next turn
in combat. Sacks of grain and jars of flaxseed can
be found in the kitchen.
The Worm: If the player characters ask Carl about
his box, he reveals that it contains his lucky worm.
He has always wanted to know more about nature
but had difficulty learning to read. One day two
years ago, he fished the worm out of a stream, and
he has done well in school ever since. He takes care
of it because it brings him luck. The worm is of
course a spertus (page 154 of the core rulebook),
which clever players may realize. Under no circumstances will Carl let anyone take the box from
him, not even for a moment.
Barley Sheaves: Sheaves of barley are hanging
from the rafters to dry after the autumn harvest.
This, along with the sheaf-­like mare’s brooms, will
hopefully be enough for the players to realize that
the sheaf is one of the three symbols to be pressed
in the floor relief in the Chamber of Riddles.
