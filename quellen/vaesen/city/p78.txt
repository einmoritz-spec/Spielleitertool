Door 2:
REUTERHOLM’S RESIDENCE
When the player characters pass through this door,
they are enveloped in darkness, just like the first time.
The door behind them is gone, but soon they hear a
murmur of voices, music, and clinking glasses. They
are in a dark room, a large linen closet, with a door –
the voices and music are coming from the other side,
and a sliver of light shines through the cracks.
Opening the door, the characters find themselves in an ornate hall with candelabras and mirrors. The decor is almost palatial in its grandeur.
Outside there is a beautiful park with impressive
flower urns, low hedges, and lavender bushes. A
short distance away is a small chapel and a hill with
a huge old tree stump. It is dusk, and a thin mist
has settled over the lawn.
The characters now realize that they are wearing
18th century clothing, with white wigs, silk waistcoats,
and ruffled shirts or extravagant dresses and powdered hairstyles. They are adults and have their usual
stats, but lack weapons and equipment. They are in
Linnaeus’s memory of his dinner with Governor Nils

Reuterholm at his residence in Falun, just before
the famous expedition in Dalecarlia in 1734.
A servant in a ruffled shirt, livery, and a white
wig enters the room and offers them a glass of wine
from a silver tray: “Welcome! Governor Reuterholm is waiting for you in the dining room – dinner will be served shortly. Your
professor is already there.”
The dining room is beautifully set for dinner,
with wines in crystal glasses. A large fireplace spreads
light and warmth throughout the room. Among the
gathered are Linnaeus himself, his student Hedda
von der Burg, who is also the goddaughter of Governor Reuterholm, as well as the governor and his
wife. Show their portraits to the players. “Welcome,
apostles, you are just in time for dinner,” Linnaeus exclaims,
and Reuterholm invites them to sit down.
Linnaeus (and everyone else) perceives the characters as members of his expedition and is completely
oblivious to talk of strange doors and riddles. They
are very excited about the upcoming expedition, and
the characters are welcome to join the conversation.
See Challenges for more information.
What no one in the group knows is that Reuterholm’s residence is built on land that contains an old
fairy mound. The great linden tree that once grew
on the mound was cut down and its wood was used to
make the beautiful door that now adorns the entrance
to the house. This angered the fairies, who caused
all kinds of trouble during the construction. Now
they seek revenge by trying to abduct Reuterholm’s
goddaughter, Hedda von der Burg.
