Linnaeus waves enthusiastically at the player characters and asks for their opinions. He then ends
the conversation by saying “ ‘Nomina si nescis, perit et
cognitio rerum.’ ‘Who knows not the names, knows not the subject.’ Remember this, my apostles.” After this, Hedda von
der Burg excuses herself and leaves the table for a
while. She goes out into the park to get some air,
walking past the chapel and over to the mound
with the tree stump. The characters can follow
her if they wish.
The Snuff Box: With a successful VIGILANCE test,
the characters see Linnaeus fidgeting with a gilded
snuff box with his monogram “CL” on it. When
asked about it, he smiles secretively and calls the
box his “little lucky charm” but will not say more about
it. Astute players may recall that the snuff box
resembles the one they may have seen August T.
Lysander carry in previous mysteries (page 25
and 43). If the players want to know if it is the
same box, you should say that it could be but that
it is impossible to say for sure. Linnaeus will not
open or part with the box under any circumstances.
The Attack of the Fairies: Shortly afterwards, a
scream is heard from the garden. Hedda von der
Burg is standing in a ring of mushrooms growing
around the stump of an old linden tree – a so-­
called fairy ring. She appears to be under attack by
a swarm of insects. With a successful VIGILANCE test,
the characters can see small winged fairies tearing
at her clothes and trying to drag her to a hole in
the ground at the roots of the tree stump. “Hurry,
my apostles! It looks like our skills are needed,” shouts Linnaeus. If any player characters are with Hedda von
der Burg on the mound, they are attacked as well.
Banishing the Fairies: The fairies will attack anyone who enters the fairy ring. For more on fairies
and how to combat them, see page 164 of the core
rulebook. Note that they get +2 to MAGIC because

·

it is dusk. Fairies can be dealt with by cunning or
violence. If the players have access to the Vaesen art
book or the Codex Occultum booklet (included in the
game’s starter set), the texts can provide useful tips
on how to banish them. Otherwise, you can let
them make LEARNING tests to gain clues (see below),
or simply have Linnaeus help them.
Reuterholm: When the interlude is over, Governor Nils Reuterholm storms out into the yard,
red with rage. “Fairies, in my own garden! Linnaeus, I told
you these creatures you’re so passionate about are a danger to
the public. They must be controlled – fought in the name of the
king!” Then the illusion of Linnaeus’s memory
fades and the characters find themselves in a cold
stone chamber with a door back to the Chamber
of Riddles.

Holy Water: Splashing holy water (slow action) at
the fairies can temporarily disperse them. They
must make a MIGHT test, and if they fail, they lose
their next turn. There is some holy water in a
baptismal font at the chapel in the manor’s park.
