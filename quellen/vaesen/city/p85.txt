AFTERMATH

LINNAEUS’S JOURNAL

After the showdown in Linnaeus’s secret library, this
mystery is over. Lysander promises to look after the
library and let the player characters visit it whenever
they want. However, he is against taking any books
from there. Linnaeus’s collection of writings technically functions as the Occult Library Upgrade at the
Society’s headquarters (page 92 of the core rulebook),
except that it requires a visit to Stockholm. If the Society already has an Occult Library, they get both effects.

HOMO FERUS
Anyone who consults Linnaeus’s journal Homo Ferus
gains +2 to LEARNING tests concerning vaesen. A character who studies the book for a few weeks and spends
5 XP gets this bonus permanently and no longer has
to flip through the book. It effectively becomes a
new talent.

THE TRUTH A BOUT LINNA EUS
An entry at the end of Homo Ferus reveals what happened
after the expedition to Dalecarlia and why Linnaeus
left the Society. The entry in the adjacent sidebar is
also available as handout 3E on page 116 and for download on the Free League website. There is nothing
the player characters can do with this information
at this time, but it will be useful in the next mystery.

My worst fears have come true! The odious
Reuterholm has taken everything from me, and
claimed for the King all the precious artifacts I
have collected in the name of truth and wisdom!
The lyre of the Neck, whose origin and power
I can only imagine, is now lost to me and has
become a tool in His Majesty’s armory. A disgrace without parallel!
But this is not my greatest misfortune.
Reuter­holm also robbed me of my lucky charm,
the little worm that has been my faithful companion since I was a boy. Without this spertus,
which gave me the Sight, I am lost! I can no
longer see the vaesen that were once visible
to me, and the world around me now seems
a dull, gray chain of inexorably passing time.
I have no choice but to renounce my membership in the Society and hide my writings
on the supernatural beings I have studied, for
the risk of these volumes falling into the wrong
hands is too great. The King’s greed knows no
bounds, and I have not the power to oppose him.
Therefore, I am forced to retire and spend
the rest of my days studying the more mundane
creations of nature. Forgive me, O Lord, but I
have no choice.
—Carl Linnaeus
