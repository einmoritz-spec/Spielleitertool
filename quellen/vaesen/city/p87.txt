BACKGROUND
The Neck, a powerful vaesen in Nordic folklore, has
come to the capital, drawn by the crowds and the revolutionary atmosphere in the streets. He has taken on
the form of the poet Erik Johan Stagnelius and has been
mingling with Stockholm’s artists and poets for years.
In moments of weakness, the Neck cannot help but
envy the humans. He has recently begun stirring up
unrest in the city, inciting aggression between policemen and rioters. Driven by a desire to connect with
people, he performs at street parties and taverns, but
his music has a powerful effect on those around him
and his presence often leads to violence and death.
Meanwhile, cork magnate August T. Lysander has
been avidly studying the writings in Carl Linnaeus’s
secret library (see the mystery The Haunted Library), particularly the journals from the botanist’s expedition
to Dalecarlia and the period that followed. Lysander
is fascinated by Linnaeus’s encounter with the Neck
and how he came across the creature’s lyre.
Lysander kept reading, his interest growing as he
learned more about Governor Nils Reuterholm who
had financed the Dalecarlia expedition on behalf of
the crown. On the expedition’s return, Reuterholm
confiscated both the Neck’s lyre and Linnaeus’s own
“lucky worm” – the spertus that had brought the botanist success as well as the Sight. Viewing vaesen as
a threat, Reuterholm demanded these artifacts in
the name of the king. Devastated, Linnaeus left the
Society shortly thereafter, hiding all his occult writings for fear they would fall into the wrong hands,
and devoted the remainder of his career to studying
more mundane creatures in nature.
Knowing that Linnaeus was the original owner
of the spertus he now carried – the vaesen that could
cost him his soul (page 6) – Lysander had an idea.
The only force that could possibly break the curse of
the spertus is another very powerful vaesen, like the

Neck. But to get the Neck’s help, Lysander must offer
something that the creature wants. And Lysander
knows just the thing.
With the help of two associates with the Sight, the
renegade Rosenbergers Helena von Arensburg and
Xavier Liebholz, Lysander has contacted the Neck and
offered him his long-­lost lyre. The Neck is a fickle
vaesen and has made no promises to Lysander, but the
thought of the lyre has stirred up a storm of emotions
within him. For the past few weeks, he has been stoking
the flames of unrest in the capital, hoping to unleash
total chaos on the streets once he gets his lyre back.
Lysander knows what is about to happen, but he
is willing to stain the streets of the capital with blood
to escape his fate. With the Neck’s help, he plans to
stage a riot on Castle Slope, use the chaos as a cover
to break into the Royal Armory, steal the Neck’s lyre,
and return it to its original owner.
But there is one thing Lysander has not taken into
account. While studying Linnaeus’s journals and
devising a plan to return the lyre to the Neck, he was
being watched by the ghost of Carl Linnaeus. Linnaeus knows that the lyre is an incredibly powerful
artifact that, in the hands of the Neck, could make all
of Stockholm dance itself to death. The old botanist’s
ghost makes a desperate attempt to warn the player
characters of the terrible fate that could befall the city.

PREVIOUS ENCOUNTERS?
The player characters first encountered the
Neck in The Haunted Library, in Linnaeus’s
memory, and may have realized that he resem­
bles the poet Stagnelius. They can confront
Stagnelius about this at the Golden Peace (page
89). If the player characters have played
through A Dance with Death in the book Seasons
of Mystery, they may also have encountered the
Neck in real life – now he is back, even if they
managed to banish him before, but perhaps
he has a score to settle with the characters…
