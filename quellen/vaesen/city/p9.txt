He took good care of the creature, and as an adult
he kept it in a golden snuff box bearing his monogram. In return, the spertus brought him luck and
success, and his career flourished.
During his 1730s expedition to Dalecarlia, funded
by County Governor Nils Reuterholm on behalf of
the king, Linnaeus and his apostles encountered
the powerful vaesen known as the Neck (page 144 of
the core rulebook). Playing a strange lyre, the Neck
had bewitched an entire wedding party into dancing
themselves to death in a remote village. Linnaeus and
his apostles managed to banish the Neck, whose lyre
was left on the riverbank.
The lyre looked ancient and was engraved with
characters that Linnaeus recognized as ancient Greek.
Could this be the lyre of Orpheus himself, the one
he played so beautifully that even Hades, god of the
Underworld, was moved to compassion? Either way,
Linnaeus realized that the lyre was a powerful magical
artifact that needed to be protected.
When he returned, Linnaeus proudly reported his
discovery to Reuterholm, but the governor’s reaction
was not at all what he had expected. To his horror,
Reuterholm declared that the lyre was to be confiscated by the Crown – he would not risk such a powerful

object falling into the wrong hands. As if that were
not enough, Reuterholm also seized the snuff box
containing Linnaeus’s spertus, which the botanist
had carelessly told him about. Despite his fear and
hatred of the supernatural, Reuterholm could not
resist taking the spertus for himself.
Without his spertus, Linnaeus lost the Sight. Devastated by this setback, he left the Society and hid
all his writings in a hidden cellar vault, fearing that
someone might use them for nefarious purposes. He
staged a fire at the Society’s headquarters and claimed
it had destroyed all records of his expedition, so that
no one would look for them. Only Hedda von der
Burg, Linnaeus’s most trusted apostle, was told the
truth, to preserve his knowledge for posterity. Using
her esoteric expertise, they sealed the secret library
with a magic lock.
Linnaeus devoted the rest of his career to the study
of more mundane creatures, but when he died many
years later, his inquisitive spirit lingered in this world.
He became a restless ghost that continued to haunt the
secret library where his journal, Homo Ferus, still rests.
The spertus brought Reuterholm some success, but
he despised the creature and treated it badly. Whether
by chance or by the spertus’ own will, the snuff box fell
out of Reuterholm’s pocket during a boat trip in the
Stockholm archipelago and vanished into the deep.
It would be a hundred years before someone found
it again – and became the third owner of the spertus.
