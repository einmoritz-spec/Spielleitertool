Bad Omens: If the characters gain her trust with
a successful INSPIRATION test, the librarian explains
that she has been unable to sleep because she keeps
seeing bad omens all around Stockholm. She has
searched the library for anything that might shed
light on the situation, but what she has uncovered has only added to her worries. See more
under Clues.
Linnaeus’s Library: If the characters wish to visit
Linnaeus’s library, they are free to do so. There is
no ghost to be seen and they can easily find their
way down there (see map on page 68). A successful INVESTIGATION test can reveal interesting
clues in the library. See more under Clues.
Information About Vaesen: In the Esoteric Section and Linnaeus’s secret library, the characters
can make a LEARNING test (with +2 in Linnaeus’s
library) to find information about night ravens,
spertuses, and the Neck. Each character may roll
once, and each success yields useful information.
See more under Clues. If the players have access to
the Vaesen art book or Codex Occultum from the Starter
Set, those may be consulted as well.
CLUES

The old bookSigrid is studying is called On the Signs
and Wonders That Preceded the Liturgical Uproar. Written
and illustrated in the 16th century by the priest
Joen Petri Klint, it describes a series of omens such
as sun dogs, dragons flying over the land, animals
born with deformities, and not least, the Neck
playing in the Stockholm Stream. Klint believed
these signs were evidence of divine displeasure at
humanity’s moral decay.
The Neck in the Stream: Sigrid tells them that she
has personally seen the Neck playing in the water
near Knights’ Isle. She opens Klint’s book and
points to a figure with accompanying text. In 1599,

Fire Mill: If the visit takes place after the fire at the
Fire Mill (step #1 of the countdown), Sigrid recalls
seeing a large raven flying low over Knights’ Bay
at dusk on the night of the fire. She is convinced
that this too was an omen.
Visitors: If the player characters ask about visitors
to Linnaeus’s secret library, Sigrid replies that the
charming “Mr. Lysander” has been there several times
in recent weeks, accompanied by a servant and two
others. She describes the latter as a strange pair –
both dressed entirely in black, and the man had
an unpleasant look on his face. The people she is
describing are Xavier Liebholz and Helena von
Arensburg (pages 99 and 100). If the characters describe them to her, Sigrid confirms that
they are the ones she saw.
Linnaeus’s Journal: Down in Linnaeus’s secret
library, there is no sign of the ghost. However,
the book Homo Ferus lies open on its lectern (unless
the player characters moved it after the last mystery), on the page where Linnaeus explains why he
left the Society and describes how the Neck’s lyre
was seized on behalf of the king (see handout 3E on
page 116, also available for download on the Free
League website). The players have probably already
read this, but there is no harm in reminding them.
The Black Feather: On the floor in a corner of
Linnaeus’s library lies a strange black feather – like
that of a raven, but unusually large.
The night raven(page 146 of the core rulebook)
is the bitter spirit of a suicide or criminal buried
in unholy ground. It takes the form of a raven-­like,
night-­black bird of oily smoke and scruffy feathers with glowing eyes. It flies low to the ground,
always at night, supposedly seeking the tomb of
Christ. A night raven can only be harmed by holy
weapons or church silver. It can be controlled by
placing three of its feathers between the pages of
a bible along with a passion flower. If the feathers
are removed, the control is broken.
