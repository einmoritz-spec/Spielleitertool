An early volume of the annual transactions of
the local historical and archaeological society
includes some notes reprinted from the works
of the antiquarian William Stukely, who visited
the area in 1721. Stukeley mentions a cave known
locally as “Old Meg’s Holt” and notes that the
name “Old Meg” refers to “an antient Standing
Stone nearby, which local Tradition holds to be
the body of a terrible Witch, turn’d to Stone by the
intervention of a Saint in the Saxon age, who was
engaged in converting the local Population.” This
is a clue that something supernatural is at work.
