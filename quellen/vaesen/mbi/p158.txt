BIBLIOGRAPHY
A great many books have been written on the folklore
and fairy lore of Britain and Ireland. The following
titles were consulted for this book, and they are recommended to readers who want to research further.

BOOKS
Arrowsmith, Nancy, and Moorse, George. A
Field Guide to the Little People (Wallaby, 1977).
Covers much of Europe, and also has a useful
bibliography.
Bord, Janet. Fairies: Real Encounters with Little People
(Carroll & Graf, 1997). A study of the
phenomenon of faerie encounters from the
Middle Ages to the present.
Bourke, Angela. The Burning of Bridget Cleary: A True
Story (Penguin USA, 2001). A young wife dies
in 1895 from her husband’s attempts to counter
supposed faerie bewitchment.
Briggs, Katharine. A Dictionary of Fairies (Penguin,
1977, republished by Pantheon Books, New
York as An Encyclopedia of Fairies). A
comprehensive reference work on the faerie
lore of Britain and Ireland.
Briggs, Katherine. The Vanishing People: A Study
of Traditional Fairy Beliefs (B.T. Batsford,
1978).
Briggs, Katharine. British Folk-­Tales and Legends: A
Sampler (Routledge & Kegan Paul, 1977).
Brooks, J. A., ed. Cornish Ghosts and Legends (Jarrold &
Sons, 1981).
Cooper, Joe. The Case of the Cottingley Fairies (Robert
Hale, 1990). A famous fairy hoax from the
1920s that even convinced the creator of
Sherlock Holmes.
Crawford, James, Lesley Ferguson, and Kristina
Watson. Victorian Scotland (Royal Commission

on the Ancient and Historical Monuments of
Scotland, 2010).
Doyle, Arthur Conan. The Complete Sherlock Holmes
(various editions). There are several versions
of this collection under similar titles. Holmes’s
adventures give a useful inside view of late-­
Victorian London and Britain. Of particular
interest are ‘The Hound of the Baskervilles’
and ‘The Adventure of the Sussex Vampire’,
in which Holmes finds mundane solutions
to seemingly supernatural cases. In Vaesen, of
course, such cases could be exactly as they seem
on the surface, and some of Holmes’s mundane
cases could turn out to have supernatural
explanations.
Dubois, Pierre. The Great Encyclopedia of Fairies (Simon
& Schuster, 2000).
Eason, Cassandra. A Complete Guide to Faeries and
Magical Beings (Weiser, 2002).
Evans-­Wentz, Walter Y. Fairy-­Faith in Celtic Countries
(Citadel 1990).
Flanders, Judith. The Victorian City: Everyday Life in
Dickens’ London (St. Martin’s Griffin 2015).
Jackson, Lee. A Dictionary of Victorian London: An A-­Z of
the Great Metropolis (Anthem Nineteenth Century
Studies, 2006).
James I of England, VI of Scotland. Daemonologie
(various editions). While it mainly concerns
witches, this book touches upon other areas
of the supernatural as seen from the early 17th
century.
Jones, Evan David. Victorian and Edwardian Wales from
Old Photographs (Batsford, 1981).
Matthews, John and Caitlin. The Element Encyclopedia
of Magical Creatures (Harper Element, 2005)
