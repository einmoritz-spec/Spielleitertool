Seth, Ronald. In the Name of the Devil (Walker, 1969).
Includes an account of the trial and confession
of Isobel Gowdie, an accused witch who
admitted to consorting with fairies in 1662.
Shakespeare, William. A Midsummer Night’s Dream.
Various editions are available of Shakespeare’s
famous fairy play.
Spenser, Edmund. The Faerie Queene (Penguin
Classics, 1988). One of several editions of
the epic poem that are currently available.
Dismissed by some as an extended work of
flattery aimed at Elizabeth I, it is nonetheless
a turning point in the treatment of fairies in
literature.
Thomson, John. Victorian London Street Life in Historic
Photographs (Dover Publications, 1994).

Wales, Gerald of (a.k.a. Giraldus Cambrensis).
The Journey Through Wales and The Description of Wales
(various editions). A 12th-­century churchman
describes various places and happenings in
Wales, including supernatural and faerie
encounters.
Wales, Gerald of (a.k.a. Giraldus Cambrensis). The
History and Topography of Ireland (various editions).
An account of Ireland in the 12th century,
including many anecdotes of miraculous and
supernatural occurrences.
Werner, Alex, and Tony Williams. Dickens’s Victorian
London: 1839–1901 (Ebury Press, 2012).
Wilde, Lady. Ancient Legends, Mystic Charms, and
Superstitions of Ireland (various editions).
Yeats, W. B. (ed.). Fairy and Folk Tales of the Irish Peasantry

1888 edition by the famous Irish poet.
