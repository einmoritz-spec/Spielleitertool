                               PREFACE
 When I was six years old, I started school. One rainy afternoon, while
 exploring my school in Stockholm’s Old Town, I came across a door I had
 never noticed before. I walked through it and found myself in an old school
 library. Standing in the middle of the room, I was overwhelmed. The walls
 were lined with shelves, packed with thousands of books. Then the librar-
 ian tapped me on the shoulder. She explained how libraries worked – that
 I could choose any book I wanted and borrow it to read.
     It felt like I had stumbled upon a treasure trove. I wandered around for
 a long time before finally picking The House of Arden by Edith Nesbit. I’m so
 grateful that the librarian did not suggest a shorter book, but simply said:
“Great choice for your first check-out! Come back and tell me what you
 think when you’re done.”
     That was the first of many visits to the library. I would sit for hours in
 one of its window niches, devouring non-fiction books on ghosts, folklore,
 mythology, magic, and medicine. Short stories and novels by Jules Verne,
 C.S. Lewis, Mary Shelley, Frances Burnett, Oscar Wilde, Susan Cooper,
 Ursula K. Le Guin, and J.R.R. Tolkien awakened my love of fantasy, hor-
 ror, and science fiction. For me, books and writing have remained portals
 through time and space and to other worlds.
     I’m so grateful to Free League for the opportunity to contribute to the
 Vaesen roleplaying game. To me, roleplaying culture is all about co-creation.
 We who write the adventures provide the framework, but what happens
 next is up to your imagination. One of my favorite parts of going to con-
 ventions is hearing gaming groups tell stories about adventures I’ve writ-
 ten. Suddenly, new doors open to parallel realities.
     This campaign book is inspired by 19th century literature, music, and
 poetry. It is also a love letter to my hometown of Stockholm, seen through
 the lens of fantasy, and to magical places like libraries and their guardians.
 In my writing, I have tried to create a place where people, mysteries, and
 vaesen meet in the hustle and bustle of the capital. I can’t wait to hear what
 you will get up to there!
                                                             Kiku Pukk Härenstam
                                     Old Town, Stockholm, April 13th 2025

· The spertus(page 154 of the core rulebook) is
  a magical being, often appearing in the form of
    a shiny black bug or a worm. It is said to be in
    league with the Devil, and humans can use its
    powers to gain wealth and success. The spertus
    usually lives in a snuff box and needs to be fed
    every day with human spit. Sooner or later the
    creature will take control of its owner, unless it
    is sold or given away. But it can only be passed
    on twice. The third person to accept the creature
    will never be rid of it, and the spertus will assume
    control of its owner.

    sitive to Christian symbols and steel. He can be
    prevented from using magic by sticking a knife in
    the ground when talking to him. He hates places

    banished by luring him to a place where he is com-
    pletely surrounded by Christian symbols and steel.
·   Mock Suns: If the player characters wish to learn
    more about mock suns, they can find information
    in the Esoteric Section. A mock sun is a mete-
    orological phenomenon in which one or more
    sun-­like points of light appear next to the sun.
    It is related to the halo phenomenon, a circle of
    light surrounding the sun. A famous painting of
    mock suns over 16th century Stockholm (adjacent)
    hangs in Stockholm Cathedral. Mock suns used
    to be called sun dogs and have long been viewed
    as bad omens of dark times ahead.
